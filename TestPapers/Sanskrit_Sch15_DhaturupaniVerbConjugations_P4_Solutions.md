## Worked Solutions

**1. (D)** The initial अ- with the bare ending -त् is the past augment of लङ्, so the time is past, not present or future. The ending -त् with सः marks the Sanskrit 'first person' (English third) singular, giving 'he read'.

**2. (A)** Present पठति, past takes the अ- augment giving अपठत्, future inserts -ष्य- giving पठिष्यति, the imperative third-singular is पठतु, and the optative is पठेत्. The option that drops the अ- augment on the past, the one that swaps present and past, and the one that swaps the imperative and optative are each wrong by one feature.

**3. (B)** The ending -तु on the third-singular is the imperative (लोट्) marker expressing a command or permission — 'let him read'. The optative 'should read' would be पठेत्, and the plain present would be पठति.

**4. (C)** The -एत् ending marks the optative विधिलिङ्, which conveys obligation or advice — 'should read'. A command 'let him read' would be पठतु, and the past 'read' would carry the augment, अपठत्.

**5. (A)** The optative expresses what should or ought to be done (advice, rule, wish), as in 'all should speak the truth'. The other choices name the past, present and future indicative, which are different लकारs.

**6. (A)** The अ- prefixed to a verb to mark past tense (the लङ् augment, as in अपठत्) is grammatically different from the negative अ- of nouns like अधर्मः ('non-righteousness'). Treating either one as the other reverses their roles.

**7. (D)** The adverb अधुना ('now') fixes the present tense, so गम् gives गच्छति. The augmented अगच्छत् is past, गमिष्यति is future, and गच्छतु is an imperative.

**8. (D)** Past tense of गम् takes the अ- augment on the present stem गच्छ-, giving अगच्छत् ('he went'). गच्छति is present, गमिष्यति future, and अगच्छन् is the past PLURAL ('they went').

**9. (A)** श्वः ('tomorrow') forces the future लृट्, so पठ् becomes पठिष्यति. पठति is present, अपठत् past, and पठेत् the optative 'should read'.

**10. (B)** ह्यः ('yesterday') fixes past tense and the plural subject बालकाः requires the plural ending, giving अगच्छन्. अगच्छत् is past SINGULAR, गच्छन्ति is present plural, and गमिष्यन्ति is future plural.

**11. (C)** अभवत् carries the past augment, भवति is present, and भविष्यति has the future -ष्य-; ordered earliest-first that is past, present, future. The other orderings misplace one or more along the timeline.

**12. (B)** The -तु ending marks the imperative third-singular 'let it be'. The present would be भवति, the optative भवेत् ('should be'), and the past अभवत् ('was').

**13. (C)** स्यात् is the optative of अस्, expressing 'should/may be'. The present 'is' is अस्ति, the past 'was' is आसीत्, and the imperative 'let it be' is अस्तु.

**14. (A)** पुरा ('long ago') marks past time, and the past third-singular of अस् is आसीत् ('was'). अस्ति is present, स्यात् optative, and अस्तु imperative.

**15. (B)** कृ gives present करोति, past अकरोत् (augment on strong stem), future करिष्यति, imperative करोतु, and optative कुर्यात् (built on the weak stem कुर्). The optative is कुर्यात्, not 'करेत्'; the past keeps its augment; and the future/imperative forms करिष्यति/करोतु are regular.

**16. (D)** कुर्यात् is the optative third-singular of कृ, 'he should do'. The first-person optative would be कुर्याम्, the imperative 'let him do' is करोतु, and the third-plural optative is कुर्युः.

**17. (B)** Advice/obligation calls for the optative विधिलिङ्, whose कृ third-singular is कुर्यात्. करोति is plain present, अकरोत् past, and करोतु the imperative.

**18. (B)** The imperative 'let him drink' uses the लोट् third-singular पिबतु (पा takes the present stem पिब-). पिबति is present, अपिबत् past, and पिबेत् the optative 'should drink'.

**19. (D)** Third-plural present takes -न्ति, giving पिबन्ति. पिबति is singular, पिबतः dual, and अपिबन् is the PAST plural ('they drank').

**20. (A)** दृश् forms its present on the stem पश्य-, so 'he sees' is पश्यति. अपश्यत् is past 'he saw', and पश्यतु is the imperative 'let him see'.

**21. (C)** Past tense puts the अ- augment on the present stem पश्य-, giving अपश्यत् ('he saw'). पश्यति is present, द्रक्ष्यति future, and पश्येत् the optative.

**22. (D)** The -न्ति ending without an augment is the present third-plural, 'they see'. The singular would be पश्यति, the past plural अपश्यन्, and 'we see' पश्यामः.

**23. (A)** Only अभाषत carries the initial अ- past augment of लङ्. भाषते is present, भाषिष्यते future, and भाषेत the optative — none of which take the augment.

**24. (B)** Atmanepada third-dual present is भाषेते. भाषतः is a parasmaipada-shaped (-तः) form, भाषन्ते is the plural, and भाषेताम् is the imperative dual.

**25. (A)** The atmanepada first-singular present is भाषे. भाषामि is the parasmaipada-style ending (wrong for this root), भाषते is third-singular, and भाषावहे is the first-dual.

**26. (C)** ते is third-plural, and the atmanepada third-plural present is भाषन्ते (with -न्ते, not the parasmaipada -न्ति भाषन्ति). भाषते is singular and भाषेते dual.

**27. (B)** भाष् is an atmanepada root, so its third-singular present must end in -ते (भाषते), not the parasmaipada -ति. The error is the wrong pada-class ending, not the subject or the root.

**28. (D)** Whether a root is parasmaipada or atmanepada is a fixed lexical property of the root, listed in the dictionary; the speaker does not choose it freely, nor does it depend on the subject's gender or the time of day.

**29. (D)** गच्छतु is the imperative third-singular 'let him go'. The present 'goes' would be गच्छति, the optative 'should go' गच्छेत्, and the past 'went' अगच्छत्.

**30. (C)** The optative second-singular (मध्यमपुरुष, English 'you') of पठ् is पठेः. पठेत् is third-singular 'he should read', पठेयम् first-singular 'I should read', and पठ the imperative 'read!'.

**31. (C)** The imperative second-singular of a class-1 parasmaipada root is the bare stem, पठ ('read!'). पठतु is third-singular 'let him read', पठसि the present 'you read', and पठेः the optative 'you should read'.

**32. (D)** The imperative first-singular of पठ् is पठानि ('let me read'). पठामि is the plain present 'I read', पठतु the third-singular imperative, and पठेयम् the optative 'I should read'.

**33. (B)** The optative third-plural of पठ् is पठेयुः, 'they should read'. The imperative 'let them read' is पठन्तु, 'we should read' पठेम, and the present 'they read' पठन्ति.

**34. (B)** बालकौ is dual, so the present third-dual पठतः is required. पठति is singular, पठन्ति plural, and अपठताम् the PAST dual ('the two read [yesterday]').

**35. (B)** The past dual takes the augment and the dual ending, giving अपठताम्. पठतः is the present dual, अपठत् the past SINGULAR, and अपठन् the past PLURAL.

**36. (C)** The proper past form is अपठत् — augment अ- plus the past ending -त्. 'अपठति' wrongly keeps the present ending -ति while also adding the augment, an impossible hybrid; either the augment or the ending betrays it.

**37. (A)** The past tense लङ् requires the अ- augment, so 'he went' is अगच्छत्. Dropping the augment ('गच्छत्') leaves no valid form; the present is गच्छति, not गच्छत्.

**38. (C)** The optative first-singular (उत्तमपुरुष) of पठ् is पठेयम्, 'I should read'. पठेत् is third-singular, पठेः second-singular, and पठानि the imperative 'let me read'.

**39. (D)** त्वम् ('you') takes the optative second-singular पठेः, and सः ('he') takes the third-singular पठेत्. Swapping them or using the first-singular पठेयम् breaks the person agreement.

**40. (A)** The optative first-plural (उत्तमपुरुष, 'we') of पठ् is पठेम. पठेयुः is third-plural 'they should', पठेव first-DUAL 'we two should', and पठाम the imperative first-plural 'let us read'.

**41. (C)** पठतु is the imperative third-singular ('let him read'), which clashes with the first-person subject अहम्. The matching imperative for 'I/let me' is पठानि, so both person and form must change together.

**42. (B)** कृ uses the weak stem कुर्- in कुर्मः ('we do'), as it does in कुर्वन्ति. The singular forms करोमि, करोषि, करोति all keep the strong stem करो-.

**43. (B)** 'Should do' is the optative, whose कृ third-singular is कुर्यात्. करोति is present 'does', करोतु imperative 'let him do', and अकरोत् past 'did'.

**44. (B)** अहम् ('I') takes करोमि and त्वम् ('you') takes करोषि. Swapping the two, or substituting the third-singular करोति for either, breaks subject-verb person agreement.

**45. (C)** कुर्वन्तु is the imperative third-plural 'let them do'. The present 'they do' is कुर्वन्ति, the optative 'they should do' कुर्युः, and 'let us do' करवाम/कुर्वाम-type first-plural.

**46. (D)** कृ does not conjugate like a class-1 root; it builds the present stem करो-, giving करोति. 'करति' wrongly treats कृ as if it added endings directly to the bare root.

**47. (D)** The first-plural present of अस् is स्मः ('we are'). अस्मि is first-singular 'I am', स्थः second-dual 'you two are', and सन्ति third-plural 'they are'.

**48. (C)** बालकौ is third-dual, so the present dual स्तः ('the two are') is needed. अस्ति is singular, सन्ति plural, and स्मः first-plural 'we are'.

**49. (B)** असि is the second-singular present, 'you are'. 'He is' is अस्ति, 'I am' अस्मि, and 'you all are' स्थ.

**50. (A)** Sanskrit प्रथमपुरुष ('first person') corresponds to the English THIRD person (he/she/it), because the numbering is reversed; it does not mean the English first person 'I'.

**51. (C)** The atmanepada optative third-singular of भाष् is भाषेत, 'he should speak'. The imperative 'let him speak' is भाषताम्, the past 'spoke' अभाषत, and the present 'speaks' भाषते.

**52. (A)** The atmanepada imperative third-singular of भाष् is भाषताम्. 'भाषतु' uses the parasmaipada -तु ending (wrong for this root); भाषते is present and भाषेत the optative.

**53. (D)** श्वः ('tomorrow') marks future, and the atmanepada future of भाष् keeps the -ते-type ending, giving भाषिष्यते. 'भाषिष्यति' wrongly uses the parasmaipada ending; भाषते is present and अभाषत past.

**54. (D)** आवाम् ('we two') agrees with the first-dual गच्छावः. The rejected sentences mismatch number (अहम् with a dual) or person (ते/त्वम् with the wrong-person verb).

**55. (C)** यूयम् ('you all') is second-plural, so the present पठथ is required. पठथः is second-DUAL, पठसि second-singular, and पठामः the first-plural 'we read'.

**56. (D)** ते is plural but अगच्छत् is the past singular; the single fix is to make the verb plural, अगच्छन् ('they went'). Both stay in the past — only the number is corrected.

**57. (A)** The flagged sentence mixes a past verb (अपठत्, लङ्) with a present verb (लिखति, लट्) for the same continuous narration, which is the tense-mismatch a reader must catch. The other three each stay within a single tense.

**58. (A)** श्वः ('tomorrow') fixes future and अहम् ('I') fixes first-singular, so गम् gives गमिष्यामि. गमिष्यति is third-singular future, गच्छामि present 'I go', and अगच्छम् past 'I went'.

**59. (C)** Obligation following 'orders that' takes the optative; the third-plural विधिलिङ् of भू is भवेयुः ('they should be'). भवन्ति is present, भवन्तु the imperative 'let them be', and अभवन् the past 'they became'.

**60. (A)** Two errors must both be fixed: लट् is the PRESENT, not the future, and भाषन्ते is the third-plural 'they speak' (atmanepada -न्ते), while 'we speak' would be भाषामहे. Options that correct only one point remain wrong.
