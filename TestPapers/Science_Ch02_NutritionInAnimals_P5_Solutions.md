## Worked Solutions

**1. (D)** Taking the bite in is ingestion; the enzyme cutting the molecule into smaller pieces is digestion; those pieces crossing the gut wall into the transport fluid is absorption; a cell finally using them to build itself is assimilation. The option that puts assimilation before absorption is impossible, because nutrients cannot be used by a cell until they have first entered the transport fluid that reaches it. The list starting with digestion before ingestion is wrong because nothing can be broken down before it is taken in. The list placing absorption before digestion fails because a large molecule cannot cross the gut wall until it has been cut into small pieces.

**2. (B)** Molecules already present in the blood are the proof: their arrival there means food was taken in, broken down, and carried across the gut wall, so the first three steps worked. The wasting-away despite their presence shows the cells are not putting them to use, which is the assimilation step. The claim that they never reached the blood contradicts the stated fact that they are circulating, so absorption did happen. Ingestion plainly occurred since food was taken in. Egestion concerns undigested residue leaving the body, not molecules already absorbed into blood, so it is unrelated to this failure.

**3. (B)** Egestion is specifically the removal of undigested food residue, which is exactly what Exit 1 carries. Exit 2 removes a gas produced inside cells during energy release, and any waste generated inside cells leaving the body is excretion. The label that calls Exit 1 excretion because it contains dead cells confuses the source: those dead cells travelled out mixed in the food residue, not as a chemical waste made for removal. The view that both are egestion ignores that egestion is tied to the food canal's leftovers, and the view that both are excretion ignores that fibre was never inside a cell.

**4. (B)** All three clips show food being taken into the body, which is ingestion; wrapping, swallowing whole, and sipping are merely different methods of the same step. They are not digestion, because none of the clips shows the chemical breakdown that happens after the food is inside. They are not absorption, because nutrients are not crossing into body fluids at the moment of feeding. They are not assimilation, because no cell is yet using anything; assimilation comes only after digestion and absorption.

**5. (A)** The external juice does not absorb anything into the fly; absorption means nutrients crossing into the body, and outside the body there is no body wall to cross. What the external juice does is start digestion, breaking solid food into a liquid that the fly then sucks in (ingestion) before any absorption occurs inside. The fly does not skip digestion; it merely starts some of it outside. It does not skip ingestion either, because sucking the liquid in IS ingestion.

**6. (D)** The point is about body plan: a single cell forms a temporary structure (the pseudopodium) that does double duty for movement and capture, whereas a many-organ body keeps a distinct fixed organ for each task. The extension does not perform digestion or absorption itself; those happen later inside a food vacuole, so the 'two chemical jobs at once' claim is wrong. It is not a permanent organ; its whole significance is that it is temporary. And it does not make bile; an Amoeba has no liver and the bile idea does not apply to single cells.

**7. (B)** Cutting teeth are the 8 incisors and tearing teeth are the 4 canines, giving 12; grinding teeth are the 8 premolars plus 12 molars, giving 20. Since 20 is greater than 12, grinding teeth outnumber cutting-and-tearing teeth, so the claim is false. The option that reverses the totals miscounts which teeth grind. There is no split of 16 and 16. And it is not the case that all 32 grind, since incisors and canines cut and tear rather than grind.

**8. (D)** The outermost shell is enamel, the hardest substance in the body; beneath it lies the softer dentine that makes up most of the tooth; and the central space carrying nerves is the pulp cavity, which is why pain begins only when the drill reaches it. Starting with dentine is wrong because dentine lies under the enamel, not outside it. Reaching the pulp cavity before the dentine is impossible because the pulp is the innermost region. The fully reversed order puts the soft inner pulp outside the hard enamel, which cannot be.

**9. (D)** Pain in a tooth comes from the nerves, which lie in the pulp cavity at the centre. A cavity hurts only when it reaches those nerves, regardless of how wide or deep it looks: Cavity X, though deep in dentine, has not reached the pulp, while shallow Cavity Y has. Hardness has nothing to do with sensation, so 'dentine is hardest' is both false (enamel is hardest) and irrelevant. Nerves are in the pulp, not the enamel. And depth alone does not decide pain, so the two descriptions can indeed both be true.

**10. (B)** The chain begins with sugar sitting on the teeth, so removing that sugar by rinsing breaks the very first link, before bacteria can even start. The antacid idea is irrelevant because the acid that decays teeth is made by mouth bacteria, not risen from the stomach. Eating more sweets adds more sugar, feeding the bacteria rather than stopping them. Chewing tough food does not harden enamel against acid and acts, if at all, only at the last link rather than the earliest.

**11. (A)** Broad ridged teeth are suited to grinding plant matter and prominent pointed teeth to tearing flesh, so skull P fits a plant-eater and skull Q a flesh-eater; tooth form reflects diet. Tooth shape says nothing about body size, so the 'larger animal' inference is unwarranted. The two skulls are described with clearly different teeth, so they are not identical. And tooth form is not random; it reliably tracks the kind of food an animal eats.

**12. (C)** Premolars and molars do the grinding, so losing exactly those teeth makes grinding fibrous food into pulp the hardest task. Biting an apple uses the incisors, which were kept, so it stays possible. Tearing meat uses the canines, which were also kept. Swallowing liquids needs no teeth at all, so it is unaffected. Only the grinding function was removed.

**13. (D)** Only the starchy cracker turns sweet because the salivary enzyme breaks starch into sugar; the cheese, being fat and protein, has nothing the salivary enzyme can act on, so it stays unsweet. Saliva does not digest every food equally, so the cheese would not secretly sweeten. The enzyme does not act on protein, which is why neither would the cheese sweeten by that route. And it does not act on fat, so the fatty cheese cannot taste sweeter than the cracker.

**14. (B)** Both tubes were warm and both contained saliva, yet only the tube with fresh saliva made sugar; the one difference was that Tube 2's saliva had its enzyme destroyed by boiling, so the enzyme is what produced the sugar. Warmth alone cannot be the cause, since both tubes were equally warm. Acid is not the agent, since boiling destroys enzymes but would not remove acid, yet Tube 2 failed. And boiled saliva is not stronger; it lost its active enzyme.

**15. (A)** Beyond starting starch digestion, saliva wets and softens dry food into a slippery lump that can slide down the food pipe; without this, a dry biscuit could not be swallowed smoothly. Saliva does not absorb sugar into the blood; absorption happens far downstream at the small intestine, not the mouth. Saliva does not make hydrochloric acid; that belongs to the stomach. And cooling is not a recognised role of saliva in swallowing.

**16. (A)** Upside-down, gravity points away from the stomach, so if anything it should pull the water out of the mouth; the fact that the water still arrives proves the food pipe's muscular waves (peristalsis) push it there. Saying gravity helps ignores that gravity is working against the water in this position. The food pipe is not a straight vertical drop, and even if it were, position clearly matters when inverted. And the food pipe does not absorb the water into the blood; the water genuinely travels to the stomach.

**17. (B)** The food pipe moves food by peristaltic muscle waves; freeze those and swallowed food cannot be carried to the stomach, so it gets stuck there. The stomach still works, so its churning is unaffected and is not the direct problem. Saliva still works, so mouth digestion is unchanged. The food pipe is not where water is absorbed, so over-drying there is not the mechanism; the failure is simply transport.

**18. (B)** The wave-pushing tube passed in seconds is the oesophagus; the churning bag holding food a few hours is the stomach; the long coiled tube that retains food many hours for digestion and absorption is the small intestine, giving shortest-to-longest as oesophagus, stomach, small intestine. The stomach is not a seconds-long passage; it churns for hours. The small intestine, not the stomach, is the long-stay coiled tube, so the orders that swap them are wrong.

**19. (A)** Mucus does not digest anything; its job is to coat and shield the stomach wall, so calling it the protein-digester is the wrong assignment, with pepsin being the actual protein enzyme. Saying the acid kills germs is correct. Saying pepsin begins protein digestion is correct. Saying the acid creates the acidic conditions pepsin needs is also correct. Only the mucus statement is false.

**20. (C)** The acid does two jobs: it creates the acidic conditions pepsin needs and it kills germs in food. With little acid, pepsin works poorly so protein digestion in the stomach suffers, and germs are less well killed. Starch digestion does not depend on stomach acid, and bile's path to the small intestine is unrelated to acid. Fat emulsification depends on bile, not acid, and water absorption is the large intestine's separate job. Saliva and peristalsis are not produced by stomach acid.

**21. (C)** The wall really is made of protein, so the 'no protein' claim is simply false; what saves it is the mucus layer coating the inner surface, which keeps the acid and pepsin from reaching the living tissue. Bile is made in the liver and acts in the small intestine, not as a stomach coating. The wall is not bone; it is muscular protein tissue. The protection is the mucus barrier.

**22. (B)** The intestinal enzymes need non-acidic conditions, so the strongly acidic paste from the stomach must have its acidity reduced once it reaches the small intestine for those enzymes to act. Making it more acidic would suit only pepsin, not the intestinal enzymes. Keeping it stomach-acidic would let pepsin work but block the intestinal enzymes. Drying it out is not how digestion proceeds; the paste stays moist.

**23. (D)** The human stomach's semi-liquid paste is chyme, and the regurgitated grass a cow re-chews is cud, so the student swapped them. They are not both chyme; chyme is specifically the human stomach's output. They are not both cud; cud is the ruminant's regurgitated grass. And a cow does not re-chew bile, which is a fat-emulsifying fluid, not a chewable mass.

**24. (A)** The finger-like projections (villi) multiply the inner surface area, so much more of the gut wall is in contact with the food and far more absorption occurs in the same length. They do not speed food up; both guts share equal transit time anyway. They do not change acidity; absorption does not work by dissolving nutrients in acid. And villi do not make bile; bile comes from the liver.

**25. (C)** Flattening the villi destroys the large absorbing surface, so even well-digested nutrients are not taken up, and the person becomes undernourished and loses weight while still eating. The mouth cannot compensate; it only starts starch digestion and does no absorption. Harder churning by the stomach cannot replace the lost absorbing surface. And bile emulsifies fat; it does not absorb nutrients, so making more cannot fix lost villi.

**26. (B)** Glucose crossing the villus wall into the blood is absorption, and the muscle cell putting that glucose to use is assimilation, so the order is absorption then assimilation. Digestion is not happening at the villus; the glucose was already made before reaching it. Absorption cannot happen at the muscle, since the glucose entered the blood back at the villus. And egestion concerns undigested residue, not glucose in a muscle.

**27. (A)** Simple molecules show up at the villi because that is where they are absorbed, but they were produced earlier by enzymes acting in the gut cavity; the villi absorb, they do not digest. The villi are not the main digesting organs; digestion happens in the cavity. They do not store the molecules; the molecules pass into the blood. And they do not churn like a stomach; absorption, not churning, is their job.

**28. (C)** Breaking a fat globule into droplets is a preparation step within digestion, helping enzymes act; the amino acid crossing the wall into the blood is absorption. Both are not absorption: emulsifying fat does not cross any wall. Both are not digestion: crossing into blood is by definition absorption. And the labels are not reversed, since emulsification stays in the cavity while crossing the wall is absorption.

**29. (C)** Organ length does not decide which job an organ does; the small intestine specialises in absorbing nutrients, and recovering water from the leftover residue is specifically the large intestine's role. So the small intestine does not also recover the water. The job split is not merely a matter of importance; it is a real division of labour. And the two do not share water recovery equally; it is chiefly the large intestine's task.

**30. (D)** Water left in the residue makes stools loose and watery, so too little reabsorption gives watery stools; reabsorbing too much dries the residue into hard stools (constipation). The reversed pairing is wrong because more water means wetter stools, not drier. Vomiting acid and fat digestion are unrelated to large-intestine water recovery. And the large intestine does handle water; recovering it is its main job.

**31. (D)** Being indigestible, the bead can only be taken in (ingestion) and later leave with the undigested residue (egestion); no enzyme breaks it, so digestion, absorption and assimilation never apply to it. Stomach acid will not turn an inert bead into nutrients. It cannot be assimilated into bone, since assimilation needs absorbed nutrients. And it is not excretion, because excretion removes waste made inside cells, not a swallowed object passing through the canal.

**32. (A)** Bile helps without being an enzyme: in the small intestine it physically breaks large fat globules into tiny droplets, vastly increasing the surface on which fat-digesting enzymes can work, so it is far from useless. Bile does not chemically cut fat into amino acids; amino acids come from protein anyway, and bile carries no enzyme. And bile does not convert fat into starch; that conversion is not a real process.

**33. (A)** Because only fatty meals are troublesome, the lost fluid must be one that mainly helps fat: that fluid is bile, which the gall bladder stored, and bile assists fat digestion by emulsifying it. The gall bladder does not make pepsin; pepsin is a stomach protein enzyme. It does not store amylase; if it had, starchy meals would suffer, which they do not. And it does not make stomach acid; the selective effect on fats rules out an effect on all foods.

**34. (A)** Bile's job is to emulsify fat; without it, fat remains in large globules that enzymes can hardly act on, so fat digestion suffers most, while starch and protein still have their working saliva, gastric and pancreatic enzymes. Bile is not a starch enzyme, so starch is not the worst hit. Bile does not activate the protein enzymes, so protein is not the worst hit. And bile is not needed for every food, so the three are not equally hampered.

**35. (D)** Saliva's enzyme chemically begins starch digestion, gastric pepsin chemically begins protein digestion, and bile only physically emulsifies fat into droplets without itself breaking the chemical bonds. The first wrong option misassigns every pairing. The second has saliva emulsifying fat and pepsin on starch, both false. And the third is impossible, since the three act on different foods, not all on starch.

**36. (B)** Pancreatic juice carries enzymes for carbohydrates, proteins and fats acting in the small intestine, so blocking it impairs all three there at once. Mouth starch digestion uses salivary enzyme, not pancreatic, so it continues. Stomach protein digestion uses pepsin, made by the stomach, not the pancreas. And the pancreas does not absorb water; that is the large intestine's job, unaffected here.

**37. (C)** The pancreatic enzymes act only in non-acidic surroundings, so the strongly acidic chyme must be neutralised in the small intestine before they can work. They do not need stronger acid than pepsin; the opposite is true. Neutralising acid does not itself digest food; it only sets the right conditions for the enzymes. And the intestine does hold moist contents; dryness is not required.

**38. (D)** The largest chamber where microbes break down cellulose is the rumen, and the chamber doing acid-and-enzyme digestion like a human stomach is the abomasum. The swap that puts microbes in the abomasum and acid in the rumen reverses their real roles. The reticulum is not the acid-and-enzyme chamber, and the omasum is not where microbial cellulose breakdown begins; the rumen is.

**39. (A)** Grass is rich in cellulose, which neither animal's own enzymes can break; the cow manages only because microbes in its rumen plus repeated cud-chewing break the cellulose down, a system the person lacks entirely. A bigger stomach alone would not help, since the limiting issue is cellulose-digesting microbes, not capacity. The person does have a stomach. And grass certainly needs digestion; eating more of an undigestible food does not nourish.

**40. (C)** Chewing the cud only grinds the grass finer; the actual breakdown of cellulose is done by microbes in the rumen, not by chewing in the mouth. The mouth has no cellulose-digesting power, so it cannot fully digest cellulose. The food pipe merely transports the cud and does not break cellulose. And the cellulose is not stored forever; it is digested in the rumen with help from the finer grinding.

**41. (B)** Grinding the cud finer exposes more grass surface, letting rumen microbes and enzymes act on more of the cellulose and free more nutrients, so more energy becomes available. The mouth does not turn cellulose into sugar for absorption there; cellulose breakdown happens in the rumen. Chewing does not release energy by heat. And finer grinding does not convert cellulose into protein; that conversion does not occur.

**42. (C)** Inside an Amoeba the food is trapped in a food vacuole, and the undigested leftover leaves when the vacuole reaches the cell surface and opens, which is egestion. A gall bladder and a rumen are organs of larger animals, not parts of a single cell. A villus belongs to the human small intestine. And throwing out leftovers is egestion, not absorption (taking in) or assimilation (using nutrients).

**43. (D)** The food vacuole is an enclosed pocket where enzymes meet food, and the human parallel is the enclosed cavity of the stomach and intestine, where digestive enzymes act on food. Human digestive enzymes do not act in the blood; the blood carries already-absorbed nutrients. They do not act inside muscle cells, where nutrients are merely used. And they do not act on the skin; food is digested inside the gut, not outside the body.

**44. (C)** Both organisms carry out all five steps; the only difference is that the Amoeba does them inside one cell while the human uses many specialised organs. The Amoeba does digest (in its food vacuole), so the 'never digests' claim is false. The Amoeba does egest leftovers when the vacuole reaches the surface, so it completes all five. And the human does assimilate, using absorbed nutrients in its cells, so 'never assimilates' is false.

**45. (A)** Starch digestion begins in the mouth with the salivary enzyme, while fat is mainly emulsified by bile and digested by enzymes in the small intestine. Neither begins in the stomach for these two foods: the stomach handles protein, not starch or fat emulsification. Starch does not first begin in the small intestine, and fat is not digested in the mouth. The large intestine recovers water and does not digest starch or fat.

**46. (B)** Protein digestion begins in the stomach with pepsin, is completed in the small intestine by its enzymes, and the resulting building blocks are absorbed there at the villi. Protein digestion does not begin in the mouth, since saliva acts on starch, not protein. It is not completed in the stomach; the small intestine finishes it. And absorption of the building blocks happens in the small intestine, not the mouth, stomach or large intestine.

**47. (A)** Dissolved sugar is mainly absorbed across the villi of the small intestine, while most water is recovered later in the large intestine. The stomach is mainly for churning and starting protein digestion, not for absorbing sugar and water. The pairing that swaps the two organs is wrong because sugar absorption is the small intestine's job and water recovery the large intestine's. And the mouth does not absorb either into the blood.

**48. (C)** At the instant the milk enters the mouth it is only being taken in, so ingestion is occurring and no step has yet been completed. Digestion has not been completed, and absorption cannot occur this early. Two steps cannot be done at the moment of entry, so assimilation is not occurring. And four steps cannot be finished before the food is even fully in, so egestion is not the only step left.

**49. (C)** The only error is putting assimilation before absorption: a cell cannot use a nutrient until that nutrient has crossed into the blood, so absorption must precede assimilation. Egestion correctly comes last, not before digestion, because it removes the final undigested residue. Ingestion must be first, not last, since food has to be taken in before anything else. And the two orders are not equivalent, because the order of steps is fixed by what each step needs from the one before.

**50. (B)** The largest gland making bile is the liver; the small sac storing bile between meals is the gall bladder; and the gland whose juice digests carbohydrates, proteins and fats is the pancreas, giving liver, gall bladder, pancreas. The orderings that move the gall bladder or pancreas out of place mismatch the descriptions, and putting the pancreas first wrongly assigns bile-making to the gland that actually makes the all-food-type juice.

**51. (C)** Without peristalsis the food pipe cannot push swallowed food to the stomach, so the food lodges in the pipe; that is the first medicine's effect. The loss of mouth sweetness on chewing is the SECOND medicine's effect (the salivary enzyme), not the peristalsis one. The stomach still works, so churning is unaffected. And the large intestine still recovers water, so that is unrelated to the food pipe.

**52. (A)** Swallowing whole and engulfing are two ways of taking food in, so they differ in ingestion, not digestion; digestion is the chemical breakdown that comes later and is not what these scenes show. They are not absorption, which is nutrients crossing into body fluids, not yet shown. They are not two kinds of digestion, since neither scene shows chemical breakdown. And assimilation is the later cell use of nutrients, also not depicted.

**53. (C)** The first person absorbs glucose into the blood but the cells cannot use it, so the failing step is assimilation; the second person gets nothing into the blood, so the failing step is absorption. The first is not absorption, because glucose did reach the blood. Neither failure is digestion, since food is broken down in both descriptions. And egestion and ingestion are not the failing steps, since the problems lie with using and with crossing into blood.

**54. (B)** Starch digestion begins first, in the mouth, by the salivary enzyme; protein digestion begins next, in the stomach, by pepsin; fat digestion begins last, in the small intestine, with bile and enzymes. Protein does not begin in the mouth, and fat does not begin in the stomach, so the first wrong option is misordered. Fat does not begin in the mouth, ruling out the second. And the three do not all begin together in the stomach; starch starts earlier in the mouth.

**55. (D)** Canines are the pointed teeth that tear flesh, and the molars and premolars are flat grinders, so they cannot take over the tearing job. Canines do not grind; that is the molars' role, so the grinding claim is wrong. Canines tear rather than cut; cutting is the incisors' job. And teeth do not absorb nutrients at all, so the absorption claim is false.

**56. (D)** The acid-churning organ is the stomach (X), met first; next is the small intestine (Y), which completes digestion and absorbs most nutrients; last is the large intestine (Z), which recovers water, so the order is X, Y, Z. Food cannot reach the small intestine before the stomach, ruling out Y first. The large intestine comes after, not before, the small intestine, ruling out the orders that put Z early.

**57. (C)** One quarter of 32 is 8, so the quarter-rule would give 8 cutting-and-tearing teeth and 24 grinders; but the real adult set has 8 incisors plus 4 canines, that is 12 cutting-and-tearing, and 20 grinders, so the quarter-rule does NOT match. Saying 24 grinders matches is wrong because the true count is 20. Reversing to 24 cutting teeth is far from the real 12. And a 16-16 split matches neither the rule nor the real set.

**58. (D)** Digestion takes place in the enclosed cavity of the gut, while the cells that use the nutrients lie elsewhere in the body and receive them via the blood, so the two locations are indeed separate. Digestion is not inside the cells, and using nutrients is not done in the gut cavity, so that pairing is reversed. Digestion does not happen in the blood. And neither digestion nor nutrient use is confined to the mouth.

**59. (D)** Swallowing whole, sipping, and grinding-before-swallowing are all ways of taking food into the body, so the heading is ingestion, and the cow's grinding teeth are part of its ingestion by readying the grass. They are not tools of digestion, since chewing prepares food but the chemical breakdown comes later. They are not absorption tools, since nothing crosses into body fluids at the mouth. And they are not assimilation tools, which would be cells using nutrients.

**60. (A)** Protein digestion begins in the stomach with pepsin, fat digestion is mainly completed in the small intestine with bile and enzymes, and water is chiefly recovered in the large intestine, giving stomach, small intestine, large intestine. Protein does not begin in the mouth, where saliva acts on starch. Fat is not completed in the stomach or digested in the mouth. And water is recovered in the large intestine, not the mouth.
