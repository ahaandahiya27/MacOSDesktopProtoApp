**Marking scheme:** **+4** for each correct answer · **−1** for each wrong answer · **0** if unattempted.

**Instructions:** Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. An odd count of odd numbers gives an odd sum; the Virahanka-Fibonacci rule adds the two previous terms (never a constant or a ratio); in the taller-children game the first child always says 0; Kaprekar's routine always lands on 6174 and Collatz always falls to 1. Do not write on this paper — mark answers on your answer sheet.

1. In the taller-children-in-front game, every child calls how many taller children stand ahead of them. A line of 6 children calls out, front to back: 0, 1, 3, 0, 2, 1. Exactly one of these calls is impossible no matter how the heights are arranged. Which one?
   (A) the 3rd child, who said 3
   (B) the 1st child, who said 0
   (C) the 5th child, who said 2
   (D) the 6th child, who said 1

2. Kishor adds up some odd numbers and some even numbers. He used 5 odd numbers and 8 even numbers. What is the parity of his total?
   (A) even
   (B) odd
   (C) it depends on the actual even numbers
   (D) it depends on the actual odd numbers

3. A Virahanka-Fibonacci-type sequence (each term is the sum of the two before it) has its 4th term equal to 13 and its 6th term equal to 34. What is its 1st term?
   (A) 3
   (B) 2
   (C) 5
   (D) 1

4. In the cryptarithm  AB + AB + AB = CAB  (A and B are the digits of a two-digit number, C is a single digit, and the three-digit answer ends in the same two digits AB), what is the two-digit number AB?

   (B) 75
   (C) 10
   (D) 50

5. In a 3x3 grid a 'supercell' is a cell strictly larger than every neighbour sharing an edge with it. If you fill the grid with 1-9 (each once) to get as MANY supercells as possible, what is the greatest number of supercells achievable?

6. A 3x3 magic square uses 1-9 once each so every row, column and main diagonal has the same sum. A student fills the centre with 4 instead of 5. Why can no valid magic square result?
