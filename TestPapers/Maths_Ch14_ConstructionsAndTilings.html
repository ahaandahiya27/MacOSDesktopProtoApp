Instructions: Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. Constructions are described in words — picture the compass arcs and straightedge lines as you read. For tiling questions, the one rule that decides everything is that the angles meeting at a point must sum to exactly 360°; watch the traps that a polygon tiles whenever 360° is "close", that you can trisect like you bisect, and that a construction is "just a careful drawing". Worked Solutions

1. (B) A straightedge is an *unmarked* ruler — its only job is to draw straight lines. It carries no scale (so it cannot measure lengths) and is not a protractor; swinging arcs is the compass's job.

2. (D) Both arcs must be the SAME radius (so each crossing is equidistant from X and Y), and that radius must exceed half of XY or the arcs won't reach far enough to cross. "Exactly half" makes the arcs meet only on the segment (no upper/lower crossings); different radii destroy the equidistance.

3. (A) The perpendicular bisector is precisely the locus of points equidistant from A and B. The midpoint is just ONE such point; the whole line is equidistant, and points on it can lie far outside the segment.

4. (C) By definition the perpendicular bisector meets the segment at a right angle, 90°, while also cutting it in half. That is exactly the "perpendicular" in the name.

5. (B) Each arc-crossing is found at equal radius from X and from Y, so it is equidistant from both endpoints — it lies on the segment's mirror line. Two such crossings fix that mirror line, which cuts XY at its midpoint. No length is ever measured; equidistance does the work.

6. (A) For corners to surround a point with no gap/overlap, copies of the interior angle must fill exactly 360°, i.e. 360° ÷ (interior angle) must be a whole number. Being a multiple of 90° or "small" is neither necessary nor sufficient (120° tiles though it isn't a multiple of 90°).

7. (C) A square's interior angle is 90°, and four of them give 4 × 90° = 360° — a full turn with no gap or overlap. Having four equal sides (B) is true but is not the tiling reason; two squares give only 180°.

8. (D) Each interior angle of an equilateral triangle is 60°, and 360° ÷ 60° = 6, so six triangles meet at every vertex.

9. (B) A regular hexagon's interior angle is 120°, and 360° ÷ 120° = 3, so three hexagons meet at each vertex (the honeycomb pattern).

10. (C) A regular pentagon's interior angle is 108°, and 360 ÷ 108 ≈ 3.33 — not a whole number. Three pentagons give 324° (a 36° gap) and four give 432° (overlap), so they cannot surround a point cleanly.

11. (A) The 60° construction copies the corner of an equilateral triangle: equal-radius arcs build three equal sides, whose corners are all 60°. A square's corner is 90°, a hexagon's 120°.

12. (D) Bisecting halves the angle: 60° ÷ 2 = 30°. (Bisecting again would give 15°, the trap option A.)

13. (C) Bisect 90° → two 45° angles. Bisect one 45° → two 22.5° pieces. The smallest piece present is 22.5°. (45° is the in-between stage; 30° comes from bisecting 60°, not 90°.)

14. (B) A 30° angle is constructible — bisect a constructed 60°. Trisecting a general angle (so 60° → three 20°s) is provably impossible with these tools, so a 20° angle "directly" is out, and an exact 1° cannot be reached by halving from 60°/90° either.

15. (C) Rearranging the same seven pieces only moves them; nothing is added or removed, so the swan has exactly the same area as the original square. "Looks bigger" and "gaps appear" are perception traps.

16. (A) Sliding, turning or flipping a piece is a rigid motion: it changes position, never size. So a fixed set of pieces re-adds to the same total area no matter the arrangement.

17. (D) A regular octagon's interior angle is 135°, and 360 ÷ 135 ≈ 2.67 — not a whole number. Two octagons give 270° (a 90° gap), three give 405° (overlap), so octagons cannot tile alone.

18. (B) Two octagons contribute 2 × 135° = 270°. The leftover to reach a full 360° is 360° − 270° = 90°, so the third polygon's interior angle is 90°.

19. (C) A regular polygon with a 90° interior angle is a square. (This is the classic octagon–square semi-regular tiling.)

20. (A) Three pentagons give 3 × 108° = 324°. The angle left uncovered is 360° − 324° = 36°. (A 0° leftover would mean they tile, which they don't.)

21. (B) Unequal radii destroy the equidistance: the two crossings are no longer the same distance from X and Y, so the line through them tilts, misses the midpoint, and is not perpendicular. It is neither the true perpendicular bisector nor an angle bisector.

22. (D) A construction is *exact by geometry* — equal radii force equal distances, guaranteeing the result without any measuring. Ruler/protractor measuring (A, C) or tracing (B) would only be approximate; that is precisely what makes constructions special.

23. (A) With compass and straightedge any angle can be BISECTED, but trisecting a *general* angle is provably impossible (you can make 30° = half of 60°, but not 20° = a third). So only A is true.

24. (A) Construct 60° (equilateral corner), bisect to 30°, bisect again to 15°. Subtracting 30° from 45° isn't a single clean construction step here, and trisecting a 45° is impossible; 15° certainly IS constructible, ruling out D.

25. (D) One dodecagon corner is 150°. The remaining angle is 360° − 150° = 210°, shared by two equal polygons: 210° ÷ 2 = 105° each.

26. (C) Only triangle (60°), square (90°) and hexagon (120°) have interior angles dividing 360° a whole number of times, so only this trio tiles each on its own. Pentagons (108°) and octagons (135°) do not.

27. (B) 360 ÷ 144 = 2.5 is not a whole number, so a whole number of decagons can never surround a point — the decagon cannot tile by itself. "2.5 tiles meet" (D) is meaningless; you can't have half a tile-corner at a vertex.

28. (A) The perpendicular-bisector construction cuts XY exactly in half, so it hands you the midpoint for free — no measuring. It does not produce an angle bisector at X, a 60° angle, or a tiling.

29. (C) Cutting and rearranging is rigid motion only — the two triangles are merely slid into a new outline, so the total area is unchanged (conservation of area under rearrangement). The diagonal's length has nothing to do with total area.

30. (D) P is built at equal distances from the two crossing points on the arms, which makes it equidistant from both arms. A point equidistant from both arms lies on the angle's bisector, so the ray V→P splits the angle into two equal halves.

31. (B) Five equilateral triangles give 5 × 60° = 300°, which is 60° short of a full 360°, leaving a 60° gap. (Six are needed to actually tile.)

32. (A) With compass and straightedge you start by swinging an arc from A across the ray, then from that crossing point swing the SAME radius to cut the arc — building the equilateral corner. Protractors, measuring or folding are not construction steps.

33. (C) The count is 360° ÷ (interior angle). A hexagon's 120° is larger than a square's 90°, so fewer hexagons fill the 360° (three vs four). It has nothing to do with physical size or "more sides means more meet".

34. (B) Two points each equidistant from both endpoints (one on each side) lie on the segment's mirror line; joining them gives the perpendicular bisector. Equidistance from the *endpoints* (not from arms) is the perpendicular-bisector signature.

35. (D) Self-tiling needs the interior angle to divide 360° evenly. Among regular polygons that holds only for the triangle, square and hexagon; the hexagon has the most sides of these, so it is the largest self-tiler. The octagon (135°) does not divide 360°.

36. (A) Tiling (tessellation) means covering a region with shapes leaving NO gaps and NO overlaps. "May overlap a little" (B) violates the definition; the others describe constructions/tangrams.

37. (C) Less than 360° at a vertex means the corners don't reach all the way round — an uncovered wedge, i.e. a gap. (More than 360° would force an overlap.)

38. (B) More than 360° means the corners run past a full turn and pile on top of one another — an overlap. (Less than 360° gives a gap; exactly 360° is the perfect fit.)

39. (D) Three triangles + two squares = 3 × 60° + 2 × 90° = 180° + 180° = 360°. The angles fill a full turn exactly, so this is a valid vertex.

40. (A) Two hexagons contribute 2 × 120° = 240°, leaving 360° − 240° = 120° to fill. Equilateral triangles are 60° each, so 120° ÷ 60° = 2 triangles complete the vertex. One triangle gives only 300° (a gap); three give 420° and four give 480° (overlaps).

41. (C) Bisect 90° → 45°; bisect 45° → 22.5°. After two bisections the smallest angle present is 22.5°. (45° and 30° are intermediate/distractor values.)

42. (D) From a constructed 60° you can bisect to 30°, bisect again to 15°, and add to make 90° (= 60° + 30°). But 20° would require trisecting 60°, which is impossible — so 20° is NOT constructible this way.

43. (D) The original is 60°; bisecting gives a 30° angle; placing the 30° beside the 60° gives 60° + 30° = 90°. (120° would be 60° + 60°.)

44. (A) Both the "tree" and the "boat" use all seven pieces of the same square, so by conservation of area under rearrangement they have exactly equal areas. Appearance (tall vs wide) does not change total area.

45. (B) The reason is pure arithmetic: only 60°, 90° and 120° divide 360° a whole number of times (6, 4 and 3 times). Equal sides, convexity and "weight" are irrelevant to whether a regular polygon tiles.

46. (C) 360° ÷ 120° = 3, so three of its corners meet at a vertex (this is the hexagon case).

47. (A) 6 × 60° = 360°, a full turn — which is exactly why six equilateral triangles tile around a point.

48. (D) Construct a right angle (90°) and bisect it: 90° ÷ 2 = 45°. Bisecting 60° gives 30°, not 45°; trisecting 135° is impossible; a protractor isn't a construction.

49. (B) Precisely, the perpendicular bisector is the set of ALL points the same distance from both endpoints (the locus). The midpoint (D) is just one of those points, not the whole set.

50. (C) 3 × 120° = 360°, a full turn — so three hexagons exactly surround a vertex with no gap or overlap.

51. (B) The regular pentagon (108°) is the simplest counterexample: 108° does not divide 360°, so it cannot tile alone. Triangle, square and hexagon all DO tile, so they're not counterexamples.

52. (D) A right angle is built from the equal-arc / perpendicular construction, which by design meets the line at 90°. Doubling a 60° gives 120° (not 90°); a pentagon or tangram is irrelevant.

53. (A) 2 × 60° + 2 × 90° = 120° + 180° = 300°. That is 60° short of 360°, so it is NOT a valid vertex — but the question only asks the total, which is 300°.

54. (C) 360 ÷ 140 ≈ 2.57 is not a whole number, so no whole number of these polygons can surround a point — it cannot tile by itself. ("2.57 meeting" is meaningless.)

55. (B) Equal-radius compass arcs locate a point equidistant from both arms exactly, without measuring — that is what makes angle bisection a true construction. A protractor or marked ruler would only measure approximately.

56. (D) Three pentagons give 324° (a 36° wedge left) and four give 432° (overlap); since neither hits exactly 360°, regular pentagons cannot tile the plane. Cutting them in half changes the shape, so that doesn't count.

57. (A) True facts: a general angle CAN be bisected with these tools, and regular pentagons CANNOT tile. Option B reverses both (trisection is impossible; pentagons don't tile), and C, D each contain a false claim (constructions need no protractor; hexagons DO tile; the perpendicular bisector hits the midpoint; squares DO tile).

58. (B) Beyond the hexagon, each regular polygon's interior angle exceeds 120° (heptagon ≈128.6°, octagon 135°, …) yet never again divides 360° a whole number of times, so none of them can self-tile. The angle is never less than 90°, never 360°, never negative.

59. (C) Three 30° angles placed in a row give 3 × 30° = 90°. (Two would give 60°; four would give 120°.)

60. (D) The single governing rule of plane tiling is that the corner angles meeting at any vertex must sum to EXACTLY 360° — a full turn — with no gap (less) and no overlap (more). "Any amount up to 360°" is the trap that allows gaps.

Solutions complete: 60 / 60.
