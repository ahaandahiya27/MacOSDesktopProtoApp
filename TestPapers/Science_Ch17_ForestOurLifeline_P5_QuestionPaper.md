**Instructions:** Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. This is the hardest tier: expect quantitative energy-flow estimates, two- and three-step food-web ripples that loop back through shared predators, nutrient-cycle breaks disguised as harmless tidying, the standing confusion of energy flow (one-way, lost as heat) with nutrient flow (cyclic), and the night-respiration paradox dressed up as proof that forests harm the air — every trap is a plausible half-truth, so reason to the end before choosing. 1. A light meter reads 100 units above the canopy, 40 units in the understorey, 12 units in the shrub layer and 3 units at the herb level. A gardener wants to transplant a fern that, in greenhouse tests, photosynthesises best at about 10 to 15 units and is scorched above 50 units. The single best planting spot, and the reason, is:
   (A) above the canopy at 100 units, because more light always means more food and faster growth for any plant
   (B) the herb level at 3 units, because the darkest spot is always the safest for a shade plant
   (C) the understorey at 40 units, because a value roughly midway down the gradient must suit a middle-sized plant
   (D) the shrub layer at about 12 units, because it falls inside the fern's best range while staying well below the level that scorches it

2. A forest holds roughly 60000 kg of green plants, which support deer totalling about 6000 kg, which support leopards totalling about 600 kg. A student reasons that if the plant mass could somehow be tripled, the leopard mass would also exactly triple to 1800 kg. The most accurate critique is:
   (A) the rough tenfold drop at each step would still hold, so tripling the producer base would tend to raise the leopard mass towards roughly 1800 kg, but real losses vary so it is an estimate, not an exact figure

   (C) tripling the plants would leave the leopards unchanged, because carnivores draw energy from prey and so are cut off from the plant supply
   (D) the leopard mass would rise far more than threefold, because energy is gained rather than lost as it moves up the chain
