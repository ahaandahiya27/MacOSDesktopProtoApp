## Worked Solutions

**1. (D)** Each ray travels straight; a ray leaving the top of the flame passes through the hole and lands low on the back screen, while a ray from the bottom lands high, so the picture is flipped. The hole does not bend light into a focus, no mirror-like reflection forms the picture, and a tiny hole does not disperse colours. Straight-line travel is the cause.

**2. (B)** The law of reflection holds at every individual point, even on a rough wall: the surface there has many tiny tilts, so the equal-angle rule still applies locally but the normals point every which way, scattering the light. The angle is not doubled, reflection is not bending into the surface, and reflection does not change colour. Equal angles at each point is the shared truth.

**3. (D)** A plane mirror always makes a virtual image: the reflected rays diverge and only seem to come from behind the mirror, so no light actually crosses that point and nothing can be captured on a screen. It is not a faint real image, the distance behind equals 60 cm (not 120 cm) and that is not why it fails, and a plane-mirror image is erect, not inverted. Being virtual is the reason.

**4. (B)** Lateral inversion swaps left and right, so the whole word is read back-to-front AND each letter is mirror-flipped: C H O I C E becomes E C I O H C with every letter reversed, shown as ƎƆIOHƆ. The word is not unchanged (it is not vertically symmetric as a whole), reversing only the order without flipping each letter is wrong, and a plane mirror does not turn images upside down — top and bottom are not swapped.

**5. (A)** The image is always as far behind the mirror as the object is in front. After walking 1 m closer, the boy is 3 - 1 = 2 m from the mirror, so the image is 2 m behind it. The separation between him and his image is 2 + 2 = 4 m. 6 m uses his old 3 m position (3 + 3); 1 m is just the step he took; 2 m is only his distance to the mirror, not to the image. So 4 m is correct.

**6. (A)** A concave mirror forms a real, inverted image of a far object (rays converge in front of the mirror), but for an object very close to it the image becomes virtual, erect and magnified — this is why it serves as a shaving mirror. So far = real inverted, close = virtual erect enlarged. It is not 'both' the same, and the lamp/fingertip cases are not swapped. The mixed answer is correct.

**7. (C)** A convex mirror always gives a virtual, erect, diminished image regardless of object position, so objects look smaller (and thus farther), never larger — that is why warnings say 'objects are closer than they appear.' It does not magnify in any light, and it does not invert (the image is erect). So the magnify claim is wrong because the image is always diminished.

**8. (C)** A convex mirror spreads reflected rays, so it captures a much wider field of view than a plane or concave mirror of the same size — ideal for surveillance. A concave mirror would magnify and narrow the view, and would not help see the whole floor; a plane mirror shows true size but a narrow view; a real image is irrelevant to coverage. Convex for wide view is correct.

**9. (A)** The steel is smooth, so parallel rays stay parallel (regular reflection) and form a glare; the paper's microscopically rough surface sends rays off in all directions (diffuse reflection), giving an even glow with no glare and no image. The paper does reflect (you can see it), light is not refracted into it to explain the glow, and it is not regular reflection — that is what the steel does. Diffuse reflection is correct.

**10. (D)** A convex (converging) lens bends parallel sunlight inward to meet at a focal point, concentrating the energy enough to burn paper. It does not diverge the rays (that is a concave lens), it transmits rather than reflects the light, and it is the concentration of rays — not absorption and re-emission — that scorches the paper. Converging to a point is correct.

**11. (B)** A plane mirror always gives a same-size image; moving away does NOT shrink it (it only looks smaller to your eye, like any distant thing), so that statement is the false one. The other three are all true: the image is same size, laterally inverted (left-right swapped), and virtual so it cannot be caught on a screen. The 'gets smaller' claim is the false statement.

**12. (A)** The sharp straight edges of the visible cone show the light is moving in straight lines (the droplets just scatter a little of it toward your eye so you can see the beam). The straight cone is not evidence of bending around droplets, the seven-colour fact is about dispersion not the beam shape, and speed change is not what the straight cone reveals. Straight-line travel is correct.

**13. (A)** A display looks unchanged in a plane mirror only if it is symmetric about a vertical line AND reads the same after the left-right swap. '00' is made of two identical vertically symmetric zeros, so it stays '00'. '80' reverses to '08'; '10' reverses to '01'; '23' becomes a flipped '32'. Only '00' is unchanged.

**14. (C)** A plane mirror always forms an image equal in size to the object, no matter how far the object stands; her image stays 1.6 m tall. Distance changes how big it looks to her eye but not the true image size, so it neither doubles nor halves, and 3.2 m confuses size with the object-image separation. The image stays 1.6 m.

**15. (D)** Placing the bulb at the focus of a concave (converging) mirror makes the reflected rays come out nearly parallel, giving a long strong beam — exactly what a torch or headlight needs. A convex mirror would spread the light and weaken the beam, a plane mirror would not collimate it, and the reason is collimation, not magnifying the bulb. Concave for a parallel beam is correct.

**16. (A)** A convex mirror's diverging reflection covers a wide field of view, so it shows a long stretch of the hidden road — even though everything looks a little smaller. A concave mirror would magnify but narrow the view (and could invert), a plane mirror shows a narrow strip, and a real image is not the point. Convex for a wide view is correct.

**17. (B)** A real image formed by a mirror is always inverted, never erect, so 'real and magnified-erect' (iv) cannot occur. The others all happen: a plane mirror gives (i) virtual same-size, a convex mirror gives (ii) virtual diminished, and a concave mirror gives (iii) real inverted for a distant object. So (iv) is the impossible one.

**18. (C)** A lens thicker in the middle is convex; held close to an object it acts as a magnifying glass, giving an enlarged erect (virtual) image — perfect for a jeweller. A concave lens is thinner in the middle and only diminishes; the two mirror options are not lenses at all (light passes THROUGH a lens). Convex lens is correct.

**19. (A)** The inner bowl of a spoon curves inward — it is concave, and at normal arm's length it gives a real inverted image, so you appear upside-down. The outer back bulges out — it is convex, always giving an erect diminished image, so you look small and upright. The types are not reversed, a curved spoon is not a plane mirror, and a spoon reflects (mirror), it is not a lens. Inner concave / outer convex is correct.

**20. (B)** A plane mirror's image is virtual: the reflected rays diverge and only appear to come from behind the mirror, so no light gathers at the image position and nothing can be projected. The image is same-size (not too small), it lies behind the mirror at the candle's distance (not in front), and it is erect (not inverted). Being virtual is the reason it cannot be projected.

**21. (D)** A lens thinner in the middle is a concave (diverging) lens; it spreads rays apart and can only form a virtual, erect, diminished image for any object position — it never makes a real or magnified image. So the only correct description is virtual, erect and diminished.

**22. (C)** In a prism the two faces are inclined to each other, so each colour bends by a slightly different total amount and the colours emerge separated as a spectrum. In flat parallel-faced glass the second face cancels the bending and the colours recombine, so no band shows. The glass adds no colour, needs no special material, and the faces are flat, not curved. Slanted (non-parallel) faces is correct.

**23. (B)** Lateral inversion reverses the display left-to-right. '10:01' read back-to-front becomes '10:01' again, and each of these digits (1 and 0) is vertically symmetric, so the mirror display still reads 10:01. It is not 10:10 or 01:01 (those mis-order the digits), and 21:51 invents reversed digit shapes incorrectly. The display is unchanged: 10:01.

**24. (B)** A concave mirror gives a real, inverted image of a distant object, but once the object is brought very close (inside the focus) the image flips to virtual, erect and enlarged — the shaving-mirror effect. So the change is real-inverted to virtual-erect-enlarged. It is not the reverse order, not fixed as real, and 'always virtual diminished' describes a convex mirror, not a concave one.

**25. (D)** His image approaches the mirror at the same 2 m/s as he does (image distance behind always equals his distance in front). So he closes on the mirror at 2 m/s and the image closes from the other side at 2 m/s, making the man-to-image gap shrink at 2 + 2 = 4 m/s. 2 m/s ignores the image's motion, 1 m/s halves it, and the gap certainly changes. 4 m/s is correct.

**26. (D)** A convex mirror always gives a diminished image and a plane mirror always gives a same-size image, so neither can enlarge the face; only a concave mirror held close magnifies (the make-up/shaving mirror). The convex does not magnify, the plane does not enlarge (close objects only fill more of your view), and they do not both work. Neither works — a concave mirror is needed.

**27. (C)** The beam reaches the far side only when all the holes (and the source and your eye) are collinear, which can only be true if light travels straight. It is not about reflection (the light passes through holes, it is not bounced), not dispersion (a single colour, no spreading into a band), and not diffuse scattering. It demonstrates straight-line travel.

**28. (C)** A convex mirror gives both a wide field of view and an always-erect (virtual, diminished) image, exactly what a side mirror needs. A concave mirror narrows the view and can invert a distant vehicle; a plane mirror is erect but has a much narrower view; a lens is not a mirror. Convex satisfies both requirements.

**29. (A)** Only a convex (converging) lens behaves both ways: held close it is a magnifier (enlarged, erect, virtual), and used to image far objects it forms a small real inverted image that can fall on a wall. A concave lens never forms a real image, a plane mirror cannot make a real inverted image of distant trees, and the device transmits light (a lens), so it is not a mirror. Convex lens is correct.

**30. (C)** Because it lands on a screen, the image is real (light actually meets there), and a real image formed by a convex lens of a distant object is inverted — matching 'small, upside-down.' It cannot be virtual (virtual images never fall on a screen), and a real lens image of this kind is inverted, not erect. Real and inverted is correct.

**31. (D)** The image sits as far behind the mirror as the object is in front: 5 cm behind. The pencil-to-image distance is therefore 5 + 5 = 10 cm. 5 cm gives only the object-to-mirror distance, 2.5 cm halves it, and 25 cm squares the number. The total is 10 cm.

**32. (D)** A prism bends violet the most and red the least, so listing from least-bent to most-bent gives red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet (VIBGYOR read backwards). The first wrong option lists most-bent to least-bent, the three-colour option drops four colours, and the last is a jumbled order. Red-to-violet (least to most bent) is correct.

**33. (D)** The law of reflection (angle of incidence = angle of reflection about the normal) fixes the one tilt that sends light from one child's eyes to the other's. A mirror does not only reflect straight back, it does not preferentially bend toward a taller child, and a plane mirror does not disperse light into colours. The equal-angle law is the reason.

**34. (C)** Held close (inside its focus) a concave mirror gives a virtual, erect, magnified image — ideal for examining a tooth. A convex mirror only diminishes, a plane mirror keeps true size (never enlarges), and a concave mirror's real image (for far objects) is inverted, which would be useless here. Concave with a virtual erect magnified image is correct.

**35. (B)** A plane mirror laterally inverts (swaps left and right). B has no vertical symmetry, so it appears as a backwards B. It is not unchanged (only letters symmetric about a VERTICAL axis stay the same), it is not flipped top-to-bottom (mirrors don't swap up-down), and a reversed B is not a normal D. A backwards B is correct.

**36. (B)** Every convex mirror, whatever its use, forms a virtual, erect, diminished image and offers a wide field of view — so the security mirror and the rear-view mirror share exactly that. Convex mirrors never form a real (screen-catchable) image, never magnify, and do not specially invert. The virtual-erect-diminished-with-wide-view property is shared.

**37. (D)** A convex mirror's outward curve diverges the reflected rays, taking in a wider field of view than a same-sized plane mirror. A concave mirror actually narrows the field (and magnifies), a plane mirror is the baseline being beaten, and a convex mirror clearly does beat it. The convex mirror gives the widest view.

**38. (C)** A concave (diverging) lens spreads parallel rays outward, so they never meet at a hot point and cannot scorch the leaf, unlike the converging convex lens. The lens does not absorb all the light (it is transparent), it transmits rather than reflects, and ordinary lens action here is not dispersion into a rainbow. Diverging the rays is the reason it fails.

**39. (B)** Each image lies as far behind the mirror as its object is in front: the boy's image is 4 m behind, the sister's 1 m behind. The boy's is farther, by 4 - 1 = 3 m. The sister's is not farther, the images are not equal (the objects differ), and 5 m wrongly adds the two distances instead of subtracting. The boy's image is farther by 3 m.

**40. (A)** A concave (diverging) lens always forms a virtual, erect, diminished image, so print seen through it looks smaller and the right way up. It cannot enlarge (that is a convex lens close up), it does not invert (the image is erect), and it changes the size (smaller), not leaving it the same. Smaller and upright is correct.

**41. (D)** Sharp edges on both the projected patch and the shadow show the light moves in straight lines: rays blocked by the hand simply do not reach the wall behind it, giving a crisp outline. Air absorption would dim, not sharpen; rays do not spread in all directions after forming the beam; and no colour change is involved. Straight-line travel explains both.

**42. (B)** A shaving mirror (enlarged erect image up close), a solar cooker reflector (concentrating sunlight to a hot point), and a torch reflector (collimating into a beam) are all jobs for a concave (converging) mirror. A convex mirror diverges light and cannot concentrate or enlarge; a plane mirror does neither; and they are not a mix. All three use a concave mirror.

**43. (B)** The prism splits white light into VIBGYOR, and the rapidly spinning disc recombines those colours so the eye sees white again — proving white light is a mixture that can be separated and re-merged. The prism does not create colour (it only separates what is already there), white is not a single pure colour, and the disc combines rather than destroys the colours. White = the seven colours combined.

**44. (A)** A plane mirror laterally inverts a clock face, and a position that reflects to read T appears at 12 - T on the real clock (for hours; here the mirror image of 4 o'clock looks like 8 o'clock). Since the mirror shows 8, the real time is 12 - 8 = 4 o'clock. It is not 8 (that is the mirror reading), and 10 or 2 do not satisfy the 12 - T relation. The real time is 4 o'clock.

**45. (A)** A convex mirror always produces a virtual, erect, diminished image, no matter where the object is. Being virtual, it can never be caught on a screen; it is never larger (always diminished); and it is never inverted (always erect). So 'virtual, erect and smaller' is the universally true statement.

**46. (A)** Because the rear-view mirror laterally inverts whatever it shows, writing the words already reversed means the mirror flips them back to normal, so the driver ahead reads SCHOOL BUS correctly. It is not for decoration, the mirror-writing does not make letters reflect more sunlight, and lateral inversion has nothing to do with reading in the dark. Correct reading in the mirror is the reason.

**47. (C)** The microscopically uneven crumpled surface tilts the tiny reflecting facets every which way, so reflected rays scatter (diffuse reflection) and never assemble into an image, unlike smooth foil (regular reflection). Light is reflected, not refracted into the metal; the foil does not absorb it all (you still see brightness); and no colours appear, so it is not dispersion. Diffuse reflection is correct.

**48. (C)** A capital letter looks unchanged in a plane mirror only if it is symmetric about a VERTICAL line (A, H, I, M, O, T, U, V, W, X, Y). F, E, R and N are not in that set — E and F are symmetric only about a horizontal line, and R and N have no such symmetry — so all four appear reversed. They are not all unchanged, E is not vertically symmetric, and N is not either. None of the four survives unchanged.

**49. (C)** For a distant object a concave mirror converges the rays to form a real, inverted image, while a plane mirror and a convex mirror always form virtual images. So only the concave one gives a real image. They do not all form real images, it is not the convex one, and it is not 'none' — the concave mirror does. Only-concave-real is correct.

**50. (A)** Both effects come from the convex lens converging (bending inward) the light: converging the Sun's parallel rays gives a hot focal point, and converging the diverging rays from nearby print produces an enlarged virtual image. The lens does not diverge (that is concave), it transmits not reflects, and the burning comes from concentration, not absorption-and-re-emission. Converging light links both abilities.

**51. (D)** Held close (inside its focus) a convex lens acts as a magnifier and forms a virtual, erect, enlarged image — virtual images cannot be projected onto a screen, unlike the real image of the far lamp. The close image is not a real one (too small or behind the lens), and it is not merely 'coloured.' It is virtual, so no screen image forms.

**52. (C)** The image is always as far behind the mirror as the object is in front, so moving the object from 7 cm to 12 cm shifts the image from 7 cm to 12 cm behind — that is 5 cm farther back. It moves farther (not closer), by 5 cm (not 10 cm, which would double it), and it certainly does move. 5 cm farther behind is correct.

**53. (D)** Sunlight entering a raindrop is dispersed (and internally reflected) much as in a prism, separating white light into the spectrum we see as a rainbow. A plain mirror would reflect white unchanged (no colours), a magnifying lens would not split colours, and a filter removes colours rather than separating them all. Each drop acts like a tiny prism.

**54. (B)** Within one uniform medium light travels in straight lines, which is why the laser shows a straight track across the room. It does not curve downward, a collimated laser does not spread into a cone over the room, and a single-colour laser does not split into seven colours on its own. Straight-line travel is the evidence.

**55. (A)** A concave mirror enlarges only when the object is close (inside the focus); for a distant object it forms a small, inverted, real image, so 'always bigger' is false. It does not magnify for every position, the colour of the object is irrelevant, and it does not always shrink either (it CAN magnify up close). 'False — distant object gives a small inverted image' is correct.

**56. (D)** A convex mirror's wider field of view lets the driver see more lanes behind the van; the trade-off is that everything looks smaller (and farther). A plane mirror shows true size but a narrower view; a convex mirror never magnifies; and it forms a virtual, not a real, image. Wider area (with smaller-looking objects) is the reason.

**57. (C)** A prism can only separate colours that are already present; a single pure colour like green has nothing to separate, so it merely bends (refracts) and emerges still green. It cannot produce a seven-colour band from one colour, cannot turn one colour into white, and does not block the light entirely. Only bent green light comes out.

**58. (B)** Paper scatters (diffusely reflects) the room's light in all directions, so light from every part of the page reaches your eyes and you read the contrast of ink and paper. The paper makes no light of its own, it does not form a mirror-like regular reflection (no image), and you do not see it by light passing up through it. Diffuse reflection of room light is why you can read it.

**59. (B)** For a word to look unchanged it must be a palindrome of vertically symmetric letters. TOOT uses T, O, O, T — all vertically symmetric — and reads the same backwards, so the mirror leaves it as TOOT. BOOK has B and K (not vertically symmetric) and is not a palindrome; CODE has C, D, E (not vertically symmetric); MIRROR has R's and is not a palindrome. TOOT is correct.

**60. (A)** A plane mirror gives a virtual same-size image, a convex mirror a virtual diminished image, and a concave lens a virtual diminished image — all correct in the first row. The second row wrongly calls the plane image real and the convex image magnified; the third wrongly makes a distant-object concave image virtual and a convex image real; the fourth wrongly says a convex lens is always virtual and a convex mirror magnifies. The first row is entirely correct.
