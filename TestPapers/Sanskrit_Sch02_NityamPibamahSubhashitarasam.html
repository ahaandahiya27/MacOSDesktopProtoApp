Worked Solutions

1. (D) सुभाषितम् is a wise / well-spoken saying. It is not a novel, a mountain, or an enemy.

2. (B) सु ('well') + भाषितम् ('spoken') = a well-spoken saying. The other splits are wrong.

3. (A) सुभाषितम् is neuter (ends in -अम्, declines like ज्ञानम्). Sanskrit gender is grammatical; this word is neuter, not feminine or masculine.

4. (A) Being neuter, सुभाषितम् has the plural सुभाषितानि. सुभाषिताः would be a masculine plural; the others are singular or stem forms.

5. (C) सुभाषितम् is neuter, so its plural is सुभाषितानि, not the masculine-looking सुभाषिताः. It is not feminine, a verb, or singular-only.

6. (A) A person is honoured by five 'va' qualities (पञ्चभिः वकारैः). It is not six, three, or ten.

7. (B) The qualities are the means of being honoured, so they take the instrumental (विद्यया, वपुषा, etc.). Not nominative, accusative, or vocative.

8. (D) The five va-qualities are knowledge, body, speech, dress and humility. The second list is the six faults, the third the purification means, and the fourth months.

9. (D) All five begin with व (विद्या, वपुस्, वाणी, वस्त्र, विनय). They do not start with स, are not faults, and are not rivers.

10. (C) विद्यया means 'by knowledge'. Sleep, fear and anger are among the faults, not the honouring qualities.

11. (C) वपुषा means 'by the body / good physique'. Speech is वाचा, dress वस्त्रेण, and humility विनयेन.

12. (C) विनयेन means 'by humility'. Knowledge is विद्यया, dress वस्त्रेण, and speech वाचा.

13. (D) वस्त्रेण means 'by dress / clothing'. Body is वपुषा, speech वाचा, and humility विनयेन.

14. (D) The verse names six faults (षड् दोषाः) to give up. It is not five, two, or ten.

15. (D) The faults are to be discarded by one who desires prosperity (भूतिम् इच्छता). Not by one desiring sleep, enemies, or laziness.

16. (B) हातव्याः means 'to be given up / abandoned'. It does not mean to collect, praise, or sell them.

17. (B) The six faults are sleep, drowsiness, fear, anger, laziness and procrastination. The second list is the five va-qualities, the third the purification means, and the fourth poetic names/objects.

18. (B) निद्रा means 'sleep'. Knowledge, humility and dress are honouring qualities, not faults.

19. (C) क्रोधः means 'anger'. Fear is भयम्, sleep निद्रा, and humility विनय.

20. (C) भयम् means 'fear'. Anger is क्रोधः, drowsiness तन्द्रा, and laziness आलस्यम्.

21. (A) तन्द्रा means 'drowsiness'. Anger is क्रोधः, fear भयम्, and procrastination दीर्घसूत्रता.

22. (C) They are two distinct faults: आलस्यम् is general laziness, दीर्घसूत्रता is procrastination (delaying specific tasks). They are not the same, nor mean fear or sleep.

23. (B) दीर्घसूत्रता ('long-thread-ness') is procrastination. General laziness is आलस्यम्, long life दीर्घायुष्, and anger क्रोधः.

24. (B) आलस्यम् is general laziness. Procrastination is दीर्घसूत्रता, fear भयम्, and drowsiness तन्द्रा.

25. (C) Laziness is framed as the great inner enemy (रिपुः) of those who would achieve. It is not a friend, teacher, or drop.

26. (A) The verse names exactly six distinct faults; merging the similar ones would miscount. It is not five, three, or ten.

27. (C) The body is purified by water (जलेन). Knowledge cleans the intellect, truth the mind, and austerity the soul.

28. (A) The mind is purified by truth (सत्येन). Water cleans the body, knowledge the intellect; dress is unrelated.

29. (B) The soul is purified by austerity (तपसा). Water cleans the body and truth the mind; sleep is a fault.

30. (B) The intellect is purified specifically by knowledge (ज्ञानेन), not by water, truth, or austerity (which clean other parts).

31. (D) बुद्धिः ज्ञानेन शुध्यति - the intellect is purified by knowledge. Water, truth and austerity clean the body, mind and soul respectively.

32. (D) The verse pairs water-body, truth-mind, austerity-soul and knowledge-intellect. The other options scramble the pairs or use one means for all.

33. (C) It means that with each falling water-drop the pot gradually fills. It is not about emptying, the ocean drying, or a mountain crumbling.

34. (A) The pot (घटः) gradually fills, drop by drop. Not the ocean, sky, or an enemy.

35. (A) The image is extended to knowledge, virtue (dharma) and wealth, which also grow gradually. Not to the faults, geography, or literature.

36. (D) Like a pot filling drop by drop, knowledge and virtue grow gradually through steady effort - not instantly, wealth-only, or effortlessly.

37. (C) क्रमशः means 'gradually / step by step'. It does not mean all at once, never, or backwards.

38. (B) Studying a little daily is steady accumulation, the जलबिन्दुनिपातेन ideal - the opposite of procrastination, laziness, or fear.

39. (B) प्रेरकः is 'one who inspires'. An enemy is रिपुः, a pot घटः, and a fault दोषः.

40. (D) An inspiring teacher is a प्रेरकः. रिपुः is an enemy, घटः a pot, and दोषः a fault.

41. (D) दीर्घसूत्रता is procrastination ('long-thread-ness') and दीर्घायुष् is 'long-lived' (long life). The other pairings are wrong.

42. (D) दीर्घायुष् means a long lifespan, a blessing - not a fault, an enemy, or procrastination (which is दीर्घसूत्रता).

43. (A) Sharing दीर्घ but differing shows the whole compound must be read; the first word alone does not fix the meaning, compounds do differ, and the second word matters.

44. (A) रिपुः means 'enemy'. A friend is मित्रम्, a pot घटः, and a wise saying सुभाषितम्.

45. (C) Laziness is called an inner enemy (रिपुः). It is not a saying, a pot, or humility.

46. (B) सङ्गतिः is 'company / association'. An enemy is रिपुः, procrastination दीर्घसूत्रता, and a pot घटः.

47. (B) Good company is valued because the company one keeps shapes one's character. Company does have effect, bad company is not best, and it is not laziness.

48. (A) Choosing good companions reflects the value of good सङ्गतिः (company). Sleep, anger and procrastination are faults, not virtues.

49. (C) They express the means of being honoured, so they are instrumental. Not nominative, genitive, or vocative.

50. (C) The instrumental means 'by / with'. 'To/for' is dative, 'of' genitive, and 'O...!' vocative.

51. (A) पञ्चभिः is the instrumental of पञ्चन् (five). Six is षष्, ten दश, and three त्रि.

52. (D) कतिभिः asks 'by how many', answered by the instrumental plural पञ्चभिः. Not nominative singular, accusative plural, or genitive singular.

53. (B) मनुष्याणाम् is genitive plural, 'of men' - whose enemy laziness is. Not instrumental, accusative, or vocative.

54. (A) रिपुः = enemy. प्रेरकः is an inspirer, सङ्गतिः is company, and घटः is a pot.

55. (B) दीर्घसूत्रता = procrastination. आलस्यम् is laziness, निद्रा sleep, and भयम् fear (anger is क्रोधः).

56. (D) बुद्धिः is purified by knowledge (ज्ञानेन). Water cleans the body, the soul is cleaned by austerity (not truth), and the body not by knowledge.

57. (A) The subhashitas give practical wisdom: qualities to gain, faults to shed, and steady effort. They are not a fox story, cooking rules, or a king-list.

58. (A) Respect comes from the five good qualities and prosperity from giving up the six faults. The other options name wealth, faults, or vices instead.

59. (C) Both verses stress steady effort and using the right means for each goal. They reject instant effortless results, laziness, and the faults.

60. (D) There are six faults, and the intellect is purified by knowledge (ज्ञानेन), not water. The other options keep one or both errors.
