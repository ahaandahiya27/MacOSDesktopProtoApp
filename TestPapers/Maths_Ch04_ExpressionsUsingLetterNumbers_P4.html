Worked Solutions

1. (C) The 90 covers the first print, so only the remaining (p − 1) prints are charged at 24 each: 24(p − 1) + 90. Treating all p prints as paid gives 24p + 90, which double-charges the included first print.

2. (A) a² = 9 so 2a² = 18; ab = (−3)(2) = −6 so −ab = +6; b² = 4 so −b² = −4. Total 18 + 6 − 4 = 20. Treating −ab as −6 (forgetting the negative a flips the sign) gives 18 − 6 − 4 = 8.

3. (B) 3(2x − 5y) = 6x − 15y; −2(4x − 3y) = −8x + 6y. Adding with the lone +y: (6x − 8x) + (−15y + 6y + y) = −2x − 8y. Forgetting to change the sign of −3y inside the second bracket gives −15y − 6y + y = −20y; merging x and y gives other traps.

4. (B) 6(3 − 2m) = 18 − 12m by distributing 6 over both terms. Test m = 1: 18 − 12 = 6 and 6(3 − 2) = 6 match. 6(3 − 12m) = 18 − 72m distributes only the first term; −6(2m + 3) = −12m − 18.

5. (A) The common difference is 3, so the rule is 3n + c. At n = 1 the value is 2, and 3(1) + c = 2 gives c = −1, so 3n − 1. Using just 3n gives 3 at n = 1 (one too many); 3n + 2 gives 5 at n = 1.

6. (C) Day 1 costs 5; the remaining (d − 1) days cost 3 each: 3(d − 1) + 5. Test d = 1: 3(0) + 5 = 5 correct. The trap 3d + 5 gives 8 for a single late day.

7. (A) p − q = 5 − (−2) = 7, so (p − q)² = 49; 2pq = 2(5)(−2) = −20, so −2pq = +20. Total 49 + 20 = 69. Mishandling the −2pq sign as −20 gives 49 − 20 = 29; reading p − q as 3 gives 9-style errors.

8. (C) Innermost: 2a − (a − 4) = 2a − a + 4 = a + 4. Next: 3a − (a + 4) = 2a − 4. Outer: 5a − (2a − 4) = 5a − 2a + 4 = 3a + 4. Test a = 0: 5(0) − {0 − [0 − (−4)]} = −(−4) = 4 = 3(0) + 4 correct.

9. (D) Each row back adds 2 seats; 4 rows back adds 4 × 2 = 8 seats: 18 + 8 = 26. Adding only 4 forgets the 2-per-row step; subtracting moves the wrong direction.

10. (A) 7th term = 49 + 1 = 50; 5th term = 25 + 1 = 26; difference 50 − 26 = 24. The +1 cancels, leaving 49 − 25 = 24. Using (7 − 5)² + something or just (7 − 5) gives small wrong values.

11. (D) Large: 800n. Small: 300(n + 3) = 300n + 900. Sum 1100n + 900, then subtract the 200 discount: 1100n + 900 − 200 = 1100n + 700. Forgetting the discount leaves 1100n + 900.

12. (B) 3x − 2y = 12 − (−2) = 14, so ÷2 = 7; xy = 4(−1) = −4. Total 7 + (−4) = 3. Reading −2y as −2 gives 10 ÷ 2 + (−4) = 1; dropping the xy sign gives 7 + 4 = 11.

13. (C) 4(x + 2) = 4x + 8; −3(2x − 1) = −6x + 3; 2(x − 5) = 2x − 10. x-terms 4x − 6x + 2x = 0. Constants 8 + 3 − 10 = 1. The x-terms cancel, leaving the constant 1. Test x = 5: 28 − 27 + 0 = 1 correct.

14. (B) 2(3x − 4) = 6x − 8; 3(x + 2) = 3x + 6. Sum (6x + 3x) + (−8 + 6) = 9x − 2. Test x = 1: 2(−1) + 3(3) = 7 and 9 − 2 = 7 match. Dropping the +6 gives 9x − 14.

15. (A) The increases are 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, … (one more each stage). Stage 5 total is 15; the step into stage 6 is the next number, 6 (giving total 21). Using 5 repeats the previous step; 21 is the stage-6 total, not the increase.

16. (D) A = x; B = 2x + 10; C = x/2. Total x + 2x + 10 + x/2 = (1 + 2 + 1/2)x + 10 = (7/2)x + 10. Forgetting pipe C's half gives 3x + 10; forgetting pipe A's own x gives (5/2)x + 10.

17. (B) a²b = 4(−3) = −12; ab² = 2(9) = 18, so −ab² = −18. Total −12 − 18 = −30. Reading ab² as 2(−9) = −18 (squaring then keeping the negative) gives −12 + 18 = 6.

18. (A) Remove the bracket changing every inside sign: −2p + 4q − 9. Combine: p-terms 7p − 2p = 5p; q-terms −3q + 4q + q = 2q; constants 5 − 9 = −4. Result 5p + 2q − 4. Not flipping the bracket signs gives 5p − 8q + 14.

19. (C) 6(4x − 9) = 24x − 54, which differs (test x = 0: −54 vs −36). The others expand to 24x − 36: 12(2x − 3) = 24x − 36 and 4(6x − 9) = 24x − 36.

20. (B) Sum 5 + 8 + 11 = 24. Common difference 3 gives 3n + c; at n = 1 the value 5 means c = 2, so 3n + 2. Check n = 2: 6 + 2 = 8 correct. Rule 3n gives 3 at n = 1, wrong.

21. (C) The coefficient of x is the signed number multiplying x, here −5; the constant term is the one with no letter, 7. Dropping the negative sign gives 5; reading the x³ coefficient gives −1.

22. (A) n² = 16, so −2n² = −32; 3n = 3(−4) = −12. Total 100 − 32 − 12 = 56. Using 3n = +12 gives 80; treating (−4)² as −16 gives 100 + 32 − 12 = 120.

23. (A) x²-terms 3x² − x² = 2x² (the count drops, the power stays); x-terms 5x + 2x = 7x; constants −2 − 7 = −9. Result 2x² + 7x − 9. Adding exponents to get x⁴ is the classic trap.

24. (C) The 60 covers the first 2 km; only (d − 2) extra kilometres are charged at 15: 15(d − 2) + 60. Test d = 2: 15(0) + 60 = 60 correct. Charging all d km gives 15d + 60.

25. (B) 5(2a − 3) = 10a − 15; −2(a + 4) = −2a − 8. Sum (10a − 2a) + (−15 − 8) = 8a − 23. Forgetting to distribute the 2 over the +4 (treating it as −0) lands on 8a − 15-style traps.

26. (C) An n-by-n square has n² tiles; removing 4 corners gives n² − 4. For n = 5: 25 − 4 = 21. Forgetting to remove corners gives 25; using 4n − 4 = 16 confuses this with a border count.

27. (B) 2a + b = 2 + 4 = 6, so (2a + b)² = 36; dividing by b = 4 gives 36 ÷ 4 = 9. Reading 2a as 'a then double' or squaring only part of the bracket changes the numerator; forgetting to square gives 6 ÷ 4-style errors.

28. (A) 4(a − 2b) = 4a − 8b; −2(2a − 3b) = −4a + 6b. a-terms 4a − 4a = 0; b-terms −8b + 6b + 5b = 3b. The a-terms cancel, leaving 3b. Sign slip on −3b inside gives −8b − 6b + 5b errors.

29. (D) Common difference 4 gives 4r + c; at r = 1 the count is 12, so 4(1) + c = 12 gives c = 8, hence 4r + 8. Check r = 2: 8 + 8 = 16 = 12 + 4 correct. Using 4r + 12 over-counts.

30. (D) Perimeter at n = 10 is 22; at n = 6 is 14; difference 22 − 14 = 8. Equivalently the rule grows by 2 per square over 4 extra squares: 2 × 4 = 8. Using just (10 − 6) gives 4.

31. (C) 5 meals cost 5f; 2 desserts at 30 cost 60. Total 5f + 60. A dessert on all 5 days would give 150, but only 2 days have a dessert.

32. (D) (x + y)/2 = 4/2 = 2; (x − y)/2 = 14/2 = 7. Difference 2 − 7 = −5. Notice the expression simplifies to y, and y = −5. Misreading x − y as 9 − 5 = 4 derails the middle step.

33. (A) Remove brackets with sign care: 3a + 2b − a + b − 2a − 4b. a-terms 3a − a − 2a = 0; b-terms 2b + b − 4b = −b. Result −b. Not flipping −(a − b) leaves stray a-terms.

34. (B) Triple: 3x. Subtract 4: 3x − 4. Double the whole result: 2(3x − 4). Test x = 2: triple 6, minus 4 = 2, doubled = 4; 2(6 − 4) = 4 matches. 2(3x) − 4 doubles before subtracting, giving a different value.

35. (B) Figure 3 has side n + 2 = 5; border tiles = 4(5) − 4 = 16. Forgetting the −4 (which removes the double-counted corners) gives 20; the full square is 25.

36. (C) 3p² = 3(4) = 12; 2pq = 2(−2)(3) = −12, so −2pq = +12; q² = 9. Total 12 + 12 + 9 = 33. Taking −2pq as −12 gives 12 − 12 + 9 = 9.

37. (D) Inside: 4 − (x − 1) = 4 − x + 1 = 5 − x. So 2x + 6 − (5 − x) + x = 2x + 6 − 5 + x + x = 4x + 1. Mishandling the sign on (x − 1) is the trap. Test x = 0: 6 − 5 = 1 correct.

38. (A) 3(2x − 1) = 6x − 3; −(x − 5) = −x + 5. Sum (6x − x) + (−3 + 5) = 5x + 2. Test x = 1: 3(1) − (−4) = 7 and 5 + 2 = 7 match. Not flipping −(x − 5) gives 5x − 8.

39. (D) Terms are 3, 5, 7, 9; sum 3 + 5 + 7 + 9 = 24. Using 2(4) + 1 = 9 finds only the 4th term, not the sum; 4 × 5 = 20 mistakes the middle value.

40. (A) One bouquet: r + 2r + 3 = 3r + 3 flowers. For m bouquets: m(3r + 3) = 3mr + 3m. Forgetting to multiply the ferns by m gives 3mr + 3; one bouquet only gives 3r + 3.

41. (C) 3 − x = 2, so (3 − x)² = 4; 2 × 4 = 8; 5 − 8 = −3. Reading 2(3 − x)² as (2(3 − x))² = 16 gives 5 − 16 = −11; ignoring the square gives 5 − 2(2) = 1-style errors.

42. (C) Remove brackets: 6m − 2n − 3m + 5n + m − n. m-terms 6m − 3m + m = 4m; n-terms −2n + 5n − n = 2n. Result 4m + 2n. Not flipping −(3m − 5n) gives the −8n trap.

43. (A) (1/2)(6x − 8) = 3x − 4; (1/3)(9x + 6) = 3x + 2. Sum (3x + 3x) + (−4 + 2) = 6x − 2. Test x = 0: (1/2)(−8) + (1/3)(6) = −4 + 2 = −2 and 6(0) − 2 = −2 match.

44. (B) Added dots = (n + 1)² − n² = n² + 2n + 1 − n² = 2n + 1. Check n = 3: 16 − 9 = 7 = 2(3) + 1 correct. These are the odd numbers 3, 5, 7, …; using 2n gives even numbers.

45. (A) The 250 covers the first 50 pages; only (t − 50) pages cost 4 each: 4(t − 50) + 250. Test t = 50: 4(0) + 250 = 250 correct. Charging all t pages gives 4t + 250.

46. (C) ab = (2)(−1) = −2; bc = (−1)(3) = −3; ca = (3)(2) = 6. Total −2 − 3 + 6 = 1. Mishandling bc as +3 gives −2 + 3 + 6 = 7.

47. (C) 5(2a + 3) = 10a + 15; −4(a + 2) = −4a − 8; −(3a − 1) = −3a + 1. a-terms 10a − 4a − 3a = 3a; constants 15 − 8 + 1 = 8. Result 3a + 8. Not flipping −(3a − 1) drops the +1, giving 3a + 6.

48. (B) A = 2(9) − 5 = 13; B = 15 + 1 = 16; C = 27 − 10 = 17; D = 12 + 4 = 16. The greatest is C at 17. Treating 2x² as (2x)² = 36 would wrongly inflate A.

49. (D) Clay for the dolls: k kg each times (k + 2) dolls = k(k + 2) = k² + 2k; plus the fixed 6 kg waste: k² + 2k + 6. Forgetting to distribute k over both terms gives k² + 6.

50. (B) 3a² = 3(16) = 48 (square first, then multiply); (3a)² = 12² = 144 (multiply first, then square); 3a + 3 = 12 + 3 = 15. Confusing 3a² with (3a)² gives 144 for both.

51. (A) (4x − y); −2(x − 3y) = −2x + 6y; 3(y − x) = 3y − 3x. x-terms 4x − 2x − 3x = −x; y-terms −y + 6y + 3y = 8y. Result −x + 8y. Sign slip on −2(x − 3y) gives the −4y trap.

52. (D) Test 4n − 1: n = 10 gives 40 − 1 = 39. Confirm n = 9 gives 35 (too small) and n = 11 gives 43 (too big). So the 10th term is 39.

53. (C) Saturday = 2(6c + 2d) = 12c + 4d. Total = (6c + 2d) + (12c + 4d) + (4c − 3d). c-terms 6 + 12 + 4 = 22c; d-terms 2 + 4 − 3 = 3d. Result 22c + 3d. Forgetting to double Saturday gives 16c.

54. (D) First bracket 2x − 1 = 3; second bracket x + 3 = 5; product 3 × 5 = 15. Adding the brackets instead of multiplying gives 8; this needs two substitutions then a product.

55. (D) The coefficient of x is the signed number on x, here +6; the constant term is the one with no letter, −9. The coefficient of x² is −1 (the sign attaches). Dropping signs gives the distractors.

56. (D) P = 16; Q = 8 + 8 = 16; R = 16 + 1 = 17; S = 12 + 6 = 18. Only P and Q share the value 16; R = 17 and S = 18 are each different. Treating 2x + 8 as 'multiply 2 by x then by 8' or reading x² as 2x would break the match.

57. (B) xy-terms 3xy + 4xy + xy = 8xy; x-terms −2x − 5x − x = −8x. Result 8xy − 8x. The xy terms keep the xy (powers do not change); merging xy with x is the trap.

58. (D) a³ = 27; b³ = (−2)³ = −8, so −b³ = +8. Total 27 + 8 = 35. Treating (−2)³ as +8 (forgetting an odd power keeps the negative) gives 27 − 8 = 19.

59. (B) Layers have 1, 3, 5, 7 bricks; total 1 + 3 + 5 + 7 = 16. Using 2(4) − 1 = 7 finds only the 4th layer; the sum of the first 4 odd numbers is 4² = 16.

60. (D) 7(a − 1) = 7a − 7; −3(2a − 5) = −6a + 15; 2(a + 1) = 2a + 2. a-terms 7a − 6a + 2a = 3a; constants −7 + 15 + 2 = 10. So 3a + 10; at a = 0 the value is the constant 10, not 0.
