Distractors weaponise the deepest misconceptions of the chapter: convex/concave mirrors swapped with convex/concave lenses, a converging device called diverging, virtual images projected onto screens, plane-mirror flips along the wrong axis, image distance scaled wrongly, a concave mirror said to always magnify, a convex mirror said to magnify, a prism said to create or add colours, red bending more than violet, a single pure colour split into a spectrum, and 'image grows as you approach' for a flat mirror. 1. A lit candle stands on a table in a room with the curtains drawn. You can see the candle from anywhere in the room, but its sharp shadow on the wall has crisp straight edges only directly behind it.    (C) is bent gently around the edges of the candle
