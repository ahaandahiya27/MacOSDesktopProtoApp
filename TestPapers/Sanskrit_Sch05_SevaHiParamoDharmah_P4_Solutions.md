## Worked Solutions

**1. (C)** A superlative of rank places one item at the top of an existing set; it does not deny the rest of the set, nor reorder it by time, nor flatten the ranking to equality. Saying service is highest concedes other duties are real yet subordinate.

**2. (B)** परमः supplies the top-of-set ranking. Strip it and service is asserted to be a duty but not ranked above others; the loss is precisely the comparative supremacy, independent of हि and of the noun meanings.

**3. (D)** The two particles pull in opposite directions: हि strengthens a stated truth, while a question particle suspends the truth to ask about it. हि neither negates, conjoins, nor governs case.

**4. (C)** The subject (the thing ranked) maps to सेवा, the emphatic to हि, the ranking superlative to परमः, and the category it tops to धर्मः. Patience is the subject, verily the emphatic, highest the superlative, virtue the category.

**5. (A)** The lesson contrasts outward distinctions with inward, enacted compassion and locates true worth in the latter. Credentials, reward, and remote donation are exactly what the chapter ranks below served compassion.

**6. (A)** An exemplar lets the reader test the abstract claim against observable choices. The physician is illustrative, not exclusive; the maxim stands on its own grammatically; and the ethics, not the vocabulary, is the point.

**7. (B)** Only परमो needs undoing: it is परमः before the सन्धि. सेवा and धर्मः are unchanged; सेवा is singular (not सेवाः), and परमम् would be a wrong neuter accusative.

**8. (C)** The rule keys on voicing of the following sound, not on its grammatical category, aspiration, or place of articulation. ध् is voiced, so -अः → ओ.

**9. (D)** The -अः → ओ change happens only before a VOICED consonant. ग् is voiced (रामो गच्छति is right); प्, क्, and त् are voiceless, so विसर्ग does not become ओ there.

**10. (C)** Sandhi is conditioned by what follows. With nothing following, there is no voiced trigger, so the विसर्ग simply stays. The ओ form is not an inherent property of the word.

**11. (C)** Both yield ओ, but before a following अ the अ is elided and shown by अवग्रह (परमोऽयम्); before a voiced consonant like ध् there is no vowel to elide, hence no अवग्रह.

**12. (B)** वृद्धि-सन्धि fuses आ + ए into ऐ, so सेवा + एव → सेवैव. The other forms misapply or skip the vowel sandhi.

**13. (B)** The chapter fixes सेवा to the giving of one's own time, body and presence. Money sent without presence — however generous and sustained — is दान, not सेवा.

**14. (B)** Presence-with-the-body is सेवा (Y); resource-transfer-from-afar is दान (X). The thesis ranks service highest, so Y's contribution is the exalted one even without money.

**15. (D)** The crowning rests on the depth of self-giving: presence engages the whole person, money only one's surplus. The distractors wrongly condemn दान, gate it by wealth, or invert the difficulty.

**16. (D)** सेवा pure of दान is presence and effort with no transfer of money or goods. The student gives only time and care; the other three give resources from a distance, which is दान.

**17. (D)** Two acts can share an end yet differ in means; the chapter distinguishes them precisely by means (presence vs transfer). दान does help, gender is irrelevant, and the child has the two definitions backwards in the last option.

**18. (A)** Subject in nominative (चिकित्सकः), object in accusative plural (रोगिणः), verb आत्मनेपद 3rd-sg (सेवते). सेवति/सेवयति are wrong voices/forms; रोगिभ्यः and रोगिणाम् are wrong cases; चिकित्सकं would make the doctor the object.

**19. (A)** णत्व is triggered by a preceding र्/ष् retroflexing a later न् → ण; it is a property of रोगिन्'s phonology, not of the case ending, not of all consonants, and not of the verb's voice.

**20. (B)** इन्-stems mask the न् in the nominative singular (where it drops and the vowel lengthens) but reveal it in oblique forms. रोगी is therefore the surface nom. sg. of रोगिन्, not a different stem, lookalike, or feminine.

**21. (A)** Two physicians is nominative DUAL (चिकित्सकौ); one sick man as object is accusative SINGULAR of रोगिन्, which is रोगिणम् (ण by णत्व). The others mismatch number or swap subject/object cases.

**22. (D)** आत्मनेपद 1st-sg present is सेवे. सेवामि is the wrong (परस्मैपद) voice, सेवसे is 'you serve', and सेवामहे is 'we serve'.

**23. (C)** The object of सेव् stands in the accusative: नृपं. Dative (नृपाय), instrumental (नृपेण), and genitive (नृपस्य) would all misassign the object's case.

**24. (C)** -क builds the agent (अध्यापकः, 'teacher'), -इन् builds the 'one characterised by/having' (धनिन्, 'wealthy'). Swapping or doubling the suffixes breaks the agent/possessive split.

**25. (A)** -वत् has two senses: 'like' (इव) and 'having/possessing'. यन्त्रवत् uses 'like'. अग्निवत् 'like fire' matches; धनवत्, बुद्धिवत्, पुत्रवत् all use the possessive sense.

**26. (B)** The comparison praises stamina and constancy, not the machine's material, price, or coldness — indeed the chapter pairs this stamina with मानवीय warmth, so coldness is the opposite of the point.

**27. (A)** By the 'like' सेन्स of -वत्, सरितवत् borrows the river's salient quality of ceaseless flow — apt for unflagging care. 'Ownership' is the wrong sense, and rigidity/harm are not a river's praiseworthy traits.

**28. (B)** उपाधिः names an earned credential, पदवी names a held office. The distractors swap them, confuse them with कषाय/यन्त्र, or collapse the distinction.

**29. (C)** A department headship is a held post → पदवी; an honorary doctorate is an earned/conferred credential → उपाधिः. Swapping or merging the two is the trap.

**30. (B)** Listing both the degree and the post is a totalising move: ALL outward marks of status are set down. It does not mean he keeps one while dropping the other, nor that he abandons medicine.

**31. (B)** परमो धर्मः places सेवा at the top; उपाधिः and पदवी are subordinated as outward distinctions. The distractors invert the ranking or flatten it to equality.

**32. (C)** The -ईय तद्धित (with initial वृद्धि) yields दैव/दैवीय 'befitting a god', exactly paralleling मानव→मानवीय. -वत्, -इन्, -क are different suffixes with different meanings.

**33. (A)** An -ईय adjective agrees with its noun in gender, number and case. A feminine singular नोun needs मानवीया. Keeping the masc.-pl. form, or shifting to neuter/accusative, breaks agreement.

**34. (D)** Among the six रसाः, कषाय is the astringent taste. Sweet, sour and bitter are मधुर, अम्ल and तिक्त respectively — distinct tastes.

**35. (B)** कषाय flips sense purely by context. A medical, treatment scene fixes the 'decoction' reading. Gender, number and word-order do not decide between the two senses.

**36. (B)** The word रस ('taste') and a food context fix the taste sense — astringent. The medical/decoction readings belong to a treatment context, which is absent here.

**37. (A)** चिकित्सक uses agent -क, रोगिन् uses characterising -इन्, यन्त्रवत् uses 'like' -वत्, मानवीय uses -ईय. The other orderings mis-pair the suffixes or wrongly assign -क to all.

**38. (C)** पदवी means a held post/rank, not a decoction; the decoction is कषायम्. The other three pairings are accurate.

**39. (A)** Agent -क names the healer (चिकित्सकः); characterising -इन् names the sick (रोगिन्); the doctor is subject, the sick the object. The distractors swap roles or suffixes.

**40. (C)** The two images are complementary: borrow the machine's stamina, keep the human's warmth. Taking the machine image to mean coldness, or dropping the stamina, misses the deliberate balance.

**41. (B)** The chapter ranks service and humane qualities above outward distinction, so a hiring rule built only on उपाधिः/पदवी misses the real measure. Wealth and machines are not the chapter's preferred substitutes.

**42. (C)** Three errors compound: the object must be accusative रोगिणः with the doctor as server; the verb is आत्मनेपद सेवते not सेवति; and -वत् is 'like', not 'having'. The full correction fixes all three.

**43. (D)** Error one: परमः is highest in rank, not first in time. Error two: in the original service is the SUBJECT ranked highest, but the paraphrase makes 'duty' the subject and 'service' the predicate. हि is not separately mistranslated.

**44. (A)** The proof is the chain of choices that subordinate status to service: dropping titles, serving tirelessly yet humanely, attending the sick in person. Each distractor reverses one link of that chain.

**45. (A)** The correct line keeps 'highest rank', specifies in-person service (not remote दान), and unites tireless stamina with humane warmth. Each distractor violates at least one of the three constraints.

**46. (D)** Degrees and posts are possessions (what one HAS); service is an enacted, ongoing deed (what one DOES). The chapter privileges the doing over the having; the distractors invert the mapping.

**47. (A)** Mentioning a thing is not endorsing it; here the terms are introduced only to be subordinated to service. The rebuttal exposes the leap from 'named often' to 'championed'.

**48. (D)** Credentials, rank and wealth he already has in abundance; what is missing is humane, selfless service — the very thing the chapter crowns highest. More titles or money would not repair that lack.

**49. (B)** The chapter's ideal fuses machine-like endurance with human warmth. B supplies the first but not the second; A supplies neither well. Neither alone is the complete model the chapter paints.

**50. (D)** The objection is substantive, not grammatical: the chapter places service above giving, so substituting दान contradicts its ranking. दानम् can fill the slot and is a noun; it is not synonymous with सेवा.

**51. (D)** रोगिणः is syncretic (it can be gen. sg. or acc. pl.), but the verb सेव् governs the accusative, so the object reading 'serves the sick (acc. pl.)' wins. सेव् does not take a genitive object, and the doctor remains the subject.

**52. (A)** Agent -क builds the doer: just as चिकित्सक is 'one who heals', सेवक is 'one who serves' — the server, not the served. -इन्, -वत्, -ईय would give the wrong role or wrong kind of word.

**53. (A)** The 'like' sense of -वत् makes यन्त्रवत् an adverb of manner, so the line says he serves machine-like (untiringly), not that he wields a machine. The instrumental sense would require a different construction and reading.

**54. (D)** परमः is an adjective in concord with धर्मः, both nominative singular; only sandhi changes its appearance. No compound is formed — adjective + noun in agreement is a phrase, and the sandhi alone joins the surface forms.

**55. (D)** Adjective and noun must agree in number, so the plural is परमाः धर्माः. Mixing singular with plural, or shifting to dual, breaks the required concord.

**56. (B)** The inference works only if doing a tangible task = real service; that is the bridging assumption. The decoction reading is settled by context, no credential is in view, and the doctor does it himself, not via a delegate.

**57. (C)** हि strengthens, not weakens, and is not interrogative; nor is it grammatically obligatory (the sentence stands without it). For maximum force, keep it for its 'verily' emphasis.

**58. (C)** The correct unpacking carries every load-bearing element: highest-rank service, in-person rather than remote, tireless yet humane, above titles. Each distractor swaps the crowned value for one the chapter explicitly ranks below service.

**59. (C)** ध् is a voiced consonant, so the final -अः becomes ओ exactly as in परमो धर्मः, giving चिकित्सको. The विसर्ग does not stay unchanged, become र्, or drop to leave -आ before a voiced consonant.

**60. (D)** The chapter ranks present, personal service above remote giving, so naming the biggest donor the 'greatest servant' confuses दान with सेवा. दान is not condemned outright, nor is it the same act as सेवा merely mislabelled.
