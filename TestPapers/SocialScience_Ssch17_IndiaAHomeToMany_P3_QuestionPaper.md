**Marking scheme:** **+4** for each correct answer · **−1** for each wrong answer · **0** if unattempted.

**Instructions:** Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. Classic P3 traps: assuming a community came to TRADE when it actually fled persecution (or was brought enslaved, like the Siddis); collapsing Islam's peaceful 7th-century arrival through Arab merchants into the much later military invasions; confusing 'refuge/opportunity', 'immigrate/emigrate', 'cosmopolitan/pluralism/tolerance/ethos'; assuming integration meant losing one's identity (it did not — synagogues, fire temples, churches and languages were kept); and treating diversity as a weakness when the chapter presents it as a source of strength, in the spirit of vasudhaiva kutumbakam. Do not write on this paper — mark answers on your answer sheet.

1. A speaker argues that India should keep out all foreigners 'to protect its culture'. Which value from this chapter most directly contradicts that argument?
