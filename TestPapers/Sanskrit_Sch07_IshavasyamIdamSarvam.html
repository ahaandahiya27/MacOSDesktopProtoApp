Instructions: Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. Questions test the verse's meaning and grammar. Watch the classic traps: ईश means 'Lord' (root ईश्), nothing to do with English '-ish'; ब्रह्म (neuter, the Absolute) differs from ब्रह्मन् (a priest) and ब्रह्मा (the creator-god); सर्वशक्तिमान् uses the -मत्/-वत् suffix ('possessing') to mean ALL-POWERFUL; दास्यन्ति is FUTURE tense ('they shall give', the -स्य- marker), not present or past; and यक्ष/गन्धर्व/किन्नर are SEMI-divine, below the gods. Worked Solutions

1. (C) It means 'all this is pervaded by the Lord'. It does not confine God to temples, deny the divine, or deny a creator.

2. (A) ईशावास्यम् = ईश्वरेण व्याप्तम् = 'pervaded by the Lord'. Not ruled by a king, made of gold, or godless.

3. (D) इदं सर्वम् means 'all this' (the whole world). Not one thing, nothing, or only the temple.

4. (B) The claim is that God pervades everything, everywhere. It is not sky-only, idol-only, or a denial of God.

5. (C) It opens the Ishopanishad. It is not the Ramayana, the Arthashastra, or a fox fable.

6. (A) If the Lord pervades all, one should treat the world with reverence and care - not contempt, greed, or indifference.

7. (A) ईश means 'the Lord'. It has nothing to do with English '-ish', and is not a fox or flower.

8. (D) ईश is from the root ईश् ('to rule, to own'). It does not mean eat, drink, or sleep.

9. (C) ईश्वर ('Lord') is a derivative of ईश. किंशुक (a flower), शृगालः (a fox) and घटः (a pot) are unrelated.

10. (B) ईश is a Sanskrit word for 'Lord' (root ईश्), not an English suffix, a fox, or a number.

11. (A) ईश and ईश्वर share the root ईश् and both name the Supreme. They are not opposites, 'student', or 'sun'.

12. (A) Neuter ब्रह्म is the impersonal Absolute. A priest is ब्रह्मन्, the creator-god ब्रह्मा, and a fox शृगालः.

13. (D) Masculine ब्रह्मन् means a priest. The Absolute is neuter ब्रह्म, the creator-god ब्रह्मा, and a flower पुष्पम्.

14. (D) ब्रह्मा is the creator-god. A priest is ब्रह्मन्, the Absolute neuter ब्रह्म, and a student छात्रः.

15. (B) ईशावास्यम् refers to the neuter ब्रह्म, the all-pervading Absolute. Not the priest, the creator-god, or none.

16. (B) Sanskrit distinguishes the three by gender and spelling. Not by colour, price, or time of day.

17. (A) They look similar but name three different things (the Absolute, a priest, the creator-god). They are not identical, the same, or verbs.

18. (C) सर्वशक्तिमान् means 'all-powerful / omnipotent'. It is not all-knowing, all-seeing, or powerless.

19. (C) सर्व ('all') + शक्ति ('power') + -मत्/-मान् ('possessing') = 'possessing all power'. The other readings are wrong.

20. (D) The -मत्/-वत् suffix means 'possessing'. 'Like' is -वत् in a different sense (समासे); 'lacking' and 'to/for' are wrong here.

21. (C) It describes God as possessing the quality of all-power (omnipotence) - not gadgets, no power, or mere physical strength.

22. (D) The suffix marks possession of the quality of all-power, not a plural of objects, a tense, or a case.

23. (B) बुद्धिमान् means 'possessing intelligence', using the same -मत्/-वत् suffix. Not 'lacking', 'like', or 'to'.

24. (A) आराधनम् means 'worship'. Meditation is ध्यानम्, Name-chanting नामकीर्तनम्, and a stain कलङ्कः.

25. (A) ध्यानम् means 'meditation'. Worship is आराधनम्, Name-chanting नामकीर्तनम्, and a family कुलम्.

26. (C) नामकीर्तनम् is chanting/singing the Name in praise. Worship is आराधनम्, meditation ध्यानम्, and a family-stain कुलकलङ्कः.

27. (C) नाम ('Name') + कीर्तनम् ('praise-singing') = chanting the Name. The other splits are wrong.

28. (B) The wise remember God by meditation and Name-chanting. Not by quarrelling, sleeping, or hoarding wealth.

29. (D) The wise respond with worship, meditation and Name-chanting - not fear, greed, or laziness.

30. (D) They are instrumental, the means of remembering. Not nominative, accusative, or vocative.

31. (B) They are semi-divine beings (upa-deva). Not full gods, ordinary humans, or asuras.

32. (A) Semi-divine beings attend the gods but are not gods themselves. They are not the supreme gods, mere mortals, or anti-god demons.

33. (C) They are semi-divine, below the देवs (gods). They are not above the gods, the same as humans, or asuras.

34. (B) गन्धर्वः is a semi-divine being. ब्रह्मा is a god (creator), शृगालः a fox, and चिकित्सकः a doctor.

35. (B) The order is gods -> semi-divine -> humans -> anti-gods. The other orders scramble the hierarchy.

36. (A) The gods rank above the semi-divine beings. Not below, equal, or unrelated.

37. (B) Naming semi-divine beings shows a hierarchy of beings, all within the all-pervading Lord. Not no order, only humans, or only gods.

38. (D) दास्यन्ति means 'they shall/will give' (future). It is not past, present, or an imperative 'should give'.

39. (C) दास्यन्ति is future tense (लृट्-लकार). It is not present, past, or imperative.

40. (B) दास्यन्ति = दानं करिष्यन्ति = 'they will give'. Not 'have given', 'are giving now', or 'let them give'.

41. (A) The -स्य- marker signals the future tense. It does not mark past tense, plural, or the dative.

42. (D) दा + स्य + न्ति = 'they will give'. Not 'they gave', 'give!', or 'they go'.

43. (A) The present 'they give' is ददति / दत्ते. दास्यन्ति is future, अदुः a past form, and देयम् a gerundive.

44. (C) ध्यायन्ति means 'they meditate'. They give is ददति, sleep स्वपन्ति, and quarrel विवदन्ते.

45. (C) Silent contemplation is ध्यायन्ति (they meditate). Not 'they will give', 'they quarrel', or 'they sleep'.

46. (B) कुलकलङ्कः means 'a stain on the family'. Not family pride, a ritual, or a semi-divine being.

47. (B) कुल ('family') + कलङ्क ('stain') = a stain on the family. The other splits are wrong.

48. (B) Such a person is a कुलकलङ्कः (a stain on the family). The others mean all-powerful, semi-divine, or a doctor.

49. (C) It warns against conduct that disgraces one's family - not against worship, meditation, or giving.

50. (D) सर्वशक्तिमान् = all-powerful. आराधनम् is worship (not meditation), दास्यन्ति is future (not past), and कुलकलङ्कः is a family-stain (not pride).

51. (D) ध्यानम् = meditation and नामकीर्तनम् = Name-chanting. The other options misdefine these terms.

52. (C) दास्यन्ति is future, and -मत्/-वत् means 'possessing'. The other pairings are wrong.

53. (B) ब्रह्म (neuter) is the Absolute and ब्रह्मा the creator-god. ब्रह्मन् is a priest; they do not all mean the same.

54. (A) गन्धर्वः is semi-divine and ब्रह्मा a (creator) god. The other options misclassify the beings.

55. (C) The theme is the all-pervading Lord and how the wise honour the Supreme. It is not the fox fable, banking, or government.

56. (A) The verse invites us to see the divine in everything, everywhere - not in one temple, nowhere, or only the sky.

57. (A) The grammar focus includes the future tense (दास्यन्ति) and the -मत्/-वत् suffix. Not only the dative, direction words, or tastes.

58. (D) Seeing God in all, the wise respond with worship, meditation and Name-chanting - not arrogance, greed, or indifference.

59. (D) Together they describe a God who is all-powerful and present everywhere - not powerless, local, or semi-divine.

60. (D) सर्वशक्तिमान् means all-powerful, and दास्यन्ति is future tense. The other options keep one or both errors.
