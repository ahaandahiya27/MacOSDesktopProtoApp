## Worked Solutions

**1. (B)** The compound transfers a warrior's valour-word onto a caretaker because heroism is gauged by the weight of the loss accepted, not by carrying arms; the other readings either reduce it to physical strength, empty out वीर-, or substitute beauty for courage.

**2. (A)** Swapping a doll for the son preserves the misdirection that saves the heir but removes the irreplaceable loss of a life, so the deed would shrink from total renunciation to mere trickery; the option claiming nothing changes ignores that the chapter's praise rests on the cost, not the rescue alone.

**3. (D)** धात्री explains धाया as the same thing; प्रसूता is a contrasting role (one who bore the child); धैर्य merely sounds similar but means steadfastness — collapsing these three distinct relations into one 'opposite' is the slip, and the other options invent false meanings.

**4. (D)** The -एन ending marks instrument and the -तुम् form is a purpose-infinitive depending on 'wished'; the sentence thus reports an intention and its means, never a completed act, so the option asserting a finished killing reverses both labels.

**5. (A)** The wicked scheme must arise first, prompting Panna's pre-emptive swap; only then can the strike fall on the substitute, which alone leaves the true heir alive to reign — placing the strike before the swap or the reign before the strike breaks causation.

**6. (B)** A determinative compound takes the gender of its final member, here the feminine अङ्गना ('woman'), so वीराङ्गना is feminine regardless of masculine वीर-; the claim that the first member fixes gender is the misconception.

**7. (A)** Success is defined by an act done, not by the intended identity; the blow landed on the substitute, so a completed killing and the prince's survival are perfectly compatible — the resolution the chapter's misdirection supplies.

**8. (B)** अमारयत् is a finite past verb reporting the deed done, while हन्तुम् is a purpose-infinitive stating the aim of the coming; only the first commits to an actual death, so the option reversing them, and the one denying any killing-verb, both fail.

**9. (C)** The -ैः ending marks the means of recording, keeping Panna's name as what is honoured and the letters as the instrument; reading it as nominative flips subject and tool, which is the precise grammatical fault to avoid.

**10. (A)** Sacrifice is measured by what is surrendered, and fulfilling a duty that exacts one's dearest possession is the height of it, not its negation; the claims that duty makes the cost nil, or that only whim could ennoble it, both misread the chapter's praise.

**11. (A)** Each nominative is its clause's doer and each accusative its undergoer: Banbir makes the plot, Panna places her son, the assassin strikes the boy — the options swapping these roles or merging all under one agent misread the case endings.

**12. (C)** The deed's force comes from loyalty unaided by blood-instinct; if Udai Singh were her own son the rescue could be ascribed to maternal instinct, diluting the demonstration of निष्ठा — so the धाया framing intensifies, and the 'strengthen' and 'no change' readings miss this.

**13. (A)** षष्टितम is the ordinal of sixty ('sixtieth'), unrelated to षोडश ('sixteen'); the chapter's century is the sixteenth, so the writer errs both in the number-word chosen and in the value it denotes — the options treating षष्टितम as 'sixteenth' are wrong.

**14. (B)** Survival means the cost did not end in a moment of death but extended into a lifetime of mourning, deepening the sacrifice; the readings that survival nullifies the risk, brings a crown, or proves the plot failed all contradict the chapter.

**15. (C)** The postposition कृते with a genitive expresses benefactive 'for the sake of', and अत्यजत् is a completed past act of renouncing; the boy is Panna's son (पन्नायाः पुत्रः), so the readings making it agency, future, or the prince's own son all misparse the case relations.

**16. (C)** Replacing one purpose-infinitive with another keeps the syntactic frame and reverses only the goal, showing -तुम् marks purpose independent of the root's meaning; the alternatives wrongly assign tense, ungrammaticality, or subjecthood to the form.

**17. (C)** Banbir's aim is reversed by the swap, Panna's loyal aim costs Chandan his life, and the passive infant Udai Singh gains everything he never sought — the rows crediting Banbir with the throne, Panna with reward, or merging all intentions misread the relationship-web.

**18. (D)** The clause explicitly says she did not fight (न अयुध्यत) and instead neutralised the deception, i.e. by substitution rather than force; the sword-fight, bribe, and treasury readings all import actions the text denies.

**19. (D)** Only the first keeps every fact and label right: सप्तमी locative for the century, an infinitive for the purpose, the son (not Panna) as the one killed, the prince's survival, and instrumental golden letters; each distractor breaks the date, a tense label, a case, or the facts.

**20. (C)** Deceit underlies the wicked scheme on the rival's side, while loyalty flowers into total renunciation on Panna's — a clean vice-to-virtue layering; the options that cross-assign सर्वस्वत्याग or निष्ठा to the wrong person scramble the moral map.

**21. (B)** Centuries are numbered one ahead of their leading digits, so the sixteenth covers 1501–1600; the peer commits the standard off-by-one slip of reading the ordinal as the boundary year 1600.

**22. (A)** A passive recasting moves the doer into the instrumental (बनवीरेण) and makes the former object the subject-patient, keeping the intention's direction intact; the distractors reverse agent and patient or replace the event entirely.

**23. (A)** History asserts that its persons, places and times exist outside the text, so they invite verification rather than the free invention of fable — the readings that flatten the genres, discard the moral, or universally doubt the names all miss this.

**24. (C)** The element -आगार denotes a room/store and combines with the activity housed there; 'food/eating' (भोजन) yields a dining-hall, just as 'sleeping' yields a bedroom — building it on a number, a person, or an infinitive gives nonsense.

**25. (A)** An effective scheme evidences cunning, so the fault lies in a corrupt will (दुष्टबुद्धि), not a weak intellect (मूर्ख); treating them as synonyms, or preferring मूर्ख, wrongly converts moral evil into a lapse of cleverness.

**26. (D)** पूज्यते (yak-passive) takes its agent in the instrumental (सर्वैः) and is present-ongoing, while -सु places Panna among the heroines; the options making सर्वैः nominative, the verb past, or वीराङ्गनासु the agent all misread the endings.

**27. (D)** Necessity to a chain of events is not virtue; the text honours moral worth, on which an instrument of murder scores nothing — the readings crediting him with hidden loyalty, the renunciation, or mere causation all confuse plot-function with merit.

**28. (B)** Sleeping is something only a living person does, so ascribing it to राजगृह (a building) is the place-as-person confusion; entering a place, a prince sleeping, and a prince being a target are all correctly matched.

**29. (C)** Facts, not beliefs, fix who died; the assassin's error about the boy's identity leaves the real outcome unchanged — the prince lives, the son does not — so the readings invoking magic belief, a knowing mercy, or a hidden death are all false.

**30. (D)** सर्व- demands all, and त्याग is renunciation of one's everything — here a life — so 'some wealth' shrinks both the scope and the substance of the loss; the claim that nothing is lost or that त्याग means a token both contradict the gloss.

**31. (C)** The aim to kill the heir, the prince's survival, and the son's death in the bed are all confirmed; the trap (iv) attributes the death to Panna herself, which the chapter denies — she lived on to grieve.

**32. (C)** One act can carry a personal loss and a public benefit simultaneously; naming each aspect is complementary, not contradictory, and neither concerns Panna's own death — so the 'contradiction', 'redundant', and 'her own death' readings all fail.

**33. (B)** The endings -एन and -ैः mark instrument, -ए marks locative singular, and -स्य marks possession; only the first option matches each ending to its true case-function, while the others scramble or flatten them.

**34. (C)** धाया is the foster-nurse (not courage — that is धैर्य), सर्वस्व is all one holds (not a small or another's portion), and कुतन्त्रम् is an evil scheme (कु- = bad); only the first triple is wholly correct.

**35. (D)** Concealment could in principle rescue the heir, but it removes the irreplaceable loss that defines सर्वस्वत्याग, downgrading the deed from supreme renunciation to mere stratagem — so 'nothing changes', 'greater sacrifice', and 'impossible' all misread the act's essence.

**36. (A)** The -तुम् infinitive is हन्तुम्, the augmented past अहन्, the -ष्य- future हनिष्यति, and the -तु imperative हन्तु; only the first row assigns each its true sense, the others permute or flatten the forms.

**37. (C)** Reserving the honour-idiom for the worthy and withholding it from the schemer encodes a value-judgement about who merits remembrance; the explanations by royal-only rule, name-length, or a value-neutral idiom all empty the choice of its moral force.

**38. (D)** The text marks Banbir's mind as corrupt and Panna's as loyal wisdom, ranking virtue-guided intelligence above cunning evil; the 'equal praise', reversed-roles, and 'pure accident' readings all flatten this moral hierarchy.

**39. (B)** स्वपुत्रम् is the accusative object actually placed and राजपुत्रस्य the genitive owner of the room, so the faithful reading is 'her own son in the prince's bedroom'; the wrong version swaps who is placed and whose room it is — two case errors, not a tense or instrumental slip.

**40. (C)** Her act dramatises loyalty exacting the dearest possible cost — the principle the story embodies rather than states; reward-for-deception is the rival's failed assumption, and the wage and peaceful-era claims are unrelated to the deed's lesson.

**41. (B)** Bookending with the label and its locative-plural echo makes the narrative an argument for the title-claim, ranking Panna among heroines at the close; the coincidence, many-women, and misprint readings all miss the deliberate frame.

**42. (A)** Without the decoy, the strike falls on the actual prince, ending the line and handing victory to the plot — exactly the catastrophe the swap averted; the alternatives wrongly keep the prince reigning, have the assassin relent, or reward Banbir.

**43. (A)** धाया is the nurse and धैर्य is steadfastness, the whole semantic gap riding on the आ/ऐ vowel contrast; the swapped-meanings and invented object/royalty glosses misidentify both words.

**44. (B)** Success against the decoy IS the misdirection: the blow landing on Panna's son is what kept it off the prince; the readings that success means failure, that the prince also died, or that the boy was spared all break the logic of the swap.

**45. (B)** The text supports that the saved heir continued the lineage but says nothing of specific reign details; the options treating his survival, Panna's existence, or the century as speculation contradict what the chapter actually states.

**46. (D)** Open combat would remove the trickery and the stealth that make him deceitful and cowardly; his deeper moral wickedness would remain, and the chapter never calls him brave, loyal, royal, or foreign in this sense.

**47. (D)** Date, role, survival, the son's death, and the honour are all right; the lone fault is grammatical — an infinitive of purpose is misapplied to the act of the son being killed, which would need a finite passive, not हन्तुम्.

**48. (C)** छलेन is instrumental (deception as the means of coming), while छलम् is the accusative object of अकरोत् (deception as the thing made); the readings that reverse them or deny deception altogether misparse the two endings.

**49. (D)** Circumstance set the dilemma but honour is awarded for the decision within it, signalled by the golden-letters tribute to her; the options crediting luck, denying any choice, or redirecting the praise to the assassin all contradict the text.

**50. (C)** Only the first is right throughout: the correct century, the moral fault दुष्ट (not मूर्ख), the purpose-infinitive हन्तुम्, the true sacrifice (Chandan's life, not Panna's own), the heir's survival, and the honour among heroines in golden letters; each distractor breaks a date, a label, a gloss, or the facts.

**51. (B)** षोडश is the plain count, and the locative ordinal षोडशे agrees with शतके for 'in the sixteenth century'; the options swapping cardinal and ordinal, using षष्ठे ('sixth'), or षष्टिः ('sixty') all confuse the number-words.

**52. (A)** The substitution links the two outcomes causally — Chandan's death in the royal bed is what diverted the blow and kept Udai Singh alive; the illness, same-age, and 'both killed' readings sever or misstate that causal link.

**53. (D)** Only the -तुम् infinitive हन्तुम् supplies the purpose of going; the past अहन्, future हनिष्यति, and imperative हन्तु are finite verbs reporting, predicting, or commanding the act, not stating the aim of the journey.

**54. (A)** त्याग is renunciation in general; सर्वस्व ('all one's own') pins it to the totality of her dearest — her son's life — which a bare त्याग would not convey; the prefix is not decorative, does not mean 'a little', and त्याग does not mean receiving.

**55. (B)** Chandan pays with his life, the ultimate loss; Panna surrenders her dearest yet survives to grieve; Udai Singh is precisely preserved — so the orderings that kill Panna, demote Chandan, or flatten the costs misread the chapter.

**56. (B)** Granting affection, the response shows that love by itself cannot justify sacrificing one's own child, so loyalty to duty is the necessary added motive the chapter foregrounds; the 'no duty', 'identical', and 'coerced' readings each erase the chapter's actual claim.

**57. (A)** A death did occur — Chandan's — so 'prevented a murder' is inaccurate; 'redirected' rightly conveys that the lethal act happened but landed on the substitute, and she used substitution, not combat, to achieve it.

**58. (B)** वीर + अङ्गना licenses 'brave woman' — courage and womanhood — but says nothing of literal warfare or rule; the options crediting kingship, denying her womanhood, or inferring foolishness all read beyond or against the compound.

**59. (D)** The sentence misstates the thing sacrificed (son's life, not her own), wrongly assigns deception to Panna, gives the wrong century, and invents a slaying of Banbir — four distinct faults, so the one-, two-, and zero-error counts all undercount.

**60. (D)** The boy is what is killed, so बालकम् is the object of हन्तुम्, while आगच्छत् ('came') governs no person-object; making it the object of 'came', the subject of 'kill', or an interchangeable nominative all misassign the case relation.
