## Worked Solutions

**1. (D)** The metaphor of drinking rasa daily frames subhashitas as a sustaining, repeated nourishment of the mind. Treating them as a one-time gulp ignores 'नित्यम्' (daily). Tying 'drink' to bodily thirst literalises a deliberately figurative verb. Calling the flavour bitter inverts 'rasa' (nectar/sweet essence).

**2. (B)** The '-आमः' ending marks उत्तमपुरुष बहुवचन (first person plural) present, hence 'we drink', matching the inclusive 'we' of the title. A third-person 'he drinks' would end '-ति'. An imperative 'drink!' would use a command ending, not the simple present. A first-person singular 'I drink' would end '-आमि'.

**3. (A)** Gender governs the plural ending: masculine अ-stems (नरः) take -आः, but neuter अ-stems take -आनि, giving सुभाषितानि. Calling सुभाषितम् feminine is wrong (it is neuter). The ending is not gender-blind. Sayings are perfectly countable — सुभाषितानि is a standard plural.

**4. (A)** सु ('well/good') + भाषित ('spoken') = सुभाषित ('well-spoken saying'), and सुभाषितरस is 'the nectar of such sayings' — a tatpurusha where the first member relates to रस like a genitive. सु never means 'bad' (that would be दुर्). It is not a coordinate pair (the two members are not equals). It is not a form of address.

**5. (C)** पूजितः ('honoured') is a -त past passive participle: नरः is the patient who is honoured BY (instrumental पञ्चभिः वकारैः) the five qualities. Reading it as an active 'he worships' reverses subject and means. It is not a command, nor a future prediction — the form is a completed/stative passive participle.

**6. (A)** सत्य belongs to the purification verse (it cleans the mind), not the honouring list, which has exactly five व-words. There are five, not six, honouring qualities. वस्त्र is genuinely one of the five. विनय is genuine; सत्य is the smuggled-in intruder.

**7. (B)** The drop-pot principle is that small repeated inputs accumulate into something large — which applies symmetrically to good and bad habits. Saying small acts have no effect denies the very gradualism the verse teaches. The verse explicitly rejects 'instant' results ('क्रमशः'). And drops manifestly DO add up — that is the whole image.

**8. (D)** षड् is the combining form of षष् ('six'), so षड्दोषाः names the six faults. सु ('well') begins a different word (सुभाषित). The 'to-be-abandoned' sense lives in हातव्याः, not in षड्. दीर्घ ('long') is the opening of दीर्घसूत्रता, unrelated to षड्.

**9. (D)** Final म् regularly becomes anusvāra before a consonant, giving नित्यं पिबामः. No two vowels met here, so it is not vowel sandhi. There is no visarga (ः) involved. Nor is any consonant doubled — प stays single.

**10. (C)** 'Honoured by knowledge/body/...' makes each quality the instrument of honour, so each appears in the instrumental case. They are not the doers (नरः, nominative, is the one honoured). They are not addressed. And they do not express possession ('of'), which would be genitive.

**11. (C)** The verse assigns गात्राणि–जलेन, मनः–सत्येन, आत्मा–तपसा, बुद्धिः–ज्ञानेन; water cleans only the body. Knowledge cleans the intellect alone, not all four. The mind is paired with truth, not all four with truth. The means are explicitly specified, so 'any choice' is wrong.

**12. (C)** गात्राणि–जलेन and आत्मा–तपसा are already right; only मनः and बुद्धिः have their means crossed, so swapping them (मनः सत्येन, बुद्धिः ज्ञानेन) fixes everything. Swapping the body/soul pair breaks the two correct rows. Body is cleaned by water, not truth. The set as given has two errors, so it is not already correct.

**13. (D)** Water's purifying role in the verse is limited to the body; the intellect's means is knowledge (ज्ञानेन). So water for the intellect is the trap. The verse does say the intellect is purified (बुद्धिः ज्ञानेन शुध्यति), so it does need a means. तपसा cleans the soul, not the intellect.

**14. (D)** With a gerundive ('the faults are to-be-given-up'), the agent who must perform the action stands in the instrumental — 'by one desiring prosperity'. Objects do not 'take the instrumental' as a rule. It is not a vocative address. And it is the person, not the faults, who desires prosperity.

**15. (D)** Each of the six is a separate fault; the apparent pairs are genuinely different shades (sleep vs drowsiness; laziness vs procrastination), so merging wrongly reduces six to four. The total is six, not four. भयम् and क्रोधः are distinct (fear vs anger), not one. And the count is six, not eight.

**16. (B)** आलस्यम् is broad inactivity, while दीर्घसूत्रता (literally 'long-thread-ness') is dragging out particular tasks. निद्रा/तन्द्रा are about sleep, not the laziness/delay split. भयम्/क्रोधः are fear and anger, unrelated to this contrast. The last option reverses the two correct meanings.

**17. (B)** निद्रा is actual sleep; तन्द्रा is the drowsy, half-asleep sluggishness that precedes or mimics it — distinct enough to be counted separately. The reverse mapping is wrong. Neither means anger. And neither means fear or procrastination — those are भयम् and दीर्घसूत्रता.

**18. (B)** Like नित्यं पिबामः, final म् becomes anusvāra before the consonant ह, giving आलस्यं हि. No visarga is present. No two vowels combine here. And ह does not trigger any nasal-doubling rule.

**19. (C)** मनुष्याणाम् is the षष्ठी (genitive) plural, telling whose enemy laziness is — 'the enemy of men'. An instrumental ('by men') would answer 'by whom', not 'of whom'. It is not an object (accusative). And it is plural ('men'), not a dative singular 'for a man'.

**20. (C)** Two nominatives joined by an implied 'is' form a subject–predicate-noun identity: 'laziness is an enemy'. Neither is the object of the other (no accusative). रिपुः is not an instrument (it would be instrumental). And there is no possession here — both share the nominative.

**21. (A)** A verb matches its subject in number: plural गात्राणि → शुध्यन्ति, singular बुद्धिः → शुध्यति. Both verbs are present, not different tenses. Neither is a command (no imperative ending). The means (जलेन/ज्ञानेन) is instrumental and does not control verb number — the subject does.

**22. (C)** Placing the enemy 'in the body' relocates the real obstacle inward — the fight is with one's own laziness. It does not say outside foes are worse. It blames laziness, not the body itself. And naming it an enemy implies it CAN and should be conquered, not that effort is futile.

**23. (B)** Daily small effort is exactly the drop-filling-pot image; postponing until the last night is the fault of दीर्घसूत्रता. A single late marathon is the opposite of 'क्रमशः'. The verse prizes gradual accumulation, not raw total hours. And Riya is diligent (not lazy) while Dev's cramming is no virtue like विद्या.

**24. (B)** One verse supplies 'steady effort', the other 'the right means for each goal'; together they teach effort + appropriate method. 'Any method, just wait' drops the right-means lesson. 'Speed over method' contradicts both gradualism and the means-matching. And both verses assume effort changes outcomes, refuting fatalism.

**25. (C)** Extending the drop image to knowledge, virtue and wealth stresses that they accrue gradually, not in one stroke. Slow growth does not mean low value — the verse praises them. The trio is virtues/goods, not the faults. And they are abstract goods, not the literal pot and drops of the base image.

**26. (A)** One metaphor urges absorbing the good, the other urges defeating the bad — together a call to active, sustained engagement on both fronts. Leaving both alone ignores both verbs ('drink', 'conquer'). Welcoming the enemy and shunning the nectar inverts both. And wisdom (helpful) and laziness (harmful) are opposites, not the same force.

**27. (C)** The compound stacks जल-बिन्दु ('water-drop') with निपात ('falling') and the instrumental ending gives 'by the falling of water-drops' — the means filling the pot. It is one fused means, not three separate ones. The instrumental marks means, not purpose ('for the sake of'). And it is not a locative 'in the place'.

**28. (A)** Passive पूर्यते makes the pot the patient that gets filled, while the instrumental drops are the means. It is not the pot doing the filling (that would be active). The relation is not reversed — drops fill the pot, not vice versa. And it is a statement of filling, not a question.

**29. (B)** The chapter teaches that company shapes character; bad company here grows the procrastination fault. The company is harmful, not good, and the outcome is a fault, not knowledge. It contradicts the claim that company has no effect. And copying friends' delay is not humility (विनय).

**30. (B)** Good company is the beneficial influence to keep near, contrasting the inner enemy to be fought. Procrastination is itself a fault to shed, not a friend. Sleep (निद्रा) is one of the six faults, not something to indulge. Anger (क्रोधः) is likewise a fault, not an ally.

**31. (D)** The motivating coach matches प्रेरकः, while the laziness conquered within matches the inner रिपुः. Calling the helpful coach an enemy and the harmful laziness an inspirer reverses both. The laziness is not an inspirer. And the supportive coach is not an enemy, so they are not both रिपवः.

**32. (D)** The agent-suffix makes प्रेरकः the doer — 'one who impels', i.e. an inspirer. It does not name the abstract act of inspiration. Nor the passive recipient who is pushed. Nor a location — the word denotes an agent, not a place.

**33. (C)** दीर्घसूत्रता is the fault of stretching tasks out (procrastination); दीर्घायुष् is the blessing of a long life. The reverse mapping is wrong. Only the first is literally 'long-thread-ness'; दीर्घायुष् concerns lifespan. And the two are NOT interchangeable — one is a fault, the other a blessing.

**34. (A)** Because दीर्घ produces both 'procrastination' and 'long life', the meaning hinges on the second member — read the whole word. The first member alone clearly does not fix it (else they would match). Sharing दीर्घ did NOT make them mean the same. And here the second member is precisely what distinguishes them.

**35. (A)** दीर्घायुष् denotes a long life, a desirable thing, so it is not among the faults to shed. The sixth fault is दीर्घसूत्रता, not दीर्घायुष्. It is not a synonym for laziness. And it has nothing to do with the घटः of the drop verse.

**36. (D)** Keeping the instrumental 'by' but changing 'five' to 'six' gives षड्भिः (instrumental of षष्). It cannot stay पञ्चभिः, which means 'by five'. षष्ठीभिः is not the needed instrumental ('by'). षट्सु is locative ('in six'), not 'by six'.

**37. (C)** Both are instrumental singular; the endings differ only because the stems belong to different declensions, not because the case differs. They are the SAME case, not two cases. Both are singular — the ending difference is class, not number. And विद्यया is a noun, not a verb.

**38. (B)** 'By humility' is the instrumental विनयेन, matching विद्यया/वस्त्रेण. The nominative विनयः would make humility the subject, not the means. विनयाय is dative ('for'), not 'by'. विनयस्य is genitive ('of'), not the means.

**39. (B)** The verse pairs the soul with austerity in the instrumental: आत्मा तपसा शुध्यति. Water cleans the body, not the soul. Knowledge cleans the intellect, not the soul. And तपः is nominative ('austerity is purified'), reversing the intended means relation.

**40. (D)** An instrumental question ('by what?') is answered by an instrumental means-word like जलेन/ज्ञानेन. A nominative subject answers 'who/what acts?', not 'by what?'. A genitive answers 'of whom?'. A vocative is a form of address, not an answer to 'by what?'.

**41. (D)** 'By how many' (instrumental) is answered by an instrumental plural like पञ्चभिः. 'By what' expects an instrumental means (e.g. ज्ञानेन), not a nominative. 'Of whom' expects a genitive (मनुष्याणाम्), not an instrumental. 'For whom' expects a dative, not a genitive.

**42. (D)** नरः is the subject that is honoured, so it takes the nominative agreeing with पूजितः. The means is पञ्चभिः वकारैः (instrumental), not नरः. It is not a dative recipient. And it is not a vocative address (which would mark a call, not a subject).

**43. (A)** Dress is genuinely one honouring quality, but the verse names five together, so honour on dress alone is incomplete. It is not the ONLY quality — there are five. वस्त्र IS among the five, so honouring it is not entirely wrong. And dress is a virtue, not one of the six faults.

**44. (A)** Refined speech is a virtue while anger is a fault; harsh angry speech sacrifices the virtue to the fault. Speech is not itself a fault — it is one of the five va-qualities. Anger is condemned, never a virtue. And वाणी (speech) and क्रोधः (anger) are entirely different words and meanings.

**45. (C)** Respect comes from the five qualities and prosperity from shedding the six faults — a both/and program. Wealth-plus-sleep ignores the qualities and indulges a fault. The verses demand both cultivation and shedding, not a choice between them. And anger/procrastination are faults to shed, not a recipe to follow.

**46. (B)** Filling the pot is the gradual-gain verse; purity ruined by a lie invokes the purification verse, where the mind is cleaned by truth. The va-qualities and faults verses are not about gradual gain or truth-cleaning. The title and inner-enemy verses do not cover drop-by-drop filling. And दीर्घायुष् (long life) is unrelated to either idea here.

**47. (B)** The verses mix cases: नरः and दोषाः are nominative while the means/agent (वकारैः, इच्छता) are instrumental, so the cases are not uniform. They are not all instrumental, nor all nominative. And the faults verse plainly contains nouns (दोषाः, भूतिम्), so 'no nouns' is wrong.

**48. (A)** The four means are water, truth, austerity and knowledge (ज्ञान); विद्या is a honouring quality, not a listed purification means. Water, truth and austerity are all genuine means. (The intellect's means is ज्ञानेन, not विद्या.)

**49. (C)** वाणी (speech) is a honouring va-quality, so it is the intruder among faults. Drowsiness, fear and procrastination are all genuine members of the six faults.

**50. (C)** The honouring list uses विद्या; the purification verse uses ज्ञान for the intellect, so the overlap the friend claims does not exist. विद्या is not literally repeated in the purification verse. विद्या does not clean the body (water does). And विद्या does appear — as a honouring quality — so 'neither verse' is wrong.

**51. (A)** क्रमशः denotes a steady, drop-by-drop progression from start to finish, not a sudden surge at the end. It does not mark a sudden moment. It is the opposite of 'all at once'. And it means gradual forward filling, not emptying or going backwards.

**52. (D)** The drop-pot principle says small repeated inputs build to a large whole — so daily small lies grow into a big bad habit. Claiming they never accumulate denies the verse's core. The image is explicitly extended beyond water to abstract growth. And drops add up rather than cancel out.

**53. (B)** All three claims are wrong: five qualities not six, mind cleaned by truth not water, and दीर्घसूत्रता is procrastination not long life. So it is not just one error. 'Mind by water' IS an error (the body, not the mind, is cleaned by water), so it is not two. And it is certainly not error-free.

**54. (A)** Five qualities, six faults, intellect by knowledge, body by water — all correct. The first wrong option flips both counts and the intellect's means. The second swaps the body/intellect means. The third swaps the mind's and soul's means (mind needs truth, soul needs austerity).

**55. (B)** 'नित्यम्' (daily) + 'पिबामः' (drink in) urges a daily habit of absorbing wisdom, dovetailing with the drop-pot's gradual accumulation. 'Once in a lifetime' contradicts 'daily'. The 'drink' is figurative absorption of wisdom, not literal water. And last-minute memorising is the very procrastination the chapter condemns.

**56. (D)** Qualities earn respect, but the second verse ties prosperity to SHEDDING the faults, so unshed faults still threaten prosperity. Qualities do not automatically cancel faults. Nor do two faults erase genuine qualities entirely. And the chapter treats faults as decidedly relevant, not ignorable.

**57. (A)** वपुस् prizes a sound body, but निद्रा and तन्द्रा are faults — virtue lies in mastering the body, not surrendering to sleep. The body is not itself a fault (वपुस् is rightly a quality). Sleep is listed among faults, not virtues. And वपुस् (body) and निद्रा (sleep) are distinct.

**58. (A)** The chapter joins cultivating qualities, shedding faults, gradual effort, and right-means-for-each-goal into one philosophy. It explicitly rejects instant results and one-size-fits-all methods. It locates the chief obstacle (laziness) WITHIN, not outside. And it condemns sleep, comfort and delay while affirming that effort matters.

**59. (C)** A gerundive expresses what OUGHT to be done, so हातव्याः = 'must be given up'. It is not a finished past act ('were given up'). It is not an action in progress ('are being given up'). And it carries obligation to abandon, not future praise.

**60. (D)** सुभाषितरसम् is what is drunk — the object of पिबामः — so it takes the accusative. The drinkers ('we') are the subject carried in the verb, not this word. It is not a means (which would be instrumental). And it is not a form of address (vocative).
