Instructions: Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. Questions test the subhashitas on speech, effort and action. Watch the classic traps: च means 'AND' (both useful AND pleasing), not 'or'; दुर्लभम् means RARE / hard-to-find, not impossible (अशक्यम्); knowledge is gathered क्षणशः (moment by moment) and wealth कणशः (particle by particle), one adverb per noun; क्रियावान् (a doer) is preferred over विकत्थन (a boaster); and सूक्तिः (a good saying) is not सूक्ष्म (subtle). Sanskrit is quoted in Devanagari. Do not write on this paper — mark answers on your answer sheet.

Worked Solutions

1. (D) The verse means rare is speech that is both useful (हितम्) and pleasing (मनोहारि). The other readings distort it.

2. (B) वचः means 'speech / words'. Action is क्रिया, wealth अर्थ, and curiosity कुतूहलम्.

3. (C) हितम् means 'beneficial / useful'. Pleasing is मनोहारि, rare दुर्लभम्, and boastful विकत्थन.

4. (A) मनोहारि means 'pleasing' (heart-stealing). Useful is हितम्, rare दुर्लभम्, and harmful अहितम्.

5. (D) Such speech is दुर्लभम् (rare). It is not common, impossible, or useless.

6. (A) च means 'and', demanding both qualities together. It is not 'or', 'not', or 'why'.

7. (C) Reading च as 'or' would lower the demanding 'both' standard to an easy 'either' one. It does make a difference and does not make the verse harder.

8. (C) का is interrogative, वा is 'or', and च is 'and'. The other orderings are wrong.

9. (A) च requires the speech to be both beneficial and pleasing at once. Not either-or, only useful, or only pleasing.

10. (C) Pleasing-but-harmful or useful-but-harsh speech is NOT the rare both-and-both ideal. It is not the ideal, impossible, or boasting.

11. (D) दुर्लभम् means 'rare / hard to find'. It does not mean impossible, common, or easy.

12. (B) It is achievable but hard (दुर्लभम्). It is not impossible, common, or not worth attempting.

13. (B) अशक्यम् means 'impossible', while दुर्लभम् means 'rare but possible'. It is not easy or pleasing.

14. (C) The ideal is hard but not impossible. It is not truly impossible, pointless, or already common.

15. (B) दुर्लभम् (rare but attainable) tells a student to keep striving - not give up, relax, or ignore it.

16. (B) हितम् speech does good and helps the listener. It is not mere flattery, always harsh, or impossible.

17. (B) मनोहारि speech is agreeable and wins the heart. It is not harsh, false, or unintelligible.

18. (C) The hardest combines being helpful AND pleasant at once. Not harsh-and-false, long-and-loud, or silent.

19. (A) A kind word that also advises well is both हितम् (useful) and मनोहारि (pleasing). Not neither, boastful, or impossible.

20. (D) क्षणशः means 'moment by moment'. कणशः is 'particle by particle'; it is not 'all at once' or 'never'.

21. (D) कणशः means 'particle by particle'. क्षणशः is 'moment by moment'; it is not 'all at once' or 'backwards'.

22. (B) The -शस् suffix makes 'by units of' adverbs (one by one). Not 'all at once', 'never', or 'to/for'.

23. (C) They are two adverbs, one per noun (knowledge, wealth). They are not one word, two verbs, or datives.

24. (D) Knowledge is gathered क्षणशः (moment by moment). Wealth is कणशः; it is not gathered all at once or never.

25. (B) Wealth is gathered कणशः (particle by particle). Knowledge is क्षणशः; not all in one day or by boasting.

26. (C) Both grow gradually, little by little. Not instantly, by luck, or without effort.

27. (A) क्रियावान् is one of action (a doer). A boaster is विकत्थन, a curious person कुतूहली, and a treasure भाण्डागारः.

28. (C) क्रिया ('action') + वान् ('possessing') = action-possessing (a doer). The other splits are wrong.

29. (B) विकत्थन means boasting / a boaster. A doer is क्रियावान्, curiosity कुतूहलम्, and a good saying सूक्तिः.

30. (C) It contrasts the doer (क्रियावान्) with the boaster (विकत्थन). Not with a saying, curiosity, or a treasure.

31. (D) The chapter prefers the doer (क्रियावान्). Not the boaster, neither, or both equally.

32. (B) It reflects a preference for deeds (कर्म) over mere speech (कथन). Not speech over deeds, boasting, or pure silence.

33. (A) Quietly doing the work makes one a क्रियावान् (one of action). Not a boaster, a family-stain, or a fox.

34. (A) सूक्तिः is a good / well-said saying. A subtle thing is सूक्ष्म, a boast विकत्थन, and a treasure भाण्डागारः.

35. (B) सु ('well') + उक्ति ('saying') = a well-spoken saying. The other splits are wrong.

36. (A) सूक्तिः is a synonym of सुभाषितम् (a wise saying). It is not boasting, treasure, or a fox.

37. (D) सूक्ष्म means 'subtle / fine / minute'. A good saying is सूक्तिः, a boast विकत्थन, and curiosity कुतूहलम्.

38. (B) They come from different roots (उक्ति 'saying' vs सूक्ष्म 'subtle'). They are not from the same root, meaning, or verb.

39. (C) कुतूहलम् means 'curiosity / eagerness to know'. Boasting is विकत्थन, character शीलम्, and wealth अर्थ.

40. (C) कुतूहलम् = कौतुकम् = 'curiosity / eagerness'. It is not boredom, anger, or laziness.

41. (A) कुतूहलम् is close to जिज्ञासा (the wish to know). वन्दे, आम्लम् and शीलम् are unrelated to curiosity.

42. (B) कुतूहलम् (curiosity) is held up as a learner's virtue. Not a boaster's, a lazy person's, or a king's.

43. (C) भाण्डागारः is a treasure-trove / storehouse. A boast is विकत्थन, curiosity कुतूहलम्, and a good saying सूक्तिः.

44. (A) Calling them a भाण्डागारः suggests a precious store to be treasured. Not worthless, a burden, or ungatherable.

45. (D) A library of wise books is a भाण्डागारः of knowledge. Not of boasting, laziness, or sourness.

46. (D) शीलम् means 'character / moral conduct'. Wealth is अर्थ, speech वचः, and curiosity कुतूहलम्.

47. (A) What truly counts is शीलम् (character). Not boasting, wealth alone, or loud speech.

48. (B) Repeating शीलम् shows a shared value: good character above outward things. Not wealth, boasting, or pure silence.

49. (A) Such a person still lacks शीलम् (character), even with fine speech and effort.

50. (D) क्रियावान् = one of action. विकत्थन is a boaster (not a doer), सूक्तिः a good saying (not boasting), and दुर्लभम् rare (not impossible).

51. (C) क्षणशः = moment by moment and कणशः = particle by particle. The other options misdefine them.

52. (C) हितम् = useful and मनोहारि = pleasing. The reversal and the other options are wrong.

53. (D) सूक्तिः = a good saying and सूक्ष्म = subtle. The other pairings reverse the meanings.

54. (A) The theme is wise speech, steady effort, action over boasting, and good character. Not the fox fable, banking, or Sun-names.

55. (A) Together they teach that good things are rare/hard but achievable through steady, patient effort. Not easy, impossible, or boast-gained.

56. (D) It teaches us to act rather than merely boast. Not to boast, stay silent always, or gather wealth dishonestly.

57. (D) Curiosity (कुतूहलम्) drives the patient gathering of knowledge. It does not encourage boasting, discourage effort, or replace character.

58. (A) It means speech both useful and pleasing is rare but achievable. The other readings drop the 'both' or the 'achievable'.

59. (B) The vocabulary values good sayings, curiosity, treasured knowledge and character. Not boasting, wealth-and-pride, or inaction.

60. (D) च means 'and' (both qualities), and दुर्लभम् means rare/hard, not impossible. The other options keep one or both errors.
