1. In triangles ABC and PQR, AB = PQ = 7 cm, AC = PR = 7 cm and BC = QR = 7 cm. A student writes "congruent by SAS, since the equal sides force a 60° included angle." Choose the most precise correct verdict.
(A) Congruent by SAS, because each triangle is equilateral so the 60° included angle is automatically equal.(B) Only similar, because three equal sides fix the shape but the size could still differ.(C) Congruence cannot be claimed, since at least one angle measurement is required by every criterion.(D) They are congruent, but the criterion is SSS — no angle was given, even though all angles do happen to be 60°.

(A) 35°(B) 60°(C) 85°(D) 120°

(A) 60°(B) 30°(C) 75°(D) 45°
4. A figure has rotational symmetry of order 4 about its centre but you are asked the smallest positive angle that maps it onto itself, AND how many lines of symmetry this alone guarantees. The correct pair is:
(A) 90°, and exactly 4 lines of symmetry are guaranteed.(B) 45°, and 4 lines of symmetry are guaranteed.(C) 90°, and 0 lines of symmetry are guaranteed by order-4 rotation alone.(D) 120°, and 2 lines of symmetry are guaranteed.
5. Triangles ABC and DEF satisfy AB = DE, ∠A = ∠D and ∠C = ∠F. A student claims congruence is impossible because the equal angle is not 'between' the side and another angle. The correct verdict is:
(A) Not congruent, because two angles and a side never determine a triangle.(B) Only similar, since two equal angles fix the shape but leave the size free.(C) Congruent by SAS, treating the two equal angles as the two equal sides.(D) Congruent (AAS): with ∠A=∠D and ∠C=∠F the third angles match, so ∠B=∠E, and AB lies between A and B, giving ASA.

7. Triangle ABC ≅ triangle ACB for the SAME triangle (note B and C swapped). Combined with the fact that this self-correspondence maps the triangle onto itself, the strongest forced conclusion is:
(A) AB = BC only, with no angle equality forced.(B) ∠A = 90°, making it a right triangle.(C) AB = AC and ∠B = ∠C, so the triangle is isosceles with apex A.(D) The triangle must be equilateral.
8. Two triangles have sides {5, 12, 13} cm and {5, 12, 13} cm respectively. Which statement is the most complete true description?
(A) They are similar but not congruent, because no angle was stated.(B) They are congruent by SSS and both are right-angled, since 5² + 12² = 13².(C) They are congruent by RHS, using the 13 cm hypotenuse and the 5 cm leg.(D) They cannot be compared without knowing which angle is the right angle.

(A) 12(B) 11(C) 6(D) 1
10. In triangle ABC, AD is drawn so that D is on BC, BD = DC, and ∠ADB = ∠ADC. Which conclusion is forced, and by which congruence?
(A) ∠BAC = 90°, by RHS on the two halves.(B) Nothing is forced, because two angles at D being equal is automatic for any point on BC.(C) AB = AC: triangles ABD and ACD are congruent by SAS (BD = DC, the equal adjacent angles at D, AD common), so the triangle is isosceles.(D) The triangle is equilateral, by SSS.

(A) 80°(B) 20°(C) 40°(D) 70°
12. Two triangles ABC and DEF have AB = DE = 8 cm, BC = EF = 6 cm and ∠C = ∠F = 50°. The safest correct verdict about congruence is:
(A) Congruent by SAS, since two sides and an angle are equal.(B) Not guaranteed — the equal angle (at C, F) is opposite AB/DE, not included between the two equal sides, so this is the ambiguous SSA case.(C) Congruent by ASA, treating ∠C and the side BC together.(D) Congruent by RHS, because 50° behaves like a fixed right angle.

Worked Solutions

1. (D) All three corresponding sides are equal, which is exactly the SSS criterion; SSS needs no angle. Naming it SAS misstates which given facts were used — no angle was supplied as data, so the criterion invoked must be SSS. The triangles ARE congruent (so 'only similar' and 'cannot claim' are wrong), but the justification is three equal sides, not an included angle.

2. (B) From the order ABC ↔ QRP, vertex C corresponds to P, so ∠P = ∠C. In triangle ABC, ∠C = 180° − 35° − 85° = 60°. Reading P as matching A (35°) or B (85°) ignores the third-letter correspondence.

3. (A) Angle sum: 2x + (x+30) + (x+30) = 180, so 4x + 60 = 180, giving 4x = 120, x = 30. The apex ∠A = 2x = 60°. Reporting x itself (30) as the apex, or reading the base angle x+30 = 60 wrongly, gives the distractors.

4. (C) Smallest rotation = 360°/4 = 90°. Rotational symmetry of order 4 does not by itself force any reflection axis (e.g. a 'pinwheel' shape turns onto itself every 90° yet has no mirror line), so 0 lines are guaranteed. Choosing 45° halves the correct step; assuming 4 mirror lines confuses rotational order with reflection count.

5. (D) Two pairs of equal angles force the third pair equal (angle sum), so ∠B = ∠E. Now AB is the side between the equal angles A and B in one triangle and DE between D and E in the other — that is ASA (the AAS data reduces to ASA). Two-angles-plus-a-side DOES fix a triangle, so 'not congruent' and 'only similar' are false.

6. (A) The altitude from the apex of an isosceles triangle bisects the base, so BM = MC = 7 cm. In right triangle ABM, AM = √(25² − 7²) = √(625 − 49) = √576 = 24 cm. Forgetting to halve the base (using 14) or mis-subtracting gives the distractors.

7. (C) The correspondence A↔A, B↔C, C↔B equates AB with AC and ∠B with ∠C (CPCT). That makes the triangle isosceles with apex A. It need not be equilateral (the third side BC is free) and ∠A is unconstrained, so the right-angle and equilateral options overreach.

8. (B) Equal triples of sides give SSS congruence directly. Separately, 25 + 144 = 169 = 13², so each triangle is right-angled — that is an extra true fact, not a missing requirement. RHS is the wrong NAME of the criterion here (we used three sides, not the right-angle/hypotenuse/leg package), and equal sides give congruence, not mere similarity.

9. (B) A regular 12-gon maps onto itself at every multiple of 30° (= 360°/12). The positive angles below 360° are 30°, 60°, …, 330° — that is 11 distinct rotations. The 360° (= 0°) return is the identity and is not a positive angle below 360°, so 12 overcounts.

10. (C) Since ∠ADB and ∠ADC are equal and sit on the straight line BC, each is 90°, and BD = DC with AD common. Triangles ABD and ACD then match by SAS (two sides and the included right angle), so AB = AC by CPCT — isosceles. The angles at D are NOT automatically equal for a general D, and nothing forces ∠A = 90° or all sides equal.

11. (C) Base angles: ∠Q = ∠R = (180° − 20°)/2 = 80°. The bisector of ∠Q splits it into two 40° parts, so ∠SQR = 40°. Forgetting to bisect leaves 80°; copying ∠P gives 20°; (180−20)/... slips give 70°.

12. (B) The equal sides are AB and BC; the equal angle is at C, which lies between BC and CA — but CA is NOT one of the matched sides, and AB is opposite C. So the matched pair is two sides and a non-included angle: SSA, which does not guarantee congruence. SAS would require the angle between the two equal sides (at B).

13. (A) Composing a reflection in a horizontal axis with a reflection in a perpendicular vertical axis through the same point is exactly a 180° rotation about that point. So order-2 (180°) rotational symmetry is forced. It need not reach order 4 (a non-square rectangle has both mirror lines but only order 2). Order 2 corresponds to 180°, not 90°.

14. (A) DE = DF means the base angles ∠E and ∠F are equal; let each be y. Apex ∠D = y + 30. Sum: (y+30) + y + y = 180, so 3y = 150, y = 50. Thus ∠F = 50°. Using ∠D = 80° as the answer mistakes the apex for a base angle.

15. (C) Corresponding sides: AB = DE = 13, BC = EF = 11, so CA = 36 − 13 − 11 = 12 cm. CA corresponds to FD, but its length comes from the perimeter, not from any angle, so 'cannot be found' is wrong; 13 and 11 are the other two sides.

16. (C) E is symmetric across a horizontal line through its middle bar, but not across a vertical line, and turning it 180° gives a backwards E — no order-2 rotation. H has both a horizontal and a vertical axis (and order-2 rotation). X has both axes and order-2/4 rotation. S has order-2 rotational symmetry but no mirror line.

17. (A) Compute the first hypotenuse: √(49 + 576) = √625 = 25 cm. Both now have hypotenuse 25 cm, a leg 7 cm and a right angle — exactly RHS, which uniquely fixes the third leg at 24 cm. The 'other leg unknown' worry is resolved by RHS; this is RHS, not the unreliable SSA, and equal sides give congruence not mere similarity.

18. (C) Parts sum to 12, so each part = 180°/12 = 15°. Angles are 45°, 60°, 75°. All differ (scalene) and all are below 90° (acute). The largest is 75°, not 90° or above, so it is neither right-angled nor obtuse; no two are equal, so not isosceles.

19. (D) Base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal: each = (180° − ∠A)/2 = (180° − 140°)/2 = 40°/2 = 20°. Forgetting to halve the remaining 40° gives 40°; mistakenly using (180−40)/2 = 70° treats 140 as an exterior angle it is not.

20. (B) BC and EF are the hypotenuses (opposite the right angle), and AB, DE are legs. So we have a right angle, an equal hypotenuse and an equal leg — precisely RHS. SAS would need the right angle BETWEEN the two given sides, but the hypotenuse is opposite the right angle, so SAS does not apply directly; RHS removes the SSA ambiguity.

21. (D) A regular n-gon has n lines of symmetry and rotational order n. Pentagon: 5 and 5; hexagon: 6 and 6. Differences are 6 − 5 = 1 for lines and 6 − 5 = 1 for rotational order.

22. (A) Perpendicular bisector of BC through A means A is equidistant from B and C: AB = AC. Perpendicular bisector of AB through C means C is equidistant from A and B: CA = CB. Combining: AB = AC and CA = CB give AB = BC = CA — equilateral. One condition alone gives only isosceles; both together force all three equal.

23. (B) The order ABC ↔ PQR pairs A↔P, B↔Q, C↔R. Hence ∠B = ∠Q and side CA ↔ RP, so CA = RP. The others mismatch the correspondence (e.g. ∠B with ∠R, or AB with QR).

24. (D) Let each base angle be b; apex = b − 30. Sum: (b−30) + b + b = 180, so 3b = 210, b = 70, apex = 70 − 30 = 40°. Reporting the base angle 70° instead of the apex is the trap.

25. (A) An isosceles non-equilateral triangle has exactly one mirror line (through the apex) and no rotational symmetry beyond 360° (order 1). A non-square rectangle has TWO mirror lines and order-2 rotation. A general parallelogram has order-2 rotation but no mirror line. A regular pentagon has 5 mirror lines and order 5.

26. (B) AB and BC are the two sides meeting at vertex B, and ∠B is the angle between them — this is the included angle. Equal AB, equal ∠B, equal BC matched the same way is SAS, which guarantees congruence. Because the angle IS included, it is not the ambiguous SSA case.

27. (D) The altitude from the apex bisects BC, so BM = √(17² − 8²) = √(289 − 64) = √225 = 15 cm. Then BC = 2 × 15 = 30 cm. Forgetting to double the half-base gives 15; mis-adding gives the others.

28. (C) Reflection is a rigid motion: it preserves all lengths and angles, so area and perimeter are unchanged and the image is congruent (not merely similar). What it does change is orientation — the figure is flipped, reversing left/right handedness. Reflection makes no extra copy and does not enlarge.

29. (B) In DEF, ∠D + ∠E = 90° (since ∠F = 90°), and ∠D = 2∠E gives 3∠E = 90°, ∠E = 30°, ∠D = 60°. Correspondence ABC↔DEF pairs B with E, so ∠B = ∠E = 30°. Taking ∠D = 60° (which corresponds to ∠A) is the misread.

30. (D) A square coincides with itself after 90°, 180°, 270° and 360° rotations — all four. Its rotational order is 4 (counting 360° as the return), and every multiple of 90° works.

31. (A) Base angles: ∠B = ∠C = (180° − 100°)/2 = 40°. The bisector of ∠B makes ∠DBC = 20°. In triangle BDC, ∠BDC = 180° − ∠DBC − ∠C = 180° − 20° − 40° = 120°. Forgetting the bisector (using ∠B = 40°) gives 180 − 40 − 40 = 100°.

32. (B) (i) is false: an isosceles triangle has a mirror line but only order-1 rotation. (ii) is false: a parallelogram (or the letter S) has order-2 rotation but no mirror line. Both implications fail.

33. (C) BC is opposite ∠A (and EF opposite ∠D); the side lies opposite one of the equal angles, not between the two equal angles A and B. That pattern is AAS, not ASA. AAS still guarantees congruence (the third angle is forced, reducing to ASA), so it is congruent — just named correctly as AAS.

34. (B) If 60° maps it onto itself, so do 120°, 180°, 240°, 300° and 360° — six positions, order 6 at least. The order is 360°/60° = 6, not 3 (which would correspond to 120°) and certainly not 60.

35. (B) With D the midpoint we have BD = DC, plus AB = AC and AD common — three equal sides, so SSS. Then ∠ADB = ∠ADC by CPCT; being supplementary on the straight line BC, each is 90°. RHS would assume the right angle we are trying to prove, and ∠BAD = ∠CAD is not yet established for SAS.

36. (C) Equal corresponding angles give similarity; a 1:1 ratio of one pair of corresponding sides means that pair is equal, which fixes the scale at 1, so all sides are equal — congruent. The named criterion is ASA/AAS, NOT AAA (AAA alone is only similarity).

37. (D) AC is the axis of symmetry of the kite, so reflection across AC fixes A and C and swaps B with D. The segment BD therefore maps onto itself with its endpoints exchanged; since reflection across AC swaps B and D, BD must meet AC at right angles and be bisected by it. It does not become AC or a side.

38. (C) ∠A = ∠B forces CA = CB (sides opposite equal angles), so the triangle is isosceles with apex C. Its axis of symmetry passes through the apex C and the midpoint of the base AB, which is the perpendicular bisector of AB. The line AB or the bisector of BC are not axes unless the triangle is equilateral.

39. (D) Parts sum to 6, so the unit is 180°/6 = 30°. Angles are 30°, 60°, 90° — a right angle is present, all three differ (scalene). It is not isosceles (no two equal) and not obtuse (largest is exactly 90°).

40. (A) ∠B corresponds to ∠E, so ∠B = x + 30. With ∠A = 3x − 10 and ∠C = 80°, angle sum in ABC: (3x − 10) + (x + 30) + 80 = 180, so 4x + 100 = 180, 4x = 80, x = 20. Mis-dropping the constant 100 or using ∠C = 70 yields the distractors.

41. (A) A regular n-gon has smallest rotation 360°/n and order n. For n = 8: 360°/8 = 45° and order 8. Pairing 45° with order 4, or halving to 22.5°, mismatches the regular-polygon rule.

42. (B) With AB = AC, ∠BAD = ∠CAD (angle bisector) and AD common, triangles ABD and ACD are congruent by SAS. Then BD = DC (so AD bisects BC) and ∠ADB = ∠ADC (CPCT); being supplementary, each is 90°, so AD ⟂ BC. Both properties hold together — the apex bisector is the perpendicular bisector of the base.

43. (B) Reflection preserves all side lengths and angles, so every corresponding part is equal — that is the definition of congruence, which allows flips (orientation reversal). Handedness changes under reflection but does not affect congruence, and this holds for any triangle, scalene or not.

44. (D) With the right angle at A and equal legs AB = AC = 6, BC is the hypotenuse: BC = √(6² + 6²) = √72 = 6√2 cm. Adding legs gives 12 (wrong); 6 ignores the hypotenuse relation.

45. (B) AB and AC meet at A, so ∠A is the included angle between the two given equal sides — this is SAS, which guarantees congruence. ASA needs two angles; only one angle (∠A) is given here, so the ASA label is incorrect even though congruence does hold.

46. (D) Order 6 means the smallest rotation is 360°/6 = 60°, and every multiple of 60° below 360° works: 60, 120, 180, 240, 300 — five angles. 360° is the identity (not below 360°), and 30° is not a symmetry of an order-6 figure.

47. (A) If the median AD (so BD = DC) is also the angle bisector (∠BAD = ∠CAD), the configuration forces AB = AC (the only triangle in which the median from a vertex coincides with the angle bisector is isosceles with apex there). It need not be equilateral or right-angled, and it is certainly not scalene.

48. (A) The parts 4 + 5 + 6 = 15, so the unit is 45/15 = 3 cm. EF = 5 units = 15 cm. Since BC corresponds to EF (B↔E, C↔F), BC = 15 cm. DE = 12 and FD = 18 are the other two sides.

49. (C) Block 'I' looks the same after a 180° turn (order 2) and has both a vertical and a horizontal mirror line. 'S' and 'N' have order-2 rotation but NO mirror line. 'P' has neither rotational symmetry of order 2 nor a mirror line.

50. (D) The apex altitude bisects BC, so the half-base is 4 cm; altitude = √(5² − 4²) = √9 = 3 cm. Area = ½ × base × height = ½ × 8 × 3 = 12 cm². Mixing up which is leg vs half-base gives 4; doubling the area mis-applies the ½.

51. (C) A 40° step gives order 360°/40 = 9. Since the construction explicitly has no reflection, there are 0 lines of symmetry — rotational order does not create mirror lines. 'Order 40' confuses the angle with the order.

52. (D) Triangles ABC and ABD are congruent by SSS (AC=AD, BC=BD, AB common). The whole figure ACBD is symmetric across line AB (it is a kite with axis AB), so reflecting across AB swaps C and D. Hence AB is the perpendicular bisector of CD — it bisects CD at right angles.

53. (C) DE = DF means base angles ∠E = ∠F. Let ∠D = d; then ∠E = ∠F = 2d. Sum: d + 2d + 2d = 180, 5d = 180, d = 36°. So ∠D = 36°; ∠E = 72° is the base angle, not the apex.

54. (B) A 120° smallest rotation gives order 360°/120 = 3, and three mirror lines match a regular 3-gon — the equilateral triangle. A regular hexagon has order 6 and six mirror lines; a square has order 4 and four lines. The equilateral triangle's order is 3, not 6.

55. (C) The correspondence A↔B, B↔C, C↔A equates AB=BC=CA (CPCT on sides) and ∠A=∠B=∠C (on angles). All three sides equal makes it equilateral. A general triangle does NOT map to itself under a cyclic relabel, so 'nothing' is wrong.

56. (A) Congruent to the 6-8-10 triangle means identical sides 6, 8, 10. Since 6² + 8² = 36 + 64 = 100 = 10², it is right-angled, so its largest angle (opposite the 10 cm side) is 90°. Congruence fixes every part, so it can be determined and is not merely acute.

57. (D) A non-square rhombus has its two diagonals as mirror lines (2 lines) and order-2 rotation; a non-square rectangle has the two perpendicular bisectors of its sides as mirror lines (2 lines) and order-2 rotation. Both have exactly 2 lines and order 2 — only the square reaches 4 and order 4.

58. (A) AD bisects the apex angle of an isosceles triangle (AB = AC), so it is also the altitude to the base: triangles ABD and ACD are congruent (SAS), making ∠ADB = ∠ADC, and being supplementary each is 90°. The base angle 50° is a distractor irrelevant to the right angle at the foot.

59. (A) A rotation is a rigid motion, so all lengths and angles, and hence area and perimeter, are preserved, giving congruence. Unlike reflection, rotation preserves orientation (no flip). Rotation never stretches or resizes, so 'different perimeter' or 'only similar' are wrong.

60. (D) Under reflection, the mirror line is the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining a point and its image. So m crosses PP' at its midpoint F at a right angle, giving PF = FP' = 10/2 = 5 cm. m cannot be parallel to PP' (it must cross it), and the two halves are always equal, never 7 and 3.
