## Worked Solutions

**1. (C)** Add each vowel's value: short अ is 1, short अ is 1, long आ is 2, short इ is 1, giving 1+1+2+1 = 5. Counting one per syllable would wrongly treat the long आ as a single unit; there is no sixth vowel to make 6, and four ignores one of the short vowels.

**2. (D)** क has short अ followed by only a single onset consonant of the next syllable, so it is light; म likewise is light; but the last syllable लम् has a short अ closed by the final consonant म्, which makes it heavy by position. So the pattern is light-light-heavy.

**3. (A)** The vowel उ is short (1 मात्रा), so the syllable is not heavy by nature. But a short vowel followed by a consonant cluster becomes heavy by position; the cluster स्त closes पु, making it गुरु. उ is not long, and conjuncts certainly do affect weight.

**4. (B)** A long vowel makes the syllable heavy 'by nature' — true no matter what follows. The other three options make a syllable heavy only 'by position', i.e. they depend on what comes AFTER a short vowel, so they are not the by-nature cause.

**5. (B)** Sum only the vowels: short इ 1, long आ 2, short अ 1, short अ 1 = 5. The conjunct द्य and the final विसर्ग add no मात्रा of their own. Counting consonants is the classic error that inflates the total.

**6. (D)** शि has a short इ closed only by the single onset व, so it is light (1 मात्रा). वः has a short अ closed by विसर्ग, making it heavy by position — yet विसर्ग adds NO मात्रा, so the vowel-time is still 1+1 = 2. Weight (गुरु) and duration (मात्रा) are separate ideas here.

**7. (D)** Weight by position depends on what FOLLOWS the vowel, not what precedes it within the same syllable. A cluster that merely begins a syllable does not by itself make that syllable heavy; you must look at what closes the vowel. (In शान्तिः the closure is the final विसर्ग.) Consonants never add मात्रा.

**8. (B)** ओ is a दीर्घ vowel worth 2, आ is 2, and the final short अ before विसर्ग is 1, giving 2+2+1 = 5. The trap is treating ओ as short; ए ऐ ओ औ are all long. The विसर्ग adds nothing.

**9. (A)** दे (ए) and वा (आ) are heavy by nature (long vowels). ल has a short अ closed by only a single onset व, so it is light. यः has a short अ closed by विसर्ग, so it is heavy by position. The full pattern is heavy-heavy-light-heavy.

**10. (A)** Both words have the same three short अ vowels, so each totals 1+1+1 = 3 मात्रा. A word-final consonant and a विसर्ग change syllable WEIGHT (the last syllable becomes guru) but add no vowel-time, so the totals are identical.

**11. (B)** In जनः the final vowel अ is short, so it cannot be heavy by nature; the विसर्ग closes it and makes it heavy by position. The other three end in long vowels (आ, ई, ओ), so their final syllables are heavy by nature, not by position.

**12. (C)** Short अ 1, long ओ 2, short अ 1, short अ 1 = 5. The trap is treating ओ as 1 (it is a long vowel worth 2) or counting one unit per syllable, which would give 4.

**13. (C)** अम् has a short अ closed by अनुस्वार, which makes it heavy by position even though अनुस्वार adds no मात्रा. बा has the long आ, heavy by nature. So both syllables are guru.

**14. (C)** A normal आ is long, worth 2 मात्रा. Extending it to 3 मात्रा pushes it into the third quantity, प्लुत. Vowels certainly can exceed 2 मात्रा — that is precisely what प्लुत is.

**15. (A)** Both syllables carry short vowels, so neither is heavy by nature. But इ is closed by the cluster न्द्र (heavy by position) and अ is closed by विसर्ग (heavy by position). Being short does not prevent position-guru; both are heavy.

**16. (A)** Short उ 1, long आ 2, short इ 1 = 4. The conjunct ष्प is a cluster of consonants and contributes no मात्रा; only the three vowels are summed.

**17. (C)** माता has two long आ vowels, so both syllables are heavy by nature: guru-guru. नदी is light-heavy (short अ then long ई); कवि is light-light (both short before single onsets); गजः is light-heavy (short अ, then short अ closed by विसर्ग).

**18. (C)** मात्रा is summed per vowel by its quantity: long ई 2 + long आ 2 + short इ 1 = 5. Counting one per akshara ignores that long vowels are worth 2 each, which is exactly the misconception to correct.

**19. (C)** In Sanskrit ए, ऐ, ओ, औ are all long vowels, each 2 मात्रा. There is no short counterpart in use here, so none of them is 1 मात्रा, and they are not प्लुत either.

**20. (A)** स has short अ closed by the cluster त्य, so it is heavy by position. त्य and व each carry a short vowel closed by only a single following onset, so both are light. ती has long ई, heavy by nature. Pattern: heavy-light-light-heavy.

**21. (A)** Before junction the two vowels are short अ (1) and long आ (2) = 3 मात्रा. They merge into one long आ worth 2. So the duration falls from 3 to 2; sandhi does not always conserve total vowel-time.

**22. (B)** Two short vowels total 1+1 = 2 मात्रा; they fuse into one long ए, also 2 मात्रा. Here duration is conserved. ए is long (2), not प्लुत (3) and not short (1).

**23. (B)** Sum the five vowels: अ 1, long आ 2, अ 1, अ 1, अ 1 = 6. Counting one per syllable would give 5 (ignoring that हा is long); counting letters inflates it further.

**24. (D)** Anusvāra and visarga contribute zero vowel-duration, so they never raise a मात्रा count. But they DO close a preceding short vowel, turning its syllable guru by position. Both the 'adds मात्रा' and the 'no effect at all' options are wrong.

**25. (D)** A 2-मात्रा vowel is दीर्घ; stretching it to 3 मात्रा makes it प्लुत. So the reciter has substituted a प्लुत for a दीर्घ — an over-lengthening, not a shortening.

**26. (C)** राजा has two long आ vowels = 4 मात्रा. रजः has two short अ vowels = 2 मात्रा (the विसर्ग adds nothing). So राजा exceeds रजः by 4 − 2 = 2 मात्रा.

**27. (A)** Either condition alone makes a syllable guru: a long vowel (by nature) or a short vowel closed by a cluster (by position). Here कार् satisfies both — the आ is long AND it is followed by the conjunct र्य — so two rules apply, though one would already suffice.

**28. (A)** Long ई 2 plus four short अ vowels (1 each) = 2+1+1+1+1 = 6. Counting one per syllable gives 5 because it misses that the first vowel is long.

**29. (A)** देवदा scans दे(ग,2)–व(ल,1)–दा(ग,2): pattern guru-light-guru and 2+1+2 = 5 मात्रा — matching both. कमलम् is light-light-guru (total 3); गायति differs; रामायणम् has five syllables and a different count.

**30. (C)** ग has short अ closed by only the single onset ज, so it stays light. जः has short अ closed by विसर्ग, making it heavy by position. So the correct scansion is light-heavy; being short does not by itself force a syllable to be light.

**31. (C)** All three vowels (उ, अ, अ) are short, worth 1 each, so the total is 3 मात्रा. The conjunct न्द and the final म् change syllable WEIGHT (सुन् becomes guru) but add no vowel-time.

**32. (D)** A दीर्घ vowel is indeed 2 मात्रा, so the first half is right. But position-guru changes syllable WEIGHT, not vowel DURATION: the short vowel is still 1 मात्रा. Conflating heaviness with extra मात्रा is the misconception.

**33. (C)** Long ई 2, long आ 2, short अ 1, short इ 1 = 6. Counting one per syllable gives 4 (missing two long vowels); the final विसर्ग adds no मात्रा.

**34. (B)** नgar (the stem नगर, ending in an open short vowel) has three short vowels each followed by at most a single onset, so all three are light. Adding म् (नगरम्) or विसर्ग (नगरः) makes the final syllable guru; रामगृहम् has a long आ, so not all-light.

**35. (B)** Before junction the vowels total 1 + 2 = 3 मात्रा. They fuse into one ऐ, a long vowel of 2 मात्रा. So the total drops from 3 to 2. ऐ is long (2), not short (1), and the values do not simply add.

**36. (B)** Both syllables carry short अ, so neither is heavy by nature. But धर् is closed by the cluster र्म and मः by विसर्ग, so each is heavy by position. The full pattern is guru-guru.

**37. (C)** Short अ 1, short अ 1, long ई 2, short अ 1 = 5. The long ई is the only 2-मात्रा vowel; counting one per syllable would wrongly give 4.

**38. (A)** ओ is a long vowel, so ordinary भो is 2 मात्रा. Drawing it out to 3 मात्रा makes it प्लुत. It was never short, and मात्रा certainly can exceed 2 (प्लुत = 3).

**39. (D)** Position-guru is governed by what closes the vowel, i.e. what comes AFTER it, not the onset cluster that comes before it. A heavy onset cluster does not by itself make its own syllable heavy; you must look forward to the closing sound.

**40. (C)** Long ओ 2, short इ 1, short अ 1 = 4. The conjunct न्द and the final विसर्ग add no मात्रा; the short इ stays 1 even though विन् is guru by position.

**41. (B)** रामः splits into रा (long आ → guru by nature) and मः (short अ + विसर्ग → guru by position), with no light syllable. कमलम् has light syllables; नदी has a light first syllable. (गङ्गा is two-syllable but its first guru is also by position via the cluster ङ्ग — yet रामः is the clean one-nature-one-position pair asked for.)

**42. (D)** प्लुत vowels, held three मात्रा, appear chiefly in vocatives, calls and chant — not in fast everyday speech, which relies on short and long vowels. प्लुत does exist and is distinct from दीर्घ (2 मात्रा).

**43. (B)** Short इ 1, long आ 2, short अ 1, short अ 1 = 5. The long आ is the only 2-मात्रा vowel; the final विसर्ग adds nothing.

**44. (D)** देव scans दे(guru, long ए)–व(laghu, short अ before single onset): heavy-light, exactly as needed. कवि is light-light; रमा is light-heavy; गुरुः is light-heavy (short उ + विसर्ग).

**45. (B)** रौति (singular) vs रुवन्ति (plural) is a number contrast in the verb, a matter of grammar, not vowel-quantity. There is no दीर्घ/ह्रस्व relationship between the two forms; and रौति is singular, not plural.

**46. (C)** Short अ 1, short उ 1, long आ 2 = 4. The single long आ accounts for the difference from a naive 3-per-syllable count.

**47. (A)** A short vowel closed by just a single onset consonant of the next syllable stays light. Only a following conjunct, अनुस्वार or विसर्ग closes it enough to make it guru by position.

**48. (D)** In राम the second syllable म has a short अ not closed by any cluster/अनुस्वार/विसर्ग, so it is light. Adding विसर्ग in रामः closes that short अ, making the final syllable guru. So राम = guru-light, रामः = guru-guru.

**49. (A)** औ is a long vowel (2), अ is short (1), आ is long (2): 2+1+2 = 5. औ being long, not short, is the key; the final विसर्ग adds no मात्रा.

**50. (D)** A final syllable is guru only if its vowel is long or is closed by a consonant/अनुस्वार/विसर्ग. कवि ends in an open short इ, so its last syllable is light, disproving the blanket claim.

**51. (D)** Long ए 2, short अ 1, long ऊ 2, short अ 1 = 6. Two long vowels (ए and ऊ) raise it above a naive 4; consonants and the final विसर्ग add nothing.

**52. (B)** The first अ is open short → light. नन् has short अ closed by the cluster न्त → heavy by position. तः has short अ closed by विसर्ग → heavy by position. Pattern: light-heavy-heavy.

**53. (A)** रामायणः splits रा–मा–य–णः: the two long आ vowels make the first two syllables heavy by nature, य has a short अ closed by a single onset so it is light, and णः has a short अ closed by विसर्ग so it is heavy by position. That gives heavy-heavy-light-heavy, matching the target pattern; the other words scan differently.

**54. (A)** Long आ 2, short अ 1, long ई 2 = 5. The conjunct त्र adds no मात्रा; the two long vowels make the total 5, not a naive 3.

**55. (D)** नदी has a short अ (1) and a long ई (2), totalling 3 मात्रा — correct. सीता is two long vowels = 4, not 2; गज is two short vowels = 2, not 3; माला is two long आ vowels = 4, not 2.

**56. (D)** A प्लुत vowel is held for 3 मात्रा by definition — three times a short vowel. Sanskrit recognises only three quantities, so there is no 4-मात्रा fourth class; 2 मात्रा would be merely दीर्घ.

**57. (C)** Metre depends on the ordered pattern of guru and laghu syllables. Since a short vowel becomes guru by position (closed by a cluster/अनुस्वार/विसर्ग) without gaining मात्रा, weight is not identical to vowel-duration — hence scansion needs the weight rules, not just a मात्रा tally.

**58. (B)** A प्लुत lasts 3 मात्रा and a ह्रस्व lasts 1 मात्रा, so a प्लुत equals three short vowels in time. Two would equal a दीर्घ (2 मात्रा), not a प्लुत.

**59. (D)** Two short इ vowels total 1 + 1 = 2 मात्रा, and they fuse into a single long ई, also 2 मात्रा. Here the total duration is conserved. The long ई is दीर्घ (2), not प्लुत (3), and nothing is lost or doubled.

**60. (B)** विसर्ग contributes zero मात्रा (claim 1 wrong); ओ is दीर्घ, worth 2 मात्रा, not short (claim 2 wrong); a short vowel closed by a conjunct is heavy by position, not light (claim 3 wrong). All three are errors.
