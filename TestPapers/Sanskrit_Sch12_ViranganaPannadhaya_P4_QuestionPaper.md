**Instructions:** Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. National-Olympiad traps: the one who DIED was Panna's own son (चन्दन), not Panna and not the prince — Panna survived to mourn, so 'self-sacrifice' is imprecise; the value sacrificed is dearer-than-self (her child), which is why the act outranks ordinary battlefield courage; मारयितुम् (-तुम्) names only the assassin's PURPOSE 'to kill' — it neither asserts success nor identifies who actually died, contrast अमारयत् (past), मारयिष्यति (future), मारयतु (imperative); वीराङ्गना = वीर + अङ्गना with savarṇa-dīrgha sandhi (अ+अ→आ) meaning 'brave woman', not a place/weapon/queen; षोडश is the cardinal 'sixteen' but षोडशम्/षोडशे is the ordinal 'sixteenth', and षोडशे शतके is LOCATIVE 'in the sixteenth century', not षष्ठे ('sixth') nor षष्टिः ('sixty'); छलेन/स्वर्णिमाक्षरैः are instrumental (by-means-of), वीराङ्गनासु is locative-plural (among) — never read them as nominatives; दुष्ट = morally wicked while मूर्ख = intellectually foolish, so the rival's fault is moral not mental; राजगृह is a PLACE (palace) but राजपुत्र a PERSON (prince); सर्वस्वत्याग = giving-up (त्याग) of one's-everything (सर्वस्व), i.e. renunciation, never receiving a reward.
