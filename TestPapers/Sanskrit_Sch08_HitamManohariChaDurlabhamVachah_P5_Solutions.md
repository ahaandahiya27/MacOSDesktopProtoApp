## Worked Solutions

**1. (A)** The minimum overlap of two sets in a fixed total is (size A + size B − total) when that is positive: twenty plus twenty-four minus forty leaves four guaranteed to be both. Assuming all useful are pleasing fixes overlap at one set's size rather than its minimum; claiming zero overlap mistakes 'rare' for 'impossible' and ignores that the counts force at least some shared members.

**2. (A)** Usefulness and pleasantness pick out genuinely different qualities; the whole point is that their co-occurrence is rare, so erasing one widens the field from a narrow overlap to a broad single class. Treating them as synonyms denies that very rarity; singling out दुर्लभम् misreads which words carry the contrast; and the objection is semantic, not a bare grammar rule.

**3. (B)** The doctor's words both help (true, actionable) and please (gentle, hopeful), meeting both halves at once. The cheerful false promise pleases but does not benefit; the contemptuous correct insult benefits but does not please; the silent friend supplies neither benefit nor pleasantness.

**4. (C)** Each extra independent condition intersects the field again, leaving a smaller subset, so more demands mean greater rarity. The 'more ways to qualify' picture confuses conjunction with disjunction; rarity is not a fixed floor; and a harder-yet-reachable goal is still दुर्लभम्, not the categorically impossible अशक्यम्.

**5. (A)** The critic owns the हितम् half and the flatterer the मनोहारि half; fusing the truthful benefit with the agreeable manner yields the rare both-AND speech. Keeping only pleasantness or only usefulness reproduces a one-half failure, and there is no reason the fusion of two strengths would yield the two corresponding weaknesses.

**6. (D)** Either quality alone is reasonably attainable; what is hard is having both at once, so attaching rarity to the conjunction names the exact difficulty. Useful speech is not by itself near-impossible; pleasing speech is not condemned (it is half the ideal); and no grammatical rule bars separate praise — the choice is rhetorical and pointed.

**7. (A)** दुर्लभम् asserts that something is hard to obtain — still obtainable — whereas अशक्यम् denies obtainability altogether; that is a difference in kind, not a matter of how close two points sit. So 'practically impossible' smuggles अशक्यम् back in; the word is neither a midpoint nor identical to the impossible extreme.

**8. (C)** Because the only changed element is the prefix, the opposite meanings must come from the prefixes, fixing दुर्- as 'hard/ill' and सु- as 'easy/well'. So the prefixes are not empty, their senses are not reversed, and they do not push the meaning the same way.

**9. (B)** Exhorting effort presupposes the goal can be reached; अशक्यम् denies that, gutting the exhortation, whereas दुर्लभम् keeps it attainable-though-rare. अशक्यम् does not mean 'common', is not a synonym of दुर्लभम्, and an unreachable goal cannot coherently justify striving.

**10. (A)** The both-AND ideal is hard but achievable, hence दुर्लभम्, while a truly unutterable speech would be अशक्यम्. The two words are not synonyms, the labels do not swap (impossible is harder than rare), and combining two qualities narrows rather than widens the field, so (ii) is certainly not easy.

**11. (A)** Across दुर्लभ, दुर्गम and दुर्बोध the prefix दुर्- consistently means 'hard to', attaching difficulty to the base act, so दुर्बोधम् is 'hard to understand'. It does not flatly negate (that would be the अशक्यम् sense), does not commend like सु-, and does not double the action.

**12. (A)** दुर्लभम् from दुर्+लभ् concerns difficulty of obtaining, i.e. scarcity of occurrence; the speech's worth is supplied by the separate adjectives हितम् and मनोहारि. So 'rarely valuable' confuses scarcity with worth; it does not mean 'always valuable'; and as a neuter-singular adjective it qualifies वचः, not the manner of speaking.

**13. (D)** A Sanskrit adjective inherits its noun's gender, number and case; वचः being neuter singular forces neuter-singular qualifiers. Adjectives do not default to masculine, their own count does not pluralise them, and unlike adverbs they do decline to agree.

**14. (C)** Agreement tracks the noun's gender: a masculine head noun pulls the adjective into masculine हितः. The neuter हितम् would now mismatch, the feminine हिता has no warrant, and a single head noun does not trigger a plural adjective.

**15. (C)** वचः derives from the stem वचस्, whose final -स् appears as visarga at word end, paralleling मनस्→मनः. It is not the prefix -र्, not a nasal -न्, and not a dental -त्; the -स् stem class is what unites वचः with मनः and तेजः.

**16. (D)** Shared neuter-singular agreement marks हितम्, मनोहारि and दुर्लभम् as qualifiers of the one noun वचः, not co-equal nouns. They are not verbs, they share rather than differ in case, and वचः ('speech') is plainly the substantive being described.

**17. (B)** The conjunction च survives, but दुर्लभम् has flipped to its opposite सुलभम् (a real, claim-weakening change), while मधुरम् merely re-words the pleasing quality (मनोहारि). It does not touch च→वा, सुलभ and दुर्लभ are opposites not synonyms, and replacing 'rare' with 'easy' loosens rather than tightens the standard.

**18. (B)** मनः in मनोहारि is the very -स् stem of वचः/मनस्, whose visarga becomes 'o' before the voiced ह. The -वान् of क्रियावान् is a possessive suffix, दुर्- is a prefix, and the -शः of क्षणशः is the distributive -शस् suffix — none are neuter -स् noun stems.

**19. (D)** The seam is a visarga (after an 'a') meeting the voiced ह, which converts the visarga to the vowel 'o', yielding मनो-. There is no vowel-meets-vowel junction (so the like-vowel rule does not apply), no consonant doubling occurs, and the shift is a rule, not a spelling whim.

**20. (C)** The visarga-to-'o' shift in मनोहारि depends specifically on the FOLLOWING sound being voiced (ह); before a voiceless क् the visarga does not become 'o'. So 'o' is not automatic before every consonant, the preceding vowel does not lengthen, and the visarga does not merely vanish.

**21. (D)** Two similar simple vowels (उ + उ) merge into the corresponding long vowel ऊ, so सु+उक्ति becomes सूक्ति; the semivowel व् would only arise if उ met an UNLIKE vowel. The 'a + i → e' rule is irrelevant (no such vowels here), the result is a long vowel not a diphthong औ, and the vowels do merge rather than stand apart.

**22. (A)** Vowel sandhi requires a vowel ending meeting a vowel beginning; since च opens with the consonant च्, the trigger is simply absent and the enclitic stands written separately. मनोहारि does end in a vowel, च is a particle not a verb, and the explanation owes nothing to emphasis.

**23. (A)** A postposed enclitic च ties the word it follows back to the preceding item, so it joins मनोहारि to हितम्, yielding the both-AND pairing. It does not reach forward to वचः/दुर्लभम्, does not attach only to a following word, and does not link all four indiscriminately.

**24. (D)** सूक्तिः coalesced because सु's उ met उक्ति's उ (vowel + vowel); in सुभाषितम् the सु meets the consonant भ्, so no vowel merger occurs and सु stays short. There is no long ऊ here, सु means 'well/good', and भाषितम् begins with a consonant, not a vowel.

**25. (A)** The suffix -शस् builds a fixed, indeclinable distributive adverb that never inflects for case, so no instrumental form exists at all. Both कणशसा and कणश्या wrongly assume it declines; and it is an adverb, not a verb to be conjugated.

**26. (D)** Study is measured in moments of time and saving in physical particles, so क्षणशः suits विद्या and कणशः suits अर्थः by the very nature of how each grows. Knowledge is not literally made of time-units, the match is principled rather than arbitrary, and the two adverbs carry distinct senses.

**27. (D)** कर्म + -शस् mirrors क्षण/कण + -शस्, producing an indeclinable 'deed by deed' adverb. It is not a finite verb, not a dative noun, and not an agreeing adjective — the -शस् form never declines to match anything.

**28. (C)** Learning by daily study sessions is accumulation over units of time, matching क्षणशः; कणशः is reserved for grain/coin-style physical saving. The growth was gradual (so an adverb does apply), and the two adverbs are not freely interchangeable — each fits a distinct mode of accumulation.

**29. (D)** Both share the identical distributive suffix -शस्; the meaning split rides entirely on the bases क्षण (time) versus कण (particle). The suffixes are the same, indeclinable adverbs carry no gender, and the two are genuinely distinct words, not variants.

**30. (C)** The common suffix signals a common pattern — incremental accumulation — even though the units differ (moments vs particles). Knowledge and wealth are not identical, -शस् is not the sole adverb-former, and the indeclinable suffix imposes no gender.

**31. (B)** The suffix -वान् means 'possessing', and क्रिया from कृ is genuine doing, so क्रियावान् is the possessor of action, a doer. -वान् is not privative, the word concerns doing rather than describing, and it carries no sense of opposing action.

**32. (B)** The doer is the one whose finished work needs no announcement (A); the boaster is the one whose words run ahead of unfinished deeds (B). Loud forecasting is talk, not doing; producing real work does not make one a boaster; and merely speaking aloud is not the mark of a doer.

**33. (B)** Both teachings put substance above show: real deeds over boasting, and genuinely beneficial-pleasing speech over hollow words — so they reinforce, not contradict, each other. The chapter does not prize talk over deeds, the contrast is not about wealth versus knowledge, and it counsels worthy speech and action, not silence.

**34. (A)** Just as pleasing words without real benefit fail the speech ideal, eloquent promises without real deeds fail the doer ideal — both substitute show for substance. The adverb-swap, the saying-vs-subtle confusion, and the rare-vs-easy slip are different traps that do not capture this show-over-substance parallel.

**35. (D)** The chapter prizes the doer whose action makes boasting superfluous, which the first proverb captures. Promising before doing, ranking boasts above deeds, and counselling inertia all invert or abandon the कर्म-over-कथन lesson.

**36. (B)** Only सूक्तिः contains the element उक्ति ('saying'), anchoring it to speech, while सूक्ष्म lacks it and means 'subtle/fine'. So they are not both speech-words, सूक्ष्म is not a kind of saying, and a shared opening syllable does not create synonymy.

**37. (A)** 'Subtle riddle' is the meaning of सूक्ष्म, the lookalike, whereas a सूक्तिः (सु+उक्ति) is a well-spoken maxim akin to सुभाषितम्. It is not a boast (विकत्थन), not a treasure-list (भाण्डागारः), and not a roster of curiosities (कुतूहलम्).

**38. (C)** कुतूहल, कौतुक and जिज्ञासा all name the desire to learn, which the chapter treats as the engine of knowledge gathered moment by moment. They concern neither boasting, nor rest, nor greed for wealth.

**39. (C)** As a desiderative of ज्ञा, जिज्ञासा is the desire to know, and that curiosity powers the patient क्षणशः study that builds विद्या. It is not a wish for wealth, not a wish to boast (which the chapter criticises), and not a wish to rest.

**40. (D)** Both a storehouse and क्षणशः-knowledge grow by gradual, repeated additions, so the image fits incremental learning. A storehouse does not fill instantly, the metaphor is meant earnestly (not ironic about coins), and it is an image of fullness, not emptiness.

**41. (A)** Fine speech and riches are outward gifts; the cheat's dishonesty exposes the inner lack the chapter prizes most — शीलम्. He already has speech and wealth, and curiosity is simply not the value at issue.

**42. (C)** The recurrence of शीलम् alongside genuine speech and deeds marks the prized figure as inwardly good, with outward acts expressing that character. The ideal is not mere outward impressiveness, not riches alone, and not eloquence alone.

**43. (D)** Only the respectful-yet-honest delivery meets both halves at once. Vague praise drops the हितम् (no real help), the humiliating rebuke drops the मनोहारि (no pleasantness), and silence drops both — each failing a different, specific half of the ideal.

**44. (C)** The verse makes the both-AND quality SUFFICIENT for rarity, not the reverse; affirming the consequent (rare therefore both) is a fallacy, since speech may be rare for many other reasons. So rarity alone tells us neither 'it is both', nor 'it is neither', nor that it is common.

**45. (C)** A wounding rebuke can help (हितम्) yet still sting (not मनोहारि), so it satisfies only the usefulness half; truth is not inherently pleasing. It is not pleasing-but-useless, and it is not wholly outside the verse since it does carry benefit.

**46. (A)** A misleading slogan pleases but does not genuinely benefit, so it lacks हितम्, and the both-AND ideal requires both qualities. मनोहारि is not condemned (it is half the ideal), pleasantness alone is insufficient, and the failure is substantive, not grammatical.

**47. (C)** Requiring both narrows the field to the overlap of the two sets, which is no larger than either alone, so such candidates are rarer — the abstract form of दुर्लभम्. Two conjoined criteria do not multiply the ways to qualify, the count does change, and 'rare' is not 'impossible'.

**48. (B)** Both clusters elevate the harder, genuine good — speech that truly helps and pleases, and learning/wealth/deeds patiently earned — over the cheap or hollow alternative. They do not exalt boasting or effortless instant results, and they are unified rather than opposed.

**49. (C)** Ideal speech is दुर्लभम् (rare-but-reachable) and knowledge/wealth grow bit by bit, so both verses point to patient, incremental effort toward hard-but-attainable goods. They are not easy/instant, not impossible, and not won by boasting.

**50. (B)** The chapter weaves both-AND speech, patient क्षणशः/कणशः accumulation, deeds over boasting, and शीलम् as the crowning value — exactly the first rule. The others invert the speech ideal, abandon effort, or drop usefulness and character.

**51. (D)** Only the first set is right throughout: rare, pleasing, doer, good saying. The other sets each smuggle in classic errors — 'impossible' for दुर्लभम्, 'useful' or 'harsh' for मनोहारि (which means pleasing), 'boaster' for क्रियावान्, or 'subtle' for सूक्तिः.

**52. (D)** च is 'and' not 'or'; दुर्लभम् is 'rare' not impossible; मनोहारि is 'heart-stealing/pleasing' not mind-harming; and क्षणशः is the distributive 'moment by moment', not 'all at once' — every one of the four is mistaken.

**53. (B)** Mirroring हितं मनोहारि च, the added words would be neuter-singular adjectives conjoined by च ('and') and agreeing with वचः. They are not 'or'-linked nouns, not adverbs on दुर्लभम्, and not a subject-object-verb triple.

**54. (B)** Naming the both-AND speech a rare yet reachable ideal frames it as something worth striving for — an ethical nudge, not bare parsing. A description of an ideal does carry value, the line is not a grammar exercise, and दुर्लभम् concerns the rarity of such speech, not market goods.

**55. (C)** सूक्तिः (wise saying), कुतूहलम् (curiosity), भाण्डागारः (treasured store of knowledge) and शीलम् (character) map onto wise speech, curiosity, valued learning and good character. The other lists invert the chapter's values.

**56. (D)** The chapter condemns hollow boasting, not worthy speech; beneficial-and-pleasing words are themselves a genuine contribution that sits alongside real deeds. So neither cluster is abandoned, and speaking and doing remain distinct activities the chapter values for the same reason — substance over show.

**57. (B)** Curiosity speaks to gain knowledge; boasting speaks to praise oneself — so eager questioning is कुतूहलम्, not विकत्थन. Frequent speaking is not automatically boasting, curiosity is expressed through asking rather than silence, and she is a curious learner, not literally a 'storehouse'.

**58. (A)** दुर्लभम् keeps ideal speech attainable, matching the message that knowledge and wealth accrue gradually through patient effort — both reward perseverance. They do not teach impossibility, do not promise instant gain, and they pull in the same direction.

**59. (B)** वचः ('speech') is the substantive, and the three adjectives describe it by sharing its neuter-singular form. दुर्लभम् and हितम् are qualifiers, not the head noun, and the line is not four parallel nouns.

**60. (B)** The faithful restatement keeps the conjunction (both), the correct sense of दुर्लभम् (rare-but-attainable), and the implied encouragement to strive. The distractors variously swap 'and' for 'or' and 'rare' for 'easy', declare impossibility, or wrongly condemn pleasing speech.
