1. A devotee insists that since सुभाषितानि are called 'rasa' (nectar) that we 'drink' (पिबामः), the truest way to honour them is to recite each one aloud exactly once at a festival and never repeat it. Why does the title's own word-choice undercut this?
   (A) The verse uses पठामः ('we read'), not पिबामः, so reciting once is exactly what it prescribes.
   (B) रसम् means literal fruit-juice, so the saying is about drinking at festivals, making the devotee correct.
   (C) नित्यम् means 'never', so the verse actually forbids drinking the sayings at all.
   (D) नित्यम् ('daily') paired with the drinking-metaphor demands repeated, habitual intake; a one-time festival recitation contradicts the 'every day' force the verse builds in.

2. In नित्यं पिबामः सुभाषितरसम्, suppose we change पिबामः to पिबामि while leaving everything else. What single grammatical fact changes, and does the chapter's communal sense survive?
   (A) The subject shifts from 'we' (first person plural) to 'I' (first person singular); the shared, communal 'we drink together daily' sense is lost.
   (B) Nothing changes; पिबामि and पिबामः are both 'we drink' and equally plural.
   (C) The tense shifts from present to past, so it would mean 'I drank'.
   (D) The verb becomes an imperative 'drink!', addressed to a single listener.

3. सुभाषितरसम् carries the accusative -अम्. A student argues: 'Since रसः (nectar) is masculine and सुभाषितम् is neuter, the compound सुभाषितरसम् must be neuter, which is why it ends in -अम्.' Evaluate.
   (A) The reasoning is correct: a compound always takes the gender of its first member, so सुभाषित makes it neuter.
   (B) The reasoning is correct because -अम् is exclusively a neuter ending in Sanskrit.
   (C) The compound is feminine, agreeing with सुभाषितरसा, and the -अम् is a typographical accusative.
   (D) The reasoning is wrong: in a tatpurusha the gender follows the LAST member (रस, masculine), and -अम् here is simply the masculine accusative singular of रस, marking it as the object drunk — not a neuter ending.

4. Why is the rasa-metaphor for subhashitas a deliberately better choice than calling them, say, 'a treasure to be locked away'?
   (A) Because rasa is more valuable than treasure, so the metaphor simply exaggerates their worth.
   (B) Because treasure can be stolen but rasa cannot, making the saying about security.
   (C) There is no real difference; both metaphors mean the sayings should be hidden and protected.
   (D) A treasure stored away is merely kept; rasa is something one drinks and absorbs, so the metaphor stresses that wise sayings must be taken in and internalised, not just possessed.

5. A teacher says the title verse is itself an example of what it praises. What is the subtlest sense in which this is true?
   (A) The verse IS a subhashita — a compact, well-spoken saying — so in advising us to drink subhashitas daily it models the very thing it recommends.
   (B) Because the verse literally contains the word रसः meaning the fruit-juice it tells us to drink.
   (C) Because the verse lists all five va-qualities, demonstrating the honour it promises.
   (D) Because the verse is a command to the teacher, who then obeys it by teaching.

6. If a friend renders the title as 'We always drink the taste of bad speech', exactly which two word-analyses has he botched?
   (A) He has read सु- as 'bad' (it means 'well/good') and treated रसम् as a negative 'taste of speech' rather than the prized 'nectar/essence' of WELL-spoken sayings.
   (B) He has misread नित्यम् as 'always' (it means 'never') and पिबामः as 'we drink' (it means 'we sing').
   (C) He has misread सु- as 'six' and भाषित as 'fault', turning the phrase into 'six faults'.
   (D) Only one error: रसम् should be 'juice', but 'bad speech' for सुभाषित is correct.

7. A pupil writes the honouring verse as 'नरः विद्यया वपुषा वाचा वस्त्रेण सत्येन पूजितः' and counts six va-qualities. Pinpoint the precise defect.
   (A) There is no defect; truth is one of the va-qualities, so six is correct.
   (B) The defect is only that वस्त्रेण should be dropped, leaving the correct five.
   (C) सत्येन ('by truth') is not a va-quality at all — it is a purification MEANS (मनः सत्येन शुध्यति); the genuine fifth quality is विनयेन (humility), so the list is wrong, not merely over-counted.
   (D) The defect is that विद्यया is the intruder, since knowledge belongs to the purification verse as ज्ञानेन.

8. विद्यया, वपुषा, वाचा, वस्त्रेण, विनयेन: a learner says 'three end in -आ/-ा-ish vowels and two in -एन, so they must be in two different cases.' Correct this.
   (A) Correct — the -आ endings are nominative and the -एन endings are instrumental, so there are two cases.
   (B) All five are in the SAME case — the instrumental (तृतीया, 'by/with') — the endings differ only because the words belong to different stem-classes (feminine ā-/ī-stems vs neuter/consonant stems), not because the case differs.
   (C) Correct — the differing endings show three are singular and two are plural.
   (D) All five are genitive ('of knowledge', etc.), and the endings differ by gender.

9. Build the instrumental phrase for a NEW honouring quality not in the verse — say 'by good conduct', using आचार (masculine a-stem, 'conduct') on the verse's pattern (नरः ... पूजितः).
   (A) नरः आचारया पूजितः, taking the -या ending seen in विद्यया.
   (B) नरः आचारस्य पूजितः, marking 'of conduct'.
   (C) नरः आचारेण पूजितः ('a man is honoured by conduct') — the masculine a-stem takes -एण in the instrumental, just like वस्त्रेण and विनयेन.
   (D) नरः आचारम् पूजितः, putting the means in the accusative.

10. Why does the chapter group exactly these five qualities — knowledge, body, speech, dress, humility — and what unifying idea (beyond all starting with व) justifies the set?
   (A) They are grouped only because they share the letter व; there is no further connecting idea.
   (B) They are grouped because all five are bodily organs that perform actions.
   (C) Together they cover the whole person — inner faculty (विद्या), physical presence (वपुस्), expression (वाणी), outward appearance (वस्त्र), and character (विनय) — so honour is earned across mind, body, and conduct, not by any one alone.
   (D) They are grouped because each one, by itself, is fully sufficient to earn complete honour.

11. A man is admired purely for his fine clothes though he is rude and idle. Judged strictly by the five-quality verse, how complete is the honour he receives?
   (A) Fully justified, since वस्त्र alone is the single quality that earns all honour.
   (B) Completely baseless, because वस्त्र (dress) is not actually one of the five qualities.
   (C) Based on a fault, because fine dress falls under the six दोषाः to be shed.
   (D) Only partial — वस्त्र (dress) is genuinely one of the five, so the honour is not baseless, but विद्या, वाणी and विनय are also required, and his rudeness and idleness fail those.

12. वाणी (good speech) is a praised quality, yet क्रोधः (anger) is a fault. A pupil who normally speaks well but lashes out in anger has, by the chapter's logic:
   (A) gained a virtue, since forceful angry speech shows command of वाणी.
   (B) undermined his own virtue — indulging the fault of anger corrupts the very speech (वाणी) that was his quality, so a fault can spoil a virtue.
   (C) stayed neutral, because वाणी and क्रोधः are unrelated and cannot affect each other.
   (D) proved that वाणी is itself a fault, since speech can carry anger.

13. A pupil reasons: 'वपुस् (a good body) is a quality, so resting the body with plenty of निद्रा and तन्द्रा must also be virtuous.' Why does the chapter refute this?
   (A) Because the body is itself a fault, so वपुस् was wrongly listed as a quality.
   (B) Because निद्रा and तन्द्रा are virtues that rest the body, so the pupil is correct.
   (C) A healthy body (वपुस्) is a virtue, but निद्रा (sleep) and तन्द्रा (drowsiness) are listed as faults; the chapter prizes MASTERY over the body, not indulgence of it.
   (D) Because वपुस् and निद्रा mean the same thing, so resting and fitness are identical.

14. नरः पञ्चभिः वकारैः पूजितः. Change ONLY the number to 'a man is honoured by three va-qualities'. Which two words must change, and how?
   (A) Only पञ्चभिः changes, to त्रयः (the nominative 'three'), and वकारैः stays unchanged.
   (B) पञ्चभिः → त्रीणि and वकारैः → वकारम् (singular), since three is treated as a unit.
   (C) Nothing changes; पञ्चभिः already covers any number of qualities.
   (D) पञ्चभिः → त्रिभिः (instrumental of त्रि, 'three'), and the list would shrink to three qualities; वकारैः itself stays plural since three is still plural.

15. Which statement about the relationship between वकारैः and पञ्चभिः is exactly right?
   (A) पञ्चभिः is instrumental but वकारैः is genitive, so they do not agree.
   (B) वकारैः is the subject in the nominative and पञ्चभिः modifies the verb as an adverb.
   (C) They agree as a numeral-noun pair in case and number — both instrumental plural — so पञ्चभिः ('by five') quantifies वकारैः ('by va-qualities').
   (D) पञ्चभिः is singular and वकारैः is plural, so the numeral and noun disagree in number.

16. नरः पञ्चभिः वकारैः पूजितः — a reader translates 'the man worships five va-things.' Two errors combine here. Name them.
   (A) पूजितः is an active present verb ('worships'), and वकारैः is correctly the object; the only error is the number.
   (B) पूजितः is a PAST PASSIVE participle, so the man is the one BEING honoured, not the worshipper; and पञ्चभिः वकारैः is instrumental ('BY five'), the means of honour, not the accusative object of worship.
   (C) नरः is accusative (the man worshipped), and पञ्चभिः is the subject; both case-readings are simply reversed.
   (D) There is no error; 'the man worships five va-things' is the verse's meaning.

17. Among the five qualities, which pairing of a quality with the FAULT that most directly negates it is correct?
   (A) विनय (humility) is most directly negated by क्रोधः (anger) — anger overthrows the calm humility that विनय names.
   (B) विद्या (knowledge) is most directly negated by वस्त्र (dress).
   (C) वपुस् (a fit body) is most directly negated by वाणी (speech).
   (D) वाणी (good speech) is most directly negated by दीर्घायुष् (long life).

18. Suppose someone has विद्या, वपुस्, वाणी and वस्त्र but is utterly arrogant. By the verse, is his honour secure?
   (A) Yes — four out of five qualities guarantees full honour regardless of arrogance.
   (B) Yes — arrogance is itself a sign of विद्या, so it adds to his honour.
   (C) No — he is missing विनय (humility), the fifth quality; arrogance fails it, so his honour is incomplete despite the other four.
   (D) No — but only because arrogance is one of the six named faults that erases all qualities.

19. A reader counts the faults as 'sleepiness (निद्रा+तन्द्रा as one), idleness (आलस्यम्+दीर्घसूत्रता as one), भयम्, क्रोधः' = four. Give the precise rebuttal.
   (A) The reader is right — there really are only four faults once duplicates are merged.
   (B) The verse names SIX distinct faults: निद्रा (full sleep) ≠ तन्द्रा (drowsy half-sleep), and आलस्यम् (general laziness) ≠ दीर्घसूत्रता (delaying particular tasks); merging each pair undercounts to four.
   (C) There are five faults, because भयम् and क्रोधः are also a single 'emotion' fault.
   (D) There are eight faults, since each merged pair actually splits into three.

20. Which of the following correctly contrasts आलस्यम् with दीर्घसूत्रता — the pair most often wrongly fused?
   (A) आलस्यम् is delaying specific tasks, while दीर्घसूत्रता is a general unwillingness to act (the reverse).
   (B) आलस्यम् is full sleep, while दीर्घसूत्रता is drowsiness.
   (C) आलस्यम् is a general unwillingness to act (doing nothing at all), while दीर्घसूत्रता is dragging out and postponing PARTICULAR tasks one has undertaken.
   (D) आलस्यम् is fear of work, while दीर्घसूत्रता is anger at work.

21. तन्द्रा and निद्रा are kept separate. A student says 'निद्रा must be the milder of the two because it has fewer syllables.' Evaluate the claim and give the real distinction.
   (A) Correct — निद्रा, being shorter, is the milder drowsiness and तन्द्रा is full sleep.
   (B) The distinction does not exist; निद्रा and तन्द्रा are perfect synonyms.
   (C) The syllable reasoning is irrelevant; in fact निद्रा is the deeper state (full sleep) and तन्द्रा the milder one (drowsy half-sleep), so even the implied ranking is reversed.
   (D) निद्रा means fear and तन्द्रा means anger, so neither is about sleep.

22. षड् दोषाः हातव्याः भूतिम् इच्छता. A learner makes भूतिम् इच्छता the SUBJECT, reading 'the desirer of prosperity gives up six faults.' Where exactly does the grammar resist this?
   (A) Nothing resists it; इच्छता is nominative and is the subject.
   (B) हातव्याः is a gerundive ('to-be-abandoned') whose agent stands in the instrumental, so इच्छता is the instrumental agent ('BY the one desiring prosperity'), not a nominative subject — the faults दोषाः are the nominative.
   (C) भूतिम् is the subject (nominative), so 'prosperity gives up the faults'.
   (D) हातव्याः is an active verb 'gives up', so a nominative subject is exactly what it needs.

23. Which single transformation would turn 'षड् दोषाः हातव्याः भूतिम् इच्छता' into a grammatical ACTIVE sentence with the same meaning?
   (A) Make the agent nominative and use an active verb: भूतिम् इच्छन् नरः षड् दोषान् त्यजेत् ('one desiring prosperity should give up the six faults') — agent now subject, faults now accusative object.
   (B) Keep हातव्याः but change दोषाः to the instrumental दोषैः.
   (C) Change इच्छता to the genitive इच्छतः, leaving everything else the same.
   (D) Replace भूतिम् with the nominative भूतिः, keeping the gerundive हातव्याः.

24. आलस्यं हि मनुष्याणां शरीरस्थो महान् रिपुः. The label शरीरस्थः ('seated in the body') is doing real philosophical work. What is it?
   (A) It marks laziness as an INNER enemy that lives within oneself, so the chief obstacle to achievement is internal and must be conquered within, not blamed on outside foes.
   (B) It means the body itself is the enemy and ought to be neglected or harmed.
   (C) It means external enemies are far more dangerous than one's own laziness.
   (D) It means the enemy lives only in other people's bodies, never one's own.

25. मनुष्याणाम् in 'आलस्यं हि मनुष्याणां ... महान् रिपुः' answers which question, and in what case?
   (A) It answers 'by whom?' and is instrumental plural — 'by men'.
   (B) It answers 'the men did what?' and is nominative plural — the subject.
   (C) It answers 'for whom?' and is dative singular — 'for a man'.
   (D) It answers 'an enemy OF WHOM?' and is genitive plural — 'of men' — showing whose enemy laziness is.

26. In 'आलस्यं ... महान् रिपुः', what is the grammatical relationship between आलस्यम् and रिपुः, and why are both nominative?
   (A) रिपुः is the object that laziness acts upon, hence both are accusative.
   (B) रिपुः is the means by which laziness operates, hence instrumental.
   (C) रिपुः possesses laziness, so रिपुः is genitive.
   (D) It is predicate identification — 'laziness IS a great enemy' — so subject (आलस्यम्) and predicate noun (रिपुः) both take the nominative and agree.

27. गात्राणि जलेन शुध्यन्ति but बुद्धिः ज्ञानेन शुध्यति. A student says the verb-ending differs because the MEANS differ (जलेन forces plural, ज्ञानेन forces singular). Correct him.
   (A) He is right — instrumental जलेन triggers a plural verb and ज्ञानेन a singular one.
   (B) The difference is tense: शुध्यन्ति is past and शुध्यति is present.
   (C) गात्राणि takes a command form while बुद्धिः takes a statement form.
   (D) The verb agrees with its SUBJECT, not its means: गात्राणि (limbs) is plural so the verb is शुध्यन्ति, while बुद्धिः (intellect) is singular so the verb is शुध्यति; the means is irrelevant to number.

28. The purification verse uses four DIFFERENT means for four parts. A boy says 'water cleans, so washing my body with water also cleans my mind.' What law of the verse does this violate?
   (A) It violates nothing; water cleans the body and the mind alike in the verse.
   (B) The verse assigns a distinct means to each goal — water for the body, truth for the mind — so washing cleans only गात्राणि; the mind (मनः) needs सत्य (truth), and one means does not serve all goals.
   (C) It violates the rule that knowledge (ज्ञानेन) is what cleans the mind, not truth.
   (D) It violates the rule that the mind cannot be cleaned by anything at all.

29. Given the set 'गात्राणि तपसा; मनः सत्येन; आत्मा जलेन; बुद्धिः ज्ञानेन', which single SWAP makes all four correct?
   (A) Swap the means of मनः and बुद्धिः, giving मनः ज्ञानेन and बुद्धिः सत्येन.
   (B) Change मनः सत्येन to मनः जलेन.
   (C) Leave it unchanged; the set is already fully correct.
   (D) Swap the means of गात्राणि and आत्मा, giving गात्राणि जलेन (body by water) and आत्मा तपसा (soul by austerity); मनः–सत्येन and बुद्धिः–ज्ञानेन are already right.

30. बुद्धिः केन शुध्यति? A learner answers जलेन, reasoning 'water cleans everything physical, and the brain is a physical organ.' Diagnose the deepest flaw.
   (A) The verse treats बुद्धिः as the intellect (a non-physical faculty) cleaned by ज्ञानेन (knowledge); water cleans only the physical body (गात्राणि), so equating the intellect with a washable organ misreads the metaphor.
   (B) The flaw is none — water does clean the intellect in the verse.
   (C) The flaw is only that he should have said तपसा, the means for the intellect.
   (D) The flaw is that the intellect needs no cleaning at all, so the question is meaningless.

31. Which part-and-means pairing exposes the SUBTLEST trap, by attaching to the intellect a means that genuinely purifies a DIFFERENT inner faculty?
   (A) बुद्धिः जलेन ('intellect by water'), because water purifies a different inner faculty.
   (B) बुद्धिः सत्येन ('intellect by truth') is the subtle trap, because truth (सत्येन) really is a purifying means — but of the MIND (मनः), not the intellect, which needs knowledge (ज्ञानेन).
   (C) बुद्धिः वस्त्रेण ('intellect by dress'), because dress is a purification means for another faculty.
   (D) बुद्धिः क्रोधेन ('intellect by anger'), because anger purifies the soul.

32. Why is सत्य (truth), not water, the fitting means for cleaning the मनः (mind)?
   (A) Because water is too scarce to spare for the mind, so truth is used as a cheaper substitute.
   (B) The mind's impurities are inner ones — deceit and crookedness — which truthfulness removes; water can rinse the body but cannot reach or cleanse the inner faculty, so each impurity needs a matching remedy.
   (C) Because truth and water are the same purifier under different names.
   (D) Because the mind is a physical organ that truth physically washes.

33. A pupil washes his hands carefully but tells lies all day and stays angry. By the purification verse, what has he actually purified, and what remains impure?
   (A) He has purified everything, since washing with water cleans body and mind alike.
   (B) He has purified the soul, which water alone suffices to clean.
   (C) He has purified the intellect, since careful washing equals knowledge.
   (D) He has purified the body (गात्राणि, by water) but left the mind (मनः) impure, since the mind is cleaned by truth (सत्येन), and his lying and anger leave it unpurified.

34. Across the two verses, विद्या appears as a honouring quality while ज्ञानेन is the intellect's purifier. A friend says 'so knowledge is double-listed.' Resolve this precisely.
   (A) Correct — विद्या and ज्ञानेन are the identical word appearing in both verses.
   (B) Correct — विद्या cleans the body in one verse and honours the person in the other.
   (C) Wrong — विद्या appears in neither verse at all.
   (D) They are NOT the same entry: विद्या (learning) is one of the five honouring qualities, while the intellect's purifying means is ज्ञानेन (knowledge) — distinct words in distinct verses, so there is no overlap to count twice.

35. गात्राणि जलेन शुध्यन्ति. Which feature of गात्राणि determines that the verb is शुध्यन्ति rather than शुध्यति?
   (A) गात्राणि is treated as one whole body, so it should logically take the singular शुध्यति.
   (B) The means जलेन is plural, and the verb agrees with the means, not the subject.
   (C) गात्राणि is neuter PLURAL ('the limbs'), and a finite verb agrees with its subject in number, so a plural subject takes the plural verb शुध्यन्ति.
   (D) गात्राणि is feminine singular, which happens to take the -अन्ति ending.

36. जलबिन्दुनिपातेन क्रमशः पूर्यते घटः. A student claims क्रमशः means 'the pot fills suddenly at the very end, once almost full.' Refute using the verse's own image.
   (A) क्रमशः means 'gradually, step by step'; the WHOLE filling is incremental — each drop adds a little — so there is no sudden final jump, only steady accumulation throughout.
   (B) Correct — क्रमशः specifically marks the sudden last-moment completion.
   (C) क्रमशः means 'all at once', so the pot truly fills instantly.
   (D) क्रमशः means 'backwards', so the pot is gradually emptied.
