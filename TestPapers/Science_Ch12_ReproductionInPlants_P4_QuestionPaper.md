Distractors weaponise the deepest confusions: pollen-transfer vs gamete-fusion, ovule->seed vs ovary->fruit vs zygote->embryo, clone-uniformity vs seed-variation, spore (no fusion) vs seed (post-fusion), agent-matched adaptations, and the precise order of life-cycle events under experimental manipulation. 1. A horticulturist propagates a seedless grape vine entirely from cuttings for forty years. A visiting scientist claims this means the variety can never produce offspring with any new trait through this method. The scientist's claim is best judged as:
   (A) unsound, because repeated cuttings gradually accumulate new traits over generations
   (B) unsound, because each cutting secretly involves pollen from a second parent
   (C) sound, but only because grapes happen to be seedless
   (D) sound, because cuttings copy a single parent's genetic material exactly, so no reshuffling of traits occurs

2. A field grown from cuttings of one elite parent and a neighbouring field grown from that parent's seeds are both exposed to a brand-new pest. The cutting field is wiped out almost uniformly; the seed field loses only part of its plants. The deepest reason the seed field fared better is that:

   (B) the seed plants carry varied combinations of traits, so some happened to resist the pest while clones shared one weakness
   (C) the cutting plants had used up their stored food and starved
   (D) seeds release a chemical that repels all pests automatically

3. A student stores two potato pieces: one with three eyes and one from which all eyes were carefully gouged out, then plants both in identical moist soil. After three weeks only one piece has sprouted shoots. The piece that failed lacked:

   (B) the buds, from which the new shoots would have grown
   (C) the roots, which only sprout from eyeless surfaces

6. A mushroom, a moss, and a fern are grouped in a lesson, and beside them sits a potato. A student is asked which one does NOT share the grouping feature. The correct answer and reason is:
