## Worked Solutions

**1. (B)** The HCF (Highest Common Factor) is the largest number that divides both numbers exactly. (A) describes the LCM — the smallest number both divide into. The HCF is about dividing INTO the numbers; the LCM is what the numbers divide into.

**2. (C)** 24 = 2³ × 3, 36 = 2² × 3². Common primes to the lowest power: 2² × 3 = 12. (72 is the LCM — taking highest powers gives the trap.)

**3. (A)** 12 = 2² × 3, 18 = 2 × 3². LCM takes every prime to its highest power: 2² × 3² = 36. (6 is the HCF; 216 = 12 × 18 is the product, which only equals the LCM for co-prime numbers.)

**4. (C)** The tile side must divide both 12 and 16 exactly, so it must be a common factor; the largest is HCF(12, 16). 12 = 2²×3, 16 = 2⁴, so HCF = 2² = 4 ft. (16 ft is the "HCF = the bigger number" trap; the HCF can't exceed the smaller dimension.)

**5. (D)** With 4 ft tiles the floor is (12 ÷ 4) × (16 ÷ 4) = 3 × 4 = 12 tiles. (A bigger tile gives FEWER tiles, so the HCF gives the fewest tiles for an exact fit.)

**6. (A)** 8 = 2³ and 15 = 3 × 5 share no prime, so HCF(8, 15) = 1 — co-prime. The others share a factor: 8 & 12 share 4, 9 & 15 share 3, 10 & 25 share 5. (Co-prime numbers need not themselves be prime — 8 and 15 are both composite.)

**7. (C)** 7 and 9 are co-prime (HCF = 1), so their LCM equals their product: 7 × 9 = 63.

**8. (D)** They ring together at common multiples of 6 and 8; the first is LCM(6, 8). 6 = 2×3, 8 = 2³, LCM = 2³×3 = 24 minutes. (48 = 6 × 8 is the trap — the product overshoots because they share the factor 2.)

**9. (A)** HCF × LCM = product, so LCM = product ÷ HCF = 216 ÷ 6 = 36. (1296 = 216 × 6 multiplies instead of divides; 210 = 216 − 6 subtracts.)

**10. (B)** For any two numbers, HCF × LCM = the product of the two numbers. This lets you find one if you know the other.

**11. (D)** HCF takes each COMMON prime to its LOWEST power: from 2² × 3 and 2 × 3², the lowest powers are 2¹ and 3¹, giving 2 × 3 = 6. (36 uses highest powers — that's the LCM; 12 keeps 2² instead of the lower 2¹.)

**12. (C)** LCM takes every prime to its HIGHEST power: 2² (from 12) and 3² (from 18), giving 2² × 3² = 36. (6 uses lowest powers — that's the HCF; 216 multiplies the two numbers.)

**13. (B)** HCF × LCM = product of the two numbers: 6 × 72 = 432. Other number = 432 ÷ 24 = 18. Check: HCF(24, 18) = 6 and LCM(24, 18) = 72. ✓

**14. (C)** Convert to cm: 700, 385, 1295. The longest exact measuring length is HCF(700, 385, 1295). 700 = 2²×5²×7, 385 = 5×7×11, 1295 = 5×7×37. Common: 5 × 7 = 35 cm. ✓

**15. (A)** The HCF divides the smaller number, so it can never exceed it — always true. (B) is false (the LCM is usually larger than both); (C) fails for e.g. 12 and 16 (HCF 4 is neither); (D) holds only for co-prime pairs.

**16. (D)** 18 = 2×3², 24 = 2³×3, 30 = 2×3×5. Common primes to lowest power: 2 × 3 = 6.

**17. (A)** 4 = 2², 6 = 2×3, 8 = 2³. LCM takes highest powers: 2³ × 3 = 24. Check: 24 ÷ 4, ÷6, ÷8 are all whole. ✓

**18. (B)** All three change together at LCM(24, 36, 54). 24 = 2³×3, 36 = 2²×3², 54 = 2×3³. Highest powers: 2³ × 3³ = 8 × 27 = 216 s. (6 s is the HCF — the trap of dividing instead.)

**19. (C)** "Divides both leaving remainder 0" means a common factor; the greatest is HCF(70, 125). 70 = 2×5×7, 125 = 5³. Common: 5.

**20. (D)** Numbers in ratio 3 : 4 can be written 3k and 4k. Since 3 and 4 are co-prime, their HCF is k. Given HCF = 5, take k = 5: the numbers are 15 and 20. Check: HCF(15, 20) = 5 and 15 : 20 = 3 : 4. ✓ (30 and 40 have HCF 10, not 5; 3 and 4 have HCF 1.)

**21. (A)** HCF × LCM = product, so HCF = (15 × 20) ÷ LCM = 300 ÷ 60 = 5. (300 forgets to divide.)

**22. (B)** Common multiples of 4 and 6 are multiples of LCM(4, 6) = 12: 12, 24, 36, 48… 18 is a multiple of 6 but not of 4, so it is NOT a common multiple. (12, 24, 36 all are.)

**23. (C)** Common factors of two numbers are exactly the factors of their HCF. HCF(24, 36) = 12, and 12 has factors 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12 — that is 6 common factors.

**24. (D)** The gears realign after a number of teeth that is a multiple of both 8 and 12 — the LCM. 8 = 2³, 12 = 2²×3, LCM = 2³×3 = 24 teeth. (96 = 8 × 12 overshoots.)

**25. (A)** HCF = 1 means the numbers are co-prime, and for co-prime numbers LCM = product. (B) is false — 8 and 15 are co-prime yet neither is prime; (C) and (D) are false (equal numbers share a factor > 1, and if one divides the other the HCF is the smaller, not 1).

**26. (B)** Euclid: 2346 = 1×1794 + 552; 1794 = 3×552 + 138; 552 = 4×138 + 0. So HCF = 138. (138 = 2 × 3 × 23.) Listing factors here would be hopeless — that's the point of Euclid's method.

**27. (D)** Smallest number divisible by 1–6 = LCM(1,2,3,4,5,6). Highest powers among them: 2² (from 4), 3 (from 3 or 6), 5 (from 5): 4 × 3 × 5 = 60. (120 and 720 are larger common multiples, not the LEAST.)

**28. (B)** Greatest equal piece length = HCF(36, 48). 36 = 2²×3², 48 = 2⁴×3, HCF = 2²×3 = 12 cm. (24 divides 48 but not 36.)

**29. (C)** With 12 cm pieces: 36 ÷ 12 + 48 ÷ 12 = 3 + 4 = 7 pieces.

**30. (A)** If h divides both a and b, then it divides a + b and a − b (since each is a sum/difference of multiples of h). This is exactly why Euclid's algorithm works.

**31. (C)** 360 = 2 × 180 = 2² × 90 = 2³ × 45 = 2³ × 9 × 5 = 2³ × 3² × 5. Check: 8 × 9 × 5 = 360. ✓ (2³×3×5 = 120 and 2²×3²×5 = 180 are wrong prime powers.)

**32. (D)** HCF takes common primes to the lowest power. Both have 2 (powers 2 and 1 → take 2¹) and 3 (powers 1 and 2 → take 3¹); 5 and 7 are not common. HCF = 2 × 3 = 6. (1260 is the LCM; 210 wrongly includes the non-common 5 and 7.)

**33. (B)** LCM takes every prime to its highest power: 2² (from the first), 3² (from the second), and 5 and 7 once each: 2² × 3² × 5 × 7 = 4 × 9 × 35 = 1260. (6 is the HCF.)

**34. (A)** A number leaving the same remainder when dividing 43, 91, 183 must divide their differences exactly: 91 − 43 = 48, 183 − 91 = 92, 183 − 43 = 140. HCF(48, 92, 140): 48 = 2⁴×3, 92 = 2²×23, 140 = 2²×5×7 → common 2² = 4. Check: 43 = 4×10 + 3, 91 = 4×22 + 3, 183 = 4×45 + 3. ✓

**35. (C)** Largest equal bag size (one type per bag) = HCF(84, 108). 84 = 2²×3×7, 108 = 2²×3³, HCF = 2²×3 = 12 kg. (24 divides neither exactly.)

**36. (D)** With 12 kg bags: 84 ÷ 12 + 108 ÷ 12 = 7 + 9 = 16 bags.

**37. (C)** The LCM equals the larger number exactly when the smaller number divides the larger. Testing each: 6 & 8 → LCM 24; 4 & 6 → LCM 12; 9 & 15 → LCM 45; but 6 & 12 → 6 divides 12, so LCM = 12 = the larger number. Hence (C) 6 and 12.

**38. (B)** If a is a factor of b, then a divides b, so the largest common factor is a itself (HCF = a) and the smallest common multiple is b itself (LCM = b). E.g. a = 4, b = 12: HCF 4, LCM 12.

**39. (D)** Next together after LCM(18, 24) minutes. 18 = 2×3², 24 = 2³×3, LCM = 2³×3² = 72 min = 1 h 12 min. 9:00 + 1:12 = 10:12 am. (9:42 adds only 42 min; 11:00 overshoots.)

**40. (B)** Remainder 5 each time means (number − 5) is divisible by 12, 15, 20 → it is a multiple of LCM(12,15,20). 12 = 2²×3, 15 = 3×5, 20 = 2²×5 → LCM = 2²×3×5 = 60. Smallest number = 60 + 5 = 65. (60 forgets to add the remainder.)

**41. (A)** Two consecutive whole numbers share no common factor greater than 1 (any common divisor would divide their difference 1), so their HCF is always 1 — they are always co-prime.

**42. (D)** Let HCF = h; LCM = 12h. h + 12h = 403 → 13h = 403 → h = 31, LCM = 372. HCF × LCM = product = 31 × 372 = 11532. Other number = 11532 ÷ 93 = 124. Check: HCF(93, 124) = 31, LCM = 372. ✓

**43. (A)** Must be divisible by LCM(8, 12, 18). 8 = 2³, 12 = 2²×3, 18 = 2×3² → LCM = 2³×3² = 72. Smallest 4-digit multiple of 72: 1000 ÷ 72 ≈ 13.9, so 14 × 72 = 1008. ✓ (1080 = 15×72 is larger; 1000 isn't a multiple of 72.)

**44. (C)** Each bouquet gets an equal share of both, so the number of bouquets must divide 24 and 36; the largest is HCF(24, 36) = 12. ✓

**45. (B)** With 12 bouquets: 24 ÷ 12 = 2 roses and 36 ÷ 12 = 3 tulips each.

**46. (A)** HCF × LCM = product: 6 × 180 = 1080. Other number = 1080 ÷ 36 = 30. Check: HCF(36, 30) = 6, LCM = 180. ✓

**47. (C)** The HCF must divide the LCM for a valid pair. (A) 4 | 24 ✓; (B) 6 | 90 ✓; (D) 5 | 60 ✓; (C) 8 ∤ 60 (60 ÷ 8 isn't whole) — impossible. So (C) cannot occur.

**48. (B)** Greatest equal piece length = HCF(144, 180, 192). 144 = 2⁴×3², 180 = 2²×3²×5, 192 = 2⁶×3 → common 2²×3 = 12 cm. Pieces = 144/12 + 180/12 + 192/12 = 12 + 15 + 16 = 43.

**49. (D)** 12 and 35 are co-prime (12 = 2²×3, 35 = 5×7 share nothing), so LCM = product = 12 × 35 = 420. (7 is just a factor; 105 = 3×5×7 misses the 2²; 60 ignores 35.)

**50. (C)** Divisible by 6, 9 and 15 means a multiple of LCM(6, 9, 15). 6 = 2×3, 9 = 3², 15 = 3×5 → LCM = 2×3²×5 = 90. Multiples of 90 between 100 and 200: 180. (90 is below 100; 135 is divisible by 9 and 15 but not 6.)

**51. (B)** All back together after LCM(4, 6, 9). 4 = 2², 6 = 2×3, 9 = 3² → LCM = 2²×3² = 36 min. (18 is a multiple of 6 and 9 but not 4.)

**52. (A)** Test HCF with 18 = 2 × 3² for each option. 24 = 2³ × 3 → common primes to lowest power 2¹ × 3¹ = 6 ✓. The others fail: HCF(15, 18) = 3, HCF(18, 18) = 18, HCF(27, 18) = 9. Only 24 gives HCF 6.

**53. (C)** Product of HCF and LCM equals the product of the numbers: 16 × 20 = 320. (No need to find HCF and LCM separately — the identity gives it directly. HCF(16,20) = 4, LCM = 80, and 4 × 80 = 320. ✓)

**54. (D)** Each child gets equal sweets and equal chocolates, so the number of children divides both 25 and 35; the greatest is HCF(25, 35) = 5. (Each child then gets 5 sweets and 7 chocolates.)

**55. (B)** HCF = product ÷ LCM = 2400 ÷ 120 = 20. Check: HCF must divide LCM, and 20 | 120. ✓

**56. (A)** number + 7 is divisible by 8, 12, 16 → a multiple of LCM(8,12,16) = 2⁴×3 = 48. Smallest such: 48, so number = 48 − 7 = 41. Check: 41 + 7 = 48 ÷ 8, ÷12, ÷16 all whole. ✓

**57. (D)** Leave together at common multiples of 15 and 25 → LCM(15, 25). 15 = 3×5, 25 = 5² → LCM = 3×5² = 75 min = 1 h 15 min. 8:00 + 1:15 = 9:15 am. (8:40 adds only 40 min; 9:30 = 90 min overshoots.)

**58. (C)** Convert: 525 cm × 378 cm. Largest square tile side = HCF(525, 378). 525 = 3×5²×7, 378 = 2×3³×7 → common 3 × 7 = 21 cm. (12, 9 don't divide 525.)

**59. (A)** With 21 cm tiles: (525 ÷ 21) × (378 ÷ 21) = 25 × 18 = 450 tiles.

**60. (D)** HCF = 13 means both numbers are 13a and 13b with a, b co-prime; LCM = 13ab = 1989 → ab = 153 = 9 × 17 = 3² × 17. Co-prime factor pairs (a, b) of 153 with a < b: (1, 153) and (9, 17). That gives 2 pairs: (13, 1989) and (117, 221). So 2 such pairs. ✓
