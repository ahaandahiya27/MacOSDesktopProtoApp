Worked Solutions

1. (B) Tens = 50, tenths = 0.5, thousandths = 0.005, with zero ones and zero hundredths: 50 + 0.5 + 0.005 = 50.505. Putting all three fives next to each other (55.55) ignores the empty ones and hundredths places. Writing 50.055 mistakenly moves the 5 from the tenths place into the hundredths place. Writing 5.505 puts the leading 5 in the ones place instead of the tens place.

2. (B) Tenths digit 4 goes one place after the point; the 9 is thousandths, so the hundredths place is empty (0): 3.409. Writing 3.49 wrongly puts the 9 in the hundredths place. 3.0409 leaves the tenths place empty and shifts everything one place too far right. 3.4009 pushes the 9 into the ten-thousandths place, one place too far.

3. (B) Multiplying 4.8 by 1000 moves the point three places right to 4800; dividing 4800 by 100 moves it two places left to 48. Net shift is one place right, so 4.8 becomes 48. Getting 4.8 assumes the two operations cancel, but +3 then -2 leaves +1. 480 forgets the divide step. 0.48 moves the point the wrong way.

4. (D) Compare place by place. All start 0.6 vs 0.59.., so the 0.5.. numbers lose at the tenths place. Among 0.6 (=0.6000) and 0.601, the hundredths are 0 vs 0, then thousandths 0 vs 1, so 0.601 wins. 0.5999 only looks big because it has the most digits, but its tenths digit 5 is already smaller than 6. 0.6 ties 0.601 until the thousandths place, where it loses. 0.59 is the smallest.

5. (B) Work left to right: 10 - 3.45 = 6.55; 6.55 - 2.8 = 3.75; 3.75 - 0.05 = 3.7. Stopping at 3.75 forgets the last subtraction. 3.6 comes from a wrong borrow in 6.55 - 2.8. 4.7 comes from misaligning 2.8 as 0.28.

6. (C) 0.007 is 7 thousandths = 7/1000, and the other is 7 ones. The ratio is (7/1000) / 7 = 1/1000. Choosing 1/100 confuses thousandths with hundredths. 1/10 confuses thousandths with tenths. 1/700 wrongly divides 1 by 7 times 100.

7. (A) The 5 is in the tenths place (0.5) and the 3 is in the thousandths place (0.003). 0.5 - 0.003 = 0.497. Choosing 0.503 reads the two digits together as one number. 0.2 subtracts the digits 5 and 3 as 5 - 3 then misplaces it. 0.5 ignores the 3 entirely.

8. (D) Halfway between 2.6 and 2.8 is their average: (2.6 + 2.8) / 2 = 5.4 / 2 = 2.7. Choosing 2.65 finds the midpoint of 2.6 and 2.7 instead of 2.6 and 2.8. 2.74 is not centred. 2.07 misreads the place of the 7.

9. (C) Tenths first: 0.55 and 0.5 and 0.505 all have tenths 5, but 0.055 has tenths 0, so it is smallest. Among the rest, hundredths: 0.55 has 5, while 0.5 (=0.50) and 0.505 have 0, so 0.55 is largest. Between 0.5 and 0.505 the thousandths decide: 0.505 beats 0.50. Order: 0.55, 0.505, 0.5, 0.055. Putting 0.505 before 0.55 wrongly reads more digits as bigger.

10. (A) 0.625 = 625/1000. Divide top and bottom by 125: 625/125 = 5 and 1000/125 = 8, so 5/8. Writing 625/100 mistakes the place value of the last digit. 5/80 over-cancels. The point form is not a fraction.

11. (A) Left to right: 6.5 - 2.78 = 3.72; 3.72 + 1.9 = 5.62; 5.62 - 0.62 = 5.0. Stopping at 5.62 forgets the last subtraction. 5.32 comes from dropping the 1.9 - 0.62 pair into a single wrong step. 4.0 mishandles the first borrow.

12. (C) Seventy thousandths means 70/1000 = 0.070, so the number is 40 + 0.070 = 40.070 (equal to 40.07). Writing 40.7 makes it seventy tenths, far too big. 40.0070 reads it as seventy ten-thousandths. 40.7000 again places the 7 in the tenths slot.

13. (D) 0.0625 = 625/10000. Dividing top and bottom by 625 gives 1/16. Writing 625/1000 misreads the last digit as thousandths. 1/160 over-cancels by a factor of 10. 5/8 is the value of 0.625, ten times larger.

14. (B) Multiplying by 1000 moves the point three places right: 2.5 becomes 2500. So 2.5 m = 2500 mm. 250 mm moves the point only two places (that is centimetres). 25000 mm moves it four places. 25 mm moves it only one place.

15. (C) Total cut = 1.45 + 0.8 + 1.25 + 0.7 = 4.2 m. Left = 6 - 4.2 = 1.8 m. Getting 1.7 comes from a wrong carry while adding the four pieces. 2.5 comes from leaving out the 0.7 piece. 1.85 misaligns 0.8 as 0.08.

16. (A) Distances from 3.5: |3.46 - 3.5| = 0.04, |3.53 - 3.5| = 0.03, |3.6 - 3.5| = 0.1, |3.4 - 3.5| = 0.1. The smallest gap, 0.03, belongs to 3.53. Picking 3.46 thinks 'fewer hundredths below' is closer, but 0.04 beats 0.03 only if you misjudge. 3.6 and 3.4 are each a tenth away.

17. (D) First add: 7.249 + 0.06 = 7.309. To the nearest tenth, look at the hundredths digit 0... wait, 7.309 has hundredths 0, so it rounds DOWN? Re-check: 7.309 - the tenths digit is 3, hundredths is 0, so it rounds to 7.3. Choosing 7.2 rounds before adding the 0.06. 7.31 rounds to hundredths, not tenths. 7.25 adds wrongly.

18. (D) Tenths first: 1.02 and 1.022 have tenths 0; 1.2 and 1.202 have tenths 2, so the two 1.0.. numbers come first. Between 1.02 (=1.020) and 1.022, thousandths 0 vs 2 puts 1.02 below 1.022. Between 1.2 (=1.200) and 1.202, thousandths 0 vs 2 puts 1.2 below 1.202. Final: 1.02, 1.022, 1.2, 1.202. Placing 1.202 before 1.2 reads more digits as bigger.

19. (D) 0.0375 = 375/10000. Divide top and bottom by 125: 375/125 = 3 and 10000/125 = 80, giving 3/80. Writing 375/1000 misreads the place by one. 3/8 is the value of 0.375, ten times bigger. 375/10000 is correct but not in simplest form.

20. (B) 2.5 L = 2500 mL (point three places right). Shared among 5 glasses: 2500 / 5 = 500 mL each. Getting 50 mL divides 250 by 5 (only moving the point two places when converting). 5000 mL forgets to divide. 250 mL divides 1250 by 5.

21. (A) Start 5.01. First step 5.00, second 4.99, third 4.98, fourth 4.97. Stopping at 4.98 counts only three steps. 4.61 subtracts tenths instead of hundredths. 5.05 counts forward.

22. (A) If x / 100 = 0.039, then x = 0.039 multiplied by 100, moving the point two places right: 3.9. Getting 0.39 moves the point only one place. 39 moves it three places. 0.0039 moves it the wrong direction.

23. (D) 8.605 has 605 in the thousandths group (six tenths, zero hundredths, five thousandths) = 605/1000, read 'six hundred five thousandths'. Saying 'sixty-five hundredths' drops the interior zero and shrinks the number to 8.65. Saying 'six hundred five hundredths' names the wrong place. 'Six hundred five' alone has no place named.

24. (C) Left to right: 100 - 27.65 = 72.35; 72.35 - 8.9 = 63.45; 63.45 - 1.05 = 62.4. Stopping at 63.45 forgets the last subtraction. 62.5 mishandles the 8.9 borrow. 62.45 misaligns 1.05.

25. (B) A number rounds to 4.3 only if it lies from 4.25 up to (but not including) 4.35. 4.36 is past 4.35, so it rounds up to 4.4 instead. The other three: 4.25 rounds up to 4.3, 4.34 rounds to 4.3, and 4.27 rounds to 4.3, so they all give 4.3.

26. (D) Tenths is two places to the left of thousandths. Each step left multiplies by 10, so two steps multiply by 10 times 10 = 100. Choosing 10 counts only one step. 1000 counts three steps. 1/100 moves in the wrong direction (right, not left).

27. (D) 2 pens = 2 x 8.75 = 17.50; 3 pencils = 3 x 4.50 = 13.50; total = 31.00. Change = 100 - 31.00 = 69.00 rupees. Getting 82.75 buys only one of each. 68.00 mis-adds the pencils as 14.50. 69.50 mis-adds the pens as 16.50.

28. (C) 40 = 8 x 5; multiply top and bottom to make a power of ten: 7/40 = 175/1000 = 0.175. (Check: 7 divided by 40 = 0.175.) Writing 0.74 just rearranges the digits. 1.75 is ten times too large (that is 7/4). 0.1750 is correct in value but has an unneeded trailing zero, so it is not simplest.

29. (D) 0.85 + 0.35 = 1.2; then 1.2 - 0.6 = 0.6. Stopping at 1.2 forgets the jump back. 0.55 mis-adds the first jump. 0.66 misaligns the 0.6 as 0.06.

30. (D) Largest: compare tenths (9,9,0,9) drops 0.099; among 0.909, 0.99, 0.9 the hundredths (0,9,0) make 0.99 biggest. Smallest is 0.099. Difference = 0.99 - 0.099 = 0.891. Getting 0.81 subtracts 0.99 - 0.18 by mis-reading. 0.9 ignores the 0.099 digits. 0.099 reports the small value itself.

31. (B) 0.009 / 0.9 = 9/1000 divided by 9/10 = (9/1000) x (10/9) = 10/1000 = 1/100. Choosing 1/10 counts only one place gap. 1/1000 counts three. 1/9 wrongly divides the digits.

32. (A) Tenths: 0.088 has 0, the rest have 8, so 0.088 is smallest. Among 0.8 (=0.800), 0.808, 0.88, hundredths are 0, 0, 8, so 0.88 is largest. Between 0.800 and 0.808, thousandths 0 vs 8 put 0.8 below 0.808. Order: 0.088, 0.8, 0.808, 0.88. Swapping 0.88 and 0.808 reads more digits as bigger.

35. (B) 12.4 + 11.85 = 24.25; 24.25 + 12.05 = 36.30; 36.30 + 11.7 = 48.0 s. Getting 47.9 drops a carry in the hundredths. 48.1 over-carries. 47.0 mis-adds the whole-second parts.

54. (A) Thousandths is two places to the RIGHT of tenths; each step right divides by 10, so two steps give 1/100. Choosing 1/10 counts one step. 1/1000 counts three. 100 moves the wrong way (left).
