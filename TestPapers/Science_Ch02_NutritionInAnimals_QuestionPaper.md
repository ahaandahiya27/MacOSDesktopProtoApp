# Olympiad Test Paper — Science (Class 7)
## Chapter 2: Nutrition in Animals

|---|---|

| **Maximum Marks** | 240 |

**Marking scheme:** **+4** for each correct answer · **−1** for each wrong answer · **0** if unattempted.

**Instructions:** Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. All questions stay within the Class-7 chapter on Nutrition in Animals — the five steps of nutrition, the human digestive system (mouth to anus, plus liver and pancreas), feeding in Amoeba, and rumination in cows. Most questions need two to four reasoning steps; watch for traps built from common misconceptions. No diagram is printed — where a structure matters, it is described in words. Do not write on this paper — mark answers on your answer sheet.

---

1. The five steps of nutrition occur in a fixed order. Which sequence is correct?
   (A) Ingestion → Absorption → Digestion → Assimilation → Egestion
   (B) Digestion → Ingestion → Absorption → Egestion → Assimilation
   (C) Ingestion → Digestion → Absorption → Assimilation → Egestion
   (D) Ingestion → Digestion → Assimilation → Absorption → Egestion

2. A student says, "Once I swallow food, my body can use it straight away." The flaw in this statement is that the food must still be:
   (A) ingested only
   (B) digested and absorbed before cells can use it
   (C) egested before it can be used
   (D) assimilated before it is digested

3. Which step of nutrition actually delivers nutrients **into the blood**?
   (A) digestion
   (B) egestion
   (C) assimilation
   (D) absorption

4. "Assimilation" is best described as the step in which:
   (A) absorbed nutrients are used by cells for energy, growth, and repair
   (B) complex food is broken into simple molecules
   (C) food is taken into the mouth
   (D) undigested waste leaves the body

5. Removal of **undigested** food from the body is called egestion. This is different from excretion because excretion removes:
   (A) the same undigested food, just by another name
   (B) waste produced inside cells (such as during energy release)
   (C) water only
   (D) bacteria from the gut

6. Why can't a single molecule of starch from a roti simply pass straight into your blood the moment you swallow?
   (A) because starch is poisonous until cooked
   (B) because the starch molecule is too large until enzymes break it into simple sugar
   (C) because the blood does not touch the stomach
   (D) because starch is absorbed only in the large intestine

7. A butterfly sipping nectar and a snake swallowing a whole egg differ mainly in their step of:
   (A) absorption
   (B) assimilation
   (C) egestion
   (D) ingestion

8. An Amoeba pushes out finger-like extensions to surround a food particle. These extensions are called:
   (A) villi
   (B) cilia
   (C) flagella
   (D) pseudopodia

9. A housefly cannot chew. It pours digestive juice onto solid food and then sucks up the liquid. This shows that, in the housefly:
   (A) no digestion occurs at all
   (B) absorption happens before ingestion
   (C) some digestion begins **outside** the body before ingestion is complete
   (D) egestion happens first

10. A mosquito's mouthparts and a cow's flat-topped teeth are different because:
    (A) mosquitoes are larger than cows
    (B) the kind of food an animal eats shapes its feeding tools
    (C) all insects suck and all mammals grind, with no exceptions
    (D) cows do not need to feed

11. An adult human has 32 teeth. Which set is used mainly for **biting and cutting** food?
    (A) incisors
    (B) canines
    (C) premolars
    (D) molars

12. A carnivore such as a dog has long, pointed teeth on each side of its front teeth, well suited to **tearing** flesh. These are the:
    (A) incisors
    (B) molars
    (C) canines
    (D) premolars

13. The hardest substance in the human body, which forms the outer covering of the crown of a tooth, is:
    (A) dentine
    (B) pulp
    (C) enamel
    (D) bone

14. The central part of a tooth, which contains nerves and blood vessels, is the:
    (A) crown
    (B) enamel
    (C) root
    (D) pulp cavity

15. Tooth decay begins when bacteria act on left-over sugar and release acid that attacks the enamel. The best single reason to brush after sweets is therefore that brushing:
    (A) makes the teeth whiter only
    (B) replaces lost enamel
    (C) removes the sugar before bacteria can turn it into enamel-attacking acid
    (D) kills the tongue's taste buds

16. Counting an adult's grinding teeth: there are 8 premolars and 12 molars. The total number of grinding teeth is:
    (A) 12
    (B) 32
    (C) 8
    (D) 20

17. Saliva does more than wet the food. The enzyme in saliva begins the digestion of:
    (A) starch
    (B) fats
    (C) proteins
    (D) water

18. If you chew a plain piece of bread for a long time without swallowing, it slowly tastes sweeter. This is because:
    (A) the bread absorbs sugar from the tongue
    (B) salivary amylase breaks starch into simpler sugars
    (C) the acid in saliva caramelises the bread
    (D) bacteria add sugar to the bread

19. Three pairs of glands in the mouth release saliva. Apart from starting starch digestion, saliva also helps by:
    (A) absorbing nutrients into the blood
    (B) storing undigested waste
    (C) producing hydrochloric acid
    (D) moistening food so it can be swallowed easily

20. After you swallow, food is pushed down the food pipe by a wave-like squeezing of its muscular wall. This movement is called:
    (A) peristalsis
    (B) rumination
    (C) emulsification
    (D) absorption

21. A person lying flat on their back can still swallow water and it reaches the stomach. The reason peristalsis can move food even against gravity is that:
    (A) gravity always helps swallowing
    (B) muscles of the food pipe contract in a sequence that pushes food forward
    (C) the food pipe is a straight vertical drop
    (D) water is lighter than air

22. The food pipe (oesophagus) carries food from the mouth region to the:
    (A) stomach
    (B) liver
    (C) small intestine
    (D) large intestine

23. The stomach is a J-shaped muscular bag. Its strong walls help digestion mainly by:
    (A) absorbing all nutrients into the blood
    (B) reabsorbing water from waste
    (C) producing bile
    (D) churning the food and mixing it thoroughly with digestive juices

24. The gastric juice of the stomach contains hydrochloric acid, mucus, and a protein-digesting enzyme called:
    (A) amylase
    (B) pepsin
    (C) bile
    (D) trypsin

25. Pepsin works best in a strongly acidic setting. The hydrochloric acid in the stomach therefore has the double job of killing germs in food **and**:
    (A) cooling the food
    (B) breaking down fats into droplets
    (C) creating the acidic conditions that let pepsin act
    (D) absorbing sugar

26. The stomach is itself made largely of protein, yet pepsin and acid do not normally digest the stomach wall. The protection comes from:
    (A) bile coating the stomach
    (B) a layer of mucus lining the inner wall
    (C) the wall being made of bone
    (D) the wall having no proteins at all

27. The thick, semi-liquid paste of partly digested food that the stomach releases into the small intestine is called:
    (A) chyme
    (B) bile
    (C) cud
    (D) saliva

28. In which order does food pass through these three organs?
    (A) stomach → oesophagus → small intestine
    (B) small intestine → stomach → oesophagus
    (C) oesophagus → stomach → small intestine
    (D) oesophagus → small intestine → stomach

29. The small intestine is the **longest** part of the food canal (about 6 metres in an adult). The single biggest reason it is so long and coiled is to:
    (A) store undigested food
    (B) provide a large surface and enough time to digest food and absorb nutrients
    (C) make hydrochloric acid
    (D) churn food the way the stomach does

30. Two secretions empty into the first part of the small intestine to complete digestion: bile from the liver and a juice rich in enzymes from the:
    (A) gall bladder
    (B) stomach
    (C) salivary glands
    (D) pancreas

31. The inner wall of the small intestine is covered with millions of tiny finger-like projections. Their main purpose is to:
    (A) grind the food
    (B) greatly increase the surface area for absorption
    (C) make the intestine acidic
    (D) store bile

32. These finger-like projections of the small-intestine wall are called:
    (A) cilia
    (B) villi
    (C) pseudopodia
    (D) sphincters

33. Inside each villus are tiny blood vessels. This tells us that, right at the villus, the step taking place is:
    (A) ingestion
    (B) digestion of protein
    (C) absorption of nutrients into the blood
    (D) egestion

34. Suppose a disease flattened the villi so the inner wall of the small intestine became smooth. The most direct result would be:
    (A) faster digestion of starch in the mouth
    (B) poorer absorption of nutrients, so the person stays undernourished even while eating
    (C) more bile produced
    (D) stronger churning by the stomach

35. The large intestine is shorter and wider than the small intestine. Its chief job is to:
    (A) digest proteins
    (B) absorb digested sugars into the blood
    (C) produce pepsin
    (D) absorb water from the undigested residue

36. If the large intestine absorbs **too much** water from the waste, the likely result is:
    (A) hard, dry stools that are difficult to egest (constipation)
    (B) loose, watery stools
    (C) vomiting of acid
    (D) faster digestion of fats

37. The undigested, solid waste is stored for a short time and then removed through the anus. This final step is:
    (A) absorption
    (B) assimilation
    (C) egestion
    (D) ingestion

38. The largest gland in the human body, which makes bile, is the:
    (A) pancreas
    (B) salivary gland
    (C) liver
    (D) gall bladder

39. Bile is stored, between meals, in a small sac called the:
    (A) rumen
    (B) duodenum
    (C) gall bladder
    (D) pancreas

40. Bile contains **no digestive enzyme**, yet it greatly helps the digestion of fats. It does this by:
    (A) chemically breaking fat into amino acids
    (B) turning fat into starch
    (C) absorbing the fat into the blood directly
    (D) breaking large fat globules into tiny droplets so enzymes can act on them more easily

41. A patient whose gall bladder has been removed often finds **fatty** meals hardest to handle. This fits the idea that bile mainly assists the digestion of:
    (A) fats
    (B) starch
    (C) proteins
    (D) water

42. The pancreatic juice contains enzymes that act on carbohydrates, proteins, **and** fats. This makes the pancreas special because most other digestive juices:
    (A) only absorb water
    (B) contain no enzymes
    (C) are made in the mouth
    (D) act on only one or two food types

43. Cows, buffaloes, goats and deer are grouped together as **ruminants** chiefly because they:
    (A) all eat meat
    (B) digest food inside a single cell
    (C) have only one stomach chamber
    (D) swallow grass quickly and later return it to the mouth to chew again

44. The partly digested grass that a cow brings back up to chew slowly while resting is called:
    (A) cud
    (B) bile
    (C) chyme
    (D) mucus

45. A ruminant's stomach has four chambers. The largest chamber, where microbes start breaking down the grass, is the:
    (A) abomasum
    (B) rumen
    (C) reticulum
    (D) omasum

46. Grass is rich in cellulose. Cows can live on grass even though, like humans, they make no enzyme to digest cellulose. They manage because:
    (A) they cook the grass first
    (B) cellulose needs no digestion
    (C) microbes living in the rumen break the cellulose down for them
    (D) their saliva contains cellulase

47. A human eats the same grass and gets almost nothing from it, but a cow thrives on it. The best single reason is that the human:
    (A) lacks both the rumen microbes and the re-chewing system that break down cellulose
    (B) has no stomach
    (C) cannot swallow grass
    (D) digests grass in the mouth

48. In which chamber of a ruminant's stomach does acid-and-enzyme digestion happen, much like in a human stomach?
    (A) rumen
    (B) reticulum
    (C) abomasum
    (D) omasum

49. Re-chewing the cud is useful because grinding the grass more finely:
    (A) makes the grass taste sweeter
    (B) turns cellulose into protein instantly
    (C) removes the need for any stomach
    (D) exposes more surface for microbes and enzymes to act on

50. Amoeba is a single-celled animal. It captures its food by surrounding it with pseudopodia. The food then ends up trapped inside a:
    (A) villus
    (B) gall bladder
    (C) food vacuole
    (D) rumen

51. Inside the Amoeba, digestive enzymes are released **into the food vacuole**. This is comparable, in a human, to enzymes acting:
    (A) inside the cavity of the stomach and intestine
    (B) inside the brain
    (C) on the skin surface
    (D) in the blood only

52. Which statement correctly compares nutrition in Amoeba and in a human?
    (A) only the human carries out digestion; Amoeba does not
    (B) the human takes in food but never assimilates it
    (C) Amoeba absorbs food but never egests
    (D) both carry out all five steps, but Amoeba does them within one cell while a human uses many organs

53. The way an Amoeba engulfs a food particle with its pseudopodia is most similar to which everyday image?
    (A) a hand cupping and wrapping around a marble
    (B) a straw sucking up juice
    (C) a sieve straining water
    (D) a knife cutting bread

54. After an Amoeba has digested and absorbed the useful part of its meal, the leftover undigested matter is:
    (A) thrown out when the vacuole reaches the cell surface (egestion)
    (B) kept forever inside the cell
    (C) turned into a new pseudopodium
    (D) passed to a neighbouring cell

55. A pseudopodium is a "temporary" structure, unlike a human's stomach. The key difference this points to is that Amoeba:
    (A) has permanent organs for each step
    (B) makes structures as needed rather than having fixed organs
    (C) cannot move at all
    (D) digests food outside its body

56. The glucose absorbed from your food is finally "burnt" inside your cells to release energy. The tiny structures in the cell where this energy release mainly happens are the:
    (A) villi
    (B) mitochondria
    (C) food vacuoles
    (D) salivary glands

    (A) ingestion
    (B) digestion
    (C) egestion
    (D) assimilation

58. A person eats well but their digestion (in the gut) works fine while their cells cannot use glucose properly. The step that is failing here is:
    (A) assimilation
    (B) digestion
    (C) absorption
    (D) ingestion

59. Trace one mouthful of rice through the body. In which organ is its **starch digestion first started** and in which is the resulting sugar **mainly absorbed**?
    (A) started in the mouth; absorbed in the small intestine
    (B) started in the stomach; absorbed in the large intestine
    (C) started in the small intestine; absorbed in the stomach

    (A) protein broken down in the large intestine; absorbed in the stomach
    (B) protein broken down only in the mouth; absorbed in the large intestine
