## Worked Solutions

**1. (C)** The gloss ईश्वरेण व्याप्तम् puts ईश्वर in the instrumental (-एण), marking the agent BY WHOM the pervading is done; इदं सर्वम् is the thing pervaded. So the Lord pervades all, not the reverse. The reading that the Lord is the object reverses agent and patient. Treating the instrumental as a place (locative sense) or as a recipient (dative sense) mistakes the case entirely.

**2. (A)** सर्वम् means 'all / the whole', and the verse says all this is pervaded by the Lord; the chapter draws the ethical lesson that one should therefore treat the world with reverence, not grab it greedily. The 'half' reading mistranslates सर्वम्. इदम् means 'this' (the present world), not 'the next world'. The verse is not temple-restricted — its whole point is everywhere-pervasion.

**3. (D)** ईशावास्यम् इदं सर्वम् teaches that the Lord pervades ALL this, so reverence is owed everywhere, not only to consecrated idols. The 'only idols' option re-imposes the very confinement the verse denies. 'Sky-only' contradicts the all-pervasion. 'Neither matters' inverts the lesson from reverence to dismissal.

**4. (B)** The chapter shows that getting the verse's meaning depends on parsing it right — knowing ईश is 'Lord' (root ईश्) and सर्वम् is 'all'. So grammar serves comprehension. The verse is a revered, well-formed Upanishadic line, not erroneous. It is meaning-rich, not meaningless. The pairing is deliberate, not accidental.

**5. (C)** इदम् ('this') + सर्वम् ('all') + ईशावास्यम् ('pervaded by the Lord') together mean 'all this is pervaded/enveloped by the Lord'. The 'single thing' reading drops सर्वम् ('all'). 'Abandoned' and 'no connection' both reverse the verse's claim of intimate, total presence.

**6. (A)** The chapter draws a reverence-and-care lesson FROM all-pervasion: if the Lord is in everything, everything deserves respect. 'Removes all duty' inverts this into licence. 'Only in temples' contradicts the verse's everywhere-claim. 'World is unreal' is not the chapter's lesson — the lesson is responsible reverence, not nihilism.

**7. (A)** The root ईश् means 'to rule, to own', so derivatives ईश and ईश्वर name the RULER/owner — the Lord. 'One who is ruled' reverses agent and patient. 'One who eats' uses an unrelated root. 'A palace' would be a place-noun, not the agent-sense both these words carry.

**8. (D)** ईश is an independent Sanskrit word meaning 'Lord', from the root ईश् ('to rule'); English '-ish' is a suffix meaning 'resembling/somewhat'. The resemblance is purely phonetic. 'Both mean somewhat-like' is false — ईश names the Supreme. ईश is a noun, not a suffix. '-ish' has Germanic, not Sanskrit, origin.

**9. (C)** The root ईश् 'to rule' yields ईश and ईश्वर, both meaning the ruling Lord. शृगाल (fox), घट (pot) and their supposed chains are unrelated to ईश्. ईश does not mean 'student' — that would be छात्र; ईश is the Lord, the ruler.

**10. (B)** ईश means 'Lord' (root ईश्), and the fox is शृगालः — so calling ईश a fox is the first error. वास्य(म्) carries the 'to be pervaded/dwelt-in' sense (cf. gloss व्याप्तम्), so dropping it is the second. The 'reading is fine' option ignores both errors. Reading ईश as 'fox' is never correct.

**11. (A)** ईश्वर is derived from the root ईश् ('to rule'), and the Lord is the one who rules all — so the first sentence is internally consistent. 'From the word for fox' and 'from the root for pot' invent false etymologies. Calling ईश्वर an English suffix repeats the '-ish' error; it is a Sanskrit noun.

**12. (A)** Correct: ब्रह्म (neuter) = the Absolute; ब्रह्मन् (masc.) = a priest; ब्रह्मा (masc.) = the creator-god. The student's set assigns creator-god to ब्रह्म (should be Absolute), Absolute to ब्रह्मन् (should be priest), and priest to ब्रह्मा (should be creator-god) — every one is wrong, so zero match.

**13. (D)** Sanskrit distinguishes these by gender/stem: neuter ब्रह्म (Absolute), masculine ब्रह्मन् (priest), masculine ब्रह्मा (creator-god) — gender and form carry the meaning. Tone of voice does not encode meaning here. They are NOT spelled identically. 'Time of day' is irrelevant to gender-based distinction.

**14. (D)** The all-pervading impersonal Supreme is the neuter ब्रह्म, which the verse's ईश names. ब्रह्मन् is a priest (a person, not the Absolute). ब्रह्मा is the personal creator-god, not the impersonal pervader. 'A fox' (शृगाल) is unrelated.

**15. (B)** A priest is ब्रह्मन्, a masculine word. ब्रह्म is neuter (the Absolute), not a priest. ब्रह्मा is the creator-god and is masculine, not neuter. ब्रह्म is neuter, never masculine.

**16. (B)** Impersonal Absolute = neuter ब्रह्म; personal creator-god = ब्रह्मा; ritual priest = ब्रह्मन्. So the order is ब्रह्म, ब्रह्मा, ब्रह्मन्. The other orderings swap at least one role — e.g. the last option puts ब्रह्मन् as creator-god and ब्रह्मा as priest, both wrong.

**17. (A)** The active creating of the world is attributed to the personal creator-god ब्रह्मा; the neuter ब्रह्म is the impersonal Absolute, so substituting it changes the meaning. They are NOT interchangeable. Neither means 'a priest' (that is ब्रह्मन्). ब्रह्म is the Absolute, not a fox.

**18. (C)** सर्व 'all' + शक्ति 'power' + the possessive suffix -मत्/-मान् = 'possessing all power', i.e. omnipotent. -मत् marks possession, never lack, so 'powerless' is the opposite. The suffix denotes possession of a QUALITY, not an enumerated list of gadgets, and it does not mean a power shared by everyone.

**19. (C)** -मत्/-वत् ('possessing') has the variants -मत्/-मान् and -वत्/-वान्; after stems like शक्ति (ending in -इ) the -म- form is used (शक्तिमत्/शक्तिमान्), while भगवत्/भगवान् takes the -व- form. Both forms still mean 'possessing'. Neither marks tense. They are not freely interchangeable here — the stem's ending governs the choice.

**20. (D)** बुद्धि+मत् ('intelligent'), धन+वत् ('wealthy'), शक्ति+मत् ('powerful') all add the possessive -मत्/-वत् suffix to a quality-noun, meaning 'possessing' it. They are nouns/adjectives, not -स्य- future verbs. They are not instrumental case-forms. The suffix affirms, never negates, the quality.

**21. (C)** -मत्/-वत् attaches to a quality-noun (शक्ति, 'power') and means 'having that quality' — here all-power, omnipotence. It does not pluralise into 'many objects'. It is a possessive suffix, not a tense marker, so 'owned' (past) is wrong. It is not a case-ending, so 'for the machines' (dative) is wrong.

**22. (D)** शक्तिमान् with the possessive -मत् means 'possessing power'; the opposite 'powerless' uses negation (अ-) or -हीन ('without'): अशक्तः, शक्तिहीनः. The first distractor flips both meanings. -मत् is not a tense marker. शक्तिमान् and शक्तिहीनः are opposites, not synonyms.

**23. (B)** The verse gives all-pervasion (everywhere) and सर्वशक्तिमान् gives all-power (omnipotence) — so together: everywhere AND all-powerful. 'Powerless' contradicts सर्वशक्तिमान् (which means 'possessing all power'). 'Confined to one temple' contradicts the verse's all-pervasion. 'Neither' denies both.

**24. (A)** नामकीर्तनम् = नाम ('Name') + कीर्तनम् ('praise-singing'), an outward vocal act. ध्यानम् is silent inward meditation. The first distractor swaps the two. आराधनम् is worship generally, not specifically vocal, and नामकीर्तनम् is precisely the vocal one — so 'all silent' is wrong.

**25. (A)** नाम ('Name') + कीर्तनम् ('praise-singing') = singing/proclaiming the Name in praise. 'A stain on the family name' confuses it with कुलकलङ्कः. 'Worship with offerings' is आराधनम्. 'Silent meditation' is ध्यानम् — but कीर्तनम् is vocal, not silent.

**26. (C)** The instrumental (-एन) marks the MEANS/instrument: the wise remember the Lord BY worship, meditation and Name-chanting. The Lord (ईश्वरम्, accusative) is the object remembered, not these three. They are not places (that would be locative) nor recipients (dative).

**27. (C)** ध्यान is a neuter -अ stem; its nominative singular is ध्यानम्, while ध्यानेन is the instrumental. ध्याने is the locative ('in meditation'). ध्यानाय is the dative ('for meditation'). ध्यानात् is the ablative ('from meditation'). Only ध्यानम् is the nominative naming form.

**28. (B)** आराधनम् is worship in general; meditation (ध्यानम्) and Name-chanting (नामकीर्तनम्) are specific ways of worshipping — so the general includes the particular. आराधनम् is not कुलकलङ्कः (a family-stain). It is a noun, not a future verb. And it is worship itself, not its opposite — the wise practise it.

**29. (D)** स्मरन्ति ('they remember') takes God as its accusative object; meditation and Name-chanting are the MEANS, so they take the instrumental. They are not the objects remembered (that is God). The agents are the wise people (nominative बुद्धिमन्तः जनाः), not the practices. They are not a place (locative).

**30. (D)** Chanting aloud = नामकीर्तनम् (vocal Name-praise); silent contemplation = ध्यानम् (meditation). The first distractor reverses the order. कुलकलङ्कः is 'a stain on the family', not a worship act. दास्यन्ति/ध्यायन्ति are verbs ('will give'/'meditate'), not the names of the two acts asked for.

**31. (B)** यक्ष, गन्धर्व and किन्नर are upa-deva — semi-divine attendants of the gods, ranking BELOW the देवs. They are not asuras (anti-gods), not supreme देवs, and not ordinary humans; they form the semi-divine tier of the hierarchy.

**32. (A)** गन्धर्व is an upa-deva (semi-divine), so it sits below the देवs yet above मनुष्य (humans) in the chapter's ranking. It is not above the देवs (they outrank it), not below humans, and the असुर/lowest position is a different tier from the semi-divine.

**33. (C)** किन्नरः is an upa-deva (semi-divine), so it ranks below the देवs — it does not outrank इन्द्रः. It is not an असुर (anti-god) nor an ordinary human; it belongs to the semi-divine tier just under the gods.

**34. (B)** यक्ष, गन्धर्व, किन्नर are all semi-divine. The first distractor includes ब्रह्मा (a creator-GOD, not semi-divine). The second includes चिकित्सकः (a doctor, a human). The third includes शृगालः (a fox, an animal). Only the all-semi-divine set is correct.

**35. (B)** ईशावास्यम् इदं सर्वम् says ALL this is pervaded, so every tier of beings — gods, semi-divine, humans, anti-gods — falls within the Lord. Excluding lower beings, or restricting pervasion to humans, or to inert matter only, all contradict the सर्वम् ('all') of the verse.

**36. (A)** The hierarchy (देव → upa-deva → मनुष्य → असुर) shows order, while ईशावास्यम् इदं सर्वम् places ALL of it within the single Lord. 'Chaos with no Lord' denies both order and pervasion. 'Only the top is real' is not the chapter's claim. 'Many separate lords' contradicts the one all-pervading ईश.

**37. (B)** The -स्य-/-ष्य- infix is the future-tense (लृट्) marker; दास्यन्ति and the gloss करिष्यन्ति both show it, so both are future: 'they shall give / shall do (the giving)'. Present 'they give' would be ददति/दत्ते. Past 'they gave' lacks -स्य-. The imperative 'let them give' (ददतु) is a different mood.

**38. (D)** दा + स्य (future) + अन्ति (3rd plural) = 'they will give'. The -अन्ति ending is plural, not singular, so 'he will give' (दास्यति) is wrong number. 'They were giving' is past-continuous, lacking the future -स्य-. 'You will give' would use a 2nd-person ending (दास्यथ/दास्यसि), not -अन्ति.

**39. (C)** Present 'they give / are giving' is ददति (parasmaipada) / ददते (atmanepada). दास्यन्ति is the FUTURE ('will give'). अदुः is a past form. देयम् is a gerundive ('to be given'), not a present-tense finite verb.

**40. (B)** दास्यति keeps the future -स्य- but uses the 3rd-singular ending -ति: 'he/she will give'. ददति is present and plural. अदात् is past singular ('he gave'). दीयते is a present passive ('it is given'), losing the future sense.

**41. (A)** The -स्य- in दास्यन्ति makes it future ('will give'); ददति is present ('give now') — so the difference is TENSE, not mere spelling. They do not both mean present. Both are 3rd-person plural (same number and person), so the number/person distractors are wrong.

**42. (D)** दानं करिष्यन्ति paraphrases दास्यन्ति with the familiar future करिष्यन्ति ('will do') — both carry the -स्य-/-ष्य- future marker, so the gloss confirms, not changes, the future tense. It does not turn the verb present, past, or imperative.

**43. (A)** ध्यायन्ति is from ध्यै ('meditate') with the present 3rd-plural ending, so 'they meditate'. There is no -स्य-, so it is not future ('will meditate' would be ध्यास्यन्ति). 'They gave' is an unrelated verb (दा) in the past. 'They sleep' is स्वपन्ति, a different root.

**44. (C)** Silent contemplation of the Lord = ध्यायन्ति ('they meditate'). विवदन्ते is 'they quarrel'. स्वपन्ति is 'they sleep'. दास्यन्ति is the future 'they will give' — none of these is contemplation.

**45. (C)** दास्यन्ति is FUTURE (it carries the -स्य- marker, 'they will give'). ध्यायन्ति ('they meditate'), ददति ('they give'), and स्वपन्ति ('they sleep') are all PRESENT. So दास्यन्ति is the odd one out by tense.

**46. (B)** कुल ('family') + कलङ्क ('stain') = 'a stain on the family' — i.e. someone who disgraces it. It is the opposite of an honour. It is not a worship ritual (that would be आराधनम्) and not a semi-divine being (यक्ष/गन्धर्व/किन्नर).

**47. (B)** कुलकलङ्कः ('a stain on the family') warns against disgraceful conduct that dishonours one's family — the opposite of the reverent, worshipful behaviour the chapter praises. Meditation, reverence, and Name-singing are the GOOD responses the chapter commends, not what कुलकलङ्कः warns against.

**48. (B)** कुलकलङ्कः is one who disgraces the family; its moral opposite in the chapter is the reverent, worshipful person who honours the Lord present in all. A 'more shameful relative' is the same fault, worsened, not the opposite. A fox and a future-tense verb are not moral categories at all.

**49. (C)** Correct: आराधनम् = worship, ध्यानम् = meditation, नामकीर्तनम् = Name-chanting. The first distractor rotates all three meanings. The second mislabels आराधनम् as a family-stain (that is कुलकलङ्कः). The third assigns a tense, a god, and an animal — none of which these worship-terms mean.

**50. (D)** दास्यन्ति is future (its -स्य- marker), and -मत्/-वत् is the possessive ('possessing') suffix. The first distractor mislabels the tense and reverses the suffix's sense. The second mislabels the tense and confuses -वत् with a case-ending. The third swaps the roles of -स्य- (future) and -मत् (possessive).

**51. (D)** Correct gender-meaning set: neuter ब्रह्म = the Absolute, ब्रह्मन् = a priest, ब्रह्मा = the creator-god. The first distractor misassigns all three and adds 'fox' (शृगालः). The second rotates the roles. 'All three = the Absolute' erases the gender distinctions that give them different meanings.

**52. (C)** Correct: गन्धर्वः is semi-divine (upa-deva), ब्रह्मा is a creator-god, and शृगालः is an animal (a fox). The first distractor demotes ब्रह्मा and promotes गन्धर्वः wrongly. The second mislabels किन्नर/यक्ष and calls ब्रह्मा a fox. The third over-promotes यक्ष and miscalls गन्धर्व a doctor.

**53. (B)** सर्वशक्तिमान् = all-powerful (the -मत् suffix means 'possessing', not 'lacking'); दास्यन्ति = future (the -स्य- marker), not past; ब्रह्मा = the creator-god, not the Absolute (that is neuter ब्रह्म). Every claim is wrong, so all three.

**54. (A)** The chapter's grammar focus is the future tense (दास्यन्ति), the possessive -मत्/-वत् suffix (सर्वशक्तिमान्), and the ब्रह्म/ब्रह्मन्/ब्रह्मा gender distinction. 'Six tastes/seasons', '-तः direction words', and 'numeral euphony' belong to other lessons, not this chapter.

**55. (C)** Because the Lord pervades all, the wise respond with reverent worship, meditation and Name-chanting; the rejected opposite is arrogance/greed/quarrelling (the कुलकलङ्क-type attitude). The other three options ARE the praised responses, not the rejected ones.

**56. (A)** The chapter's theme is the all-pervading (ईशावास्यम् इदं सर्वम्) and all-powerful (सर्वशक्तिमान्) Lord, honoured by the wise through आराधन/ध्यान/नामकीर्तन. The fox tale, barter/money lesson, and forts belong to entirely different chapters/subjects, not sch07.

**57. (A)** The chapter uses grammar (future दास्यन्ति, possessive -मत्, the ब्रह्म trio) to help students correctly read the metaphysical verse — meaning and grammar work together. It is not story-only, not about a fox, and the grammar supports rather than contradicts the verse.

**58. (D)** Hoarding, scorn and family-shame are the opposite of the chapter's reverence-ethic; such a person ignores the all-pervading Lord and is a कुलकलङ्कः ('a stain on the family'). He is NOT honouring the Lord (नामकीर्तनम्), nor a semi-divine being, nor a meditator on ब्रह्म — those are the praised, contrary descriptions.

**59. (D)** Internalising 'all this is pervaded by the Lord' yields reverent care everywhere, shown through आराधन/ध्यान/नामकीर्तन. Temple-only reverence contradicts the everywhere-pervasion. Worshipping semi-divine beings instead of the Lord inverts the hierarchy. 'Humans need do nothing' contradicts the active honouring the chapter prescribes.

**60. (D)** All three parts of the correct statement check out: the verse means 'all this is pervaded by the Lord', दास्यन्ति is future ('they shall give'), and the chapter's ethic favours giving/worship over hoarding. The first distractor mistranslates the verse and the tense. The second shrinks सर्वम् to one temple and calls the all-powerful Lord powerless. The third miscalls ब्रह्मा the Absolute and ध्यानेन (instrumental, 'by meditation') a nominative subject.
