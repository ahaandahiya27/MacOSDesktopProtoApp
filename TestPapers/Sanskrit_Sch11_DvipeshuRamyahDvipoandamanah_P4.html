Worked Solutions

1. (D) The avagraha records the elision of an initial short अ of the second word once the preceding विसर्ग had turned into ओ. No consonant is lost, no long vowel forms, and it is not a nasal mark — it is purely the trace of a dropped अ.

2. (D) द्वीपः and अण्डमानः are two distinct nominative words joined by visarga-sandhi across the title; it is not one compound. The phrase identifies the Andaman as the island in question, so only the word-join reading with the apposition meaning holds.

3. (C) The pattern is अः + अ → ओ + ऽ (the अ elided, marked by avagraha). सूर्यः+अस्तम् therefore yields सूर्योऽस्तम्, with the avagraha obligatory and no vowel-merger or nasal.

4. (B) The avagraha exists only to record the अ that was elided in sandhi; reversing the sandhi restores exactly that अ. It is not added by a spelling rule, not migrated from द्वीपो, and the avagraha is not itself a sounded अ.

5. (B) रम्य agrees with the noun it qualifies in gender, number and case — here the feminine singular nominative गङ्गा, giving रम्या. It does not agree with the locative नदीषु, and it is not invariable.

6. (B) Saying 'among the islands, the Andaman is the lovely one' selects one member out of a set — the निर्धारण use. No action's place is named, there is no motion sense, and the locative is never the passive agent.

7. (A) The क्तवतु form सोढवान् names the agent who completed the action; the क्त form सोढः names the object that underwent it. They differ in voice (active vs passive orientation), not tense, and are not interchangeable.

8. (C) Stretches of time over which an action runs stand in the accusative of duration. 'Ten years' is neither the subject, nor an instrument, nor a single point 'in the tenth year' — it measures how long the enduring lasted.

9. (A) Forming the past participle of सह् triggers a regular sound-change of the aspirate-plus-त cluster into ढ, giving सोढ. It is not an error, not influenced by दश, and not a random substitution.

10. (A) द्वीप is preserved as a fixed lexical form with लengthened ई; the regular यण्-outcome द्व्यप् is not what tradition handed down. The word still transparently means 'two-watered' (island), so अप् is meaningful, not silent, and the द्व+ईप story is fabricated.

11. (A) Both the noun and its qualifying adjective move to the dual together (द्वीपौ रम्यौ); concord in number is obligatory. द्वीप does inflect, the adjective cannot lag behind, and no gender change is involved.

12. (B) उप in this nominal compound carries its locational sense 'near/beside', naming the sea adjacent to Bengal — the Bay of Bengal. It does not double the word, सागर means 'sea/ocean' not mountain, and उप is not a negation.

13. (C) The loanword is kept because it labels a precise design (solitary cell-by-cell isolation) for which no single native word was at hand. Sanskrit can certainly form compounds, the word is genuinely English, and the retention reflects naming convenience, not a legal order.

14. (B) काल spans 'black' and 'time/death', so कालापानी resonates with doom and a possibly final exile over the seas. The other glosses (comfortable, short, sweet) invert the grim connotation the name actually carries.

15. (B) The first element decides the meaning: कारा 'confinement' makes कारागार a prison, while 'work' makes कारखाना a factory. The other options swap or muddle these roots.

16. (B) A feminine इ-stem like जनजाति takes -ः / -ी / -अयः in the nominative singular/dual/plural, giving जनजातिः, जनजाती, जनजातयः. The other strings use impossible or neuter-style endings.

17. (A) Per the lesson these are surviving indigenous peoples, the Sentinelese famously uncontacted, and the law is in force precisely to shield them. Both the 'extinct' premise and the 'repealed law' claim are false.

18. (B) रम्यः carries the masculine-singular-nominative ending, so by concord the noun it qualifies must share those three features — exactly what द्वीपः (masc. sg. nom.) has.

19. (D) स्वातन्त्र्यवीर means 'hero of/for freedom', a tatpuruṣa where वीर is the head and स्वातन्त्र्य stands in a genitive relation. It is not a dvandva of equals, not a selfish bahuvrīhi, and not a negation — and the chapter's narrative of suffering for national freedom fits only this reading.

20. (A) Here a final आ meets an initial अ, merging into a single long आ (दीर्घ-सन्धि), and अतीत ('gone beyond') yields 'beyond imagination'. There is no visarga, no consonant junction, and no guṇa ए.

21. (A) मुक्तः with कारागारात् takes the ablative of separation, 'freed FROM the prison'. It is not locative ('in'), not instrumental ('by'), nor genitive ('of').

22. (D) The क्तवतु participle must agree with its subject in gender and number; सावरकरः (masc. sg.) pairs only with सोढवान् (masc. sg.). A plural subject needs सोढवन्तः, a feminine needs सोढवती, and सोढ्वा (gerund) cannot serve as the finite verb.

23. (A) समूह ('group') plus द्वीप ('island') is a cluster of islands, precisely an archipelago. A peninsula is one near-island, a continent is a single landmass, and a reef is coral, not a group of islands.

24. (C) Coral and coconut palms are markers of warm tropical-island, sea-fringed landscapes, not alpine, desert or urban settings.

25. (C) रत्न (ending in अ) + आकर (beginning in आ) merge by दीर्घ-सन्धि to रत्नाकर, 'jewel-mine' — a poetic name for the sea. It is not कालापानी, not 'king-maker', and not 'three-storeyed'.

26. (B) The phrase names the prison and the dread of trans-oceanic exile; reading it as literal black water loses that proper-name, symbolic force. There is no real black sea, river or drinking-water at issue.

34. (D) A neuter अ-stem in the accusative shows -म् / -ए / -आणि, giving वर्षम्, वर्षे, वर्षाणि. The masculine endings and the un-retroflexed -अनि are wrong.

36. (D) The क्तवतु form marks a completed past action, so it presents the endurance as a finished historical fact, not a present, future or imagined one.

37. (B) क्तवतु participles do inflect for gender; the feminine of सोढवान् is सोढवती. It is not invariable, the plural सोढवांसः changes number not gender, and सोढ्वा is the indeclinable gerund.

40. (D) जनजाति denotes a defined indigenous/Scheduled community, whereas सामान्यजन is everyone in general. They are not synonyms, not swapped, and neither means prisoners or guards.

42. (C) The Sentinelese are famed as an uncontacted, legally-protected people. They are not town-dwelling moderns, not jail-builders, and not colonial-era settlers.
