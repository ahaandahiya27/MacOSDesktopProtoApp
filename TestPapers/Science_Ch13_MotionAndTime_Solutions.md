## Worked Solutions

**1. (C)** Periodic motion repeats at regular intervals; oscillatory motion is the special to-and-fro kind. The Earth's revolution repeats every year (periodic) but is circular, not to-and-fro — periodic and *not* oscillatory. The pendulum, guitar string and swing all oscillate.

**2. (B)** Leg 1 speed = 100/5 = 20 m/s; leg 2 = 100/4 = 25 m/s. Equal distances took unequal times → speed changed → non-uniform. ("Straight line" and "equal distance" do not by themselves mean uniform.)

**3. (A)** 2 min = 120 s. Speed = 6 ÷ 120 = 0.05 m/s. (Dividing by 2 instead of 120 gives the trap 3 m/s.)

**4. (D)** 72 × 5/18 = 4 × 5 = 20 m/s.

**5. (B)** 15 × 3.6 = 54 km/h. (Dividing by 3.6 gives the reverse-direction trap 4.17.)

**6. (D)** Equal distances, different speeds → total distance ÷ total time, not the mean of the speeds. Let each leg be d: time = d/60 + d/40 = 5d/120 = d/24; average = 2d ÷ (d/24) = 48 km/h. (The mean (60+40)/2 = 50 is the classic trap.)

**7. (C)** Anya's time = 60/40 = 1.5 h. Ravi's time = 30/30 + 30/60 = 1 + 0.5 = 1.5 h. They tie. (Trap: Ravi's higher top speed makes people pick him, but he loses time on the slow first half.)

**8. (A)** A slanted line = moving at steady speed; a horizontal line = at rest; a steeper slanted line = moving again but faster. So: moves, then stops, then moves faster.

**9. (A)** Time period = 40 s ÷ 20 oscillations = 2 s.

**10. (C)** The SI unit of time is the second.

**11. (D)** 90 km/h = 25 m/s; distance in 10 s = 25 × 10 = 250 m. (900 m forgets the conversion.)

**12. (B)** Total distance = 50 km; total time = 2 + 1 (rest) + 1 = 4 h; average = 50/4 = 12.5 km/h. (Ignoring the rest gives 50/3 ≈ 16.7 — the trap.)

**13. (D)** A longer simple pendulum swings more slowly → its time period increases.

**14. (A)** A sundial uses the position of the Sun's shadow.

**15. (B)** Slope = speed; the steeper line (P) has the greater speed, so P is faster.

**16. (C)** 36 km/h = 36 × 5/18 = 10 m/s. Y = 12 m/s > 10 m/s, so Y is faster.

**17. (C)** 20 min = 1/3 h; distance = 54 × 1/3 = 18 km. (Forgetting to convert minutes inflates the answer.)

**18. (A)** Time = 240 ÷ 80 = 3 h.

**19. (D)** A sundial needs sunlight to cast a shadow, so it cannot work on a cloudy night. A water clock, sand clock and pendulum clock all work in the dark.

**20. (B)** A body moving along a circular path shows circular motion.

**21. (B)** A curve whose slope keeps increasing means speed is increasing → non-uniform motion with increasing speed.

**22. (C)** Times are equal (2 h each), so the mean is valid: distance = 40×2 + 60×2 = 200 km; time = 4 h; average = 50 km/h. (Contrast Q6, where equal *distances* make the mean wrong.)

**23. (A)** A simple pendulum's period depends on length and gravity, not on the bob's mass. Same length → equal periods.

**24. (D)** Distance rises by 5 m each second (5, 10, 15, 20) → speed = 5 m/s, constant → uniform motion at 5 m/s.

**25. (D)** From t = 2 s to t = 4 s the distance stays 20 m → the object is at rest in that interval.

**26. (B)** Original period = 60/30 = 2 s (not needed for the answer). New period = 3 s, so oscillations in 60 s = 60 ÷ 3 = 20.

**27. (C)** Convert all to m/s: P = 5; Q = 21.6 ÷ 3.6 = 6; R = 300 m ÷ 60 s = 5. Q (6 m/s) is fastest.

**28. (A)** Equal distances (180 km each): t₁ = 180/90 = 2 h, t₂ = 180/60 = 3 h; total 360 km in 5 h → 72 km/h. (Mean 75 km/h is the trap.)

**29. (A)** Convert to km/h: 10 m/s = 36 km/h; 30 km/h = 30 km/h; 720 m/min = 720 × 60 = 43200 m/h = 43.2 km/h. Increasing: 30 km/h < 10 m/s < 720 m/min.

**30. (D)** A shorter pendulum has a smaller period → more oscillations per minute. Y is shorter, so Y makes more. (Mass is irrelevant.)

**31. (B)** Speed = 100 ÷ 12.5 = 8 m/s.

**32. (C)** 54 km/h = 15 m/s; 2 min = 120 s; distance = 15 × 120 = 1800 m. (6480 = 54 × 120, mixing units; 900 = 15 × 60 forgets a minute.)

**33. (C)** Distances per second 5, 3, 1 are decreasing → speed is decreasing → non-uniform, slowing down (typical of going up a slope).

**34. (D)** The y-value is the distance from the start; where the two lines meet, both objects are at the same distance from the start at that instant. (Meeting says nothing about equal speeds.)

**35. (A)** Same distance D: speed_A = D/2, speed_B = D/3. Since 1/2 > 1/3, A is faster. Ratio A : B = (1/2) : (1/3) = 3 : 2.

**36. (B)** The odometer records total distance travelled.

**37. (B)** A flattening curve means the slope (speed) is decreasing → the object is slowing down.

**38. (A)** Total distance = 120 + 90 = 210 km. Total time = 2 h + 20 min + 1 h 40 min = 2 + 1/3 + 5/3 = 4 h. Average = 210 ÷ 4 = 52.5 km/h. (Ignoring the halt and using 3 h gives the trap 70 km/h.)

**39. (D)** Oscillatory motion is a special periodic motion, so every oscillatory motion is periodic; but a periodic motion can be non-oscillatory (e.g., circular revolution). Hence "every oscillatory motion is periodic, but not every periodic motion is oscillatory."

**40. (C)** Slope = (20 − 0) ÷ (4 − 0) = 5 m/s.

**41. (D)** Slope = (30 − 6) ÷ (8 − 2) = 24 ÷ 6 = 4 m/s. (Forgetting to subtract the starting values, e.g. 30/8 or 6/2, gives the trap values.)

**42. (C)** Time = 540 ÷ 30 = 18 s. Speed in km/h = 30 × 3.6 = 108 km/h.

**43. (B)** Period = 45 ÷ 30 = 1.5 s. 3 min = 180 s → oscillations = 180 ÷ 1.5 = 120.

**44. (A)** Total distance = 120 km; total time = 1 + 0.5 (stop) + 1 = 2.5 h; average = 120 ÷ 2.5 = 48 km/h.

**45. (A)** Motion along a straight line is rectilinear motion.

**46. (B)** Average speed = total distance ÷ total time. Distance comes from the odometer; total time from a clock. (The speedometer gives only the instantaneous speed.)

**47. (C)** Distance rises 8 m every 2 s → speed = 8/2 = 4 m/s, constant → uniform motion at 4 m/s.

**48. (D)** Speed_P = 60 ÷ 1.5 = 40 km/h; speed_Q = 80 ÷ 2 = 40 km/h → equal.

**49. (C)** 90 km/h = 90 × 5/18 = 25 m/s. The car (25 m/s) beats the bike (22 m/s) by 3 m/s.

**50. (A)** Distances rise by 2, 6, 10 m in successive seconds — the gaps grow → non-uniform, speeding up.

**51. (B)** A fan blade goes round and round → circular motion. A swinging pendulum goes to-and-fro → oscillatory motion. In order: circular and oscillatory.

**52. (D)** Distance is measured from the start; returning to the time axis (distance 0) means the object has come back to its starting point.

**53. (B)** 1.5 km = 1500 m; 25 min = 1500 s; speed = 1500 ÷ 1500 = 1.0 m/s.

**54. (D)** Distance = 300000 × 5 = 1500000 km.

**55. (A)** Each leg takes 0.5 h (20/40, 30/60, 10/20). Total distance = 60 km; total time = 1.5 h; average = 60 ÷ 1.5 = 40 km/h.

**56. (C)** A longer pendulum has a longer period, so the clock ticks fewer times each real second and runs slow. To make it run slow, lengthen the pendulum.

**57. (D)** A line getting steeper means the slope (speed) is increasing → speeding up.

**58. (C)** Bus: 100 ÷ 50 = 2 h from 9:00 → arrives 11:00. Car: starts 9:30, 100 ÷ 100 = 1 h → arrives 10:30. The car arrives first. (Trap: the bus's half-hour head start is smaller than the car's one-hour speed advantage.)

**59. (A)** Convert all to m/s: 8 m/s; 36 km/h = 10 m/s; 600 m/min = 10 m/s; 0.5 km/min = 500 m/min ≈ 8.33 m/s. The slowest is 8 m/s.

**60. (B)** Speed_A = 100 ÷ 4 = 25 m/s; speed_B = 150 ÷ 5 = 30 m/s. B is faster by 30 − 25 = 5 m/s.
