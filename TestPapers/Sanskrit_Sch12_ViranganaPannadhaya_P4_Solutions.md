## Worked Solutions

**1. (A)** The compound joins 'brave' with 'woman' precisely to claim battlefield-grade valour for a woman acting in a caretaker's role; she neither commanded an army (which would suit वीरः/सेनापति) nor ruled (which राज्ञी would imply), and the term plainly affirms, not denies, female courage.

**2. (C)** The act's historical weight rests on two joined facts — a child died AND the future king lived; remove the prince's survival and the renunciation, though still real, no longer secures the dynasty, which is what the chapter celebrates. The sacrifice would still be Panna's child's, not Panna's own.

**3. (D)** Because the prince was not her own child, no natural maternal pull bound her to him; choosing his life over her real son's makes the deed an act of chosen निष्ठा (loyalty to duty), which is morally heavier than instinct — and the option crediting mere wages misreads the chapter's praise.

**4. (A)** वीर ends in अ and अङ्गना begins with अ; two like simple vowels (अ+अ) merge into the long counterpart आ, giving वीराङ्गना. Guṇa needs unlike vowels (अ+इ→ए), vṛddhi needs अ+ए→ऐ, and there is no visarga at the junction.

**5. (B)** The chapter states the assassin came मारयितुम् (infinitive of purpose), so reporting a completed past killing already misuses the form; and even granting a killing, the victim in the prince's bed was Panna's own son — so naming the prince as the one killed compounds a factual error onto the grammatical one.

**6. (B)** The scheme comes first; it prompts Panna's pre-emptive swap; the swap makes the assassin's strike fall on the wrong child; and only because the true heir is thereby spared can he later rule. Placing the killing before the swap, or the reign before the killing, breaks the cause–effect order.

**7. (D)** The ending -ैः marks the instrumental (the means by which Panna's name is written), so the golden letters are the tool of remembering, not the thing remembered; rendering them as the grammatical subject swaps the instrumental for a nominative. It is already plural, and the word is a noun, so number and part-of-speech are not the issue.

**8. (A)** A carefully built कुतन्त्रम् that almost worked shows capacity, not stupidity; the chapter's word दुष्ट pins the fault to a corrupt will. Calling him मूर्ख would shift blame from moral evil to mental weakness — an unwarranted excuse — and the two words are not synonyms.

**9. (B)** The boy in the bed was Panna's son (ii true), the prince lived (iii true), and the assassin came to kill the heir (iv true); claim (i) is the classic trap — Panna herself was not the one killed, so it alone is false.

**10. (A)** An infinitive of purpose (-तुम्) attaches to a finite verb to state the aim; here it tells why the sending happened. It is not itself the finite verb (that is अप्रेषयत्), not the subject (दुष्टबुद्धिः), and not an adjective of the object.

**11. (C)** One may grant warm feeling for the prince, yet love for a fostered child cannot by itself justify killing one's own; the decisive extra factor is fidelity to the entrusted duty of a धाया — which is exactly what the chapter foregrounds, and there is no claim she was coerced.

**12. (D)** Glossed स्वीयं सम्पूर्णम् ('one's complete own'), the word denotes the totality of what one holds dearest, which differs by person; for a mother that can be her child's life, not money or only her own life — and it is no mere token.

**13. (D)** Two faults must go: षष्ठे ('sixth') must become षोडशे ('sixteenth') for the date, and the false completed killing अमारयत् must yield to the chapter's purpose-statement (he wished/came to kill, मारयितुम्), since the prince was not actually killed. The other options fix at most one fault or introduce a new one.

**14. (B)** His use of छल (trickery) and a hidden assassin marks him as both deceitful and cowardly; an open challenge would remove exactly those two traits. The chapter never calls him brave or loyal, and his moral wickedness would not be erased merely by fighting openly.

**15. (C)** धाया denotes the wet-nurse role, while धैर्य denotes steadfastness; the vowel contrast (आ vs ऐ) carries the whole semantic difference. The other options either reverse the genuine glosses or invent unrelated meanings.

**16. (A)** The locative plural -सु expresses 'in/among the group', situating Panna inside that honoured set; the bare nominative would only list the category, dropping the placement-among-them. The instrumental sense ('by means of') belongs to a different ending.

**17. (D)** The deception worked precisely because the assassin's belief was false; the chapter reports what truly happened (Panna's son died, the prince lived), not the mistaken aim. A killer's wrong belief cannot transfer the death onto the spared child.

**18. (B)** The infinitive of purpose -तुम् uniquely answers 'to what end / why'; the past (अमारयत्) answers what was done, the future (मारयिष्यति) what will be done, and the imperative (मारयतु) reports a command — each a different question.

**19. (B)** The chapter frames her sacrifice as unique because what she gave was not her own life but her son's — the dearest of her possessions; giving up that is harder than risking oneself, which is the soldier's case. Pay and the number of foes are not the point.

**20. (C)** The prefix कु- conveys badness, making कुतन्त्रम् an evil scheme; सर्व- conveys totality, so सर्वस्व is 'all one owns' and its त्याग a complete renunciation. Glossing कु- as good or सर्व- as small reverses the very force the words carry.

**21. (C)** An ordinal century N covers years (N-1)·100+1 to N·100, so the sixteenth century is 1501–1600, i.e. the 1500s; the slip avoided is treating the ordinal as a count of years. षष्ठे would mean the sixth century, not the 1500s.

**22. (B)** Panna's blood-son was Chandan, while Udai Singh was the royal child she nursed; she sacrificed Chandan to save Udai Singh, and Banbir was the rival who sought the prince's death — relationships the wrong options scramble.

**23. (B)** स्वपुत्रम् (accusative) is what is placed, राजपुत्रस्य (genitive) shows whose room, and शयनागारे (locative) is where; the exact reading is 'her own son into the prince's bedroom.' The distractors mis-assign subject, possessor, or case.

**24. (D)** Writing a name in golden letters is an idiom of high honour reserved for the admirable; applying it to a treacherous killer would reverse the praise the phrase exists to give. The phrase carries evaluation, not mere length or royalty.

**25. (D)** The productive -आगार means 'room/store', so a treasury joins it to कोष ('treasure') → कोषागार. Building it on 'prince', 'to kill', or 'sixteenth' yields nonsense for 'treasury'.

**26. (C)** Historical writing stakes a claim to real referents, so the named ruler, kingdom and century are presented as actual and checkable, unlike a fable's invented cast — yet the moral significance remains, not is discarded.

**27. (B)** सः in the nominative is the doer, राजपुत्रम् in the accusative is the one to be killed, and मारयितुम् gives the purpose of the finite verb आगच्छत्. The other options misread case roles or confuse the infinitive with a finite verb.

**28. (C)** Sacrifice is measured by what is given up; that the loss flowed from duty does not lessen its cost — her son's life. The chapter honours her for fulfilling, not dodging, that costly duty; pay and later regret are not the test.

**29. (D)** Without the substitute, the assassin would have found and killed the actual heir, the dynasty would have lost its successor, and the wicked plot would have won — which is exactly the catastrophe Panna's act prevented.

**30. (D)** अमारयत् is a past finite verb reporting that the killing happened, while मारयितुम् is only the purpose attached to 'made a deception'; so the first reports a deed done, the second merely an aim. The contrast is real and not reversed.

**31. (B)** Had she died, hers would resemble a soldier's one-moment sacrifice; living means she carried the grief of her son's death afterward, deepening the cost. Survival here magnifies rather than diminishes the heroism, and it does not mean she risked nothing.

**32. (C)** -एन marks instrument, -ए marks locative singular, -स्य marks possession, and -सु marks locative plural; the first option assigns each its true function. The others scramble the endings or wrongly flatten all to nominative.

**33. (A)** The chapter sets धाया (the nurturing foster-nurse) against प्रसूता (the birth-mother); gluing 'gives birth' onto धाया collapses that very contrast — the births belong to the प्रसूता. The two are not synonyms, and धाया is a woman's role, not the child.

**34. (A)** Only the first gets every label right — षोडशे is locative, मारयितुम् is the infinitive, and the sacrifice was the son's life saving the heir. The others mislabel अमारयत् as an infinitive, the locative as instrumental, or distort the facts (her own life, foolishness, an honest challenge).

**35. (C)** The valour-term वीराङ्गना licenses the inference of admired courage but says nothing about literal warfare; one cannot read 'soldier' or 'ruler' out of it, and the term certainly affirms (not denies) both her womanhood and her bravery.

**36. (C)** The plan worked by misdirection: the assassin did kill, but killed the decoy; that completed killing of the wrong child is exactly what spared the heir. Success against the substitute and the prince's survival are two sides of the same deception.

**37. (A)** छल (deceit) and कुतन्त्रम् (evil scheme) are the rival's vices; निष्ठा (loyalty) is Panna's motive and सर्वस्वत्याग (total renunciation) its result — a clean vice-vs-virtue pairing the distractors cross-wire.

**38. (D)** The cardinal षोडश must become the ordinal in the locative, षोडशे, to agree with शतके and mean 'in the sixteenth century'; leaving the cardinal is the defect. शतके is correctly locative, and षोडश does not mean 'sixth'.

**39. (C)** Both रक्षितुम् ('to protect') and मारयितुम् ('to kill') are infinitives of purpose; swapping them keeps the same grammatical slot and only reverses the aim from harm to help — showing -तुम् marks purpose regardless of the verb's meaning.

**40. (C)** His aim was to kill the rightful heir and seize power; what came about was the heir's escape (via the swap) and the plot's failure — the precise reversal the chapter underscores. He never sought to protect anyone, nor was Panna his target's death.

**41. (D)** A single act can carry a private cost and a public benefit at once; 'a mother's loss' names what Panna paid, 'a kingdom's salvation' names what Mewar gained — complementary, not contradictory, and neither concerns Panna's own death.

**42. (D)** The compound वीराङ्गना ends in अङ्गना (feminine) and stands in apposition to the feminine पन्ना, both nominative; सर्वैः (-ैः) is the instrumental agent of the passive पूज्यते. The masculinity of वीर- does not override the feminine head अङ्गना.

**43. (A)** Causal necessity to a narrative confers no virtue; the chapter measures heroism by moral worth, on which the assassin — an instrument of the wicked plot — scores nothing. The renunciation was Panna's, and heroism is not mere event-causation.

**44. (C)** Recasting to a passive sense moves the agent बनवीर into the instrumental (बनवीरेण) and makes the boy the grammatical patient, preserving who intends to kill whom. The other options reverse the agent and patient or change the event entirely.

**45. (B)** Panna's act concretely shows loyalty to an entrusted duty overriding even the dearest personal good — the principle the story dramatises. Reward for deception is the rival's failed assumption, and the other options are unrelated to the deed's lesson.

**46. (A)** Sleeping is something only a person does, so attaching it to राजगृह (the palace) commits the place-as-person error; the prince (राजपुत्र) is the one who could sleep. The other sentences correctly keep place-actions with the place and person-actions with the person.

**47. (D)** Circumstance set the dilemma, but the lasting honour (golden letters) is bestowed for the choice she made within it — the costly, loyal one — which is a matter of character. The text neither credits luck nor denies her agency, and the praise is hers.

**48. (A)** The rival acts upon the heir's life; Panna acts upon (relocates) her son; the assassin acts upon the boy in the bed — each clause has its own distinct agent and patient, which the first option alone gets right.

**49. (C)** A 'donation' implies giving something one can spare, whereas सर्वस्वत्याग is the renunciation of all one holds dearest — here a child's life; the softer word drains away the totality and finality. त्याग is not a small gift, and the doer is unchanged.

**50. (B)** The text marks Banbir's mind as corrupt (दुष्टबुद्धि) and exalts Panna's loyal wisdom; the lesson favours intelligence ruled by virtue over cunning serving wickedness — not an equal praise of both, nor an accident, nor a reversal of who is wise.

**51. (D)** The postposition कृते with the genitive expresses 'for the sake of / for the benefit of'; here the son's death served the prince's benefit. It does not make the prince the agent or a location, and it adds the benefactive sense beyond bare possession.

**52. (A)** The first sentence is right on the date, role, survival, son's death, and honour, but misuses मारयितुम् — an infinitive naming the assassin's purpose 'to kill', not a description attachable to the son who was killed; that is its single fault. The others stack several errors each.

**53. (D)** The rescue worked through the substitution, not through any combat; Panna placed her son where the assassin expected the prince, redirecting the strike. The chapter describes no sword-fight, bribe, or treasury-hiding.

**54. (A)** Only the first triple is wholly correct: brave-woman, one's-entire-own, and evil-scheme. The others slip on at least one gloss — turning वीराङ्गना into a place/queen/weapon, or सर्वस्व into a small gift/another's wealth, or कुतन्त्रम् into a good plan/bedroom/nurse.

**55. (B)** The chapter treats deception as an aggravating trait of the wicked plot, not a mitigating one; striking by trickery makes the act more dishonourable, not less. Responsibility stays with the deceiver, and the chapter clearly assigns blame to the rival's side.

**56. (C)** The chapter supports the inference that the rescued heir took his place in the Maharana lineage, but it does not supply reign-length, battles, or laws — those go beyond the text. His survival and the century are stated, not speculative.

**57. (B)** Beginning with वीराङ्गना as a label and ending by ranking her वीराङ्गनासु ('among heroines') brackets the narrative as a proof of that label — a deliberate frame, not coincidence, error, or a shift away from Panna.

**58. (B)** Only the first is right throughout: correct century (षोडशे), the rival's moral fault (दुष्ट not मूर्ख), the infinitive of purpose (मारयितुम्), the true sacrifice (Chandan's life, not Panna's own), the heir's survival, and the honour among heroines in golden letters. Each distractor breaks the date, a gloss, a grammar label, or the facts.

**59. (A)** The chapter uses धात्री to explain धाया — they share the meaning 'foster-nurse', a synonym pairing; धैर्य merely sounds similar but means something else (steadfastness). Confusing a clarifying synonym with a misleading homophone reverses the two relations; धात्री means neither courage, bedroom, nor prince.

**60. (A)** Chandan pays with his life — the ultimate loss; Panna does not die but surrenders her dearest and lives on grieving; Udai Singh is precisely the one preserved, losing nothing. The distractors wrongly kill Panna, demote Chandan, or flatten the unequal costs.
