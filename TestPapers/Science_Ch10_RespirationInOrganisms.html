Worked Solutions

1. (C) Respiration breaks down food to release energy, and the chief fuel is glucose (a simple sugar). Proteins and fats can be used in special situations, but the everyday fuel of the cell is glucose.

2. (B) Breathing is only the physical exchange of air (taking in oxygen-rich air, giving out carbon-dioxide-rich air). Respiration is the chemical process inside the cells that breaks down glucose to release energy. They are not the same — breathing supplies the oxygen that respiration uses.

3. (D) Carbon dioxide turns limewater milky. Exhaled air is rich in CO₂, so the limewater goes milky. ("Stays clear" wrongly assumes exhaled air has no CO₂.)

4. (A) Aerobic respiration: glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy. Option (C) is photosynthesis written backwards, and (B) is the anaerobic (yeast) equation.

5. (D) Without oxygen (anaerobic), yeast carries out fermentation: glucose → alcohol + carbon dioxide + energy. Lactic acid is what *our muscle* cells make, not yeast.

6. (A) When muscles run short of oxygen during a sprint, they respire anaerobically and make lactic acid, which builds up and causes the ache and cramp.

7. (C) "An-aerobic" literally means "without air (oxygen)". So anaerobic respiration is the breakdown of glucose in the absence of oxygen.

8. (A) On inhalation the diaphragm contracts, flattens and moves down; together with the ribs swinging up and out, this enlarges the chest cavity so air flows in. (Option D pairs the right diaphragm move with the wrong volume change — a trap.)

9. (C) Air travels: nostrils → nasal cavity → trachea (windpipe) → lungs. The other orders scramble this path.

10. (A) Insects such as cockroaches take in air through tiny holes called spiracles. (Gills are for fish, stomata and lenticels are for plants.)

11. (A) Fish do not have lungs; their gills extract oxygen *dissolved* in the water. Boiling drives the dissolved oxygen out, so the fish suffocates even though it is surrounded by water.

12. (D) 18 breaths/minute × 5 minutes = 90 breaths. (36 would be 18 × 2; 360 multiplies by 20 by mistake.)

13. (B) An earthworm has no lungs or gills; it takes in oxygen through its moist skin, which is why it must stay damp.

14. (A) The trachea is the windpipe — the tube carrying air from the throat toward the lungs.

15. (B) Hard exercise makes muscles respire faster, so they need more oxygen and produce more carbon dioxide. Breathing speeds up to supply the extra O₂ and clear the extra CO₂.

16. (D) A frog is special: it can take in oxygen both through its moist skin and through its lungs.

17. (D) Leaves exchange gases through tiny pores called stomata. (Lenticels are on stems, spiracles on insects, alveoli in human lungs.)

18. (B) Respiration — the actual breakdown of glucose to release energy — happens inside the body's cells, not in the lungs or blood (those only transport the gases).

19. (B) Aerobic respiration breaks glucose down completely, releasing much more energy per glucose molecule than anaerobic respiration, which only breaks it down partly (into alcohol or lactic acid).

20. (A) The diaphragm is the dome-shaped sheet of muscle below the lungs that drives breathing movements.

21. (C) Lactic-acid cramps ease once oxygen returns and the acid is cleared; rest, gentle massage or a hot bath all help, so the body can finish breaking down the lactic acid.

22. (D) Water holds far less dissolved oxygen than the same volume of air, so a fish must keep passing a steady stream of water over its gills to gather enough oxygen.

23. (B) On exhalation the diaphragm relaxes (domes up) and the ribs move down and in, so the chest cavity gets smaller and air is pushed out.

24. (D) A correct set must match each organ to the right organism: gills (fish), lungs (human), moist skin (earthworm). The other sets mismatch organs and organisms.

25. (B) The mirror fogs because exhaled air carries extra water vapour, which condenses on the cold glass.

26. (C) Swimming hard makes the muscles respire faster, so the body must take in more oxygen and remove more carbon dioxide — hence the jump from 15 to 35 breaths per minute.

27. (C) Yeast fermenting in dough gives off carbon dioxide; the trapped bubbles of CO₂ make the bread rise.

28. (B) Soil has air in the spaces between its particles; roots take in oxygen from this trapped air for their own respiration.

29. (A) Breathing rate is lowest at rest and rises during exercise (the opposite of option B, which is the trap).

30. (B) The tiny balloon-like air sacs in the lungs are the alveoli; their thin walls let oxygen pass into the surrounding blood.

31. (A) Plants respire both day and night. (They photosynthesise only in light, but respiration never stops — the trap is thinking plants only respire at night.)

32. (C) In respiration we take in oxygen and give out carbon dioxide. (Taking in CO₂ and giving out O₂ is photosynthesis in plants — a common mix-up.)

33. (D) Woody stems exchange gases through small openings called lenticels. (Stomata are on leaves.)

34. (D) Compared with inhaled air, exhaled air has less oxygen (some was used up) and more carbon dioxide (a product of respiration).

35. (A) In the dough, yeast lacks oxygen and respires anaerobically, producing alcohol and carbon dioxide — the CO₂ makes the dough rise.

36. (D) From the spiracles, air travels through a branching network of fine air tubes called tracheae that carry it directly to the insect's tissues.

37. (A) One inhalation, in order: diaphragm contracts → ribs move up and out → chest expands → air rushes in. The other orders describe exhalation or scramble the steps.

38. (C) A burning candle and a respiring animal both take in oxygen and release energy plus carbon dioxide — respiration is like a slow, controlled "burning" of glucose.

39. (C) A fish does not have lungs; it uses gills. Humans, frogs and dogs all use lungs (a frog also uses its skin), so the fish is the odd one out.

40. (D) A flame needs oxygen; if the candle goes out, the gas does not support burning. Fermentation gives off carbon dioxide, which puts the flame out.

41. (C) The number of breaths taken in one minute is the breathing rate. (Heart rate and pulse count heartbeats, not breaths.)

42. (C) High up, the air is thinner (lower pressure), so each breath carries less oxygen; the body feels short of oxygen and breathing speeds up — that breathless feeling.

43. (A) The incorrect pair is "cockroach — gills". A cockroach breathes through spiracles and tracheae, not gills. The other three pairings are all correct.

44. (D) When oxygen can't reach the muscles fast enough during vigorous exercise, they switch partly to anaerobic respiration, forming lactic acid.

45. (D) Yeast in sugar solution respires anaerobically, releasing carbon dioxide that collects in and inflates the balloon. (Yeast does not *make* oxygen.)

46. (A) Every living cell — in plants as well as animals, awake or asleep — respires to release the energy it needs to stay alive.

47. (B) Tube X receives *exhaled* air (CO₂-rich) and tube Y receives ordinary room air. The exhaled-air tube (X) turns milky far faster because exhaled air carries much more carbon dioxide. So "X turns milky much faster than Y" is the correct statement.

48. (D) The voice box sitting at the top of the windpipe is the larynx.

49. (C) The accurate correction to "fish breathe water" is that fish take in oxygen *dissolved* in water using their gills — they do not breathe water into lungs, nor make their own oxygen.

50. (C) Resting rate is about 15–18 per minute; after climbing stairs it rises well above that, so ~30 per minute is the expected value. (16 is a resting value; 0 and 5 are impossible after exertion.)

51. (B) When the chest cavity expands, the same amount of air now fills a bigger space, so the pressure inside the lungs drops below outside pressure and air rushes in. (Lungs do not "pull" air like a magnet.)

52. (B) Earthworms breathe through moist skin using oxygen from soil air. Heavy rain fills the soil spaces with water, leaving too little air, so the worms surface to get oxygen.

53. (C) In the dark a plant cannot photosynthesise, but it still respires: it uses oxygen and releases carbon dioxide.

54. (A) Aerobic respiration gives carbon dioxide + water; yeast fermentation gives alcohol + carbon dioxide. The product common to both is carbon dioxide.

55. (B) The hairs and sticky mucus in the nasal cavity trap dust and germs, cleaning (and warming and moistening) the air before it reaches the lungs.

56. (C) On exhalation the diaphragm relaxes and the chest cavity shrinks; squeezing the air into a smaller space raises the pressure inside the lungs above outside pressure, so air flows out.

57. (B) The seeds used up the oxygen (candle goes out) and released carbon dioxide (limewater turns milky) — the signs of living seeds respiring, not photosynthesising.

58. (B) The correct comparison is "a fish uses gills while a cockroach uses tracheae." The other options swap the breathing organs between animals.

59. (A) Both a bicycle pump and the breathing system work by changing the volume of a space, which changes the pressure and so moves air in and out. (The pump does not burn glucose; that part is unique to respiration.)

60. (D) Every living cell keeps respiring even during sleep, so it needs a constant oxygen supply — which is why breathing never stops, awake or asleep.

Solutions complete: 60 / 60.
