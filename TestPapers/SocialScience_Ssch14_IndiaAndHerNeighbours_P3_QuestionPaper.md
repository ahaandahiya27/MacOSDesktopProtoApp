**Paper 3 — (progressively harder)**

**Marking scheme:** **+4** for each correct answer · **−1** for each wrong answer · **0** if unattempted.

**Instructions:** Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. A neighbour need not share a land border (Sri Lanka and the Maldives are maritime neighbours); an 'open border' like India–Nepal still IS a border, merely visa-free; and Indian culture spread outward to South-East Asia peacefully through trade, travel and religion, never by conquest. Do not write on this paper — mark answers on your answer sheet.

1. A textbook lists India's neighbours and places both Sri Lanka and Pakistan in the same single column titled 'land neighbours'. The error in this list is that:
   (A) Pakistan is actually a maritime neighbour and should be moved to the other column
   (B) neither country is a neighbour of India, so both should be deleted
   (C) Sri Lanka shares a long Himalayan land border and is correctly placed
   (D) Sri Lanka shares no land border with India and belongs under maritime neighbours, not land neighbours

2. Which statement best captures why the sea makes a country a neighbour just as a land boundary does?
   (A) A shared sea connects two coasts and lets people, goods and ideas travel between them, so a maritime link is a real neighbourly link
   (B) The sea only separates countries and can never connect them
   (C) Only a fence or wall on land can ever join two countries
   (D) Sea links matter only for countries that have no land neighbours

3. India has both land and maritime neighbours. The single best reason India can have maritime neighbours at all is that:
   (A) India is completely landlocked and surrounded by mountains
   (B) India shares a land border with every island in the world
   (C) the Indian Ocean has no other countries touching it
   (D) India has a long coastline and sits centrally in the Indian Ocean, so other coastal lands lie across the sea from it

4. A student argues, 'Because the Maldives is a tiny group of islands far out in the sea, it cannot count as a neighbour.' The strongest reply is that:
   (A) the Maldives must first build a bridge to India before it counts
   (B) distance and size do not decide neighbourliness; the Maldives shares the Indian Ocean with India and is a close maritime neighbour
   (C) only the largest country in a region can be a neighbour
   (D) the Maldives is actually one of India's land neighbours

5. Of the following, the country that is a neighbour of India through the SEA rather than across a land boundary is:
   (A) Bhutan
   (B) Nepal
   (C) Sri Lanka
   (D) Afghanistan

6. Which pairing of a neighbour with the correct kind of link is right?
   (A) Bangladesh — maritime neighbour; Maldives — land neighbour
   (B) Nepal — maritime neighbour; Sri Lanka — land neighbour
   (C) China — maritime neighbour; Myanmar — island neighbour
   (D) Bangladesh — land neighbour to the east; Maldives — maritime neighbour

7. If you sailed in a straight line south from India's southern tip, the FIRST neighbouring country you would reach is:
   (A) China, which lies to the far north across the Himalayas
   (B) Afghanistan, which lies to the north-west
   (C) Bhutan, the landlocked Himalayan kingdom
   (D) Sri Lanka, lying just across the narrow strait to the south-east

8. The India–Nepal border is often called an 'open border'. The most accurate meaning of this is that:
   (A) there is no border line between the two countries at all
   (B) Nepal is simply a part of India with no separate government
   (C) the border exists and is recognised, but people may cross it freely without a visa or passport
   (D) the border is sealed with a high wall and cannot be crossed

9. Two students debate the India–Nepal border. Aria: 'It is open, so it is not really a border.' Vir: 'It is a border, just one people cross freely.' Who is right, and why?
   (A) Aria, because openness means the two countries are legally one
   (B) Aria, because borders only exist where there are walls
   (C) Vir, because an open border is still a recognised boundary between two sovereign states; only the crossing rules are relaxed
   (D) Neither, because Nepal and India share a maritime border, not a land one

10. Compared with the India–Nepal border, the India–China border in the Himalayas is best described as:
   (A) an open, visa-free border just like the India–Nepal one
   (B) a maritime border running across the Indian Ocean
   (C) a land border too, but one that is not visa-free and is shaped by high mountains
   (D) not a border at all, since mountains separate the two countries

11. The fact that the Himalayas form much of the boundary between India and China best illustrates the idea that:
   (A) natural features of the land, like a great mountain range, can mark the border between countries
   (B) every border in the world is drawn along a coastline
   (C) mountains make any contact between neighbours impossible forever
   (D) geography has nothing to do with where borders lie

12. A neighbour that is reached neither by a shared land border nor purely across open sea, but historically by an OVERLAND route through the mountains to the north-west, is:
   (A) Afghanistan, linked to India by the ancient northern overland route
   (B) the Maldives, reached only across the Indian Ocean
   (C) Sri Lanka, reached only by sailing south
   (D) Indonesia, reached far across the eastern seas

13. Myanmar is called India's 'gateway to South-East Asia' chiefly because it:
   (A) is an island lying far out in the Pacific Ocean
   (B) lies on India's eastern land border and links India by land toward the South-East Asian countries
   (C) blocks all land contact between India and South-East Asia
   (D) shares no border with India and lies across the sea

14. A claim says, 'Nepal is landlocked, so it has no use for the sea.' The best refutation is that:
   (A) Nepal has a long ocean coastline of its own
   (B) landlocked countries never trade with anyone abroad
   (C) even landlocked Nepal needs the sea for trade, and its goods usually reach the ocean through Indian ports
   (D) Nepal reaches the sea through Chinese ports across the high Himalayas only

15. Both Nepal and Bhutan share the same key geographic feature that shapes their economies. That feature is that they are:
   (A) island nations dotted across the Indian Ocean
   (B) landlocked Himalayan countries with no coastline, depending on India for access to the sea
   (C) long-coastline maritime neighbours of India
   (D) desert countries with no mountains at all

16. Bhutan generates much of its electricity from fast-flowing Himalayan rivers. This is possible mainly because Bhutan:
   (A) is a mountainous country where water falling from a height can turn turbines (hydroelectricity)
   (B) has a flat coastline ideal for tidal power
   (C) imports all its electricity from distant oceans
   (D) burns sea water to make power

17. Bhutan measures progress through 'Gross National Happiness' rather than money alone. Compared with a country that judges success only by wealth, Bhutan's approach gives extra weight to:
   (A) the size of its army and its number of weapons
   (B) the total number of islands it controls
   (C) the wellbeing of people, care for the environment, good governance and culture
   (D) how many tall city skyscrapers it can build

18. A landlocked neighbour and a maritime neighbour differ most clearly in that:
   (A) the landlocked one has the longer coastline of the two
   (B) the landlocked one has no coast and must borrow a sea route, while the maritime one is itself reached across the sea
   (C) the maritime one can never trade by sea
   (D) both have identical access to the ocean

19. Which reasoning correctly explains why landlocked Nepal and Bhutan still benefit from India's coastline?
   (A) India's coastline gives Nepal and Bhutan their own beaches
   (B) the coastline lets the two countries become islands
   (C) a coastline is useless to any country that does not own it
   (D) their overseas goods can pass through Indian ports, so India serves as their gateway to the sea

20. Compare Pakistan and China as India's neighbours. The shared truth about BOTH is that they:
   (A) share a land border with India
   (B) are both maritime neighbours reached only by sea
   (C) are both landlocked countries with no coastline
   (D) share an open, visa-free border like Nepal's

21. The phrase used for India and Pakistan, 'a shared heritage but a divided history', means that the two countries:
   (A) have absolutely nothing in common in culture or past
   (B) are today a single country with one government
   (C) were never connected at any point in the past
   (D) grew from common cultural roots yet were separated into different nations

22. Which comparison of Sri Lanka and the Maldives is most accurate?
   (A) both are landlocked Himalayan neighbours of India
   (B) Sri Lanka is an archipelago while the Maldives is a single mountain
   (C) both are maritime neighbours, but the Maldives is an archipelago of many small islands while Sri Lanka is a single large island
   (D) both share a long land border with India

23. Among India's neighbours, the one most accurately described as a 'newborn neighbour' that became a separate nation relatively recently is:
   (A) China, one of the world's oldest states
   (B) Nepal, an ancient Himalayan kingdom
   (C) Bangladesh
   (D) Sri Lanka, an old island civilisation

24. Consider two neighbours: Afghanistan and the Maldives. The key difference in HOW each has historically connected with India is that:
   (A) Afghanistan links across the sea while the Maldives links overland
   (B) both linked only through the Himalayas to the north
   (C) both linked only across the Indian Ocean to the south
   (D) Afghanistan linked to India by overland routes through the north-west, while the Maldives links across the sea

25. A list of India's land neighbours to the WEST and NORTH-WEST would correctly include:
   (A) Sri Lanka and the Maldives
   (B) Pakistan and Afghanistan
   (C) Myanmar and Bangladesh
   (D) Bhutan and Nepal

26. Which of India's neighbours lies to the EAST, sharing a long land border and many transboundary rivers with India?
   (A) Afghanistan, lying to the north-west
   (B) Bangladesh
   (C) the Maldives, lying to the south-west across the sea
   (D) Pakistan, lying to the west

27. A 'transboundary river' shared by India and Bangladesh creates a need for the two countries to:
   (A) build dams to stop the river crossing the border entirely
   (B) pretend the river does not flow between them
   (C) stop speaking to each other about the river forever
   (D) cooperate on how the shared waters are used and managed

28. The Ganga and the Brahmaputra both flow from India into Bangladesh before reaching the sea. The best lesson this teaches about neighbours is that:
   (A) rivers ignore political borders, so upstream and downstream neighbours are tied together and must manage water jointly
   (B) rivers stop flowing the moment they reach a border
   (C) each country can use a shared river without affecting the other
   (D) borders completely separate the two countries' water supplies

29. Which statement about the Himalayas as a shared geographic feature is most accurate?
   (A) the Himalayas lie wholly within India and touch no neighbour
   (B) the Himalayas are a desert that blocks all rivers
   (C) the Himalayas are shared by several neighbours and feed great rivers that flow into India and beyond
   (D) the Himalayas are a maritime feature out in the Indian Ocean

30. That India shares both the Himalayas and major rivers with several neighbours shows that the neighbourhood is bound together by:
   (A) a single shared government over all the countries
   (B) one common currency used by every neighbour
   (C) borders that keep each country's geography fully separate
   (D) a common physical geography that crosses political borders

31. A river beginning in the Himalayas, flowing through India, and emptying into the sea after passing into Bangladesh best illustrates the concept of a:
   (A) landlocked lake with no outlet to the sea
   (B) strait separating two large landmasses
   (C) river that flows entirely within one country
   (D) transboundary river crossing the borders of more than one country

32. Why does shared geography often push neighbours toward cooperation rather than conflict?
   (A) shared geography means one country can fully control everyone's resources
   (B) features like rivers and mountains span borders, so managing them well requires neighbours to work together
   (C) shared rivers and ranges belong to no country, so no one need discuss them
   (D) geography is purely decorative and has no practical effect on neighbours

33. Among India's neighbours, the country whose territory lies largely 'in the lap of the Himalayas' and has no coastline is:
   (A) Nepal
   (B) Sri Lanka, a southern island
   (C) the Maldives, a chain of low islands
   (D) Bangladesh, a low-lying delta by the sea

34. The shared use of Himalayan-fed rivers by India and its northern and eastern neighbours best supports the conclusion that:
   (A) each country's rivers are entirely its own and affect no one else
   (B) the rivers vanish at every international border
   (C) neighbours' fortunes are linked by the same waters, making cooperation over rivers a regional necessity

35. Thai kings have taken the name 'Rama' and an old Thai capital was called 'Ayutthaya', echoing Ayodhya. The best explanation is that:

   (A) yes, large Indian armies marched in and ruled these lands

   (D) Indian culture spread there mainly through trade, travel and religion, not by military conquest

   (A) India physically built the temple as a colony it ruled

   (A) a source of ideas, faith and culture shared peacefully across the region
