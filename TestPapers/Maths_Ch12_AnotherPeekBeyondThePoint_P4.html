Worked Solutions

1. (D) 24 × 35 = 840. The factors have 2 + 2 = 4 decimal places, so the product needs 4 decimal places: 0.0840 = 0.084. Counting only 3 places gives the wrong 0.84.

2. (B) Multiplying by 100000 shifts the point 5 places right. Starting from 0.0035, five shifts give 350. Shifting only 4 places gives the wrong 35.

3. (C) Round to 50 × 0.5 = 25. The exact value 25.382 rounds to 25; misplacing the point to 253.82 gives 250.

4. (D) Scale both by 1000: 6300 ÷ 18 = 350. Scaling only the divisor (6.3 ÷ 18 = 0.35) then mishandling gives 35.

5. (B) 0.625 = 625/1000. Dividing top and bottom by 125 gives 5/8. Stopping at 625/100 forgets there are three decimal places (denominator 1000).

6. (C) The first digit after the point is the tenths place, so 4 stands for 4 tenths = 0.4. Reading it as hundredths confuses the position by one place.

7. (C) 125 × 48 = 6000. The factors carry 2 + 2 = 4 decimal places, so 0.6000 = 0.6. Treating the trailing zeros as extra place value gives 6 or 0.06.

8. (C) 4 × 4 = 16. Each factor 0.04 has 2 decimal places, so the square needs 2 + 2 = 4 places: 0.0016. Counting only 3 places gives 0.016.

9. (A) ×1000 shifts the point 3 right (4700), then ÷100 shifts it 2 left (47). Net effect is one shift right. Forgetting the division leaves 4700.

10. (D) Divide: 0.24 ÷ 0.08. Scale both by 100: 24 ÷ 8 = 3. Treating it as 0.24 ÷ 8 = 0.03 forgets to scale the divisor.

11. (D) Scale both by 10000: 840 ÷ 12 = 70. Scaling unevenly (84 ÷ 12 = 7) leaves a factor of ten missing.

12. (D) Estimate 0.4 ÷ 0.014 ≈ 28. Exactly: scale both by 1000, 392 ÷ 14 = 28. Misplacing the point gives 2.8 or 280.

13. (A) 0.015 has 3 places and 0.24 has 2 places, so the product has 3 + 2 = 5 decimal places (any trailing zeros are still counted before simplifying).

14. (A) 0.024 = 24/1000. Dividing top and bottom by 8 gives 3/125. Writing 24/100 uses the wrong denominator (only two places); 3/25 = 0.12.

15. (C) 3.6 ÷ 0.9 = 4 (36 ÷ 9). Then 4 × 0.05 = 0.20 = 0.2. Doing 4 × 0.5 by misreading the factor gives 2.

16. (D) Moving the point from 0.0006 to 6.0 is 4 places right, which is ×10000. Counting only 3 places gives 1000 (→ 0.6, not 6).

17. (A) 104.50 × 0.4: 1045 × 4 = 4180, with 2 + 1 = 3 decimal places → 41.80. Forgetting the 0.4 has one decimal place gives ₹418.

18. (B) 2.4 × 4 = 9.6 (24 × 4 = 96, one decimal place). 24 × 4 = 96 and 2.4 × 40 = 96, both ten times too big; 0.24 × 4 = 0.96, ten times too small.

19. (C) Scale both by 1000: 12000 ÷ 24 = 500. Scaling only the divisor (12 ÷ 24 = 0.5) misses three zeros.

20. (A) 1.2 + 0.8 = 2.0, then 2 ÷ 0.05: scale both by 100, 200 ÷ 5 = 40. Dividing 2 ÷ 5 = 0.4 forgets to scale the divisor.

21. (A) Estimate 30 × 0.25 = 7.5, so the answer is near 7.4. Exactly: 296 × 25 = 7400, with 1 + 2 = 3 places → 7.400 = 7.4.

22. (B) 45 × 6 = 270. The factors have 3 + 1 = 4 decimal places, so 0.0270 = 0.027. Counting only 3 places gives 0.27.

23. (A) (0.2)³ = 0.008 (2×2×2 = 8, three decimal places). Then 0.008 × 100 shifts the point 2 right = 0.8. Stopping at one shift gives 0.08.

24. (D) The 4 is in the tenths place worth 0.4; replacing it with 9 makes it 0.9, an increase of 0.5. Reading it as hundredths gives the wrong 0.05.

25. (B) Cost = 52.50 × 1.6 = 84.00 (525 × 16 = 8400, three decimal places). Change = 100 − 84 = 16. Confusing cost with change gives ₹84.

26. (C) Left to right: 0.6 × 1000 = 600; ÷100 = 6; ×10 = 60. Dropping the final ×10 gives 6.

27. (C) 0.0036 ÷ 0.09: scale both by 100, 0.36 ÷ 9 = 0.04. Dividing 0.0036 ÷ 9 = 0.0004 forgets to scale the divisor.

28. (D) Scale both by 10000: 180000 ÷ 225 = 800. Estimating 18 ÷ 0.02 ≈ 900 confirms order of magnitude; 80 is ten times too small.

29. (B) Factor out 0.8: (2.5 + 1.5) × 0.8 = 4 × 0.8 = 3.2. Adding only one product (2.0) and adding 1.2 wrongly, or misplacing the point, gives 0.32.

30. (B) 5.736 × 100 = 573.6. The ones digit is 3 (573.6 has 5 hundreds, 7 tens, 3 ones, 6 tenths). Reading the original ones digit 5 ignores the shift.

31. (D) 36 × 36 = 1296. The factors have 2 + 2 = 4 decimal places, so 0.1296. Counting only 3 places gives 1.296.

32. (A) Cut total = 0.95 + 0.8 + 0.65 = 2.4 m. Remaining = 3.6 − 2.4 = 1.2 m. Forgetting to subtract from the plank and reporting the cut total gives 2.4.

33. (D) ×1000 → 12500; ÷10000 shifts 4 left → 1.25. Net is one shift left from 12.5. Shifting too far gives 0.125.

34. (B) 13 ÷ 8 = 1.625 (8 into 130 is 16 r2 → .6; 8 into 20 is 2 r4 → .02; 8 into 40 is 5 → .005). 1.375 is 11/8, not 13/8.

35. (A) Round to 0.5 ÷ 0.01 = 50. The exact value 52.04... is near 50; misplacing the point gives 5 or 500.

36. (A) 30.6 ÷ 0.85: scale both by 100, 3060 ÷ 85 = 36. Multiplying instead of dividing gives 26.01.

37. (C) 3.60 × 450 = 1620 (36 × 45 = 1620, then place the point: 3.60 has 2 places, 450 has 0, product 1620.00 = 1620). Using 0.45 litre directly forgets the litre→mL conversion.

38. (B) ×10000 shifts 4 right → 720; ÷1000 shifts 3 left → 0.72. Net one shift right. Stopping after the multiply gives 720; an extra shift gives 7.2.

39. (C) 5 × 5 × 5 = 125. Each factor 0.05 has 2 decimal places, so the cube needs 2 + 2 + 2 = 6 places: 0.000125. Counting only 5 places gives 0.00125.

40. (B) 0.6 × 0.3 = 0.18, then 7.2 ÷ 0.18: scale both by 100, 720 ÷ 18 = 40. Dividing by 0.18 as if by 1.8 gives 4.

41. (A) Scale both by 10000: 48000 ÷ 16 = 3000. Scaling unevenly drops a factor of ten, giving 300.

42. (C) 9.6 ÷ 0.35: 0.35 × 27 = 9.45, and 0.35 × 28 = 9.80 exceeds 9.6, so 27 full jugs; remainder 9.6 − 9.45 = 0.15 litre. Ignoring the remainder claims 0 left.

43. (D) Scale both by 10: 0.18 ÷ 4 = 0.045. Dividing 0.018 ÷ 4 = 0.0045 forgets to scale the divisor.

44. (C) 125 × 64 = 8000. The factors have 3 + 2 = 5 decimal places, so 0.08000 = 0.08. Counting too few places gives 0.8.

45. (B) 5.6 × 1000 = 5600; 5.6 × 100 = 560; difference 5600 − 560 = 5040. Subtracting before shifting, or one wrong shift, gives 504.

46. (D) Multiply top and bottom by 4 to get 28/1000 = 0.028. Using denominator 100 instead (28/100 = 0.28) is the common slip.

47. (B) Unit rate = 67.50 ÷ 1.5 = 45 per metre. Then 45 × 4 = 180. Multiplying 67.50 × 4 ÷ 1.5 the wrong way, or misplacing the point, gives 18.

48. (C) Round to 0.04 ÷ 0.005 = 8. Exactly, scale both by 10000: 392 ÷ 49 = 8. Misplacing the point gives 0.8 or 80.

49. (D) 1.5 ÷ 0.025: scale both by 1000, 1500 ÷ 25 = 60. Dividing 1.5 ÷ 25 = 0.06 forgets to scale the divisor.

50. (D) After the point the order is tenths(0), hundredths(5), thousandths(0), ten-thousandths(6). So the 5 is in the hundredths place = 0.05.

51. (B) 0.4 + 0.6 = 1.0, then 1 ÷ 0.008: scale both by 1000, 1000 ÷ 8 = 125. Dividing 1 ÷ 8 = 0.125 then misplacing gives 12.5.

52. (A) 8.5 × 0.5 = 4.25, then 4.25 ÷ 0.05: scale both by 100, 425 ÷ 5 = 85. Dividing 4.25 ÷ 5 = 0.85 forgets to scale the divisor.

53. (C) (0.06)² = 0.0036. (0.06 ÷ 100) = 0.0006. Ratio = 0.0036 ÷ 0.0006 = 6. Mishandling the place shift gives 60.

54. (D) Number of people = 500 ÷ 62.50 = 8. Snack share = 60 ÷ 8 = 7.50. Dividing 60 by 80 gives the wrong 0.75.

55. (C) Each piece = 2.4 ÷ 8 = 0.3 m; each part = 0.3 ÷ 3 = 0.1 m. Stopping after the first cut gives 0.3.

56. (B) Numerator 1.2 × 0.5 = 0.6; denominator 0.4 × 0.3 = 0.12. Then 0.6 ÷ 0.12: scale both by 100, 60 ÷ 12 = 5. Dividing 0.6 ÷ 12 gives 0.05.

57. (A) Multiply top and bottom by 25 to get 225/1000 = 0.225. Using denominator 10000 (0.0225) shifts the point two places too far.

58. (A) 1.44 ÷ 0.018: scale both by 1000, 1440 ÷ 18 = 80. Dividing 1.44 ÷ 18 = 0.08 forgets to scale; multiplying gives 0.0259.

59. (A) 2 hours = 120 minutes. Loss = 0.045 × 120 = 5.4 litres (45 × 120 = 5400, three decimal places → 5.400). Multiplying by 2 instead of 120 gives 0.09.

60. (B) Rate = 150 ÷ 0.4 = 375 per kg. Then 375 × 2.5 = 937.50. Stopping at the per-kg rate gives 375; misplacing the point gives 93.75.
