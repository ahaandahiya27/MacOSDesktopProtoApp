## Worked Solutions

**1. (C)** Sum the moves with the start: 5 + (−8) + (−6) + 11 + (−4) + 9 + (−13). Group the negatives: −8 −6 −4 −13 = −31; group the positives with the start: 5 + 11 + 9 = 25. So 25 + (−31) = −6. A common slip drops the start value or mishandles a sign, giving −11 or −16; another reads the answer with the wrong sign to get +6.

**2. (C)** Positives: 34 + 8 + 6 = 48. Negatives: −27 −19 −15 = −61. Total: 48 + (−61) = −13. Adding all magnitudes ignoring signs gives 109; combining only the negatives gives −61; reporting the right size with the wrong sign gives +13.

**3. (D)** Since b − a is the opposite of a − b, if one is positive the other is negative; so the claim that both are positive is false. The other three are all true: subtracting a negative adds, integers are closed under subtraction but division can leave the integers, and subtraction is not associative.

**4. (B)** Work left to right: (−360) ÷ (−6) = 60; 60 ÷ (−5) = −12; (−12) ÷ 2 = −6; (−6) ÷ (−3) = 2. There are three negative divisors plus the negative dividend — four negatives in all gives a positive result. Stopping at −6 or mismanaging the parity of signs yields −2, +6, or −6.

**5. (C)** Do × and ÷ first, left to right. (−5) × (−6) = 30. For the last term, (−4) × (+3) = −12, then −12 ÷ (−2) = 6; it is subtracted: −6. So (−8) + 30 − 6 = 16. Treating the subtracted 6 as +6 gives 28; mishandling the first product's sign gives −2.

**6. (B)** An integer plus its own opposite is 0 by the additive-inverse property: (−83) + 83 = 0. Multiplying by 0 gives 0 but by the zero-product rule, not the inverse property. The third equals 166, and the fourth equals −83, neither being 0.

**7. (C)** There are five factors of (−1); an odd count of negatives makes the product negative, so the (−1) factors multiply to −1, giving a × (−1) = −a, the opposite of a. With an even count it would be a itself, but five is odd. It is not always negative (depends on a's sign) nor 0.

**8. (D)** Start −7, then −5, +9, −8, +3. Sum the changes: −5 +9 −8 +3 = −1. So −7 + (−1) = −8 °C. Forgetting the start and summing only changes gives −1; misreading a fall as a rise can give −2 or +6.

**9. (A)** Magnitudes: 2 × 3 × 1 × 4 × 5 × 1 = 120. Count the negative factors: −2, −1, −4, −5, −1 — that is five negatives (the +3 is positive). Five is odd, so the product is negative: −120. An even miscount gives +120; adding magnitudes mistakenly gives ±15.

**10. (C)** Changes: +18 −27 −14 +31 −9. Climbs: 18 + 31 = 49. Descents: 27 + 14 + 9 = 50. Net = 49 − 50 = −1 m. Twice this is −2 m. Forgetting to double gives −1; sign error on the doubling gives +2.

**11. (D)** An even number of negative factors gives a positive product, so four negatives multiply to a positive — the claim that it is negative is wrong. The others hold: opposite signs divide to negative, three negatives (odd) multiply to negative, and e.g. (−2) − (−9) = +7.

**12. (B)** Net = 6 − 9 + 4 − 11 + 8 − 7 + 2. Rights (with start): 6 + 4 + 8 + 2 = 20. Lefts: 9 + 11 + 7 = 27. So 20 − 27 = −7. Dropping a step or swapping a direction gives −5, +7, or −9.

**13. (A)** A positive product needs an EVEN number of negative factors. Among 0 through 9, the even options 0, 2, and 8 are all possible; 5 is odd and would force a negative product, so it is impossible.

**14. (D)** Originally 6 negatives (even) → positive product. Turning one positive factor negative raises the negative count to 7 (odd), so the product becomes negative. It is not zero (no factor is 0), and the sign does change.

**15. (A)** First term: (−108) ÷ (−9) = 12. Second: (−7) × (−4) = 28. Third: (−2) × 8 = −16, then −16 ÷ (−4) = 4, subtracted → −4. Total: 12 + 28 − 4 = 36. Adding the last term instead of subtracting gives 44; sign slips give 28 or 4.

**16. (A)** "How much greater" means (−17) − (−63) = −17 + 63 = 46. Adding the magnitudes gives 80; reversing the subtraction gives −46.

**17. (B)** (−3)⁴ = 81 (even power, positive), (−3)³ = −27, (−3)² = 9, (−3)¹ = −3. So 81 + (−27) − 9 + (−3) = 81 − 27 − 9 − 3 = 42. Treating odd powers as positive, or −(−3)² as +9, gives the larger wrong values.

**18. (A)** Left to right for × and ÷: 72 ÷ (−8) = −9, then −9 × 3 = −27, and it is subtracted: 40 − (−27) = 40 + 27 = 67. Last term: (−54) ÷ (−6) = 9. Total 67 + 9 = 76. Reading 40 − (−27) as 40 − 27 gives 22; other sign slips give 58 or 4.

**19. (B)** a + 0 = a and a × 1 = a, so 0 and 1 are the additive and multiplicative identities; the only integers that are their own reciprocals are 1 and −1. Most integers have no integer reciprocal (e.g. 3's reciprocal is 1/3), 1 is not the additive identity, and any integer × 0 = 0, not the integer.

**20. (C)** Magnitude is 1×2×3×4×5×6×7 = 5040. There are seven negative factors; seven is odd, so the product is negative: −5040. An even miscount gives +5040; summing instead of multiplying gives ±28.

**21. (D)** Total rise = (−80) − (−720) = 640 m over 8 steps, so each step is 640 ÷ 8 = +80 m. After 5 steps: −720 + 5 × 80 = −720 + 400 = −320 m. Using 640÷8 wrongly as 90, or stopping at −400 (the rise, not the depth), are the traps.

**22. (A)** Each bracket is −n, so the product is (−n) × (−n) × (−n) = (−1)³ × n³ = −n³ (three negatives → negative). It is not n³ nor −3n, and its sign follows n³, so it is not always positive.

**23. (A)** Nine copies of −14: 9 × (−14) = −126. Miscounting as 8 or 10 gives −112 or −140; sign error gives +126.

**24. (B)** First bracket: (−3) − 2 = −5, so (−150) ÷ (−5) = 30. Second bracket: (−4)×(−3) = 12, 12 + (−2) = 10, subtracted → −10. So 30 − 10 = 20. Forgetting to subtract the second bracket gives 30 then 40; sign slips give −10 or 0.

**25. (A)** Writing 97 = 100 + (−3) and multiplying each part by (−12) is distribution of multiplication over addition. The result is −1200 + 36 = −1164. The associative, commutative, and inverse properties describe different rearrangements, not this expansion.

**26. (B)** Work × and ÷ strictly left to right. (−288) ÷ 8 = −36; −36 × (−1) = 36; 36 ÷ (−2) = −18; −18 × (−3) = 54. Then 54 + (−5) = 49. Stopping before the +(−5) gives 54; a single sign slip in the chain flips it to −49; misreading +(−5) as −(−5) gives 59.

**27. (B)** Correct: 14 × 6 = 84. Wrong: 8 × (−4) = −32. Unattempted: 3 × (−1) = −3. Total: 84 − 32 − 3 = 49. Ignoring the unattempted penalty gives 52; counting only the positive marks gives 84; other slips give 45.

**28. (C)** (−9) × x + 13 = −50 → (−9)x = −63 → x = (−63) ÷ (−9) = 7. Forgetting to move the 13 gives x from (−9)x = −50; sign errors in the final division give −7.

**29. (D)** First bracket: (−9) − (−4) − 7 = −9 + 4 − 7 = −12. Second bracket: (−2) − (−5) = −2 + 5 = 3. So (−6) − (−12) + 3 = −6 + 12 + 3 = 9. Reading −(−12) as −12 gives the negative answers.

**30. (B)** Brackets: (−7) − (−15) = 8; (−4) − 6 = −10; (−2) + (−3) = −5. Then 8 × (−10) = −80; (−80) ÷ (−5) = 16. Stopping at −80 (forgetting the division) or sign slips give the other options.

**31. (A)** Evaluate: (−4)×(−3) = 12; (−24)÷4 = −6; (−2)×(−2)×(−2) = −8; (−18)÷(−6) = 3. Sorted least→greatest: −8, −6, 3, 12, i.e. the cube, then −6, then 3, then 12. Mixing up −8 and −6, or descending order, gives the distractors.

**32. (B)** An integer plus its opposite is 0; 0 × 47 = 0; then 0 − 19 = −19. Forgetting the final subtraction gives 0; sign error gives +19.

**33. (C)** Start −4; ×3 → −12; +20 → 8; ×(−2) → −16. Forgetting the ×(−2) sign gives +16; multiplying −12 by −2 before adding gives wrong paths to ±40 or +8.

**34. (B)** (−2)³ = −8, (−3)² = 9, so −8 × 9 = −72. (−4)² = 16, 16 ÷ (−2) = −8, subtracted → −(−8) = +8. (−1)⁷ = −1. Total: −72 + 8 + (−1) = −65. Reading −(−4)²÷(−2) as −8 (not +8) gives −81; treating (−1)⁷ as +1 gives −63.

**35. (D)** For ANY starting integer n: ×(−1) then ×(−1) gives n (two negatives cancel), and +0 leaves it unchanged — so it returns n every time. The others depend on the specific value (e.g. ×0 destroys n) and do not return every integer to itself.

**36. (C)** Any product containing a factor of 0 is 0, regardless of how many negative factors there are. The hidden 0 collapses everything; sign-parity reasoning about the twelve negatives is irrelevant. It is not −78 (that is the sum −1−2−…−12).

**37. (B)** (−5)³ = −125, (−5)² = 25, so −125 ÷ 25 = −5; then × (−2) = 10. For the second term, (−3)² = 9 and (−1)³ = −1, so 9 × (−1) = −9. Total: 10 + (−9) = +1. A sign slip on the first term gives −1; treating (−1)³ as +1 gives +19; treating (−5)³÷(−5)² as +5 gives −19.

**38. (B)** Credits: 180 + 310 = 490. Debits: 240 + 95 + 150 = 485. Net = 490 − 485 = +5. Doubled = +10. Forgetting to double gives +5; sign error gives −10; adding all magnitudes gives wrong values.

**39. (B)** p×q×r×s is a product of four negatives (even) → positive. The sum p+q+r+s of four negatives is negative. Positive × negative = negative. It is never zero (no factor is 0), and the sign is always determined.

**40. (D)** (19 − 34) = −15, × (−4) = 60. (−96) ÷ (−8) = 12. (−3) × 5 = −15, subtracted → +15. Total: 60 + 12 + 15 = 87. Subtracting 15 instead of adding gives 57; dropping a term gives 45 or 27.

**41. (C)** Six negative factors — six is even — so the product is positive (it equals +10395). An odd miscount would say negative; there is no zero factor; −36 would be the sum, not the product.

**42. (A)** (−156) ÷ x + 7 = −6 → (−156) ÷ x = −13 → x = (−156) ÷ (−13) = 12. Sign error in the final division gives −12; ignoring the +7 gives a different x.

**43. (D)** Integers are closed under +, −, and ×, but not ÷ (e.g. 7 ÷ 2 is not an integer). Subtraction IS closed, so the first distractor is wrong; not all four are closed; and subtraction belongs in the closed set too, so 'only + and ×' is wrong.

**44. (B)** Inside: (−2)³ = −8; (−4)×(−3) = 12; −(−5) = +5; sum = −8 + 12 + 5 = 9. Then (−3) × 9 = −27. (−2)⁴ = 16, subtracted → −16. Total: −27 − 16 = −43. Treating (−2)⁴ as −16 (so −27 −(−16)) gives −11; sign error gives +43.

**45. (D)** A positive product with a negative sum means both integers are negative. We need two negatives multiplying to 20 and summing to −9: −5 and −4 give product 20 and sum −9. The pair 5 and 4 sums to +9; −10 and 1 give product −10; −20 and −1 sum to −21.

**46. (C)** Rewrite as adds of opposites: −23 + 38 − 17 + 9 − 12 + 6. Positives: 38 + 9 + 6 = 53. Negatives: −23 −17 −12 = −52. Total: 53 − 52 = +1. A single sign slip flips it to −1 or shifts the magnitude to ±39.

**47. (A)** (−196) ÷ (−7) = 28; 28 ÷ (−2) = −14. (−5) × (−3) = 15, subtracted → −15. (−4)² = +16. Total: −14 − 15 + 16 = −13. Mishandling the chained division sign gives +1; treating (−4)² as −16 gives the lower values.

**48. (A)** Evaluate: (−2)⁵ = −32; (−3)³ = −27; (−5)² = +25; (−9)−(−4)−6 = −9+4−6 = −11. The most negative is −32. Treating (−2)⁵ as positive, or stopping at (−3)³, would mislead.

**49. (C)** Credits: 275 + 600 = 875. Debits: 450 + 130 + 315 = 895. Balance = 875 − 895 = −₹20. Sign error gives +20; summing only one side gives −650 or +875.

**50. (D)** 7 × (−5) = −35, subtracted → +35: (−13) + 35 = 22. Next: (−72) ÷ (−9) = 8, × 2 = 16, added → 38. Then − 6 → 32. Forgetting to ×2 the division term gives 16-less paths; sign slips give 48 or 20.

**51. (D)** Net = 6 − 11 + 5 − 8 + 3 − 7 + 4. Ups (with start): 6 + 5 + 3 + 4 = 18. Downs: 11 + 8 + 7 = 26. End = 18 − 26 = −8. Three floors below: −8 − 3 = −11. Forgetting the extra 3 gives −8; sign error on it gives −5.

**52. (A)** Factor out (−18) using the distributive property: (−18) × [(−5) − (+3) + (−2) − (+4)]. The bracket is −5 − 3 − 2 − 4 = −14, so the result is (−18) × (−14) = 252. Dividing by (−18): 252 ÷ (−18) = −14. (Equivalently, dividing the whole product by (−18) just returns the bracket, −14.) A sign slip gives +14; mis-summing the bracket gives ±4.

**53. (C)** −6² means −(36) = −36; (−6)² = +36; (−2)³ = −8, subtracted → +8; (−1)⁸ = +1. Total: −36 + 36 + 8 + 1 = 9. Treating −6² as +36 gives 81; sign error on (−2)³ gives −7.

**54. (D)** Numerator bracket: (−5)×(−4) = 20, 20 − 8 = 12. Denominator bracket: (−2)+(−1) = −3. So 12 ÷ (−3) = −4, × (−3) = 12. Then − (−4) = +4: 12 + 4 = 16. Forgetting the final + 4 gives 12; sign slips give ±8.

**55. (A)** A negative product means opposite signs; a positive sum means the positive number has the larger magnitude. We need them to multiply to −48 and add to +2: 8 + (−6) = 2 and 8 × (−6) = −48. The pair −8 and 6 sums to −2; 12 and −4 sum to +8; −12 and 4 sum to −8.

**56. (D)** Any product with a factor of 0 equals 0, no matter the parity of the negative factors. The hidden 0 overrides the sign reasoning, so it is neither negative nor positive and does not depend on the count.

**57. (D)** Changes sum: −3 −2 +6 −4 −1 +2 −5 +3 = −4. Final after start: 5 + (−4) = 1 °C. Lowered by 4 more: 1 − 4 = −3 °C. Forgetting the extra 4 gives +1; sign error on it gives +5.

**58. (C)** Innermost: (−8) + (−3) − (−5) = −8 −3 +5 = −6. Next: (−6) − (−6) = 0. Then (−10) − {0} + (−2) = −10 − 0 − 2 = −12. Mishandling the double negative inside gives −18 or −6.

**59. (C)** Even negatives + odd negatives = odd total number of negative factors, and an odd count gives a negative product. No factor is 0, so it is not zero; 'even' describes a count, not a sign.

**60. (A)** Numerator bracket: (−9)×(−2) = 18, 18 − 6 = 12. Denominator bracket: (−4)+1 = −3. So 12 ÷ (−3) = −4; × (−5) = 20; ÷ 2 = 10. Then − (−7) = +7: 10 + 7 = 17. Forgetting the final + 7 gives 10-based slips; sign error gives −17.
