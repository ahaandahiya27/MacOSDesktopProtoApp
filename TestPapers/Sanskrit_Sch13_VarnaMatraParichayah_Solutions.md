## Worked Solutions

**1. (C)** वर्णमात्रा refers to a vowel's duration (how long it is sounded). It is not about colour, caste, or spelling.

**2. (D)** A मात्रा is a unit of time (duration). It is not weight, distance, or money.

**3. (A)** One मात्रा is the duration of a short vowel. Long and prolonged vowels take two and three; a consonant is not the measure.

**4. (B)** मात्रा's root meaning is 'measure' (cf. mensuration). It does not mean colour, caste, or sound.

**5. (B)** वर्णमात्रा belongs to phonetics, the study of sounds. Not history, mathematics, or geography.

**6. (D)** Sanskrit recognises three vowel quantities. Not two, four, or one.

**7. (D)** They are ह्रस्व (short), दीर्घ (long) and प्लुत (prolonged). Not just two, nor pitches, nor tenses.

**8. (B)** The common mistake is forgetting प्लुत and assuming only two lengths. Not adding a fourth, ignoring short vowels, or counting consonants.

**9. (A)** Sanskrit adds a third length, the प्लुत. It does not have only one, none, or ten.

**10. (A)** They differ in duration. Not colour, spelling, or caste.

**11. (B)** Shortest to longest: ह्रस्व (1), दीर्घ (2), प्लुत (3). The other orders are wrong.

**12. (C)** ह्रस्व means 'short' (1 mātrā). Long is दीर्घ (2), prolonged प्लुत (3); it is not silent.

**13. (C)** अल्पकालिकः ('short-time') is ह्रस्व. दीर्घ is लम्बकालिकः and प्लुत अतिविलम्बकालिकः; consonants are not meant.

**14. (C)** A ह्रस्व vowel lasts one mātrā. Two is दीर्घ, three प्लुत; it is not zero.

**15. (A)** अ, इ, उ are short (ह्रस्व) vowels. आ, ई, ऊ are long, the drawn-out calls are प्लुत, and क, ख, ग are consonants.

**16. (A)** दीर्घ means 'long' (2 mātrās). Short is ह्रस्व (1), prolonged प्लुत (3); it is not silent.

**17. (C)** लम्बकालिकः ('long-time') is दीर्घ. ह्रस्व is अल्पकालिकः and प्लुत अतिविलम्बकालिकः; consonants are not meant.

**18. (C)** A दीर्घ vowel lasts two mātrās. One is ह्रस्व, three प्लुत; it is not four.

**19. (C)** आ, ई, ऊ are long (दीर्घ) vowels. अ, इ, उ are short, the drawn-out call is प्लुत, and consonants are not vowels.

**20. (A)** प्लुत means 'prolonged' (3 mātrās). Short is ह्रस्व (1), long दीर्घ (2); it is not silent.

**21. (A)** अतिविलम्बकालिकः ('very-delayed-time') is प्लुत. ह्रस्व is अल्पकालिकः and दीर्घ लम्बकालिकः; consonants are not meant.

**22. (B)** A प्लुत vowel lasts three mātrās. One is ह्रस्व, two दीर्घ; it is not zero.

**23. (B)** प्लुत vowels appear mainly in chanting and dramatic calls. Not everyday quick speech, number-writing, or silent reading.

**24. (D)** A प्लुत vowel is drawn out, held three times as long as a short vowel. It is not said quickly, skipped, or whispered.

**25. (D)** Said quickly, a प्लुत collapses into a long (दीर्घ) vowel. It does not become merely short, a consonant, or a word.

**26. (C)** कुक्कुटः रौति means 'the rooster crows'. It is not about a crow flying, a peacock dancing, or a mongoose running.

**27. (A)** कुक्कुटः means a rooster / cock. A crow is वायसः, a sparrow चटका, and a pigeon कपोतः.

**28. (A)** कुक्कुटः is onomatopoeic, mimicking the rooster's call. It is not an English loan, a number, or a verb.

**29. (A)** The crow naturally spans short, long and prolonged sounds, illustrating the three mātrās. It is not soundless, silent, or consonant-only.

**30. (C)** Panini used कुक्कुटः to illustrate vowel-quantity. Bankim Chandra, Savarkar and Aryabhata are from other chapters.

**31. (C)** रौति is singular, '(it) crows / cries'. The plural is रुवन्ति; it does not mean fly or run.

**32. (D)** रुवन्ति is plural, '(they) cry / crow'. The singular is रौति; it does not mean fly or run.

**33. (C)** They differ in number (singular vs plural), not tense, gender, or vowel length.

**34. (B)** For one rooster the singular रौति is correct. रुवन्ति is plural, and the others are unrelated verbs.

**35. (B)** For many roosters the plural रुवन्ति is correct. रौति is singular, रौतु imperative, and अरौत् past.

**36. (B)** It illustrates verb-subject agreement in number. Not vowel length, caste, or direction words.

**37. (C)** वायसः means 'a crow'. A rooster is कुक्कुटः, a peacock शिखी, and a sparrow चटका.

**38. (A)** शिखी means 'a peacock'. A crow is वायसः, a pigeon कपोतः, and a mongoose नकुलः.

**39. (D)** कपोतः means 'a pigeon'. A peacock is शिखी, a sparrow चटका, and a cuckoo कोकिलः.

**40. (C)** चटका means 'a sparrow'. A crow is वायसः, a peacock शिखी, and a pigeon कपोतः.

**41. (B)** नकुलः means 'a mongoose'. A mouse is मूषकः, a crow वायसः, and a pigeon कपोतः.

**42. (D)** मूषकः means 'a mouse / rat'. A mongoose is नकुलः, a peacock शिखी, and a sparrow चटका.

**43. (B)** पुंस्कोकिलः means 'a male cuckoo'. It is not a female crow, a peacock, or a mongoose.

**44. (D)** वायसः, शिखी, नकुलः and चटका are all names of birds and animals. Not vowels, verbs, or castes.

**45. (B)** भेदः means 'a type / kind'. A sub-type is उपभेदः; it is not a vowel or a verb.

**46. (C)** उपभेदः means 'a sub-type'. A main type is भेदः; it is not a vowel or a bird.

**47. (A)** An उपभेदः is a subdivision of a भेदः. It is not larger, the same, or unrelated.

**48. (D)** ध्यानं निदधतु means 'please pay attention'. It does not mean go away, sit down, or keep silent forever.

**49. (A)** ध्यानं निदधतु is a polite imperative (a respectful request). It is not a past tense, a question, or a noun.

**50. (D)** A teacher says ध्यानं निदधतु to politely ask students to pay attention. Not to scold, dismiss, or count them.

**51. (D)** In वर्णमात्रा, वर्ण means a letter / sound (phonetics). It is not caste, a bird, or a verb here.

**52. (B)** That वर्ण means both 'sound' and 'caste' shows polysemy - one word, several meanings by context. It is not a lack of vocabulary or single-sense.

**53. (A)** Here वर्ण means sound / letter, since the chapter is about वर्णमात्रा (sound-duration). Not caste, colour-only, or a bird.

**54. (A)** कुक्कुटः = rooster. वायसः is a crow (not peacock), चटका a sparrow (not pigeon), and शिखी a peacock (not crow).

**55. (D)** ह्रस्व = 1 mātrā and प्लुत = 3 mātrās. दीर्घ is 2 mātrās; the reversal and other values are wrong.

**56. (D)** रौति = singular and रुवन्ति = plural. The reversal and 'both same' options are wrong.

**57. (C)** नकुलः = mongoose and मूषकः = mouse. The reversal and 'both same' options are wrong.

**58. (B)** Its purpose is to teach the three vowel quantities (mātrā) and related vocabulary. Not empires, banking, or the food cycle.

**59. (D)** Vowel length is phonemic - it can change meaning and matters in chanting. Sanskrit has consonants and many vowels, and length does matter.

**60. (B)** Sanskrit has three vowel-lengths and रौति is singular (रुवन्ति is plural). The other options keep one or both errors.
