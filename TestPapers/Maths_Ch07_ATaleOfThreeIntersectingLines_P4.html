Worked Solutions

1. (C) The angles 4k+5k+6k = 180°, so k = 12° and the angles are 48°, 60°, 72°. The interior angle at A is the smallest, 48°. The exterior angle at A is 180 − 48 = 132°. Reading the exterior angle as the interior 48° itself, or as 180 minus the largest 72° = 108°, gives the wrong values.

2. (D) The third side x must satisfy 17 − 8 < x < 17 + 8, i.e. 9 < x < 25. The whole numbers strictly between are 10 through 24, which is 24 − 10 + 1 = 15 values. Using ≤ at both ends gives 9..25 = 17; using one inclusive end gives 16; subtracting 17 − 8 = 9 mistakes the count for the difference.

3. (D) Sum: (3x+5) + (2x−5) + 4x = 9x = 180°, so x = 20°. Then ∠A = 65°, ∠B = 35°, ∠C = 80°. The largest is ∠C = 80°. Quoting ∠A = 65° as the largest, or stopping at ∠B = 35°, are the traps.

4. (C) The exterior angle ∠QRS equals the sum of the two remote interior angles ∠P + ∠Q = 132°. With ∠P = 2∠Q, we get 3∠Q = 132°, so ∠Q = 44° and ∠P = 88°. Quoting ∠Q = 44° as ∠P, or using 180 − 132 = 48° as the remote sum, gives the distractors.

5. (B) The bisector AD splits ∠A into two equal parts, and ∠BAD = 34° is one half, so ∠BAC = 2 × 34° = 68°. Quoting one half 34° as the whole, or taking 90 − 34 = 56° as ∠A, are the named errors.

6. (B) With two sides 11 and 11, the third side x satisfies 0 < x < 22. Whole numbers 1 through 21 give 21 values. Counting 0..22 inclusive gives 22; using 11 − 11 = 0 as a usable side over-counts; excluding both ends of 1..21 wrongly gives 20.

7. (A) AM is a median, so it splits ABC into triangles AMB and AMC sharing point M on the straight line BC; ∠AMB and ∠AMC are supplementary, and ∠AMB is the exterior angle of triangle AMC at M, equal to the sum of its two remote interiors ∠MAC + ∠MCA. A median need not be perpendicular, need not bisect the angle, and there is no reason AM = AB.

8. (D) Since ∠B = ∠C, the sides opposite them are equal; call each y. The side opposite ∠A is 14. Perimeter: 14 + y + y = 50, so 2y = 36 and y = 18. The side opposite ∠B is 18 cm. Quoting the leftover 36 as one side, or copying the given 14 cm, are the errors.

9. (D) The exterior angle at A equals ∠B + ∠C and the exterior angle at B equals ∠A + ∠C, so their sum is (∠A + ∠B) + 2∠C = (180 − ∠C) + 2∠C = 180 + ∠C = 260. Thus 5y + 4y = 260, giving 9y = 260 and y = 260/9 ≈ 28.9°. Treating the two exteriors as summing to 360 minus the third exterior, or rounding to a whole degree, give the decoys.

10. (A) The third angle is 180 − 42 − 96 = 42°, so two angles equal 42° and the triangle is isosceles. The longest side lies opposite the largest angle 96°, and the two sides opposite the equal 42° angles are equal and shorter. So the two 42°-opposite sides are equal and both shorter than the 96°-opposite side. Pairing the largest angle with a short side reverses the side-angle relationship.

11. (A) The exterior angle ∠ACD equals the sum of the remote interior angles ∠A + ∠B. So 4∠A = ∠A + 36°, giving 3∠A = 36° and ∠A = 12°. Setting 4∠A = 36 gives 9°; copying ∠B = 36°; or reading the exterior 48° as the answer, are the traps.

12. (B) The third side x satisfies 16 − 9 < x < 16 + 9, i.e. 7 < x < 25, so x ranges over 8 to 24. The odd values are 9, 11, 13, 15, 17, 19, 21, 23 — that is 8 values. Counting all 17 integers, or miscounting the odd ones as 7, give the distractors.

13. (A) Let each base angle be b; the two base angles total 2b, and the vertex equals this sum, so vertex = 2b. Total: vertex + 2 base angles = 2b + 2b = 180°, so b = 45° and vertex = 2b = 90°. Taking b = 45° as the vertex, or solving 3b = 180 → 60°, are the named errors.

14. (B) Let ∠C = c. Then ∠B = c + 12 and ∠A = c + 36. Sum: 3c + 48 = 180, so 3c = 132 and c = 44°. Then ∠B = 56°, ∠A = 80°. The largest is ∠A = 80°. Quoting ∠B = 56°, or mis-adding the offsets, give the wrong answers.

15. (B) An 8-8-8 triangle has three equal sides (equilateral) and three 60° angles (all acute). The 20-20-140 triangle has two equal angles, hence two equal sides (isosceles), and a 140° angle makes it obtuse. Calling the equilateral 'right', or the obtuse triangle 'acute', are the misconceptions.

16. (D) An interior angle and its exterior angle are supplementary: interior + exterior = 180°. With exterior = 3 × interior, we get 4 × interior = 180°, so interior = 45°. Solving 3 × interior = 180 → 60°, or quoting the exterior 135°, are the traps.

17. (A) The third side x satisfies 23 − 14 < x < 23 + 14, i.e. 9 < x < 37. Smallest whole value 10, largest 36. Difference = 36 − 10 = 26. Using inclusive ends 9 and 37 gives 28; the difference 23 − 14 = 9 is a decoy; the upper bound 37 is another.

18. (A) The exterior angle at A is 110°, so the interior vertex angle ∠A = 180 − 110 = 70°. The two base angles are equal and sum to 180 − 70 = 110°, so each base angle = 55°. Quoting the apex 70° as a base angle, or copying 110°, are the named errors.

19. (B) ∠BIC = 90° + ½∠A = 90° + ½(80°) = 90° + 40° = 130°. Using 180 − ∠A = 100°, the 100°-apex value 140°, or ½(180 − 80) = 50°, are the traps.

20. (D) Two equal sides (6, 6) make it isosceles. The angle opposite the longest side 10 is the largest; since 6² + 6² = 72 < 10² = 100, that angle exceeds 90°, so the triangle is obtuse. Calling it acute ignores the wide angle opposite the long side; 'scalene' ignores the two equal sides.

21. (A) Sum: (x−10) + (x+10) + 2x = 4x = 180, so x = 45. The angles are ∠A = 35°, ∠B = 55°, ∠C = 90°. A 90° angle makes the triangle right-angled. Mis-solving 4x = 180 to a wrong x, or overlooking that ∠C = 2x reaches exactly 90°, leads to the acute/obtuse traps.

22. (D) The tightest inequality has the two shortest summing past the longest: (x − 2) + x > x + 2, i.e. 2x − 2 > x + 2, so x > 4. The smallest whole number greater than 4 is 5. Taking x > 2 from positivity gives 3; using x ≥ 4 gives 4; over-shooting gives 6.

23. (B) The exterior angle ∠ACD equals the sum of the two remote interior angles ∠A + ∠B = 60 + 70 = 130°. (Equivalently ∠C = 180 − 130 = 50°, and 180 − 50 = 130°.) Using 180 − 70 = 110 reads the wrong vertex; quoting ∠C = 50; or 180 − 60 = 120 picks the wrong remote angle.

24. (B) Let the vertex angle be v; each base angle is 3v. Sum: v + 3v + 3v = 7v = 180°, so v = 180/7 ≈ 25.7°. Forgetting one base angle gives 5v = 180 → 36°; treating base = vertex doubled gives others.

25. (D) Validity requires the third side x < 12 + 12 = 24, so x ≤ 23. For the angle opposite x to be obtuse we need x² > 12² + 12² = 288, i.e. x > 16.97, so x ≥ 17. Both conditions give 17 ≤ x ≤ 23; the largest is 23 cm. 16 is just below the obtuse boundary; 24 violates the triangle inequality.

26. (C) ∠AIB = 90° + ½∠C = 90° + ½(90°) = 90° + 45° = 135°. Using ∠C itself 90°, using ½∠C = 45°, or a mis-applied fraction giving 120°, are the traps.

27. (A) Since ∠C = ∠A + ∠B and ∠A + ∠B + ∠C = 180, we get 2∠C = 180, so ∠C = 90°. Solving ∠A + ∠B = 90 with ∠A = 3∠B gives ∠B = 22.5°, ∠A = 67.5°; quoting ∠A as ∠C is the trap.

28. (D) Check 7² + 24² = 49 + 576 = 625 = 25². Since the square of the longest side equals the sum of the squares of the other two, the angle opposite 25 is exactly 90°, so the triangle is right-angled. Assuming the long side forces obtuse without checking, or calling a scalene triangle acute, are the errors.

29. (D) Interior ∠A = 180 − 150 = 30°, interior ∠B = 180 − 140 = 40°. Then ∠C = 180 − 30 − 40 = 110°. Adding the exteriors 150 + 140 = 290 then 360 − 290 = 70 gives the exterior at C, not the interior; ∠A and ∠B mistaken as the answer give 30/40.

30. (C) Since AB = AC, ∠C = ∠B = 50°, so ∠BAC = 180 − 50 − 50 = 80°. In triangle ABD, AD = BD makes it isosceles with ∠BAD = ∠ABD = 50°. Then ∠DAC = ∠BAC − ∠BAD = 80 − 50 = 30°. Quoting ∠BAC = 80°, or ∠BAD = 50°, or reading 50 − 30 = 20 from a wrong subtraction, are the traps.

31. (B) With two equal sides 15 and 15, the apex angle opposite the base x is acute when x² < 15² + 15² = 450 (x < 21.2), and the two base angles, being equal and summing to less than 180 with the apex positive, stay below 90° for every positive x. So all whole bases from 1 up to 21 give an acute triangle; the smallest is 1 cm. Picking 2 over-restricts; 16 and 22 confuse the obtuse/validity boundaries.

32. (C) ∠BIC = 90° + ½∠A = 90° + ½(100°) = 90° + 50° = 140°. Using 180 − 100 = 80°, using ½(180 − 100) = 40°, or the right-triangle value 130°, are the traps.

33. (B) The angles are 20°, 60°, 100° (since 9k = 180, k = 20). BC is opposite the smallest angle 20°, and the shortest side lies opposite the smallest angle, so BC is the shortest. Pairing the smallest angle with the longest side reverses the relationship.

34. (A) Interior ∠ACB = 180 − 125 = 55° and interior ∠ABC = 180 − 118 = 62°. Then ∠A = 180 − 55 − 62 = 63°. Adding exteriors 125 + 118 = 243 then 360 − 243 = 117 gives the exterior at A; copying 55 or 62 mistakes a base angle for ∠A.

35. (A) Let each equal side be s; 16 + 2s = 40, so 2s = 24 and s = 12 cm. Check validity: 12 + 12 = 24 > 16, valid. Using s = base = 16, taking half the base 8, or quoting the leftover 24 for two sides as one side, are the errors.

36. (D) ∠A + ∠B + ∠C = 2∠B + ∠B + (3∠B − 20) = 6∠B − 20 = 180, so 6∠B = 200 and ∠B = 200/6 ≈ 33.3°. Treating 6∠B = 180 → 30°, or 5∠B = 200 → 40° by dropping a coefficient, are the named slips.

37. (A) The overall valid range is 30 − 18 < x < 30 + 18, i.e. 12 < x < 48, satisfied by every x with 18 ≤ x ≤ 30. So all integers 18 through 30 qualify: 30 − 18 + 1 = 13 values. Dropping one endpoint gives 12; both gives 11; the full range width is a decoy.

38. (C) ∠A + 3∠A + 5∠A = 9∠A = 180, so ∠A = 20°, ∠B = 60°, ∠C = 100°. Since ∠C = 100° > 90°, the triangle is obtuse. Mis-solving 8∠A = 180 to land ∠C at 90° gives the right-angled trap.

39. (A) When the foot of the altitude from A falls outside segment BC, beyond C, the angle at C is obtuse — the perpendicular overshoots past the obtuse vertex C. Attributing the obtuseness to A, or to the far vertex B, mistakes which vertex the foot landed beyond.

40. (D) For isosceles, the third side must equal 21 or 13. A third side of 21 (giving 21, 21, 13) is valid since 21 + 13 > 21. A third side of 13 (giving 21, 13, 13) needs 13 + 13 = 26 > 21, valid. Both work, so 2 values. Counting only one, or wrongly rejecting 13, gives the other options.

41. (A) In triangle ABH, ∠AHB = 90° (AH is perpendicular to BC) and ∠B = 30°, so ∠BAH = 180 − 90 − 30 = 60°. Copying ∠B = 30°, the right angle 90°, or assuming an isosceles 45° split, are the named errors.

42. (B) Validity: 2k + 5k = 7k > 6k, valid for all k > 0. The largest side is 6k; compare (6k)² = 36k² with (2k)² + (5k)² = 4k² + 25k² = 29k². Since 36k² > 29k², the angle opposite 6k exceeds 90°, so the triangle is obtuse for every k. The fixed ratio means the type cannot depend on k.

43. (B) AD bisects ∠A = 50°, so ∠DAC = 25°. In triangle ACD: ∠C = 180 − ∠ADC − ∠DAC = 180 − 95 − 25 = 60°. Using the full ∠A = 50° instead of its half gives 35°; quoting the half 25° as ∠C; or 180 − 95 = 85° forgetting ∠DAC, are the traps.
