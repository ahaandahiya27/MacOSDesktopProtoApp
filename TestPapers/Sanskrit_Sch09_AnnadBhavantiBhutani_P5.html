1. (D) Each source in the verse appears in the ablative as the thing another link comes from; beings appear only as what arises from food, with no further link drawn out of them, so they close the chain rather than feed it. Counting beings as a source would require a clause 'from beings, X arises', which the verse never gives; the claim that beings source action confuses the actor with a verse-link the text does not state, and number or gender does not bar a word from being a source.

2. (A) Rain is produced by yajna (यज्ञाद्भवति पर्जन्यः) and in turn feeds food, which sustains beings, so it sits mid-stream as both effect and onward cause. Action is the head and is not produced by yajna; yajna is produced by action not by rain; beings are the tail and cause nothing further in the verse.

3. (B) The wheel turns because the beings sustained by food are the agents who carry out action (including selfless yajna), so the tail feeds the head and participation keeps it spinning. Food and rain are intermediate products, not the doers of action, and ब्रह्म is the all-pervading ground of the whole wheel, not a substitute for the action-link.

4. (A) Yajna in the verse directly produces rain, so an absence of yajna strikes the rain-link first and only then cascades down through food to beings. Food and beings sit further along the chain and fail later, and action is the upstream cause that is not itself produced by yajna.

5. (B) Every link is joined by an ablative-of-source, and an ablative cannot be flipped without flipping which thing is the origin; reversing the arrows therefore asserts false sources the verse never states. The order is fixed by those cases, beings are named first only for emphasis not as a source, and the √भू verbs mark arising, not destruction.

6. (D) The chapter explicitly ties the ancient cycle to modern science (e.g. रसायनशास्त्रम्) to show two angles on one interdependent world, so an added biological loop only deepens, not contradicts, the same lesson. Naming different links does not make them contradictory, the chapter denies the 'never meet' view, and it does not claim the verse is a science text.

7. (D) All three -आत् forms name the origin from which something proceeds, which is precisely the अपादान कारक carried by the ablative. The instrument (करण), recipient (सम्प्रदान) and location (अधिकरण) are different कारक roles taking instrumental, dative and locative respectively, none of which means 'from'.

8. (A) Agency/means is the instrumental (अन्नेन), but the verse uses the ablative अन्नात्, which marks the source of emergence rather than a doer; beings come FROM food, they are not fabricated BY it. The dative would wrongly mean 'for food' and the genitive 'of food', neither of which expresses the source the ablative gives.

9. (C) The source 'seeds' must stand in the ablative plural बीजेभ्यः ('from the seeds') while the product अङ्कुराः is the nominative subject the verb agrees with, exactly mirroring अन्नात्/भूतानि. The nominative बीजानि makes seeds the subject, the locative बीजेषु means 'in the seeds', and the dative बीजाय means 'for a seed' — none expresses 'from'.

10. (B) Cases follow कारक relations: the source-of-emergence (अपादान) is food, so food is ablative, while the thing that arises is the subject in the nominative; importance, length, or position do not decide case. The other options invent non-grammatical criteria or contradict the very definition of अपादान.

11. (A) For an अ-stem like अन्न the ablative singular is अन्नात् and the dative singular is अन्नाय — distinct forms — so the morphology alone proves it is ablative. The two cases are not homophonous here, and 'becoming' does not force a dative; the source-meaning and the -आत् form agree.

12. (D) Agreement is with the nominative subject भूतानि, which is plural, so the verb is plural; the ablative source plays no part in determining the verb's number. The 'many grains' idea, the euphony idea, and the position idea all ignore the rule that the verb agrees with its subject's number.

13. (B) Neuter a-stem nouns have one and the same form (भूतानि) for nominative and accusative plural, so it serves as both subject and object. भूताः/भूतान् are masculine endings and भूतैः is the instrumental, none of which is the neuter nom./acc. plural the question needs.

14. (A) One is a conjugated verb expressing the act of becoming; the other is a participle pressed into service as a noun naming the things that have become. They are not both verbs, not both nouns, and their categories are not swapped — the finite verb is भवन्ति and the noun is भूतानि.

15. (C) The word literally means 'that which has come to be', and a life-cycle verse selects exactly that 'living being' sense; the ghost sense, a later popular extension, does not fit the topic. The plural ending does not fix meaning, the ghost sense does exist in some Sanskrit texts, and भूत is neuter, not feminine.

16. (B) The voicing is triggered specifically by the voicedness of the following sound, so it applies before voiced consonants but not before unvoiced ones, where a different (assimilative) change occurs. It is therefore neither unconditional, nor sentence-final-only, nor limited to nasals.

17. (D) Before the UNVOICED च् the त् does not voice to द्; it assimilates toward च् instead, so तद्चित् is wrong (the correct sandhi is तच्चित्/तश्चित्-type). The other three all join a final त् to a voiced sound (भ् or a vowel), so they correctly voice to द् just as in अन्नाद्भवन्ति.

18. (D) The d-sound is the sandhi surface of an underlying ablative त्, so the genuine first word is अन्नात्; the द् is not an original ending, अद्- is not a prefix here, and there is no short-vowel stem अन्नाद. Restoring त् recovers the true ablative अन्नात्.

19. (C) Sandhi alters only the surface sound, never the grammatical case, so अन्नाद् remains the ablative 'from food'; the claim that it becomes nominative is false. The other three correctly state the t→d voicing, its voiced-भ् trigger, and the unchanged case.

20. (D) A final unvoiced stop before a voiced sound shifts toward the voiced/assimilated series — क्→ग् before the voiced vowel (वागीशः) and ट्→ण् before the nasal म् (षण्मासाः) — the same voicing/assimilation principle as त्→द् in अन्नाद्. The alternatives keep the stops unchanged, aspirate them, or double them, none of which is the attested sandhi.

21. (A) All three derive from √भू ('become'), so each step is an arising-from-a-source; the prefixes सम्- ('together') and समुद्- ('up/forth together') intensify or shade the becoming but never convert it into making or destroying. Nor are they mere spelling variants — one is a finite verb and two are derived nouns of the same root.

22. (A) Using √भू first as a verb (भवन्ति) and then as a noun (सम्भवः) repeats the becoming-idea across word-classes, reinforcing the single theme of emergence-from-a-source. It does not switch roots, does not push the arising into the past, and does not reverse the chain's direction.

23. (D) भूत is नपुंसकलिङ्ग (neuter), and the -आनि ending is the neuter plural, so the pupil is wrong on both gender and number. The two distractors each concede one false claim, and the last endorses both errors.

24. (A) The optative marker here is the -ईय- formant before the ending; जानाति shares the जा- and ends in -त् too, so neither of those distinguishes the moods, and prefix-absence is not an optative signal. Only the -ईय- sets जानीयात् apart from the present.

25. (C) जानीयात् is विधिलिङ् (advice), जानाति is लट् (present fact), ज्ञास्यति is लृट् (future), and जानीहि is लोट् (direct command). The other rows shuffle these labels or wrongly fold all four into the optative.

26. (C) Explaining an optative with another optative keeps the prescriptive 'ought' force intact, which is why the gloss-form matters and is not irrelevant. The gloss does not shift the mood to present, the tense to past, or the form to a command.

27. (D) वदेत् and पालयेत् are optatives of counsel, and the matching 'should know' is the optative जानीयात्; ज्ञास्यति merely predicts a future fact, not an obligation. जानाति states a present fact and अजानात् reports the past — neither expresses 'should'.

28. (A) The optative counsels what ought to be done, the imperative orders it directly, and the future simply foretells it — three distinct shades of force. The distractors equate them, swap the command/advice roles, or misassign prediction and command.

29. (D) The -य gerundive expresses 'to be X-ed', so ज्ञेय means 'to be known', exactly as कार्य means 'to be done'. It is neither a plural of the participle, nor the agent (ज्ञाता), nor the abstract noun (ज्ञानम्).

30. (C) A gerundive takes its agent in the instrumental (सर्वैः) and means 'is to be known', so the passive-prescriptive sentence is correct. The others treat ज्ञेयः as a past verb, as a proper name, or as an active subject — none of which is the gerundive use.

31. (D) Each is a distinct derivation: an optative verb, a -य gerundive, an agent noun, and a past passive participle. The other rows scramble the four labels or wrongly call all of them past participles.

32. (B) The gerundive looks to the object as something that ought to be known (passive necessity), while the present participle looks to the agent doing the knowing (active, ongoing). The distractors reverse the two or wrongly make them the same type.

33. (C) बोद्धव्यः is the -तव्य gerundive ('to be/must be understood'), the structural twin of the -य gerundive ज्ञेयः. The other three are a present verb, a past verb and an abstract noun — none expresses the prescriptive 'must be understood'.

34. (B) ब्रह्म (neuter) is the impersonal Absolute, ब्रह्मा (masculine) the creator-god, and ब्रह्मन् a priestly officiant. The other rows misassign the genders and meanings or wrongly collapse the three into one.

35. (A) व्याप्त means 'pervaded/spread through', so सर्वव्याप्त is 'pervading all' — omnipresence, which fits a ground immanent in every link. Power, knowledge and mercy are different ideas (omnipotence, omniscience, compassion), not what सर्वव्याप्त states.

39. (B) Sanskrit neuter for ब्रह्म conveys that the Absolute is impersonal and beyond gender, not that it is an inert object; the chapter treats it as the conscious all-pervading ground (सर्वव्याप्तः परमेश्वरः). It is neither lifeless, nor feminine, nor plural.
