## Worked Solutions

**1. (D)** A rim point sweeps a closed circle, so the motion is circular, and because it repeats the same path at fixed intervals it is periodic. It never reverses to-and-fro about a mean position, so it is not oscillatory; nor does it travel along a straight line, so rectilinear is wrong.

**2. (D)** Total path is 144 + 96 = 240 m in 12 + 8 = 20 s, giving 240/20 = 12 m/s; the same value drops out of either leg alone (144/12 and 96/8), confirming the pace is steady. Averaging the two leg speeds or dividing only one leg by the whole time gives the wrong numbers.

**3. (C)** Multiply by 5/18: 360 x 5/18 = 100 m/s. Multiplying by 3.6 instead (1296) reverses the conversion, and dividing by 36 (10) or by 6 (60) applies a wrong factor.

**4. (A)** Take 6 km of trail so each leg is 2 km. Times are 2/2 = 1 h, 2/3 h and 2/6 = 1/3 h, totalling 1 + 2/3 + 1/3 = 2 h for 6 km, so 6/2 = 3 km/h. The plain mean of 2, 3 and 6 (3.67) is the equal-distance trap.

**5. (C)** Let each way be 150 km: out takes 3 h, back takes 2 h, so 300 km in 5 h gives 60 km/h. The plain mean (62.5) ignores that more time is spent on the slower leg.

**6. (D)** For a simple pendulum the period is set by the length alone; equal 1 m strings give equal periods. The bob's mass and the (small) release angle do not change it, so the mass and amplitude distractors are wrong.

**7. (C)** The dial that swings up and down tracks the instantaneous speed, so it is the speedometer; the counter that only climbs records cumulative distance, so it is the odometer. The swapped-label option reverses the two instruments.

**8. (C)** 30 m/s x 3.6 = 108 km/h, which exceeds the train's 100 km/h, so the swift wins. Dividing by 3.6 (8.3) or multiplying by 6 (180) misconverts the speed.

**9. (A)** Total distance 24 + 12 = 36 cm = 0.36 m over total time 8 + 4 + 6 = 18 s gives 0.36/18 = 0.02 m/s. Forgetting the 4 s pause (0.36/14 ~ 0.026, rounded toward 0.03) or ignoring one leg inflates the figure.

**10. (D)** The last segment rises 70 - 40 = 30 m in 12 - 10 = 2 s, a slope of 15 m/s, which is steeper than the first segment's 40/4 = 10 m/s. The flat part is at rest, and reading 40 off the axis instead of using the slope is the y-value trap.

**11. (B)** Equal 40 km chunks in equal 1 h intervals mean the speed is constant, the definition of uniform motion along a straight track. A growing cumulative total or a longer total path does not make speed change, and the rails here are straight.

**12. (A)** The needle repeats a to-and-fro stroke about a mean position at a fixed rate, which is oscillatory and hence also periodic. It does not trace a circle, and because the stroke repeats it is not non-periodic or random.

**13. (A)** P's slope is 45/5 = 9 m/s and Q's is 30/5 = 6 m/s, a difference of 3 m/s. Subtracting the distances (45 - 30 = 15) ignores that you must compare slopes, not endpoint heights.

**14. (C)** The whole trip must take 360/80 = 4.5 h. The first 240 km at 60 km/h already used 240/60 = 4 h, leaving only 0.5 h for the last 120 km, so 120/0.5 = 240 km/h. Splitting the remaining distance by the target speed (100 or 120) ignores how little time is left.

**15. (C)** Originally 90/45 = 2 s per oscillation; halving the period gives 1 s each, so in 90 s it makes 90/1 = 90 oscillations. Leaving the count unchanged ignores the shorter period, and 180 doubles a step too far.

**16. (D)** Convert every option to m/s. 1500 m/min = 1500/60 = 25 m/s. 84 km/h = 84000/3600 ~ 23.3 m/s, just below. 20 m/s is given directly. 0.4 km/min = 400 m/min ~ 6.7 m/s. The largest value is 25 m/s, so the fastest speed is 1500 m/min; the 84 km/h figure is a deliberately close runner-up that still converts to under 24 m/s, and the bare 20 m/s and the slow 0.4 km/min are clearly smaller.

**17. (C)** 60 s < 3600 s < 86400 s < 604800 s, so minute, hour, day, week is the increasing order. The other lists swap an hour below a minute or a week below a day.

**18. (D)** The object goes out 80 m, waits, then returns 80 m, so the path covered is 80 + 80 = 160 m even though it ends at the start. Reading 80 m counts only the outward trip, and 0 m mistakes net displacement for distance travelled.

**19. (B)** Speed is the slope, (42 - 12)/(9 - 3) = 30/6 = 5 m/s. Dividing 42 by 6 (7) uses the wrong rise, and 42/9 mixes a y-value with the wrong time.

**20. (C)** Total 50 + 60 + 70 = 180 km in 3 h gives an average of 60 km/h, but since unequal distances were covered in equal time intervals the motion is non-uniform. Calling it uniform contradicts the changing hourly distances; 180 is the total, not the average.

**21. (B)** Total distance 36 + 36 = 72 km over total time 2 + 3 = 5 h gives 72/5 = 14.4 km/h. The plain mean of the two leg speeds (18 and 12 give 15) over-counts the faster leg's contribution.

**22. (B)** The tip traces a complete circle and returns to the same spot at fixed intervals, making it circular and periodic. It never reverses to-and-fro (so not oscillatory), does not move in a straight line, and is far from random.

**23. (C)** Period scales with the square root of length, so to halve the period the length must be quartered (because the square root of 1/4 is 1/2). Halving the length only shortens the period by the square root of 1/2, not to half.

**24. (A)** 108 x 5/18 = 30 m/s, not 36, so that line is wrong. The other three check out: 18 x 5/18 = 5, 45 x 5/18 = 12.5, and 144 x 5/18 = 40.

**25. (D)** A crossing point shares the same time and the same distance value, so both objects are 30 m from the start at 6 s. Equal positions say nothing about their slopes, so their speeds need not match, and neither stops nor reverses.

**26. (A)** A periodic motion repeats at fixed intervals, so equal gaps between ticks make a dependable timekeeper. Random or non-uniform ticking would give unequal intervals, and the rectilinear label describes a path, not timing.

**27. (A)** Total distance 90 + 90 = 180 km over total time 1.5 + 0.5 + 1 = 3 h gives 60 km/h. Leaving out the 0.5 h park (180/2.5 = 72) is the trap of not counting rest time.

**28. (A)** The constant trickle of sand through the neck takes the same time each run, which is what makes the interval repeatable. The bulbs do not swing, the grains do not pulse, and sunlight plays no part.

**29. (B)** 72 km/h = 20 m/s, and 20 x 90 = 1800 m. Multiplying 72 by 90 directly (6480) forgets the unit conversion, while 1080 and 108 use the wrong factor.

**30. (B)** A rising line means distance keeps increasing, so the object moves forward; a slope that flattens means the speed is dropping. A falling line would be needed for backward motion, and a horizontal line for rest.

**31. (A)** Frequency is the number of oscillations per second, 24/36 = 2/3 per second. Dividing time by oscillations (36/24 = 1.5) gives the period, not the frequency, and the raw counts are not rates.

**32. (B)** Average speed is total distance over total time, so you read distance from the odometer and time from a clock. A single speedometer glance gives only one instant's speed, and a sundial measures neither distance nor a precise short interval.

**33. (B)** Each successive second covers less ground (7, then 5, then 3 m), so the speed is falling: non-uniform and slowing. Moving forward does not make it uniform, and shrinking gaps rule out speeding up.

**34. (C)** Distance is speed times time, 300000 x 500 = 150000000 km, i.e. 150 million km. Using 300000 alone ignores the travel time, and the other figures misplace the factor of 500.

**35. (C)** A's constant slope competes with B's steepening slope; since B's line bends upward it can become steeper than A and cross above it, ending ahead. Neither shape guarantees the lead the whole way, and equal starts do not mean equal positions thereafter.

**36. (D)** Total 200 + 300 = 500 m over 25 + 75 = 100 s gives 500/100 = 5 m/s. Averaging the two leg speeds (8 m/s and 4 m/s gives 6) over-weights the short fast leg.

**37. (D)** Slopes are 120/8 = 15 m/s and 72/8 = 9 m/s, whose ratio 15 : 9 simplifies to 5 : 3. The inverted ratio swaps the objects, and equal times do not make equal speeds.

**38. (A)** A flat line means the distance stays at 50 m, so no ground is covered and the speed is zero. Reading the 50 m height as a speed is the y-value trap, and the 7 s width is a time, not a speed.

**39. (B)** A shorter pendulum has a shorter period, so it completes more swings per minute; the 1 m one wins. A longer string actually swings less often, and the count does not scale directly with length.

**40. (A)** The flag does move back and forth, but the gusts make the intervals irregular, so it is not periodic. A clock pendulum, by contrast, repeats at fixed intervals; the flag neither traces a circle nor moves in a straight line.

**41. (B)** Total distance 100 + 140 = 240 km over total time 1 + 1/3 + 2 = 10/3 h gives 240 / (10/3) = 72 km/h. Ignoring the 20 min idle (240/3 = 80) over-states the average.

**42. (A)** Out takes 3/6 = 0.5 h, back takes 3/18 = 1/6 h, so total 6 km in 0.5 + 1/6 = 2/3 h gives 6 / (2/3) = 9 km/h. The plain mean of 6 and 18 (12) is the equal-distance trap.

**43. (B)** 36 mm = 0.036 m, and 0.036/6 = 0.006 m/s. Forgetting that millimetres are thousandths of a metre shifts the decimal point and yields the larger wrong values.

**44. (D)** Its period is 54/36 = 1.5 s, so in 3 minutes = 180 s it makes 180/1.5 = 120 oscillations. Reusing the 36 count or scaling by the wrong factor gives the other figures.

**45. (C)** The last segment climbs 90 - 30 = 60 m in 11 - 8 = 3 s, a slope of 20 m/s, beating the first segment's 30/3 = 10 m/s; the flat part is at rest. Reading the 30 m height as a speed is the y-value trap.

**46. (B)** A water clock depends only on the steady flow of water, so darkness and clouds do not stop it; those conditions defeat a sundial, which needs sunlight. Hence none of the sky-and-Sun conditions disable a water clock.

**47. (B)** For a fixed distance, less time means more speed, so the order of increasing time (Q 20 min, P 30 min, R 40 min) reverses to the order of decreasing speed: Q fastest, then P, then R. Equal distance does not mean equal speed when times differ.

**48. (D)** Times are 60/30 = 2 h, 60/60 = 1 h and 60/90 = 2/3 h, totalling 11/3 h for 180 km, so 180 / (11/3) = about 49 km/h. The plain mean of 30, 60 and 90 (60) is the equal-distance trap.

**49. (C)** 252 km/h = 252 x 5/18 = 70 m/s, and 70 x 25 = 1750 m. Multiplying 252 by 25 directly (6300) skips the conversion, and the other figures use the wrong factor.

**50. (C)** A horizontal line means the distance is not changing, so the object is at rest, not moving fast; that statement is false. The other three correctly describe slope as speed, steeper as faster, and a straight slant as uniform motion.

**51. (D)** 1.8 km = 1800 m and 30 min = 1800 s, so 1800/1800 = 1 m/s. Treating 30 min as 30 s (60 m/s) or as 0.5 h without converting to m/s yields the wrong values.

**52. (C)** Lengthening the pendulum lengthens its period, so each tick takes longer and the clock falls behind. Mass does not set the period, and a longer pendulum certainly still swings, just more slowly.

**53. (A)** Returning to distance zero on a from-start graph means the object came back to where it began. The rise-then-fall shape shows out-and-back motion, not continuous speeding up or a permanent stop, and circles are not represented this way.

**54. (B)** Slope is (30 - 10)/(5 - 1) = 20/4 = 5 m/s. Dividing 30 by 5 (6) or reading the final y-value (30) ignores that you need the change in distance over the change in time.

**55. (A)** By 11:00 the walker has covered 6 x 2 = 12 km and keeps gaining 6 km each hour, while the cyclist gains 12 km each hour, closing the 12 km gap at 12 - 6 = 6 km/h, which takes 12/6 = 2 h, i.e. at 1:00; the cyclist has then ridden 12 x 2 = 24 km, exactly reaching the town. The earlier times under-count the gap or the closing rate.

**56. (B)** To clear a post, the train travels its own length, 80 m, in 4 s, giving 80/4 = 20 m/s. Multiplying length by time (320) or dividing the wrong way gives the other figures.

**57. (A)** Both lines start together at the origin, and the steeper one rises faster, so car A is always ahead and the gap widens; the lines never cross again. A gentler line cannot be farther, and the gap is not constant.

**58. (D)** 63 x 5/18 = 17.5 m/s, not 18.5, so that one is wrong. The others are correct: 36, 90 and 126 times 5/18 give exactly 10, 25 and 35 m/s.

**59. (D)** Distance is 24540 - 24300 = 240 km over the full 3 h, giving 240/3 = 80 km/h. Subtracting the break from the time (240/2.5 = 96) ignores that average speed uses the total elapsed time, break included.

**60. (D)** Slope is (49 - 14)/(9 - 2) = 35/7 = 5 m/s. Dividing 49 by 7 (7) uses the wrong rise, and reading 49/9 mixes a y-value with the wrong time.
