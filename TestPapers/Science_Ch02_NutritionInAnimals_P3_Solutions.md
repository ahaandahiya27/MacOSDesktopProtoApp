## Worked Solutions

**1. (D)** Glucose entering the blood is absorption; its later use to build new body tissue (growth) is assimilation, so the correct pair in order is absorption then assimilation. 'Digestion then absorption' is wrong because the blood-appearance step is absorption, not digestion — digestion happened earlier, before glucose existed. 'Absorption then egestion' is wrong because growth uses the nutrient, it does not throw it out as waste. 'Assimilation then absorption' reverses the order — a nutrient must enter the blood (absorption) before cells can use it (assimilation).

**2. (B)** Glucose reaches the cells (so it was absorbed into blood) but the cells cannot use it — assimilation is the step that fails, while absorption clearly works. 'Absorption is failing' is wrong because glucose does reach the cells, proving it crossed into the blood. 'Digestion is failing' contradicts the statement that food is broken down normally. 'Egestion is failing' is irrelevant — the problem is the cells using nutrients, not removing undigested waste.

**3. (C)** Egestion (X) throws out undigested material that never entered cells, whereas excretion (Y) removes wastes such as carbon dioxide produced by cells — so X is egestion and Y is excretion. 'Both are egestion' is wrong because cell-made waste is excretion, a different process. The two 'swapped' options are wrong because they reverse which process handles undigested fibre versus cell-made waste.

**4. (A)** Ingestion just means taking food into the body, and both swallowing a frog and sipping nectar do exactly that, so it is ingestion. 'Digestion' is wrong because neither act is the chemical breakdown of food; that comes after. 'Absorption' is wrong because the mouth does not pass these foods directly into blood. 'Egestion' is wrong because food is going in, not out.

**5. (D)** The student swapped absorption and assimilation; since cells can only use nutrients after those nutrients have entered the blood, absorption must precede assimilation. 'Egestion before digestion' is wrong because you cannot remove undigested residue before food is even broken down. 'Ingestion last' is absurd — food must be taken in first. 'No error' is wrong because the swapped order breaks the cause-and-effect chain.

**6. (D)** An Amoeba is one cell, so it performs every step inside that cell using a pseudopodium and a food vacuole rather than dedicated organs — that is the correct picture. 'The human lacks organs' is backwards; humans have many organs. 'A tiny stomach and intestine' is wrong because a single cell has no such organs. 'Both have separate organs' is wrong because the Amoeba clearly does not.

**7. (D)** Saliva starts starch digestion in the mouth, stomach acid and pepsin start protein digestion, and fat breakdown chiefly begins in the small intestine with bile and pancreatic juice — so the order is starch, protein, fat. The other options misassign the starting organs: protein and fat do not begin in the mouth (only starch does), and the three do not all begin together in the stomach, since starch already started in the mouth and major fat digestion waits for the small intestine.

**8. (C)** Cutting teeth are incisors (8) and tearing teeth are canines (4), leaving 32 − 8 − 4 = 20 grinding teeth, which are the premolars and molars. '12, all molars' ignores the 8 premolars that also grind. '16, canines and incisors' is wrong because those are cutting/tearing teeth, not grinding. '8, all premolars' leaves out the 12 molars.

**9. (A)** Below the hard enamel lies the dentine, and deeper still is the pulp cavity holding the nerves; decay 'beneath the enamel but not at the nerves' is therefore in the dentine with the pulp still safe. 'Reached the pulp' is wrong because the nerves are untouched. 'Enamel only' contradicts 'beneath the enamel.' 'Root vs crown' confuses position in the jaw with the inward layering of a tooth.

**10. (C)** Crackers are starchy, and salivary amylase converts starch into simpler sugars, which tastes sweet — protein digestion starts later, in the stomach. 'Fat was digested' is wrong; saliva does not act on fat. 'Protein was digested' repeats the student's error — the enzyme in saliva works on starch, not protein. 'The tongue added sugar' is wrong; the sugar comes from breaking down the cracker's own starch.

**11. (B)** The wave-like muscular contractions called peristalsis push food along the food pipe even without gravity's help, which is why an upside-down astronaut can still swallow. 'Gravity' is contradicted by the upside-down, weightless setting. 'Absorption' is wrong; the food pipe does not absorb water into blood. 'Emulsification' involves bile acting on fat in the small intestine, not water in the food pipe.

**12. (D)** Stomach acid does two things — it kills many germs in the food and it makes the stomach acidic so pepsin can act on protein; the acid itself is not the digesting enzyme. 'Digests fats and cools food' is wrong; fat-droplet formation is bile's job and acid does not cool food. 'Absorbs sugars' is wrong; the stomach is not where sugars are absorbed. 'Only digests food' is the misconception being corrected.

**13. (A)** Mucus secreted by the stomach lining forms a protective barrier between the wall and the acidic, pepsin-rich juice, preventing self-digestion. 'No protein' contradicts the stem, which says the wall is mostly protein. 'Bile coats the stomach' is wrong; bile is made in the liver and acts in the small intestine. 'Made of bone' is wrong; the stomach wall is muscle and soft tissue, not bone.

**14. (B)** Acid both activates the right conditions for pepsin and kills germs, so too little acid most directly weakens protein digestion in the stomach and germ-killing. 'Starch cannot be digested' is wrong; starch digestion uses amylase in the mouth and small intestine, not stomach acid. 'Bile cannot break fat' is wrong; bile's action does not depend on stomach acid. 'Water cannot be absorbed' is unrelated to stomach acid.

**15. (C)** The stomach's semi-liquid output is called chyme, while the grass a ruminant brings back up to chew is called cud — two different things. Swapping the names (cud for the paste, chyme for the grass) is the classic mix-up and is wrong. 'Bile' is neither; bile is a liver secretion. 'Chyme for both' is wrong because cud is regurgitated grass in a ruminant, not the human stomach's paste.

**16. (C)** Length and villi together maximise the surface for absorbing nutrients and give enough time to finish digestion, which is the real purpose. 'Store waste' is wrong; storing undigested waste is the large intestine's role, not the small intestine's. 'Make it acidic' is wrong; the small intestine is not acidic. 'To churn' is wrong; churning is the stomach's job.

**17. (C)** Villi provide the surface for absorbing nutrients, so flattening them cuts absorption sharply and the person grows undernourished even while eating. 'Faster mouth digestion' is unrelated to the intestinal wall. 'Stronger stomach churning' is unrelated; the stomach is upstream and unaffected. 'More bile' has nothing to do with villi being flattened.

**18. (B)** Bile is produced by the liver, acts in the small intestine, and physically breaks fat into small droplets to expand the surface for enzymes — it needs no enzyme of its own. 'Made by the pancreas, into amino acids in the stomach' is wrong on the source, product (amino acids come from protein), and place. 'Turns fat into starch' is chemically impossible. 'Absorbs fat in the stomach' is wrong; bile does not absorb, and this happens in the small intestine.

**19. (D)** Because only fatty meals become hard to handle, bile (stored in the gall bladder) must mainly aid fat digestion. 'Made pepsin' is wrong; pepsin is a stomach enzyme for protein, not made by the gall bladder. 'Absorbed starch' is wrong and would harm starch meals, which are fine here. 'Bile digests proteins' contradicts the observation that protein meals are normal.

**20. (B)** Pancreatic juice is notable for containing enzymes for all three main food types at once, while juices like saliva (starch) or gastric juice (protein) are more limited. 'No enzymes' contradicts its enzyme-rich nature. 'Made in the mouth' is wrong; it is made by the pancreas and acts in the small intestine. 'Acts only on water' is wrong; it acts on carbohydrates, proteins and fats.

**21. (A)** Bile (the fat-emulsifying liquid) is made by the liver, and the enzyme-rich juice for all food types is from the pancreas. Swapping their products (pancreas-makes-bile, liver-makes-pancreatic-juice) is wrong. The stomach and salivary glands do not empty into the small intestine. The gall bladder only stores bile, not enzymes, and the liver does not make acid.

**22. (A)** The large intestine's job is to absorb water; absorbing too little leaves the residue watery (loose stools), and absorbing too much leaves it hard and dry (constipation). Reversing these effects is wrong. Vomiting acid and fat digestion are unrelated to large-intestine water handling. 'Unchanged' is wrong because water absorption is exactly what this organ does.

**23. (B)** Glucose passing through the villus into blood is absorption, and the cells then use it (assimilation) after it is carried there. 'Digestion at the villus, then egested' is wrong on both counts; glucose is already digested, and useful glucose is not thrown out. 'Egestion then ingestion' reverses everything. 'Assimilation at the villus' is wrong; assimilation happens in the cells, after absorption, and glucose is not digested again.

**24. (A)** Cows digest grass because microbes in their rumen break down cellulose and they re-chew cud; humans have neither, so they get little from grass. 'No stomach' is false; humans have a stomach. 'Grass needs no digestion' is wrong; its cellulose must be broken down. 'Human digests grass in the mouth' is wrong; humans cannot digest cellulose anywhere.

**25. (C)** Cellulose is first broken down by microbes in the large rumen, and the human-stomach-like acid-and-enzyme digestion occurs in the abomasum. Swapping rumen and abomasum is the classic error. The reticulum and omasum are the other chambers and are not where the human-like acid digestion mainly occurs.

**26. (D)** Re-chewing grinds the grass finer, increasing surface area so microbes and enzymes act more effectively on the tough cellulose. 'Turns cellulose into protein' is false; chewing does not transmute one nutrient into another. 'Sweeter, absorbed in the mouth' is wrong; cows do not absorb sugar in the mouth. 'Removes the need for a stomach' is wrong; the stomach chambers are essential.

**27. (A)** Amoeba digests its meal inside a food vacuole and later expels the undigested residue as egestion when the vacuole reaches the surface. 'Gall bladder' and 'excretion of cell waste' are wrong; Amoeba has no gall bladder and this is undigested food (egestion), not cell-made waste. 'Villus / absorption' is wrong; villi are human structures and the leftover is removed, not absorbed. 'Rumen / assimilation' is wrong; rumens are in ruminants and the leftover is not used by the cell.

**28. (A)** In both, digestion happens in an enclosed space outside the body's cells — the food vacuole in Amoeba and the cavity of the stomach and intestine in humans. 'Inside the blood' is wrong; the blood carries absorbed nutrients, it does not digest them. 'On the skin surface' is wrong; humans do not digest food on their skin. 'Inside the brain' is unrelated to digestion.

**29. (D)** Being single-celled, the Amoeba forms structures like pseudopodia on demand instead of keeping fixed organs as humans do. 'Permanent organ for every step' contradicts the temporary nature of the pseudopodium. 'Digests outside its body' is wrong; digestion happens inside the food vacuole. 'Cannot move' is wrong; the pseudopodia also let it move and engulf food.

**30. (A)** Cells release energy from glucose mainly in their mitochondria, and using absorbed nutrients this way is part of assimilation. 'Villi / absorption' is wrong; villi absorb nutrients, they do not burn them, and absorption is the prior step. 'Food vacuoles / digestion' is wrong; food vacuoles are Amoeba structures, and burning glucose is not digestion. 'Salivary glands / ingestion' is unrelated to releasing energy in cells.

**31. (D)** Humans have just two sets of teeth in a lifetime: the milk teeth (about 20) appear in early childhood and begin to fall out around age 6–8, and are replaced by a permanent set of up to 32. So the child's lost front tooth is a milk tooth giving way to a permanent one, and the parent's larger count is the completed permanent set. (A) is wrong because teeth are replaced only once, not continuously year on year. (B) is wrong because the baby's teeth are milk teeth and do change. (C) is wrong because teeth do not grow from chewed food; the extra adult teeth come with the permanent set.

**32. (D)** The food pipe's muscular walls squeeze in waves (peristalsis), moving food along even when gravity cannot help. 'Rigid like a bone' is wrong; it is muscular and flexible. 'Absorbs food into blood' is wrong; the food pipe does not absorb nutrients. 'Filled with bile' is wrong; bile is in the small intestine, not the food pipe.

**33. (B)** The chain starts with sugar feeding bacteria, so removing the sugar by brushing breaks the chain and prevents acid attack. 'Eating more sugar' would feed the bacteria more, worsening it. 'Replace lost enamel' is wrong; brushing cannot regrow enamel. 'Avoiding chewing' does nothing about the sugar the bacteria feed on.

**34. (B)** Tooth shape matches diet — sharp pointed teeth tear meat, broad flat teeth grind plant food. 'Larger animals always flatter' is false; shape tracks diet, not size. 'Identical teeth' contradicts the stated difference. 'Random and unrelated' ignores the clear food-to-tooth match.

**35. (B)** Salivary amylase starts breaking starch into sugar in the mouth, but the food is swallowed within seconds — long before the change is complete — and the stomach's acid then halts the amylase. Starch digestion is finished only in the small intestine, where the pancreatic juice and the intestinal juice act on it. (A) is wrong: the stomach's acid and pepsin act on protein, not starch, and the acid in fact stops amylase. (C) is wrong: the mouth only begins starch digestion; food is swallowed before it can finish there. (D) is wrong: the large intestine absorbs water and does not complete digestion.

**36. (D)** Saliva's amylase acts on starch, gastric pepsin on protein, and bile emulsifies fat — the correct matching. The two 'rotated' options misassign each secretion to the wrong food type. 'All act on starch' is wrong; only saliva acts on starch, while gastric juice handles protein and bile handles fat.

**37. (B)** Food is digested in the gut cavity, then absorbed nutrients travel by blood to cells where they are used — keeping the two places separate. 'Digestion inside cells' reverses it; cells use, not digest, the food. 'Digestion in the blood' is wrong; blood transports absorbed nutrients. 'Both in the mouth' is wrong; most digestion and all cell use occur far from the mouth.

**38. (A)** Bile emulsifies fat into droplets, so blocking bile most directly harms fat digestion. 'Starch' is wrong; bile is not a starch enzyme (amylase does that). 'Protein' is wrong; pepsin acts in the stomach, not via bile. 'All three equally' is wrong; bile is specifically tied to fat handling.

**39. (B)** Pseudopodia surround and enclose the food, just like a hand cupping a marble; a straw sucking juice is the opposite of engulfing, so it is clearly wrong. The option naming the straw as 'best' reverses the right answer. A knife slicing bread describes cutting, not engulfing, so it is not the best match. A sieve straining water describes filtering, which is also not engulfing.

**40. (C)** Food is churned in the stomach, then most nutrients are absorbed in the small intestine, and finally water is recovered in the large intestine — that order. The alternatives misplace the stomach after the small intestine, run the sequence backwards, or put the large intestine before the small intestine, none of which match the real path of food.

**41. (C)** Assimilation is the constructive use of absorbed nutrients by cells, while egestion is throwing out the undigested residue — opposite in purpose. The first distractor swaps the two definitions. The other two wrongly claim the terms mean the same thing, when one is using nutrients and the other is removing waste.

**42. (D)** Only the tube with saliva produced sugar, so something in saliva — the enzyme amylase — converted starch to sugar. 'Warmth alone' is ruled out because both tubes were warm but only Tube 1 changed. 'Water breaks starch' is ruled out because Tube 2 had water and no change. 'Naturally becomes sugar' is ruled out because the water tube showed no sugar.

**43. (C)** Villi multiply the absorbing surface, so the villi-lined gut absorbs more even at the same length. 'More acidic' is wrong; villi do not change acidity. 'Shorten the path' is wrong; villi add surface, they do not shorten the gut. 'Produce bile' is wrong; bile comes from the liver, not the villi.

**44. (C)** An indigestible pebble is taken in (ingestion) and later removed as undigested matter (egestion), with no digestion, absorption or assimilation. 'Digested into nutrients' is wrong; a pebble cannot be broken down by stomach acid into food. 'Assimilated into bones' is wrong; only absorbed nutrients can be assimilated. 'Excreted as cell waste' is wrong; this is undigested matter (egestion), not a waste made inside cells.

**45. (C)** The liver is the largest gland and makes bile, the gall bladder stores bile between meals, and the pancreas makes a juice acting on all three food types — in that order. 'Pancreas, rumen, liver' is wrong; the rumen is a cow's stomach chamber, not a gland, and the order is muddled. 'Liver, pancreas, gall bladder' wrongly makes the pancreas the storage sac. 'Gall bladder, liver, pancreas' wrongly makes the gall bladder the largest gland.

**46. (B)** Re-chewing the cud grinds grass finer, but the cellulose is actually broken down by microbes in the rumen, not in the mouth. 'Fully digest in the mouth' is the misconception being corrected. 'Only in the food pipe' is wrong; the food pipe transports, it does not digest cellulose. 'Do not digest at all' is wrong; ruminants do digest grass, thanks to rumen microbes.

**47. (A)** Both perform ingestion through egestion, but the Amoeba does it all in one cell while a human spreads the work across organs. 'Amoeba never digests' is wrong; it digests inside its food vacuole. 'Never egests' is wrong; it expels undigested matter at the cell surface. 'Human never assimilates' is wrong; humans clearly use nutrients to grow and repair.

**48. (D)** Stomach pepsin needs acid, but the small intestine's enzymes do not, so the chyme is made less acidic on entering the small intestine. 'Even more acidic' is wrong; not all enzymes like strong acid — the intestinal ones do not. 'Kept exactly as acidic' is wrong; the environment changes between organs. 'Dried out completely' is wrong; digestion needs a watery medium.

**49. (A)** Bile emulsification, enzyme action on fat, and absorption of the products all mainly happen in the small intestine. 'The stomach' is wrong; bile and the fat-digesting enzymes act in the small intestine, not the stomach. 'The mouth' handles only starch and does not digest or absorb fat. 'The large intestine' chiefly absorbs water, not fat products.

**50. (A)** Grass is rich in cellulose, which no mammal's own enzymes break down, so cows rely on rumen microbes — hence the extra chambers. 'Protein' is wrong; protein is not the special challenge of grass and is handled by ordinary digestion. 'Sugar dissolving instantly' is wrong; grass is not mainly sugar. 'Fat broken by the rumen' is wrong; the rumen's special job is cellulose breakdown by microbes, not fat.

**51. (B)** Absorption is nutrients entering the blood, so the second student is correct, and the first student is describing assimilation (cells using nutrients). 'The first is right, second describes digestion' reverses the truth and mislabels absorption as digestion. 'Both are right / same thing' is wrong; absorption and assimilation are distinct steps. 'Removal of waste' is egestion or excretion, not absorption.

**52. (C)** The long coiled tube where most nutrients are absorbed into the blood is the small intestine. 'Oesophagus' is wrong; it is the short food pipe where food spends only seconds to minutes. 'Stomach' is the churning bag, described separately. 'Large intestine' mainly absorbs water, not most nutrients.

**53. (A)** Pseudopodia serve a double duty: they let the Amoeba move and also engulf food. 'Producing bile' is wrong; Amoeba has no liver or bile. 'Absorbing oxygen only' is wrong; pseudopodia are not solely breathing organs and they do capture food. 'Storing waste forever' is wrong; undigested matter is egested, not stored permanently.

**54. (C)** Digested sugar is mainly absorbed in the small intestine, while most remaining water is absorbed later in the large intestine. The reversed option misplaces both. 'Both in the stomach' is wrong; the stomach mainly churns and starts protein digestion. 'Both in the mouth' is wrong; the mouth neither absorbs sugar nor most water.

**55. (B)** Peristalsis pushes food down the food pipe, so stopping it most directly blocks food from reaching the stomach. 'Digested faster in the stomach' is wrong; stomach digestion is unaffected by the food pipe's muscle waves. 'More water absorbed' in the large intestine is unrelated. 'Starch digestion in the mouth stops' is wrong; salivary amylase still works in the mouth.

**56. (D)** Pancreatic juice carries enzymes for carbohydrates, proteins and fats, so losing it impairs digestion of all three in the small intestine. 'Only starch in the mouth' is wrong; that is salivary amylase, which is normal here. 'Only protein in the stomach' is wrong; that is gastric pepsin, also normal. 'Only water absorption' is wrong; that is the large intestine, unrelated to pancreatic enzymes.

**57. (D)** Canines are sharp, pointed teeth used for tearing, while the broad premolars and molars grind, so the student is correct. The friend is wrong because canines are not flat grinders. 'Incisors for grinding' is wrong; incisors cut. 'Molars for cutting' is wrong; molars grind.

**58. (B)** By the time molecules reach the villi they are already digested; the villi's job is to absorb them into the blood, not to digest. 'Digest and store permanently' is wrong on both counts; villi absorb, they do not store. 'Digest but not absorb' is the reverse of their true role. 'Only churn food' is wrong; churning is the stomach's job, not the villi's.

**59. (C)** Cud is partly digested grass regurgitated from the stomach and re-chewed, not new grass taken in from outside. 'Fresh grass a second time' is wrong; the cud comes back up from the stomach. 'Chews its own saliva' is wrong; it chews regurgitated grass. 'Grass that grew inside the stomach' is impossible; grass does not grow inside an animal.

**60. (A)** Protein digestion begins in the stomach, fat digestion is completed in the small intestine, and most water is recovered in the large intestine — that order. 'Mouth; stomach; small intestine' is wrong; protein does not begin in the mouth and water is not chiefly recovered in the small intestine. The remaining options jumble the organs so each role is in the wrong place.
