## Worked Solutions

**1. (B)** The angles total 180°, split as 2k + 3k + 5k = 180°, so k = 18°; the angles are 36°, 54° and 90°. The largest is 90°, and a bisector halves it into two angles of 45° each. The 90° choice is the whole angle (not halved); 36° and 30° come from halving a wrong angle.

**2. (C)** The interior angle at R = 180° − 140° = 40°; the interior angle at Q = 180° − 130° = 50°. Then ∠QPR = 180° − 40° − 50° = 90°. Adding the two interior angles (40°+50°=90°) and stopping there gives the trap value; 50° and 40° are the other two interiors.

**3. (A)** The third side x satisfies 18 − 7 < x < 18 + 7, i.e. 11 < x < 25. The smallest whole number is 12, the largest is 24, and 12 + 24 = 36. Using ≤ at the ends would wrongly give 11 and 25 (sum 36 too, but for the wrong reason) — the correct strict bounds give 12 and 24. The 25 distractor is the forbidden 18+7.

**4. (A)** All three sides differ, so it is scalene. Checking the largest side: 9² + 12² = 81 + 144 = 225 = 15², so the angle opposite the 15 cm side is exactly 90° — a right triangle. Acute would need the square of the longest to be LESS than the sum of the other squares; obtuse would need it greater; here it is equal. No two sides are equal, so it is not isosceles.

**5. (B)** Sum: (3x+12)+(2x−6)+(4x−6) = 9x = 180, so x = 20. Then ∠A = 72°, ∠B = 34°, ∠C = 74° (check 72+34+74 = 180). The largest is ∠C = 74°. The 72° distractor is ∠A (the second-largest), 34° is the smallest, and 70° is a slip.

**6. (A)** The third angle is 180° − 38° − 109° = 33°. The longest side lies opposite the largest angle (109°) and the shortest opposite the smallest (33°), giving 33° < 38° < 109° for the side order shortest to longest. Reversing the chain pairs the longest side with the smallest angle, which is the classic inversion error.

**7. (C)** The exterior angle equals the sum of the two remote interiors: ∠ACD = ∠A + ∠B, so 5∠A = ∠A + 24°, giving 4∠A = 24° and ∠A = 6°. Treating the exterior as equal to ∠A alone, or as the adjacent interior, breaks the relation; 30° would be 5∠A, not ∠A.

**8. (A)** Valid third side x satisfies 7 < x < 33. For isosceles, the third side must equal one of the given sides: x = 20 (valid, since 7<20<33) or x = 13 (valid, since 7<13<33). Both lie in range, giving 2 values. Forgetting that BOTH equalities are allowed gives 1; the answer is not 0 because both 13 and 20 are in range.

**9. (B)** The three exterior angles of any triangle sum to 360°: 7y + 5y + 6y = 18y = 360, so y = 20. The exterior angle at C is 6y = 120°, so the interior angle at C is its supplement, 180° − 120° = 60°. The 120° distractor is the exterior (not interior) angle at C; 40° and 80° are the interiors at A and B.

**10. (D)** With equal sides 8 and 8, the apex angle (opposite b) is a right angle when b² = 8² + 8² = 128, i.e. b = √128 ≈ 11.31, and obtuse when b² > 128. Since 11² = 121 < 128 but 12² = 144 > 128, the smallest whole-number base giving an obtuse apex is 12. b = 11 still gives an acute apex; b = 16 is degenerate (8 + 8 = 16 fails the inequality).

**11. (B)** In right triangle ABH, ∠AHB = 90° and ∠B = 35°, so ∠BAH = 55°. Since ∠BAC = 90°, ∠HAC = 90° − 55° = 35°. (Equivalently, in triangle AHC, ∠AHC = 90° and ∠C = 55°, giving ∠HAC = 35°.) The 55° trap is ∠BAH, not ∠HAC.

**12. (A)** The angle between the two interior bisectors is ∠BIC = 90° + ½∠A. So 116° = 90° + ½∠A gives ½∠A = 26°, ∠A = 52°. Forgetting the +90° and writing 116 = ½∠A gives 232 (impossible); using 90 − ½∠A gives a negative; 64° would be ∠A if ∠BIC were 122°.

**13. (B)** Third side x satisfies 21 − 8 < x < 21 + 8, i.e. 13 < x < 29, so x runs over 14 to 28. The multiples of 3 in that range are 15, 18, 21, 24 and 27 — five values. Including 12 (just below the range) or 30 (just above) would give 6 or 7; 4 drops one valid multiple.

**14. (A)** Let ∠A = a. Then ∠B = a − 30 and ∠C = a − 54. Sum: 3a − 84 = 180, so 3a = 264, a = 88°. Since both differences are positive, ∠A is the largest at 88°. Then ∠B = 58° and ∠C = 34° (check 88+58+34 = 180). The distractors are the two smaller angles and a sign-slip.

**15. (A)** In a triangle that is right-angled at C, both base angles A and B are acute, so the foot of the perpendicular from C falls strictly inside AB, and by definition CH ⊥ AB. H is the midpoint of AB only when the triangle is also isosceles, which is not guaranteed; CH = half of AB is a special (isosceles) case; H falls outside only when the foot is dropped from an acute vertex of an OBTUSE triangle, not here.

**16. (D)** Angles are 45°, 60°, 75° (since 3k+4k+5k=180, k=15). The largest angle 75° = ∠C, so its opposite side c is longest; the smallest angle 45° = ∠A, so a is shortest. Hence c > b > a. The a > b > c choice inverts the rule (longest side opposite SMALLEST angle), the classic trap.

**17. (C)** Compare the square of the longest side (8² = 64) with the sum of the squares of the other two (5² + 6² = 25 + 36 = 61). Since 64 > 61, the angle opposite the longest side is obtuse, so the triangle is obtuse. Right would need 64 = 61; acute would need 64 < 61. The sides are all different, so not equilateral.

**18. (B)** ∠CBE is the exterior angle at B (equal to ∠A + ∠C) and ∠BCF is the exterior angle at C (equal to ∠A + ∠B). Adding: (∠A+∠C) + (∠A+∠B) = 112 + 124 = 236, i.e. 2∠A + (∠B+∠C) = 236. Since ∠B+∠C = 180 − ∠A, we get 2∠A + 180 − ∠A = 236, so ∠A + 180 = 236, ∠A = 56°. The 124° distractor is one exterior angle; 68° and 64° are the interior B and C.

**19. (B)** Third side x satisfies 30 − 11 < x < 30 + 11, i.e. 19 < x < 41, so x runs over the whole numbers 20 through 40 — that is 21 values in all. Among 20..40 the even values are 20, 22, …, 40 (11 of them) and the odd values are 21, 23, …, 39 (10 of them). Evens exceed odds by 11 − 10 = 1. The 21 distractor is the total count; 0 wrongly assumes equal parity counts on a 21-long run.

**20. (A)** In an isosceles triangle with AB = AC, the median from the apex A is also the altitude, so ∠AMB = 90°. In triangle ABM, ∠BAM + ∠B + ∠AMB = 180°, giving 27° + ∠B + 90° = 180°, so ∠B = 63°. The 27° distractor confuses ∠B with ∠BAM; 54° is the full apex angle 2×27°.

**21. (D)** Sum: (2x+15)+(x+20)+(3x+25) = 6x + 60 = 180, so x = 20. Then ∠A = 55°, ∠B = 40°, ∠C = 85°. All three are below 90°, so the triangle is acute. The largest, 85°, is close to but not 90°, so it is not right; none exceeds 90°, so not obtuse.

**22. (C)** 5k+12k+13k = 30k = 60, so k = 2; sides are 10, 24, 26 cm. Test the longest: 10² + 24² = 100 + 576 = 676 = 26², so the triangle is right-angled with longest side 26 cm. Acute/obtuse fail the equality test; 20 cm would be 10k, not the longest.

**23. (D)** (9,9,17): 9+9=18>17 ✓ valid. (6,7,13): 6+7=13, not greater than 13, so degenerate ✗. (8,15,17): 8+15=23>17 ✓ valid. (5,5,11): 5+5=10<11 ✗. Two valid. The trap is counting (6,7,13) as valid because the sum equals the third side, but equality fails the strict inequality.

**24. (B)** The exterior angle ∠ACD = ∠A + ∠B = 64° + 48° = 112°. Its bisector splits it into two equal parts of 112° ÷ 2 = 56° each. The 112° distractor is the whole exterior angle (not halved); 28° halves a wrong base; 44° is unrelated.

**25. (B)** Since AB = AC, the base angles are equal: ∠B = ∠C = (180 − 80)/2 = 50°. In triangle ABD, AD = BD makes it isosceles with ∠DAB = ∠B = 50°. Then ∠DAC = ∠BAC − ∠DAB = 80° − 50° = 30°. The 50° distractor is ∠DAB itself; 20° and 80° come from misassigning the equal angles.

**26. (D)** With equal sides 14 and 14, the angle opposite the third side b is acute when b² < 14² + 14² = 392, i.e. b < √392 ≈ 19.8. The two base angles are always acute, so the triangle is acute exactly when b² < 392, giving b ≤ 19 (19² = 361 < 392 but 20² = 400 > 392). The largest whole b is 19. b = 20 makes the apex obtuse; 27 is near the degenerate bound.

**27. (C)** Let ∠C = c. Then ∠A = 3c and ∠B = 3c + c − 20 = 4c − 20. Sum: 3c + (4c − 20) + c = 8c − 20 = 180, so 8c = 200, c = 25°. Then ∠A = 75°, ∠C = 25°, ∠B = 4(25) − 20 = 80° (check 75+80+25 = 180). The 75° distractor is ∠A, not ∠B; 100° drops the −20°.

**28. (A)** First triangle: longest side 12, and 7² + 8² = 49 + 64 = 113 < 144 = 12², so the angle opposite 12 is obtuse → obtuse triangle. Second triangle: all of 50°, 60°, 70° are below 90° → acute. So the first is obtuse and the second is acute. The first is not right (113 ≠ 144); the second has no 90° angle.

**29. (C)** Let ∠B = ∠C = b, so ∠A = 180 − 2b. BD bisects ∠B, so ∠DBC = b/2. In triangle BDC the angles ∠DBC = b/2, ∠C = b and ∠BDC = 105° sum to 180°: b/2 + b + 105 = 180, giving 3b/2 = 75, so b = 50°. Then ∠A = 180 − 2(50) = 80°. The 50° distractor is each base angle b; 30° is the half-angle remainder; 70° is a slip.

**30. (C)** Since 5² + 12² = 25 + 144 = 169 = 13², the triangle is right-angled at the vertex opposite the 13-side. In a right triangle, two of the altitudes are the legs themselves and all three altitudes pass through the right-angle vertex, so the orthocentre is exactly that vertex. The orthocentre lies outside only for an obtuse triangle; the midpoint of the hypotenuse is the circumcentre, not the orthocentre.

**31. (A)** The third side x satisfies 7 < x < 25, so the perimeter 25 + x lies in 32 < perimeter < 50. We also need perimeter > 40 and whole. The smallest whole number above 40 within (32,50) is 41 (corresponding to x = 16, which is in range). 40 is not greater than 40; 26 ignores the >40 condition.

**32. (B)** The angle between the two interior bisectors is ∠BIC = 90° + ½∠A = 90° + ½(100°) = 140°. (The fact that ∠B = ∠C is extra information not needed for this formula.) The 40° distractor is the base angle ∠B itself; 80° is ∠B + ∠C; 130° uses a wrong half-angle.

**33. (C)** Three consecutive numbers summing to 9 are 2, 3, 4 (and 2+3 = 5 > 4, so it is a valid triangle). Test the longest: 2² + 3² = 4 + 9 = 13, while 4² = 16. Since 16 > 13, the angle opposite the 4 cm side is obtuse, so the triangle is obtuse. Acute would need 16 < 13; right would need 16 = 13; the sides are unequal so not equilateral.

**34. (B)** Let ∠B = b, ∠A = 2b, ∠C = 2b + b + 30 = 3b + 30. Sum: 2b + b + (3b + 30) = 6b + 30 = 180, so 6b = 150, b = 25°. Then ∠A = 50°, ∠B = 25°, ∠C = 105° (check 50+25+105 = 180). The largest is ∠C = 105°. The 75° distractor is ∠A + ∠B; 90° and 120° come from arithmetic slips.

**35. (D)** The triangle is obtuse (140° > 90°). When an altitude is dropped from a vertex of an acute angle in an obtuse triangle, its foot lands on the EXTENSION of the opposite side, i.e. outside the triangle. Only the altitude from the obtuse vertex lands inside. Feet at midpoints would be for medians; the feet do not coincide with the obtuse vertex.

**36. (A)** Triangle ADE has AD = AE, so it is isosceles with apex angle ∠A = 36°. The two base angles are equal and sum with 36° to 180°: each = (180 − 36)/2 = 72°. So ∠ADE = 72°. The 36° distractor is the apex; 108° is the supplement of a base angle; 54° halves wrongly.

**37. (C)** The third side x satisfies 13 < x < 25, so smallest = 14, largest = 24. Their product is 14 × 24 = 336. Using 13 × 25 (the forbidden equal-sum endpoints) gives 325; 322 = 14 × 23 (wrong largest); 350 = 14 × 25 mixes one wrong endpoint.

**38. (A)** The exterior angle at A is 180° − ∠A, and it equals 3∠A, so 180 − ∠A = 3∠A, giving 4∠A = 180, ∠A = 45°. Then ∠B + ∠C = 135°, and with ∠B = 2∠C we get 3∠C = 135°, so ∠C = 45° (and ∠B = 90°). The 90° distractor is ∠B; 30° and 60° come from misusing the exterior relation.

**39. (D)** Two angles are equal (30° and 30°), so the sides opposite them are equal — the triangle is isosceles. One angle is 120° > 90°, so it is obtuse. It cannot be scalene (two equal angles force two equal sides), nor acute (120° exceeds 90°), nor equilateral (the angles are not all 60°).

**40. (A)** ∠C = 180 − 90 − 30 = 60°. The bisector of the right angle at A gives ∠BAD = 45°. In triangle ABD, ∠ADB = 180 − ∠B − ∠BAD = 180 − 30 − 45 = 105°. The 75° distractor is the supplement ∠ADC; 45° is ∠BAD; 60° is ∠C.

**41. (C)** With equal sides 25, the base b satisfies 0 < b < 50 for a triangle. The two base angles are always acute, so the triangle is acute exactly when the apex angle (opposite b) is acute, i.e. b² < 25² + 25² = 1250, so b < √1250 ≈ 35.36, giving b ≤ 35. Combined with b ≥ 1, the whole numbers 1 through 35 work — 35 values. b = 36 makes the apex obtuse; 49 counts up to the degenerate bound.

**42. (D)** ∠A = 180 − 70 − 60 = 50°, so its bisector gives ∠DAC = 25°. In triangle ADC, ∠ADC = 180 − ∠DAC − ∠C = 180 − 25 − 60 = 95°. The 85° distractor is the supplement ∠ADB; 25° is ∠DAC; 60° is ∠C.

**43. (C)** Since ∠A + ∠B + ∠C = 180°, we have ∠B + ∠C = 180 − ∠A. Substituting into the given relation: ∠A = (180 − ∠A) − 40 = 140 − ∠A, so 2∠A = 140, ∠A = 70°. The 110° distractor is ∠B + ∠C; 40° and 90° are arithmetic traps.

**44. (B)** Isosceles third sides are 11 (sides 11, 11, 13) or 13 (sides 11, 13, 13); both satisfy 2 < x < 24, so both are valid. Case 11, 11, 13: longest 13, and 11² + 11² = 242 > 169 = 13² → acute. Case 11, 13, 13: longest 13, and 11² + 13² = 290 > 169 → acute. Both are acute, hence not obtuse — count is 2. The trap is assuming one of them must be obtuse.

**45. (D)** Let ∠A = 2∠B = 6∠C = k. Then ∠A = k, ∠B = k/2, ∠C = k/6. Sum: k + k/2 + k/6 = (6k + 3k + k)/6 = 10k/6 = 5k/3 = 180, so k = 108°. The largest is ∠A = 108°. Then ∠B = 54°, ∠C = 18° (check 108+54+18 = 180). The 54° distractor is ∠B; 90° and 120° are slips.

**46. (D)** Acute means ALL three angles below 90°. (44,46,90): has a 90°, right not acute. (60,60,60): all below 90° → acute ✓. (80,80,20): all below 90° → acute ✓. (100,39,41): 100° > 90° → obtuse. So exactly 2 are acute. The trap is counting the 90° triple as acute.

**47. (A)** Base angles: ∠B = ∠C = (180 − 20)/2 = 80°. BD bisects ∠B, so ∠ABD = 40°. In triangle ABD, ∠A = 20° and ∠ABD = 40°, so ∠BDA = 180 − 20 − 40 = 120°. The 100° distractor is the supplement ∠BDC; 80° is the full base angle; 40° is ∠ABD.

**48. (A)** The third side x satisfies 50 − 11 < x < 50 + 11, i.e. 39 < x < 61. The whole numbers are 40 through 60 inclusive, a count of 60 − 40 + 1 = 21. Using ≤ at the ends would wrongly add 39 and 61; 39 is the difference (a tempting but wrong endpoint count).

**49. (D)** Angles 1k+2k+3k = 180 give k = 30°, so 30°, 60°, 90° — a right triangle. The longest side lies opposite the 90° angle (the largest), not opposite the smallest. The 7 cm smallest side faces the 30° angle. So it is right-angled with the longest side opposite the right angle.

**50. (B)** The interior angle ∠ACB = 180° − 50° − 70° = 60°. The exterior angle ∠ACD = ∠A + ∠B = 50° + 70° = 120° (also its supplement 180° − 60° = 120°). So the exterior exceeds the interior by 120° − 60° = 60°. Treating the exterior as equal to the interior, or reversing which is larger, are the traps; 120° is the exterior angle, not the difference.

**51. (C)** Since 10² + 24² = 100 + 576 = 676 = 26², the triangle is right-angled at the vertex opposite the 26 cm side (the hypotenuse). From that right-angle vertex the altitude to the hypotenuse is a true perpendicular landing strictly inside the triangle. It is not obtuse (the square test gives equality, not greater-than), the sides are unequal so it is not isosceles, and 10 + 24 = 34 > 26 so the inequality holds.

**52. (D)** ∠BIC = 90° + ½∠A = 90° + 42° = 132°. The excess over ∠A is 132° − 84° = 48°. The 6° distractor is 90 − ½∠A; 42° is ½∠A; 90° forgets to subtract ∠A.

**53. (C)** If the third side c is the hypotenuse (longest), then c² = 12² + 16² = 144 + 256 = 400, so c = 20 cm, which satisfies 4 < c < 28. The 28 distractor is the forbidden sum; 4 is the difference; 11 would be a leg case (√(16²−12²) is not whole here).

**54. (D)** The bisector of ∠B gives ∠ABD = 30°. In triangle ABD, ∠A = 50°, ∠ABD = 30°, so ∠BDA = 180 − 50 − 30 = 100°. The 80° distractor is its supplement ∠BDC; 70° is ∠C; 110° is a slip.

**55. (D)** The three exterior angles of any triangle sum to 360°. So exterior at C = 360 − 115 − 125 = 120°. (Equivalently, interiors at A and B are 65° and 55°, so interior C = 60° and its exterior is 120°.) The 240° distractor is 115 + 125; 60° is the interior angle at C, not its exterior.

**56. (C)** Sum: (x+5) + (3x−25) + (2x+20) = 6x = 180, so x = 30. The angles are 35°, 65° and 80° (check 35+65+80 = 180). The largest is 80°. The 65° distractor is the middle angle, 35° the smallest, and 95° a slip.

**57. (B)** In an isosceles triangle with AB = AC, the perpendicular from the apex A bisects the base, so DC = BD = 9 cm. With E between D and C and DE = 4 cm, EC = DC − DE = 9 − 4 = 5 cm. The 13 distractor adds instead of subtracts; 9 cm is DC alone; 4 cm is DE.

**58. (D)** Using the given external-bisector relation ∠BJC = 90° − ½∠A = 90° − 20° = 70°. (Contrast with the INTERNAL bisectors, which give 90° + ½∠A = 110°.) The 110° distractor is exactly that internal-bisector value, the classic confusion; 20° is ½∠A; 90° forgets the subtraction.

**59. (C)** The valid third side satisfies 5 < x < 17. For isosceles the third side must equal 6 or 11; both 6 and 11 are in range. Case x = 6 (sides 6, 6, 11): longest is 11, and 6² + 6² = 72 < 121 = 11², so the angle opposite 11 is obtuse — isosceles AND obtuse. Case x = 11 (sides 6, 11, 11): longest 11, and 6² + 11² = 157 > 121, so it is acute. Hence only x = 6 works. The 5 distractor is the forbidden lower bound; 16 is acute.

**60. (B)** The exterior angle ∠ACD is the supplement of the interior base angle ∠ACB, so ∠ACB = 180° − 124° = 56°. Since AB = AC, the base angles are equal, so ∠ABC = ∠ACB = 56°. Then the vertex angle ∠BAC = 180° − 56° − 56° = 68°. The 56° distractor is each base angle; 62° halves the exterior; 124° is the exterior angle itself.
