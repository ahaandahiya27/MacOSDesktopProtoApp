1. A grammarian writes the inequality 'सेवा > every other धर्मः' to capture परमः. A second grammarian objects that this overstates the line. Who is right, and why?
(A) The second is right: परमः only means service is one good duty among equals, so any inequality at all overstates it.(B) The second is right: परमः means service comes first in the day's sequence, a temporal order the inequality cannot represent.(C) The first is right, but only because परमः means service is the sole duty, so the inequality should read 'सेवा = the only धर्मः'.(D) The first grammarian is right: a superlative of rank asserts exactly that no other duty surpasses service, which the strict inequality 'greater than each of the rest' encodes precisely.
2. Suppose Sanskrit had a comparative degree word परतरः ('higher than') alongside परमः ('highest'). Rewriting the thesis as सेवा हि परतरो धर्मः would change the meaning to:
(A) No change at all, since परतर and परम are exact synonyms in every context.(B) 'Service is the FIRST duty in time', because the comparative degree always carries a temporal sense.(C) 'Service is not a duty at all', because the comparative negates the predicate noun.(D) 'Service is the HIGHER duty' — a two-way comparison implying just one other duty service is being set above, weaker than ranking it over all.
3. Four students rank duties. Only one ranking is what सेवा हि परमो धर्मः actually licenses. Which?

4. A motto-writer wants 'truth is verily the supreme law' built slot-for-slot on सेवा हि परमो धर्मः (subject — emphatic — superlative-adjective — predicate-noun). The correct fill of the परमः slot is the word for:
(A) 'truth' — the subject, since the subject occupies the परमः slot.(B) 'verily' — the emphatic, since परमः itself carries the emphasis in the line.(C) 'law' — the predicate noun, since परमः and धर्मः are a single fused word.(D) 'supreme/highest' — the ranking adjective, agreeing with the predicate noun 'law'.
5. If someone argued 'since परमः means highest, सेवा हि परमो धर्मः must mean service is the highest-RANKING profession a person can hold', the precise error is that they:
(A) Read परमः as a temporal 'first', which is the only mistake in the sentence.(B) Read सेवा as 'salary', so the line becomes about pay rather than rank.(C) Made no error; 'highest-ranking profession' is exactly what धर्मः means here.(D) Read धर्मः as 'profession/post' rather than 'duty/right action', smuggling in the पदवी sense the chapter explicitly ranks below service.
6. The thesis ranks सेवा above other duties. A reader infers 'therefore the chapter forbids doing any other duty until all service is finished.' This inference is invalid because:
(A) It is actually valid; 'highest duty' does mean 'the duty to finish before all others'.(B) It is invalid because the chapter ranks service LOWEST, not highest.(C) Ranking a duty highest says nothing about a sequence of performance; 'highest in worth' does not entail 'must be completed first in time'.(D) It is invalid only because there are no other duties for service to precede.
7. A teacher removes हि and reads सेवा परमो धर्मः, then claims the meaning is now the opposite. Evaluate:
(A) She is right: without हि the sentence means 'service is NOT the highest duty'.(B) She is wrong about 'opposite': the assertion 'service is the highest duty' survives intact; only the confirming 'verily/indeed' force is lost, leaving a flatter but same-meaning claim.(C) She is right: without हि the sentence becomes a question, 'is service the highest duty?'(D) She is wrong, but only because हि is grammatically mandatory and the sentence is now ungrammatical.

9. A student says 'हि must mean the same as the English word "because", since both can introduce a reason.' Within this single line सेवा हि परमो धर्मः, the safest correction is:
(A) Here हि functions as a confirming emphatic within one clause ('verily'); it is not joining a reason-clause to a result-clause, so 'because' overfits.(B) Correct as stated: हि is exactly the conjunction 'because' and joins two clauses here.(C) हि means 'but', marking a contrast between सेवा and धर्मः.(D) हि means 'or', offering सेवा as one of several alternative duties.

11. Apply the very rule that gives परमो धर्मः to the join सेवकः + सेवते. The correct result is:
(A) सेवको सेवते — ओ appears, since any word boundary triggers it.(B) सेवकः सेवते — no ओ, because the following स् is a voiceless sibilant.(C) सेवकः सेवते changes to सेवकस् सेवते by adding स्.(D) सेवको सेवते — ओ appears because सेवते is a verb.
12. Consider परमः + आचारः. By correct sandhi this yields:
(A) परम आचारः — the विसर्ग drops and the preceding अ stays short before the following आ (it does not become ओ).(B) परमो आचारः — ओ forms because आ is a vowel and all vowels are voiced.(C) परमोऽऽचारः — ओ plus a double अवग्रह.(D) परमाचारः — the words fuse into one with आ.
13. The chapter form परमो धर्मः and the form रामोऽगच्छत् (रामः + अगच्छत्) both show ओ. The true difference is:
(A) There is no difference; both add an अवग्रह invisibly.(B) In परमो धर्मः the ओ is from a vowel merger, while in रामोऽगच्छत् it is from a consonant.(C) रामोऽगच्छत् is simply wrong; -अः never changes before अ.(D) Before धर्मः a voiced consonant follows so -अः → ओ with no अवग्रह; before अगच्छत् a short अ follows, which is absorbed and marked by अवग्रह (ऽ).
14. A student claims परमो in परमो धर्मः is the locative singular परमे that 'softened' to ओ. The decisive refutation is:
(A) Correct: परमो is indeed locative परमे, since locatives commonly end in ओ.(B) परमो is the dual परमौ shortened, agreeing with two duties.(C) परमो is परमः (nominative, agreeing with the nominative धर्मः) whose final विसर्ग became ओ by sandhi; a locative परमे would not agree with the nominative subject-complement धर्मः and would not arise here.(D) परमो is the neuter परमम् with its म् dropped before धर्मः.

27. Why can चिकित्सकः रोगिणः सेवते NOT mean 'the physician serves FOR the sick (on their behalf)' with रोगिणः read as a dative-like beneficiary?

Worked Solutions

1. (D) परमः is a superlative of RANK. 'Highest among duties' means precisely that for every other genuine duty, service outranks it — exactly the inequality सेवा > each other धर्मः. The first grammarian's notation is faithful. The claim that परमः leaves service merely 'one among equals' contradicts the very word 'highest' and would make the line empty. Reading परमः as a temporal 'first of the day' substitutes a time-order for a rank-order — the recurring परमः=time trap. And reading it as 'sole duty' (service is the ONLY one) over-reads the superlative into a uniqueness claim: a superlative ranks others below, it does not abolish them; other duties still exist.

2. (D) A comparative (-तर) compares two things and says one outranks the other; a superlative (-तम/परम) ranks one above ALL the rest. So परतरः would shrink the claim from 'highest of all duties' to 'the higher of two', a genuine weakening. The synonym option ignores the comparative/superlative distinction entirely. The 'first in time' reading recycles the परमः=time error and wrongly assigns it to degree-of-comparison. The negation reading is baseless: comparatives modify, they do not negate.

3. (A) 'Highest duty' places service at the apex with others genuinely below it — the only ranking the superlative supports. A tie for first contradicts 'highest' (a superlative singles out one). Placing service at the BOTTOM inverts परम ('supreme') into a base/foundation, a misreading of the word's direction. And a one-entry list mistakes the superlative for a uniqueness claim — परम ranks others below, it does not delete them.

4. (D) The four slots map as सेवा=subject ('truth'), हि=emphatic ('verily'), परमः=ranking adjective ('supreme'), धर्मः=predicate noun ('law'). So the परमः slot takes 'supreme'. Putting 'truth' there confuses subject with adjective. Putting 'verily' there confuses the ranking adjective with the emphatic particle हि. Treating परमः+धर्मः as one fused word ignores that they are an adjective agreeing with its noun, joined only by sandhi — परमः is independently the superlative.

5. (D) The misreading keeps परमः='highest' correct but corrupts धर्मः: in this chapter धर्मः is moral DUTY/right action, not a job-rank. Turning it into 'profession/post' imports the पदवी idea — the very outward distinction the chapter ranks below सेवा — so it inverts the lesson. परमः is read correctly here, so the 'temporal first' option misdiagnoses the fault. सेवा is untouched, so 'salary' is irrelevant. And 'no error' endorses the corruption of धर्मः.

6. (C) The inference slides from rank ('highest in worth') to temporal priority ('do first/finish before others') — the classic परमः rank-vs-time confusion, here disguised as a scheduling conclusion. A worth-ranking imposes no timetable. The 'actually valid' option commits exactly that slide. Saying the chapter ranks service lowest reverses परम. And 'no other duties exist' over-reads the superlative as a uniqueness claim, which it is not.

7. (B) हि is a confirming/causal emphatic ('verily, indeed'). Dropping it removes emphasis only; the core predication 'service IS the highest duty' is unchanged and fully grammatical. It never negates (so it cannot flip to 'not the highest'), never interrogates (so no question arises), and is not syntactically required (the sentence stands without it). Hence 'opposite' is false on every count, and the ungrammatical claim is also false.

8. (C) हि intensifies and confirms; 'indeed' captures that added force while keeping the same assertion. The interrogative rendering treats हि as a question marker (it never is). The negative rendering treats it as 'not' (it never is). 'Service and the highest duty' treats it as a conjunction 'and' (it is not a connective). Only 'indeed' matches the emphatic/confirming role.

9. (A) Although हि can have a causal flavour in longer passages, in this single-clause maxim there is no second clause for it to subordinate; it simply confirms/intensifies ('verily'). Calling it 'because' here invents a clause-link that the sentence does not contain. It is not adversative 'but' (no contrast is drawn between subject and predicate) and not disjunctive 'or' (no alternatives are listed). The emphatic reading is the only one the structure supports.

10. (A) विसर्ग from -अः becomes ओ only when the FOLLOWING sound is voiced. ग् is voiced, so परमो गुणः is correct. क्, त्, प् are all voiceless (unvoiced stops), before which -अः keeps its विसर्ग (परमः कालः, etc.) — it does NOT turn to ओ. The trap is assuming any consonant triggers ओ; the conditioning factor is voicing of the next sound, exactly as ध् (voiced) triggered परमो धर्मः.

11. (B) The -अः → ओ change requires a following VOICED sound. स् is a voiceless sibilant, so no ओ forms; सेवकः keeps its visarga before स् (the visarga-before-sibilant outcomes differ, but ओ is impossible here). The 'any boundary triggers ओ' option ignores the voicing condition. The 'add स्' option invents a change. The 'because it is a verb' option repeats the परमो धर्मः misdiagnosis — the trigger is the next SOUND's voicing, never the word's part of speech.

12. (A) Before a vowel OTHER than short अ, final -अः loses its विसर्ग and the words simply stand अ + (vowel): परमः + आचारः → परम आचारः. The ओ outcome is reserved for -अः before a VOICED CONSONANT (as in परमो धर्मः) and for -अः before short अ (giving परमोऽ...). Before आ there is no ओ — so 'परमो आचारः' wrongly extends the consonant rule to vowels, the double-अवग्रह form is spurious, and fusing into परमाचारः misapplies a long-vowel sandhi that does not occur here.

13. (D) Two distinct outputs of the same -अः source: (1) before a voiced CONSONANT (ध्) → ओ, written plainly: परमो धर्मः. (2) before a short अ → ओ with the following अ swallowed and shown by अवग्रह: रामोऽगच्छत्. So the difference is the अवग्रह, triggered by the following अ. Option two reverses which case is vowel-driven vs consonant-driven. 'No difference' ignores the अवग्रह. And '-अः never changes before अ' is plainly false — रामोऽगच्छत् is the standard result.

14. (C) धर्मः is nominative singular (the predicate noun), so its qualifying adjective must also be nominative singular: परमः. परमः + धर्मः → परमो धर्मः by विसर्ग-सन्धि. A locative परमे would clash in case with the nominative धर्मः and has no role here. The 'dual परमौ' option invents a number mismatch (one duty, not two). The 'neuter परमम् minus म्' option both mis-genders the masculine धर्मः and invents a non-existent म्-drop. Only the nominative-plus-sandhi account fits.

15. (D) व् is voiced, so बालकः + वदति → बालको वदति is right. The others begin with voiceless sounds — च् (चलति), प् (पतति), त् (त्यजति) — before which -अः does NOT become ओ. Each wrong option illegitimately extends the ओ-change to a voiceless onset, the exact trap behind परमो धर्मः: only a following voiced sound triggers ओ.

16. (C) The -अः → ओ change fires before any VOICED sound, which includes the voiced nasals and semivowels. न् is a voiced nasal, so नरः + नमति → नरो नमति is correct. The distractors all begin with voiceless sounds — ख् (aspirated voiceless stop), छ् (voiceless), and स् (voiceless sibilant) — before which -अः keeps its visarga and does NOT become ओ. As with परमो धर्मः before voiced ध्, the trigger is the voicing of the following sound, not its being a nasal, stop, or any particular class.

17. (C) मानवीय is a 'pertaining-to/befitting' taddhita meaning 'befitting a human', and such derivations regularly take वृद्धि of the first vowel (मानव itself shows मा-). The Sun-adjective on the same pattern with वृद्धि is सौर/सौर्य ('solar'). The distractors all swap in the WRONG suffix: -वत् ('like'), -इन् ('possessing'), -क (agent). मानवीय uses none of these; only the 'befitting/pertaining-to' taddhita (with वृद्धि) matches.

18. (A) An -ईय adjective agrees with its noun in gender, number and case. कर्माणि is neuter nominative PLURAL, so the adjective takes the neuter nom. pl. ending -आनि: मानवीयानि कर्माणि. Keeping masculine -आः fails gender agreement. The feminine -आ and neuter singular -अम् fail both gender and/or number. Agreement, not the lexical meaning, decides the ending.

19. (A) दया is feminine singular, so the -ईय adjective takes the feminine singular -आ: मानवीया दया, meaning compassion that befits a human, i.e. humane compassion. The masculine -अः and neuter -अम् fail gender agreement, and the plural -आः fails number agreement with the singular दया. The gloss 'befitting a human' is fixed by -ईय; the ending is fixed by agreement.

20. (B) मानवीय is a -ईय derivative; देशीय ('pertaining to a country') is built on the same -ईय suffix and is glossed correctly, so it is the right pair. The distractors falsely attribute मानवीय's job to other suffixes: -इन् (possessive/characterising), -वत् ('like'), and agent -क. None of those builds 'befitting/pertaining to'; only -ईय does. Each wrong option encodes the suffix-confusion the chapter warns against.

21. (D) Adding -ईय turns the noun मानव into the adjective मानवीय ('humane/befitting a human'); the -आः is the masculine nom. pl. ending agreeing with गुणाः ('qualities'), not a headcount of humans. So 'the humans' confuses the derived adjective with the base noun's plural. मानव is masculine (not feminine), and it certainly can be pluralised — so the other two diagnoses are also wrong. The real error is noun-vs-adjective.

22. (C) सेव् is आत्मनेपदी; the 2nd-person dual आत्मनेपद ending is -एथे, giving सेवेथे. The object 'two sick men' is accusative dual रोगिणौ (ण by णत्व). So युवां रोगिणौ सेवेथे is correct. सेवथः is a परस्मैपद ending (wrong voice). रोगिभ्यां is instrumental/dative dual, but सेव् governs the ACCUSATIVE, so the object must be रोगिणौ. सेवयथः injects a causative -अय and a परस्मैपद ending — doubly wrong.

23. (D) In a Sanskrit passive the agent goes to the INSTRUMENTAL (चिकित्सकेन), the former accusative object becomes the nominative subject (रोगिणः, here nom. pl.), and the verb agrees with it: सेव्यन्ते. Hence चिकित्सकेन रोगिणः सेव्यन्ते. Keeping the physician nominative and the sick accusative is just the active frame mislabelled. Reversing the roles (sick serve physician) inverts the meaning. Putting the agent in the dative (चिकित्सकाय) uses the wrong case — the passive agent is instrumental, not dative.

24. (A) A wish uses the optative (विधिलिङ्). For an आत्मनेपद verb the 3rd-sg optative ends in -एत: सेवेत. सेवतु is an imperative form and belongs to परस्मैपद, wrong on both mood and voice. सेवेत् carries the परस्मैपद optative ending; since सेव् is आत्मनेपदी it must be सेवेत (आत्मनेपद). सेविष्यते is future tense, not a wish at all.

25. (A) If the sick are now the agents, they take the nominative plural रोगिणः and the verb is 3rd-pl आत्मनेपद सेवन्ते; the served physician becomes the accusative object चिकित्सकम्. Hence रोगिणः चिकित्सकं सेवन्ते. Two nominatives (रोगिणः चिकित्सकः) leaves no object. रोगिभिः is instrumental, but the agent of an active verb is nominative, not instrumental. सेवन्ति is परस्मैपद, but सेव् is आत्मनेपदी — a voice error.

26. (B) सेव् conjugates in आत्मनेपद, where the 1st-sg present ending is -ए: सेवे. The -मि ending is परस्मैपद, so सेवामि is wrong for this verb. सेवसि is a परस्मैपद 2nd-sg form (also wrong person/voice). सेवयामि wrongly inserts the causative -अय; plain 'I serve' needs no causative. The fix is the आत्मनेपद सेवे.

27. (B) सेव् takes a direct ACCUSATIVE object; रोगिणः is the acc. pl. of रोगिन्, i.e. 'the sick' as the ones directly served. A 'for the sick / on their behalf' beneficiary would be dative रोगिभ्यः, which the sentence does not use. So the dative-beneficiary reading has no morphological support. सेव् does not govern the dative (refuting the first distractor), रोगिणः is not exclusively genitive (it is here accusative plural), and सेवते is active आत्मनेपद, not passive — so 'by the sick' is wrong too.

28. (A) The past imperfect (लङ्) prefixes the augment अ- to the stem and uses tense endings; for an आत्मनेपद verb the 3rd-sg is असेवत ('was serving'). असेवत् carries the परस्मैपद ending, but सेव् is आत्मनेपदी, so the आत्मनेपद form असेवत is required. सेवते is the present, not the past at all. असेवयत् inserts the causative -अय ('caused to serve') and also takes a परस्मैपद ending — wrong on two counts. The augment अ- plus the आत्मनेपद ending gives असेवत.

29. (A) णत्व (न→ण) needs a triggering र/ष earlier in the word with only permitted sounds between. रोग- HAS an र, so रोगिन् → रोगिणः. स्वामिन् has NO र or ष, so its न् stays: स्वामिनः. The trap 'every इन्-stem retroflexes' generalises णत्व illegitimately; it is conditioned by an र/ष trigger, not by the stem class. स्वामिभिः is instrumental, and स्वामी is the nominative singular — neither is the accusative plural.

30. (C) The genitive plural of इन्-stems is -आम्, and णत्व gives रोगिणाम् ('of the sick'). रोगिणः is the accusative/nominative plural, not genitive — a common confusion since both carry ण. रोगिभ्यः is dative/ablative plural ('to/from the sick'). रोगिषु is locative plural ('among/in the sick'). Only रोगिणाम् is the genitive plural.

31. (D) One physician = nominative singular चिकित्सकः; many sick = accusative plural रोगिणः. The other options mismatch number/case: चिकित्सकाः is plural (many physicians) with रोगिणम् singular (one sick); चिकित्सकम् is accusative (wrong case for the subject); चिकित्सकौ/रोगिणौ are dual (two of each). Only चिकित्सकः ... रोगिणः encodes 'one ... many'.

32. (B) रोगिन् is a single इन्-stem. Its nominative singular loses the न् and lengthens इ→ई, surfacing as रोगी; the oblique cases restore the न् (with णत्व), giving रोगिणम्, रोगिणः, etc. So the two forms are the same word in different cases. They are not separate stems, not a gender pair (the इन्-stem here is masculine throughout), and रोगिणम् is the genuine accusative — रोगीम् is not a valid form. The hidden न् is the key the nominative masks.

33. (D) The agent ('the does-er') takes -क like चिकित्सक: सेवकः ('one who serves'). The one CHARACTERISED BY a condition takes -इन् like रोगिन्: शोकिन् ('marked by sorrow'). So the consistent pair is सेवकः / शोकिन्. The other pairings swap the suffixes (making the server a 'characterised' noun or the sufferer an agent), violating the chapter's -क-for-agent, -इन्-for-characterised system.

34. (C) चिकित्सकः: agent -क ('one who heals'). रोगिन्: -इन् marks one CHARACTERISED by रोग. यन्त्रवत्: -वत् here = 'like' (manner of tireless action), NOT 'having'. मानवीय: -ईय = 'befitting/pertaining to a human'. Every distractor scrambles these — most dangerously by giving -वत् its 'possessive/having' sense (wrong here) or making -इन् an agent. Only the correct row keeps each suffix in its chapter role.

35. (B) -वत् has two senses: 'like/इव' (manner) and 'possessing/having'. यन्त्रवत् is the 'like' sense. गुणवत् ('possessing virtues') uses the OTHER, possessive sense, so it does not parallel यन्त्रवत्. वायुवत्, अग्निवत्, मेघवत् all mean 'like X' — the same manner sense as यन्त्रवत् — so they ARE good parallels. The question asks for the odd one out, which is the possessive गुणवत्.

36. (C) यन्त्रवत् is an adverb 'like a machine' (manner of tireless service); -वत् here is 'like', not a marker turning यन्त्र into a served object. To actually 'serve the machine' you would need the accusative यन्त्रम्. So the rendering both mistranslates -वत् and invents a non-existent object. The first distractor's verb 'fix' is wrong (सेवते is correct आत्मनेपद). The second wrongly gives -वत् the possessive sense and makes सेवते passive. 'No errors' endorses the mistranslation.

37. (C) A simile imports only the relevant point of comparison. यन्त्रवत् borrows the machine's tireless, steady, never-flagging action — NOT its coldness. The chapter pairs that stamina with मानवीयाः गुणाः (warmth/compassion), so the ideal is machine-like in endurance and human in heart; no contradiction. The critic mistakes a partial simile for a total identity. यन्त्रवत् never means 'cold indifference' here, and मानवीयाः गुणाः is not abandoned — both are explicitly held together.

38. (B) यन्त्रवत् = tireless, steady, repetitive action like a machine. The relief worker ladling for ten hours without flagging captures exactly that stamina. Owning a computer mistakes -वत् for the possessive 'having a machine'. Treating people coldly imports the machine's indifference, which the chapter does not borrow. A robot-staffed factory literalises 'machine' rather than using it as a simile for human tirelessness. Only the steady, untiring server fits.

39. (A) यन्त्री = यन्त्रिन्, an इन्-stem, which carries the possessive/characterising sense: 'one having/operating a machine' (a machinist). That is quite different from यन्त्रवत् ('like a machine', the tireless-manner simile). So swapping in यन्त्री would shift from a simile of stamina to a literal 'machine-owner', changing the praise entirely. It does not mean 'like' (-वत्'s job), is not the -ईय 'befitting' sense, and is active in sense, not passive.

40. (B) पदवी = the post/rank one holds ('Chief of Surgery'); उपाधिः = the qualification one earns by study (the M.D.). So (i)=पदवी, (ii)=उपाधिः. The chapter ranks सेवा above both these outward distinctions. The first distractor swaps the two definitions; the second collapses both into उपाधिः and inverts the ranking; the third collapses both into पदवी and falsely equalises post with service. Only the correct mapping plus 'service above both' fits.

41. (A) The chapter targets the PURSUIT — making titles one's aim — not their mere possession. One can hold an उपाधिः and a पदवी while refusing to chase them, valuing सेवा instead. So 'he had neither' over-reads renunciation-of-pursuit into renunciation-of-possession. The other two distractors swap the definitions (उपाधिः is the degree, पदवी the post) and so misidentify what he supposedly kept or lost.

42. (D) The correct sentence ranks सेवा above both and defines उपाधिः (degree earned) and पदवी (post held) accurately. The first distractor inverts the ranking AND mis-defines उपाधिः as 'a post earned by study' (it is a qualification, not a post). The second inverts the ranking AND mis-defines पदवी as 'a degree held in office' (it is a post, not a degree). The third flattens the chapter's hierarchy into equality, contradicting परमः.

43. (C) The chapter ranks सेवा and मानवीयाः गुणाः above उपाधिः/पदवी; the advert flips this, treating credentials/rank as essential and humane qualities as optional — a direct inversion. The chapter does NOT prize उपाधिः/पदवी most (it ranks them below service), so the first option is false. पदवी IS discussed in the chapter, so the second is false. And धनम् is not crowned highest — सेवा is — so the third is false.

44. (B) पदवी = post/rank held; उपाधिः = qualification earned — the correct pairing. The first distractor swaps उपाधिः to 'post' and absurdly makes पदवी a decoction. The second gives यन्त्रवत् the possessive sense ('has a machine'), but here -वत् means 'like'. The third claims कषाय is disambiguated by gender, whereas it is fixed by CONTEXT (medical scene vs taste discussion). Only the पदवी/उपाधिः statement holds.

45. (C) Context, not gender or number, fixes कषाय. In (1) a वैद्य makes a रोगिन् drink it — a medical scene, so 'decoction/medicine'. In (2) it is a रसः ('taste') in a fruit — a taste discussion, so 'astringent'. Hence (1)=decoction, (2)=astringent taste. The reversed option mixes up the cues. The two 'always one sense' options deny the very context-flip the chapter teaches: कषाय genuinely has both senses.

46. (C) The chapter is explicit that कषाय flips between decoction and astringent-taste purely by CONTEXT — a medical scene vs a taste discussion — never by gender or number. So pinning the meaning to a gender ending is a false shortcut. Both the 'neuter=medicine' rule and its reversal invent a gender-based distinction. The 'by number' option likewise contradicts the context-only principle. Context is the only reliable cue.

47. (D) The inference 'his service is concrete' rests on the assumption that a hands-on, in-person act of care (brewing the medicine) IS enacted service, not just sentiment or remote giving — which is exactly सेवा's defining mark (time, body, presence). Reading कषायम् as 'astringent taste' breaks the medical scene entirely. Tying it to earning an उपाधिः contradicts the chapter's renunciation of title-chasing. And delegating to an assistant would REMOVE the personal hands-on element the argument depends on.

48. (A) सेवा requires the giver's own time, body and presence; here goods are merely transferred at a distance with no one present to serve — the signature of दान. That the goods happen to be medicines does not convert दान into सेवा; the boundary is presence, not the kind of help. So it is not सेवा (no presence), not 'both' (presence is wholly absent), and not 'neither' (giving goods at a distance is precisely दान).

49. (C) P gives time, body and presence — सेवा; Q transfers resources from afar — दान. The chapter ranks सेवा (the more personal, costly self-giving) above दान, so P is crowned. Calling Q's money सेवा confuses transfer-at-a-distance with presence. 'Both सेवा' ignores that Q is never present. 'Both दान' wrongly recasts P's hands-on nightly care as mere giving — it is the very model of सेवा.

50. (B) The defining ingredient of सेवा is personal presence (time + body). Money is not part of that definition, so attaching it changes nothing essential; but strip away presence and only the resource-transfer remains, which is दान. Hence presence is necessary and sufficient to keep it सेवा. Money is not 'tainted' (the chapter honours दान too), it is not legally required for सेवा, and दान is the LOWER, not higher, category in this ranking.

51. (D) Two faults: (1) labelling a cheque-writer (दान, giving at a distance) a 'servant' (सेवा, requiring presence) — a category swap; (2) treating the biggest gift as the supreme deed, when the chapter ranks personal service above giving regardless of amount. The 'only wording' option denies both errors. दान is not condemned as immoral. And a cheque is not सेवा at all, so 'rank one cheque below another' misses the boundary entirely.

52. (D) चिकित्सकः is the -क agent; रोगिन् is the -इन् characterised noun; यन्त्रवत् is the -वत् 'like'; मानवीय is the -ईय 'befitting'. Each distractor permutes these incorrectly — e.g. assigning -क to रोगिन् (which is -इन्) or -वत् to चिकित्सकः (which is -क). Only the first row keeps every suffix with its true exemplar, the core of the chapter's word-building system.

53. (B) In this chapter यन्त्रवत् uses -वत् in its 'like/इव' sense — 'like a machine' (tireless), NOT the possessive 'having a machine'. So that pair is the wrong one. उपाधिः (degree), रोगिन् (the sick, characterised by रोग), and मानवीयाः (humane) are all correctly glossed. The trap is the dual sense of -वत्; only here it must be 'like'.

54. (C) Defect 1: सेव् is आत्मनेपदी, so सेवति (परस्मैपद) must be सेवते. Defect 2: यन्त्रवान् is the possessive 'having a machine'; the chapter's word is यन्त्रवत् ('like a machine', tireless) — a sense error. Defect 3 (thematic): the doctor renounces the pursuit of उपाधिः, so 'and he gains a degree' as praise contradicts the message. That is three distinct defects. 'Zero/one/two' all under-count, and the claim that यन्त्रवान् is fine endorses the possessive misreading.

55. (B) The correct sentence: 'ranks highest' = परमः as rank; 'in person rather than a distant gift' = सेवा over दान; 'tirelessly like a machine' = यन्त्रवत् as simile; 'more than any degree' uses उपाधिः rightly. Distractor three turns सेवा into money and यन्त्रवत् into machine-OWNERSHIP, and makes a post the goal. Only the first satisfies all four.

56. (B) The chain equivocates on 'highest': परमो धर्मः ranks DUTIES and puts सेवा at the top; the conclusion silently shifts 'highest' to the doctor's POST (पदवी) — a different scale the chapter explicitly subordinates to service. The chain does not misread परमः as temporal (it keeps 'rank'), so that diagnosis is wrong; and सेवा is precisely a duty (the highest), refuting the last option.

57. (B) उपाधिः and पदवी are things one HAS (a degree, a post). सेवा is what one DOES, expressed in acts like brewing कषायम् with मानवीयाः गुणाः. The first distractor puts service on the HAS side (it is an activity) and the degree/post on the DOES side (they are possessions) — fully inverted. The second mislabels उपाधिः/पदवी as 'work'. The third treats सेवा as merely 'had'. Only the correct row sorts them right.

So naming दान the परमो धर्मः would crown the LOWER of the two, contradicting the chapter's value-order — the substantive objection. दानम् can grammatically fill a subject slot (it is a neuter noun, not a verb), so the 'impossible to form' and 'is a verb' objections are false. And दान is NOT a synonym of सेवा (presence vs distant giving), so 'merely redundant' is wrong too.
