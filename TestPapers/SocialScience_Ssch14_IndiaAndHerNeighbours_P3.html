Worked Solutions

1. (D) A land neighbour shares a land boundary; Sri Lanka is separated from India by the sea (the Palk Strait), so it is a maritime neighbour and is mis-filed. Pakistan does share a land border, so it is correctly a land neighbour and need not move. The claim that neither is a neighbour ignores that both are close neighbours. Sri Lanka has no Himalayan border at all.

2. (A) The chapter treats maritime neighbours as genuine neighbours because a shared sea has long carried trade, travel and culture between coasts. Saying the sea only separates ignores centuries of sea-borne exchange. A land wall is not the only way to be joined. Sea links matter regardless of whether a country also has land neighbours.

3. (D) Maritime neighbours exist because India's long coast faces the Indian Ocean, with lands like Sri Lanka and the Maldives across the water. India is not landlocked; it has an extensive coastline. It does not share land borders with islands. The Indian Ocean is rimmed by many countries, which is precisely why India has maritime neighbours.

4. (B) Neighbourliness rests on a shared sea or border, not on a country's size or exact distance, so the small Maldives is still a maritime neighbour. No bridge is needed for a maritime link. There is no rule that only large countries qualify. The Maldives has no land border with India, so it is maritime, not land.

5. (C) Sri Lanka is separated from India by the Palk Strait and is a maritime neighbour. Bhutan and Nepal are landlocked Himalayan neighbours joined by land. Afghanistan is linked to India historically by overland routes, not by sea.

6. (D) Bangladesh shares a long land border with India and the Maldives is reached only across the sea, so this pairing is correct. Bangladesh is land, not maritime, and the Maldives is maritime, not land. Nepal is landlocked and Sri Lanka is across the sea, so that pairing is reversed. China is a land neighbour across the Himalayas and Myanmar is a land neighbour, not an island.

7. (D) Sri Lanka sits just off India's southern tip, across a narrow strait, so it is the nearest land you would reach by sailing south. China lies to the north beyond the Himalayas, the opposite direction. Afghanistan is to the north-west by land. Bhutan is landlocked in the northern Himalayas, unreachable by sailing south.

8. (C) An open border is a real, agreed boundary that people may cross without visas — open in access, not absent. Saying there is no border confuses 'open' with 'non-existent'. Nepal is a separate sovereign country, not part of India. An open border is the opposite of a sealed wall.

9. (C) An open border keeps the legal line between two nations while easing crossing, so Vir is right. Openness does not merge the two states. Borders exist by agreement, not only where walls stand. India and Nepal share a land border, not a maritime one.

10. (C) Both are land borders, but the India–China boundary is not the open, visa-free kind that India–Nepal is, and it runs through the high Himalayas. It is not visa-free like Nepal's. It is on land, not across the ocean. A mountain range marks a border rather than removing it.

11. (A) A boundary following a mountain range shows geography shaping borders. Not every border is coastal; many follow rivers or mountains. Mountains hinder but never wholly prevent contact, as old passes and trade show. The example directly contradicts the idea that geography is irrelevant to borders.

12. (A) Afghanistan was tied to India by the old northern overland route, the Uttarapatha, through the north-west. The Maldives and Sri Lanka are reached across the sea, not overland. Indonesia lies far across the eastern seas, a maritime link, not an overland one.

13. (B) Myanmar's position on India's eastern land border makes it the overland doorway toward South-East Asia. It is not a Pacific island. As a gateway it opens, rather than blocks, land contact. It does share a land border with India, so it is not a sea-only neighbour.

14. (C) A landlocked country still depends on the sea for overseas trade and routes its goods through a neighbour's ports — for Nepal, mainly India's. Nepal has no coast of its own. Landlocked countries do trade abroad; they simply borrow a route to the sea. The far easier and main gateway for Nepal is Indian ports, not crossing the high Himalayas to the north.

15. (B) Nepal and Bhutan are both landlocked Himalayan states without coastlines, relying on India to reach the sea. Neither is an island nation. Neither has a coastline, so they are not maritime neighbours. They are mountainous, not desert, lands.

16. (A) Steep mountain rivers give the falling water needed for hydroelectricity, which suits Bhutan's terrain. It is landlocked, so it has no coastline for tidal power. It produces, not merely imports, power. Burning sea water is not a real source of electricity.

17. (C) Gross National Happiness counts wellbeing, environment, governance and culture beyond pure income. It is not a measure of military strength. Bhutan, being landlocked, has no islands to count. It is not a measure of skyscrapers either.

18. (B) A landlocked country has no coastline and depends on a neighbour for sea access, whereas a maritime neighbour is a coastal land reached over the sea. A landlocked country has no coastline at all, let alone a longer one. A maritime neighbour trades by sea by definition. Their sea access is plainly different, not identical.

19. (D) India's ports act as transit points so landlocked neighbours can ship goods overseas — India is their sea gateway. The coast does not give them beaches of their own. A coastline cannot turn an inland country into islands. A neighbour's coast is far from useless; it provides the sea access landlocked states lack.

20. (A) Both Pakistan and China have land boundaries with India. Neither is a sea-only neighbour. Pakistan in fact has a coastline, so 'both landlocked' is false. Neither shares the visa-free open arrangement that marks the India–Nepal border.

21. (D) The phrase points to deep common roots alongside a later political separation into distinct nations. It is not a claim of having nothing in common. They are not one country today. And they were closely connected historically, not unconnected.

22. (C) Both are reached across the sea, yet they differ in form: the Maldives is a chain of small islands and Sri Lanka is one big island. Neither is landlocked or Himalayan. The description is the reverse of calling Sri Lanka the archipelago. Neither shares a land border with India.

23. (C) Bangladesh emerged as a separate nation comparatively recently, earning the 'newborn neighbour' description. China is a very old continuing state. Nepal is a long-standing Himalayan kingdom. Sri Lanka has an ancient civilisation. None of the latter three fits 'newborn'.

24. (D) Afghanistan's ties ran along old overland routes to the north-west, whereas the Maldives, a sea-girt archipelago, links by sea. The statement that reverses these is wrong. Neither connects through the northern Himalayas as a pair. They do not both connect by the southern ocean — only the Maldives does.

25. (B) Pakistan lies to India's west and Afghanistan to the north-west, both historically land-linked. Sri Lanka and the Maldives are maritime neighbours to the south. Myanmar and Bangladesh lie to the east. Bhutan and Nepal lie along the northern Himalayas, not the west.

26. (B) Bangladesh lies to India's east, sharing a long land border and rivers that flow across the boundary. Afghanistan is to the north-west. The Maldives is a maritime neighbour to the south-west. Pakistan lies to the west. Only Bangladesh fits the eastern, river-sharing description.

27. (D) Because a transboundary river serves both countries, sharing it sensibly calls for cooperation over its waters. Stopping the flow with dams would harm the downstream country and breed conflict, not solve it. Ignoring the river does not make the shared dependence vanish. Refusing to talk worsens, rather than manages, a shared resource.

28. (A) A river crossing a border binds upstream and downstream neighbours, so joint management makes sense. Rivers do not halt at borders; that is why they are called transboundary. What one country does upstream affects the other downstream, so use is not independent. Shared rivers connect, rather than separate, water supplies.

29. (C) The Himalayan range stretches across several countries and is the source of major rivers feeding the region. It is not wholly within India; Nepal, Bhutan and others share it. It is a snow-fed mountain system, not a desert, and it gives birth to rivers rather than blocking them. It is a land feature, not a maritime one.

30. (D) Shared mountains and rivers mean the region's physical geography spills across borders, linking the countries. There is no single government over them; each is sovereign. They do not share one currency. The very fact that geography is shared shows borders do not fully separate it.

31. (D) A river that flows across one or more borders on its way to the sea is transboundary. It is not a closed landlocked lake. A strait is a narrow sea channel, not a river. By definition it does not stay within one country.

32. (B) Because rivers and ranges straddle borders, sensible management needs joint effort, encouraging cooperation. No single country can fully control a feature shared with others. Shared resources demand more discussion, not less. Geography has very practical effects, such as on water and trade.

33. (A) Nepal nestles in the Himalayas and is landlocked, with no coastline. Sri Lanka is a southern island with a coast. The Maldives is a low island chain in the sea. Bangladesh is a delta country with a coastline. Only Nepal fits the Himalayan, coastless description.

34. (C) Common rivers tie neighbours' water security together, so cooperation is a practical necessity. The rivers are shared, not entirely each country's own. They cross borders rather than vanishing at them. Water is among the most important shared concerns, not unimportant.

35. (C) The echoes of Indian names reflect cultural influence carried peacefully by traders, travellers and teachers. There was no Indian conquest of Thailand. Thailand did not rule India. The pattern of names and epics is too consistent to be coincidence.

36. (D) Indian ideas, religion and art travelled to South-East Asia by peaceful exchange, not conquest. There were no large conquering Indian armies in these lands. The influence is ancient, not recent. Monuments and stories show clear traces, so denying any influence is false.

37. (B) Angkor Wat shows Indian religious and artistic ideas taking root and being adapted in South-East Asia. India did not rule Cambodia as a colony to build it. Far from having no contact, Cambodia absorbed strong Indian influence. The temple was built locally, not shipped from India.

38. (B) The Ramakien is a distinctly Thai version of the Ramayana, adapted to local culture. It is not an unchanged copy. It is an epic, not a trade route. It is a story, not a Buddhist school.

39. (B) Borobudur, a vast Buddhist monument reflecting Indian influence, stands in Indonesia. It is not in Bhutan, Afghanistan or the Maldives. Its location across the eastern seas shows Indian culture spreading by maritime contact.
