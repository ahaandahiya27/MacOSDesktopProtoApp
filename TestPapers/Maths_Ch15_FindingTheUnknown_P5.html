1. (B) Work outward. 3 − (x − 4) = 3 − x + 4 = 7 − x; then 2x − (7 − x) = 2x − 7 + x = 3x − 7; so 5(3x − 7) − 7 = 15x − 35 − 7 = 15x − 42. The equation 15x − 42 = 8x + 14 gives 7x = 56, so x = 8. Substituting back: the bracket equals 17 and 5·17 − 7 = 78 = 8·8 + 14. Failing to flip the innermost sign leaves a smaller root near six; a stray negative sign on the constant produces a negative root; leaving the outer bracket un-multiplied by five lands at four.

2. (A) Build outward: 5 − (x − 2) = 7 − x; then 2x − (7 − x) = 3x − 7; so 4(3x − 7) = 12x − 28 and the left side is 9x − (12x − 28) = −3x + 28. The equation −3x + 28 = 3x − 8 gives 36 = 6x, so x = 6. Check: the bracket value is 11, 4·11 = 44, 9·6 − 44 = 10 and 3·6 − 8 = 10. Mishandling the subtracted bracket doubles the apparent root to twelve; a sign slip gives a negative; not distributing the four lands at four.

3. (D) Inside: x − (3 − 2x) = x − 3 + 2x = 3x − 3, so 2(3x − 3) = 6x − 6, and the left side is (6x − 3) − (6x − 6) = 3, a constant. The equation 3 = x + 5 gives x = −2. Check at x = −2: 3(−5) − 2[−2 − (3 + 4)] = −15 + 18 = 3 and −2 + 5 = 3. Dropping a sign on the double bracket makes the x-terms fail to cancel and yields two or one; reading the right side as x − 5 gives eight.

4. (D) Let the number be x. Then 4x − 9 = 2x + 7, so 2x = 16 and x = 8. Check: 4·8 − 9 = 23 and 2·8 + 7 = 23. Mis-signing the subtraction gives a negative; dividing sixteen by sixteen instead of two gives one; reporting the intermediate 2x = 16 gives sixteen.

5. (D) Multiply every term by the LCD 12: 3(3x − 1) − 2(2x + 3) = x + 11. Expand: 9x − 3 − 4x − 6 = x + 11, so 5x − 9 = x + 11, giving 4x = 20 and x = 5. Check: (14)/4 − (13)/6 = 3.5 − 2.1666… = 1.3333… and (16)/12 = 1.3333…. A sign error on −2(2x + 3) shifts the root negative; clearing only one denominator gives three or seven.

6. (B) After spending one-fourth he keeps three-fourths; spending one-third of that keeps two-thirds of three-fourths, i.e. one-half of the original. So half the money is ₹360, and the original was ₹720. Check: spends 180 leaving 540, then spends 180 leaving 360. Stopping after the first step reports the 540 remainder; treating both fractions as taken from the original (7/12 spent) gives the wrong 480-ish value; 900 is a multiplication slip.

7. (D) Let the integers be n, n + 1, n + 2. The first plus the third is 2n + 2, and this equals the second plus four: 2n + 2 = (n + 1) + 4. So n = 3 and the middle integer is n + 1 = 4. Check: 3 + 5 = 8 = 4 + 4. Reporting n itself gives three; reporting the largest gives five; an off-by-one in the set-up gives two.

8. (B) Let the son be s, so the father is 60 − s. In twelve years the son is s + 12 and the father is 72 − s, with 72 − s = 2(s + 12). Expand: 72 − s = 2s + 24, so 48 = 3s and s = 16. Check: son 16, father 44; in twelve years 28 and 56, and 56 = 2 × 28. Reporting the father's present age gives 44; mishandling the added twelve gives twenty; a division slip gives twelve.

9. (D) Multiply every term by the LCD 6: 2(3x − 1) − (x + 2) = 6x − 10. Expand: 6x − 2 − x − 2 = 6x − 10, so 5x − 4 = 6x − 10, giving 6 = x. Check: (17)/3 − (8)/6 = 5.6666… − 1.3333… = 4.3333… and (26)/6 = 4.3333…. A dropped minus on (x + 2) flips the sign; clearing one denominator only gives three or two.

10. (C) Let the one-way distance be d. Then d/12 + d/18 = 5; multiplying by 36 gives 3d + 2d = 180, so 5d = 180 and d = 36. Check: 36/12 = 3 h and 36/18 = 2 h, total 5 h. Averaging the two speeds to fifteen and dividing 75 by fifteen wrongly suggests 75; thirty and forty-five are arithmetic slips.

11. (C) Let the daughter be d, so the mother is 4d. Eight years ago: 4d − 8 = 8(d − 8). Expand: 4d − 8 = 8d − 64, so 56 = 4d and d = 14; the mother is 4 × 14 = 56. Check: eight years ago 6 and 48, and 48 = 8 × 6. Reporting the daughter gives fourteen; reporting the mother's past age gives forty-eight; a constant slip gives sixty-four.

12. (B) Multiply every term by the LCD 6: 2(4x − 1) − (5x − 1) = 4x − 9. Expand: 8x − 2 − 5x + 1 = 4x − 9, so 3x − 1 = 4x − 9, giving 8 = x. Check: (31)/3 − (39)/6 = 10.3333… − 6.5 = 3.8333… and (23)/6 = 3.8333…. Distributing −(5x − 1) as −5x − 1 corrupts the constant and shifts the root; clearing one denominator gives four or two.

13. (A) Let the number be n. Then (2/3)n = (1/2)n + 4, so (2/3 − 1/2)n = 4, i.e. (1/6)n = 4 and n = 24. Check: two-thirds of 24 is 16, half is 12, and 16 = 12 + 4. Doubling the gap gives forty-eight; treating the difference as one-third gives twelve; treating it as one-half gives eight.

14. (C) Multiply every term by the LCD 8: 2(4x + 1) − (3x − 3) = 6x + 7. Expand: 8x + 2 − 3x + 3 = 6x + 7, so 5x + 5 = 6x + 7, giving −2 = x. Check: (−7)/4 − (−9)/8 = −1.75 + 1.125 = −0.625 and (−5)/8 = −0.625. A sign slip on −(3x − 3) flips the answer to positive two; mis-clearing gives −1 or three.

15. (D) After A two-thirds remain; B takes one-fourth of that, leaving three-fourths of two-thirds, which is one-half of the sum; C takes one-half of that, leaving one-fourth of the sum. So one-fourth equals ₹150 and the sum is ₹600. Check: A 200 leaves 400, B 100 leaves 300, C 150 leaves 150. Stopping a step early gives 300 or 450; over-counting the fractions gives 900.

16. (C) Let them be n, n + 2, n + 4, n + 6. Their sum is 4n + 12, and three times the largest minus two is 3(n + 6) − 2 = 3n + 16. Setting 4n + 12 = 3n + 16 gives n = 4, so the integers are 4, 6, 8, 10 and the smallest is 4. Check: sum 28 = 3 × 10 − 2. Reporting the second term gives six; the largest gives ten; the third gives eight.

17. (A) Let the units digit be u and tens digit u − 1, number 10(u − 1) + u = 11u − 10. The condition 11u − 10 = 6u gives 5u = 10, so u = 2, tens digit 1, number 12. Check: 6 × 2 = 12 and 1 = 2 − 1. Reversing the digit relation gives twenty-one; ignoring the units-digit link gives twenty-four or thirty-six.

18. (B) Multiply every term by the LCD 8: 2(2x + 1) − (x − 1) = 5x + 1. Expand: 4x + 2 − x + 1 = 5x + 1, so 3x + 3 = 5x + 1, giving 2 = 2x and x = 1. Check: (3)/4 − (0)/8 = 0.75 and (6)/8 = 0.75. A sign slip on −(x − 1) gives −1; mis-clearing the denominators gives three or two.

19. (D) Let A's share be a. Then a + a/2 + a/3 + a/4 = 900. Over the LCD 12 this is (12a + 6a + 4a + 3a)/12 = 25a/12 = 900, so a = 432. Check: 432 + 216 + 144 + 108 = 900. Using only B's half gives 216; mis-summing the fractions gives 360 or 300.

20. (D) Innermost: 3 − (1 − x) = 2 + x; then 2x − (2 + x) = x − 2; then 4 − (x − 2) = 6 − x. The equation 6 − x = 2x − 6 gives 12 = 3x, so x = 4. Check: 4 − [8 − {3 + 3}] = 4 − [8 − 6] = 2 and 2 × 4 − 6 = 2. A flipped nested sign gives a negative root or two; not distributing the outer subtraction gives six.

21. (B) Downstream time is 24/12 = 2 hours, so the upstream leg takes 5 − 2 = 3 hours, giving an upstream speed of 24/3 = 8 km/h. Check: 2 + 3 = 5 hours. Taking the same speed both ways gives twelve; mis-dividing gives six; averaging the legs gives ten.

22. (C) Expand: 5x − 10 − 3x + 12 = 2x + 2, so the left side is 2x + 2 and equals the right side 2x + 2 for every x — infinitely many solutions. Solving and reading off a single root suggests 'exactly one'; reading the equal constants as unequal suggests 'none'; a linear equation can never have 'exactly two'.

23. (A) Let the son be s, so the father is 3s + 5. In ten years: 3s + 15 = 2(s + 10) + 7 = 2s + 27, so s = 12. Check: father now 41; in ten years son 22, father 51, and 2 × 22 + 7 = 51. Reporting the father's present age gives 41; mishandling the added ten gives eight or ten.

24. (C) Cross-multiply: (3x − 2)(2x + 1) = (3x + 4)(2x − 5). Left: 6x² + 3x − 4x − 2 = 6x² − x − 2. Right: 6x² − 15x + 8x − 20 = 6x² − 7x − 20. The 6x² cancels: −x − 2 = −7x − 20, so 6x = −18 and x = −3. Check: both sides equal (−11)/(−11) = 1. A dropped minus gives positive three; transposition slips give five or negative five.

25. (C) Let the numerator be n and denominator n + 5. After adding four: (n + 4)/(n + 9) = 1/2, so 2(n + 4) = n + 9, giving 2n + 8 = n + 9 and n = 1. Check: original 1/6 becomes 5/10 = 1/2. Reporting the denominator gives six; off-by-one set-ups give five or nine.

26. (A) Innermost: x − (2x − 4) = −x + 4; then 3x − (−x + 4) = 4x − 4; doubling gives 8x − 8. The equation 8x − 8 = 5x + 4 gives 3x = 12, so x = 4. Check: the bracket is 4·4 − 4 = 12, 2 × 12 = 24 and 5 × 4 + 4 = 24. A nested-sign error gives a negative root or two; a constant slip gives one.

27. (C) Let the angle be x; its complement is 90 − x, and x = (90 − x) + 20. So 2x = 110 and x = 55. Check: complement 35, and 55 = 35 + 20. The complement 35 is a trap; using the supplement instead of the complement gives 70 or 110.

28. (A) Let B = b. Then A = (3/4)b and C = (5/4)b, so the total is (3/4 + 1 + 5/4)b = 3b = 720, giving b = 240. Check: A = 180, B = 240, C = 300, total 720. Reporting A gives 180; reporting C gives 300; a slip gives 320.

29. (B) Multiply every term by the LCD 12: 3(5x + 1) − 2(3x − 5) = 6x − 5. Expand: 15x + 3 − 6x + 10 = 6x − 5, so 9x + 13 = 6x − 5, giving 3x = −18 and x = −6. Check: (−29)/4 − (−23)/6 = −7.25 + 3.8333… = −3.4166… and (−41)/12 = −3.4166…. A sign slip on −2(3x − 5) flips the root to positive; mis-clearing gives −3 or 3.

30. (D) Let the son be s and the man 2s. Twenty years ago: 2s − 20 = 4(s − 20). Expand: 2s − 20 = 4s − 80, so 60 = 2s and s = 30; the man is 2 × 30 = 60. Check: twenty years ago 10 and 40, and 40 = 4 × 10. Reporting the son gives thirty; reporting the man's past age gives forty; doubling wrongly gives eighty.

31. (D) Milk is (7/9) × 90 = 70 L and water (2/9) × 90 = 20 L. Adding water keeps milk at 70 L; for 7 : 5 the water must be (5/7) × 70 = 50 L, so the amount added is 50 − 20 = 30 L. Check: 70 : 50 = 7 : 5. Re-stating the original water gives twenty; halving the gap gives fifteen; reporting the final water total gives fifty.

32. (C) Innermost: x − (2x − 5) = −x + 5; then 2 − (−x + 5) = x − 3; tripling gives 3x − 9. The equation 3x − 9 = 2x − 6 gives x = 3. Check: 3{2 − [3 − 1]} = 3 × 0 = 0 and 2(3 − 3) = 0. A nested-sign flip gives a negative root or five; a constant slip gives one.

33. (B) Let tens t and units u with t + u = 12. Reversing gives 10u + t = (10t + u) − 18, so 9u − 9t = −18 and t − u = 2. With t + u = 12, t = 7 and u = 5, so the number is 75. Check: reversed 57 = 75 − 18 and 7 + 5 = 12. The reversed 57 is a trap; 84 and 93 meet the digit sum but not the decrease-by-18 condition.

34. (A) Multiply every term by the LCD 15: 5(4x − 4) − 3(5x − 5) = 4x − 11. Expand: 20x − 20 − 15x + 15 = 4x − 11, so 5x − 5 = 4x − 11, giving x = −6. Check: (−28)/3 − (−35)/5 = −9.3333… + 7 = −2.3333… and (−35)/15 = −2.3333…. A sign slip on −3(5x − 5) flips the root; mis-clearing gives −3 or 3.

35. (C) In one hour A fills 1/8, B fills 1/12, C drains 1/24; the net fill rate is 1/8 + 1/12 − 1/24 = 3/24 + 2/24 − 1/24 = 4/24 = 1/6 of the tank, so it fills in 6 hours. Ignoring the drain (rate 5/24) drifts toward 4; averaging the times gives 8; adding the times gives 44.

36. (B) Expand the left: 6x − 6 − 4x + 12 = 2x + 6. The identity 2x + 6 = 2x + k holds for all x only when k = 6. Forgetting the +12 gives 0; a sign error gives −6 or 2.

37. (A) Let profitable articles be p, so loss articles number 60 − p. Then 15p − 10(60 − p) = 400, i.e. 25p − 600 = 400, so 25p = 1000 and p = 40. Check: 40 earn ₹600 and 20 lose ₹200, net ₹400. The complement 20 is a swap trap; sign errors give 44 or 16.
