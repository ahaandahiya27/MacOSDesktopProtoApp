## Worked Solutions

**1. (A)** Congruent means one figure can be placed exactly on the other — that requires the **same shape AND the same size**. "Same area only" (a 2×6 and a 3×4 rectangle) or "same shape only" (an enlargement) fail this.

**2. (B)** ≅ is the congruence symbol. ∼ means "similar", ≈ means "approximately", = is ordinary equality.

**3. (C)** A 150% copy keeps the shape but multiplies every length by 1.5, so it is bigger. Same shape, different size = **similar but not congruent**. (Congruent needs equal size.)

**4. (D)** SSS, SAS and ASA each pin a triangle down completely. **AAA (three equal angles)** fixes only the shape — the triangles can be different sizes (similar), so it does not guarantee congruence.

**5. (B)** Three pairs of equal corresponding sides is exactly the **SSS** criterion.

**6. (C)** Fold an isosceles triangle along its axis of symmetry (the altitude from the apex): the two halves coincide, so the **two base angles** are forced equal. The equal sides face equal angles.

**7. (D)** Drawing a base and swinging two arcs from its ends, **the arcs meet at two points** (one above, one below the base), giving two different triangles. A third side removes the ambiguity, so SSS fixes the triangle.

**8. (A)** AB = DE and BC = EF are two sides, and ∠B is the angle **between** them (the included angle). Two sides + included angle = **SAS**.

**9. (C)** The included angle of two named sides is **the angle where those two sides meet** (their common vertex). This is the key word in SAS.

**10. (D)** Two angles (∠P = ∠X, ∠Q = ∠Y) with the **included** side PQ = XY give **ASA**, and the vertices already correspond in order P↔X, Q↔Y, R↔Z, so ∆PQR ≅ ∆XYZ. (AA alone would only give similarity, but here a side is also equal.)

**11. (A)** All three sides equal ⇒ all three angles equal; they sum to 180°, so each is 180 ÷ 3 = **60°**. (Not 90° and not 360 ÷ 3.)

**12. (B)** Apex 40°, so the two equal base angles share 180 − 40 = 140°, giving 140 ÷ 2 = **70°** each. (40° is the apex itself — the trap.)

**13. (D)** Equal angles are faced by equal sides. ∠B and ∠C are equal, so the sides opposite them — AC (opposite B) and AB (opposite C) — are equal: **AB = AC**.

**14. (A)** A right angle, equal hypotenuses and one equal leg is precisely the **RHS** criterion. (SSS would need the third side proved equal first; RHS gives it directly.)

**15. (B)** Equal angles fix only the shape; "twice as large" means a different size. So the triangles are **similar but not congruent** — AAA is not a congruence rule.

**16. (C)** AB = AC ⇒ ∠B = ∠C. With ∠A = 80°, 2∠B = 180 − 80 = 100, so ∠B = **50°**. (80° is ∠A; 100° is the sum of the base angles.)

**17. (A)** The altitude makes ∠ADB = ∠ADC = 90° (right angle), AB = AC (hypotenuses), AD common (a leg) → **RHS**. The matching base angles then equal by CPCT.

**18. (B)** AAA guarantees only **the same shape but possibly different size** (similar). It is not a congruence criterion.

**19. (C)** The equal angle ∠A sits **between the two equal sides** AB and AC, so this is the included angle — **SAS**. (If the angle were opposite a side it would be the unreliable SSA case.)

**20. (D)** Here ∠A is at vertex A while the given sides are AB and BC; ∠A is NOT between them — it is a **non-included angle (SSA)**, which can fit two different triangles. So congruence is **not guaranteed**.

**21. (B)** In ∆ABC, ∠C = 180 − 50 − 65 = 65°. Congruence ∆ABC ≅ ∆DEF makes ∠F correspond to ∠C, so ∠F = **65°**.

**22. (C)** Under ∆ABC ≅ ∆PQR the correspondence is A↔P, B↔Q, C↔R. Side BC (between B and C) corresponds to **QR** (between Q and R).

**23. (D)** CPCT lets you conclude corresponding parts are equal — but **only after** the two triangles have been shown congruent. It is a consequence of congruence, never a step to prove it.

**24. (A)** Two equal sides with a 60° included angle: the third side, by the isosceles property the base angles are equal, and equal base angles with a 60° apex force all angles to 60°, so all three sides become equal — the triangle is **equilateral**. (A 5-5 triangle with a 60° apex is the classic equilateral.)

**25. (C)** A triangle has six measurements, but only **3** well-chosen independent ones (e.g. SSS, SAS or ASA) are needed to fix it completely. Two are too few; six is unnecessary.

**26. (D)** ∠A = ∠B = 70°, so the sides facing them are equal: side opposite A is BC, side opposite B is AC, hence **AC = BC** (isosceles). (Watch which sides face the equal angles.)

**27. (A)** All three pairs of sides equal (4, 6, 9 each) ⇒ congruent by **SSS**. Equal corresponding sides means same size too, not merely similar.

**28. (B)** AB = AC ⇒ ∠B = ∠C: 2x + 10 = 3x − 20 → 30 = x, so x = **30**. (Check: ∠B = 70° = ∠C.)

**29. (D)** A diagonal splits a square into two triangles with the two sides of the square equal, the included right angle equal, and the diagonal shared — by SAS (or SSS) they are **congruent**. Folding maps one exactly onto the other.

**30. (A)** ∆ABC ≅ ∆DEF gives A↔D, B↔E, C↔F. Side AC corresponds to DF, so AC = DF = **9 cm**. (AB = DE = 7 cm is a different pair — the trap.)

**31. (B)** Congruent ⇒ similar (true), every equilateral triangle is isosceles (true), AAA fails to prove congruence (true). The **false** statement is "**similar triangles are always congruent**" — similar triangles can differ in size.

**32. (C)** PQ = PR ⇒ ∠Q = ∠R = 65°. ∠P = 180 − 65 − 65 = **50°**. (65° is a base angle, not the apex.)

**33. (A)** Base 5 cm with the two base angles (50° and 60°) equal in the same positions: two angles + the included side ⇒ **ASA**.

**34. (B)** First triangle: legs 6, 8 ⇒ hypotenuse 10. Second: hypotenuse 10, one leg 6 ⇒ other leg √(100 − 36) = 8. Right angle + equal hypotenuse + equal leg (RHS) makes them **congruent**, with the missing leg also 8 cm.

**35. (C)** The letter order in ∆ABC ≅ ∆DEF pairs the vertices in place: A↔D. So ∠A corresponds to **∠D**.

**36. (D)** AD is a median ⇒ BD = DC; AB = AC (given); AD common. Three pairs of equal sides ⇒ **SSS**.

**37. (B)** Exterior angle 100° at the apex ⇒ interior apex angle = 180 − 100 = 80°. Base angles share 180 − 80 = 100°, so each = **50°**. (Using 100° directly as the interior apex is the trap.)

**38. (C)** Congruent triangles must be identical in size, hence equal area — so two triangles with **different areas** can never be congruent. (Equal perimeter + SSS, equal angles, or SAS are all consistent with congruence.)

**39. (D)** ∠L = ∠R, ∠M = ∠S are two angles, and the side LM = RS lies **between** ∠L and ∠M — included side. Two angles + included side ⇒ **ASA**.

**40. (A)** Base angle 75° ⇒ both base angles 75°, summing to 150°. Apex = 180 − 150 = **30°**.

**41. (C)** ∠A = 90°, AB = AC ⇒ ∠B = ∠C; they share 180 − 90 = 90°, so each = **45°**.

**42. (D)** With the right angle counted as one of the parts, **two pairs of equal legs (with the included right angle)** gives SAS congruence using exactly the right angle + two sides. ("All three angles" is mere AAA — shape only; a right angle + one acute angle with no side given is still only angles (AAA), fixing shape not size; and equal perimeters alone prove nothing.)

**43. (A)** Three fixed side-lengths leave **exactly one** triangle up to congruence (SSS pins it completely). Any two such triangles can be laid exactly on top of each other.

**44. (B)** AB = AC ⇒ ∠B = ∠C = (180 − 100)/2 = 40°. The biggest angle is the apex, **∠A at 100°** (each base angle is only 40°).

**45. (D)** In an isosceles triangle the bisector of the apex angle is also the median and altitude to the base, so it bisects BC: **BD = DC**. (∆ABD ≅ ∆ACD by SAS, then CPCT.)

**46. (A)** Two equal sides and an equal angle that is **not** between them is the SSA pattern, which can fit two different triangles. So it **does not guarantee congruence**.

**47. (B)** Every point on the perpendicular bisector of BC is equidistant from B and C; since A lies on it, AB = AC, so the triangle is **isosceles**. (It says nothing about a right angle or the longest side.)

**48. (C)** Congruent triangles are identical copies, so all corresponding sides are equal and the perimeters match: **24 cm**.

**49. (A)** AB = AC ⇒ ∠B = ∠C, and ∠B + ∠C = 180 − 40 = 140°, so ∠C = **70°**. (40° is ∠A, not a base angle.)

**50. (B)** **Two angles and the included side (ASA)** fix a unique triangle. Three angles give only shape (AAA), two sides alone are ambiguous, and a single side-and-angle is far too little.

**51. (C)** 1 : 1 : 2 over 180° gives angles 45°, 45°, 90°: two equal angles (isosceles) and a right angle — **isosceles and right-angled**.

**52. (D)** DE = DF ⇒ ∠E = ∠F. With ∠D = 2∠E: ∠D + ∠E + ∠F = 2∠E + ∠E + ∠E = 4∠E = 180 → ∠E = **45°**.

**53. (B)** All sides equal (7, 7, 7) ⇒ equilateral ⇒ every angle = **60°**.

**54. (C)** SAS uses two sides and the angle between them. The named sides are AB and AC, both meeting at A, so ∠A is included between **AB and AC**.

**55. (D)** AC = AD, BC = BD (given) and AB is the common base — three pairs of equal sides ⇒ **SSS**.

**56. (A)** AB = AC ⇒ ∠B = ∠C = (x + 20)°; with apex ∠A = (2x)°: 2x + (x + 20) + (x + 20) = 180 → 4x + 40 = 180 → x = 35. So ∠A = 2x = **70°**. (Check: base angles 55° each, 70 + 55 + 55 = 180. ✓)

**57. (C)** Equal area does NOT force equal shape and size — a tall thin triangle and a short wide one can share an area. So the claim is **false**.

**58. (D)** AB = AC, so the altitude from apex A to base BC also bisects BC (isosceles property): M is the midpoint, giving BM = BC/2 = 10/2 = **5 cm**. (6.5 wrongly halves a slant side; 12 is the altitude length √(13² − 5²).)

**59. (A)** ∆ABC ≅ ∆ACB pairs A↔A, B↔C, C↔B, so AB corresponds to AC ⇒ **AB = AC**: the triangle is isosceles. (This need not make it equilateral or right-angled.)

**60. (B)** 2 : 2 : 5 over 180° (9 parts of 20°) gives 40°, 40°, 100°: two equal angles ⇒ **isosceles** (and obtuse, but isosceles is the classification asked).
