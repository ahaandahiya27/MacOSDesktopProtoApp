## Worked Solutions

**1. (D)** Work left to right keeping track of size. 0.025 × 0.4 = 0.01 (25 × 4 = 100, with 3 + 1 = 4 decimal places gives 0.0100). Then 0.01 × 1000 shifts the point 3 right = 10. Finally 10 ÷ 0.05: scale both by 100 to get 1000 ÷ 5 = 200. Dropping one of the powers of ten while chaining lands on 20 or 2000.

**2. (C)** 0.25 = 25/100; dividing numerator and denominator by 25 gives 1/4. Leaving it as 25/100 is unsimplified; 1/40 would be 0.025, ten times too small; 2/5 = 0.4.

**3. (C)** First cut: 1.9 ÷ 0.12, scale both by 100 to get 190 ÷ 12 = 15 full pieces using 15 × 0.12 = 1.80 m, leaving 1.9 − 1.80 = 0.10 m. Second cut: 0.10 ÷ 0.03, scale by 100 to get 10 ÷ 3 = 3 full pieces (using 0.09 m), leaving 0.01 m. Total full pieces = 15 + 3 = 18. Stopping after the first cut gives 15; mis-counting the leftover gives 16; treating every piece as a 0.03 m piece gives 63.

**4. (B)** Divide 0.12 ÷ 0.004. Scale both by 1000: 120 ÷ 4 = 30. Dividing 0.12 ÷ 4 = 0.03 forgets to scale the divisor; an extra power of ten gives 300 or 3.

**5. (D)** (0.05)² needs 2 + 2 = 4 places, (0.2)³ needs 1 + 1 + 1 = 3 places; a product of the two carries 4 + 3 = 7 decimal places. (The digit product is 25 × 8 = 200, and 0.0025 × 0.008 = 0.0000200, which indeed sits at the seventh place before trimming.) Adding instead of summing place counts, or stopping at the larger of the two, gives 4 or 3-based wrong totals.

**6. (D)** 5.6 × 1000 = 5600 (shift 3 right). 56000 ÷ 100 = 560 (shift 2 left). Difference 5600 − 560 = 5040. Subtracting before shifting, or one wrong shift, gives 504 or 50400.

**7. (C)** To turn a decimal into a percent, multiply by 100 (shift the point two places right): 0.0375 × 100 = 3.75, so 3.75%. Shifting only one place gives 0.375%; shifting three gives 37.5%; forgetting to shift gives 0.0375%.

**8. (B)** Estimate 5 × 20 ÷ 0.1 = 100 ÷ 0.1 = 1000, so the answer is near 1000. Exactly: 4.95 × 19.8 = 98.01; then 98.01 ÷ 0.099, scale both by 1000 to get 98010 ÷ 99 = 990. Misplacing the point gives 99 or 9900.

**9. (A)** Inside: 2.4 ÷ 0.6 = 4 (24 ÷ 6) and 0.9 ÷ 0.3 = 3 (9 ÷ 3); their sum is 7. Then 7 × 0.05 = 0.35. Doing 7 × 0.5 by misreading the factor gives 3.5; misplacing the point gives 0.035 or 35.

**10. (C)** Unit rate = 420 ÷ 0.8 = 525 per kg (scale both by 10: 4200 ÷ 8 = 525). Then 525 × 2.5 = 1312.50. Stopping at the per-kg rate gives 525; multiplying 420 × 2.5 forgets to find the rate first and gives 1050; misplacing the point gives 131.25.

**11. (B)** 1.2 hours = 1.2 × 3600 = 4320 seconds. Loss = 0.015 × 4320 = 64.8 litres (15 × 4320 = 64800, with three decimal places → 64.800). Using minutes (72) instead of seconds gives 1.08; misplacing the point gives 6.48 or 648.

**12. (C)** Moving the point from 0.0009 to 9 is 4 places right, which is ×10000 — a 1 followed by 4 zeros. Counting only 3 places (×1000) gives 0.9, not 9.

**13. (B)** (1.2)² = 1.44 and (0.8)² = 0.64. Difference 1.44 − 0.64 = 0.80 = 0.8. (Checking by the identity (1.2−0.8)(1.2+0.8) = 0.4 × 2.0 = 0.8 confirms it.) Squaring the difference 0.4 to get 0.16 is the classic trap; misplacing the point gives 0.08 or 8.

**14. (B)** 3.0405 × 1000 shifts the point 3 places right = 3040.5. Reading place values: 3 thousands, 0 hundreds, 4 tens, 0 ones, 5 tenths. So the tens digit is 4. The ones digit is 0, the thousands digit is 3, and the tenths digit is 5.

**15. (C)** 3/8 = 0.375 (8 into 30 is 3 r6 → .3; into 60 is 7 r4 → .07; into 40 is 5 → .005). As a percent, 0.375 × 100 = 37.5%. Forgetting to multiply by 100 gives 0.375%; one too few shifts gives 3.75%.

**16. (B)** Round to 0.08 ÷ 0.01 = 8. Exactly: scale both by 10000, 784 ÷ 98 = 8. Misplacing the point gives 0.8 or 80.

**17. (C)** Numerator 0.6 × 0.5 × 0.4 = 0.12 (digits 6×5×4 = 120, three decimal places → 0.120). Denominator 0.2 × 0.3 = 0.06. Then 0.12 ÷ 0.06: scale both by 100, 12 ÷ 6 = 2. Dividing 0.12 ÷ 6 = 0.02 forgets to scale; an extra power of ten gives 20.

**18. (A)** Mileage = 0.6 ÷ 0.04: scale both by 100, 60 ÷ 4 = 15 km per litre. Dividing 0.6 ÷ 4 = 0.15 forgets to scale the divisor; an extra power of ten gives 150.

**19. (B)** 0.3 × 0.4 = 0.12, then 7.2 ÷ 0.12: scale both by 100, 720 ÷ 12 = 60. Finally 60 − 10 = 50. Subtracting first, or mishandling the division to 6, gives 5 or other wrong values.

**20. (D)** If 0.0125 ÷ x = 0.5, then x = 0.0125 ÷ 0.5 = 0.025 (scale both by 10: 0.125 ÷ 5 = 0.025). A common error multiplies instead, giving 0.00625, or misplaces the point to 0.25 or 0.0025.

**21. (C)** (0.04)² = 0.0016 (4×4 = 16, four decimal places). (0.3)² = 0.09 (two decimal places). Sum = 0.0016 + 0.0900 = 0.0916. Adding the bases (0.04 + 0.3 = 0.34) instead of the squares is the trap; misplacing the point gives 0.916 or 0.00916.

**22. (A)** After the point the order is tenths(0), hundredths(8), thousandths(1), ten-thousandths(3). The 8 is in the hundredths place worth 0.08; replacing it with 9 makes it 0.09, an increase of 0.01. Reading it as tenths gives 0.1; as thousandths gives 0.001.

**23. (D)** 1/8 = 0.125, and 0.125 × 100 = 12.5%. Forgetting one shift gives 1.25%; reading the denominator as the percent gives 8%; 12.8% is a miscalculation.

**24. (B)** Estimate 0.03 ÷ 0.017 ≈ 1.8. Exactly: scale both by 1000, 30.6 ÷ 17 = 1.8 (or 306 ÷ 170 = 1.8). Misplacing the point gives 18 or 0.18.

**25. (C)** Numerator 2.5 × 0.04 = 0.1 (25 × 4 = 100, three decimal places → 0.100). Denominator 0.5 × 0.2 = 0.1. Then 0.1 ÷ 0.1 = 1, and 1 + 1 = 2. Forgetting the +1 gives 1; mishandling the division gives 11 or 1.1.

**26. (C)** Cost = 62.50 × 2.4 = 150.00 (625 × 24 = 15000, three decimal places → 150.000). Paid 200, change = 200 − 150 = 50. Confusing cost with change gives 150; misplacing the point gives 15.

**27. (A)** Full length = 1.44 ÷ 0.024: scale both by 1000, 1440 ÷ 24 = 60 m. Half remains, so 60 ÷ 2 = 30 m. Forgetting to halve gives 60; mishandling the division gives 3 or 300.

**28. (A)** 0.9 ÷ 0.06 = 15 (scale by 100: 90 ÷ 6 = 15). 0.08 ÷ 0.4 = 0.2 (scale by 10: 0.8 ÷ 4 = 0.2). Product 15 × 0.2 = 3. Losing a power of ten in either division gives 30, 0.3 or 0.03.

**29. (B)** Each factor 0.12 has 2 decimal places, so the square has 2 + 2 = 4 places: 12 × 12 = 144 → 0.0144. Counting only 2 or 3 places misplaces the point.

**30. (C)** 6.05 × 100 = 605 (shift 2 right). 605 − 40 = 565. The tens digit of 565 is 6 (5 hundreds, 6 tens, 5 ones). Reading the ones digit gives 5; forgetting the subtraction leaves 605 whose tens digit is 0.

**31. (D)** 0.2 × 100 = 20, so a 20% discount. Forgetting to multiply by 100 gives 0.2%; one too few shifts gives 2%; one too many gives 200%.

**32. (B)** Cut total = 0.85 + 1.15 + 0.6 = 2.6 m. Remaining = 4.5 − 2.6 = 1.9 m. Strips = 1.9 ÷ 0.4 = 4.75, so 4 full strips. Rounding up gives 5; mis-subtracting gives 3; treating 1.9 ÷ 0.4 as 1.9 ÷ 4 then scaling wrongly gives 47.

**33. (A)** 1.5 + 2.5 = 4.0, then 4 ÷ 0.016: scale both by 1000, 4000 ÷ 16 = 250. Dividing 4 ÷ 16 = 0.25 forgets to scale the divisor; an extra power of ten gives 2500 or 25.

**34. (B)** Unit rate = 120 ÷ 1.6 = 75 per metre (scale by 10: 1200 ÷ 16 = 75). Then 75 × 2.5 = 187.50. Stopping at the rate gives 75; multiplying 120 × 2.5 without finding the rate gives 300; misplacing the point gives 18.75.

**35. (D)** Estimate 200 × 0.005 = 1, so the answer is near 1. Exactly: 198 × 49 = 9702, with 0 + 4 = 4 decimal places → 0.9702. Miscounting the place gives 9.702 or 0.09702.

**36. (A)** 0.06 ÷ 0.0025: scale both by 10000, 600 ÷ 25 = 24. We need 0.06 × x = 24, so x = 24 ÷ 0.06 = 400 (scale by 100: 2400 ÷ 6 = 400). Losing a power of ten gives 40 or 4000.

**37. (C)** 0.025 has 3 decimal places and 0.0004 has 4, so the product has 3 + 4 = 7 places: 25 × 4 = 100 → 0.0000100, the 1 sitting at the fifth place but the full written form carries 7 places. Counting only the larger factor gives 4; adding wrongly gives 6 or 8.

**38. (D)** Total = 0.45 × 1200 = 540 (45 × 1200 = 54000, two decimal places → 540.00). Spends 1/3 → 540 ÷ 3 = 180 spent, so keeps 540 − 180 = 360 (which is 2/3 of 540). Reporting the amount spent gives 180; reporting the total gives 540; misplacing the point gives 36.

**39. (A)** 0.7 × 1000 = 700; ÷100 = 7; × 0.5 = 3.5. Dropping the final factor gives 7; misplacing the point gives 35 or 0.35.

**40. (B)** A percent means 'per hundred', so 0.6% = 0.6 ÷ 100 = 0.006 (shift the point two places left). Shifting only one place gives 0.06; forgetting to divide gives 0.6.

**41. (A)** Round to 0.05 ÷ 0.012 ≈ 4. Exactly: scale both by 1000, 50.4 ÷ 12 = 4.2 (or 504 ÷ 120 = 4.2), closest to 4. Misplacing the point gives 40 or 0.4.

**42. (D)** 0.36 ÷ 0.6 = 0.6 (scale by 10: 3.6 ÷ 6 = 0.6). 0.36 ÷ 0.06 = 6 (scale by 100: 36 ÷ 6 = 6). Sum 0.6 + 6 = 6.6. Mis-aligning the place values gives 66, 0.66 or 6.06.

**43. (B)** 9.8 ÷ 0.35: scale both by 100, 980 ÷ 35 = 28 exactly, so 28 full jugs and 0 litres left (28 × 0.35 = 9.80). Stopping at 27 leaves a whole jug's worth; treating it as a non-integer gives 2.8.

**44. (D)** (0.2)³ = 0.008 (three decimal places). × 1000 shifts 3 right = 8. Then 8 ÷ 0.4 = 20 (scale by 10: 80 ÷ 4 = 20). Stopping at 8 ÷ 0.4 mishandled gives 2; an extra power of ten gives 200.

**45. (A)** 37.5% = 0.375 = 375/1000; dividing numerator and denominator by 125 gives 3/8. Leaving it as 375/1000 is unsimplified; 3/80 = 0.0375 (ten times too small); 37/100 = 0.37.

**46. (A)** Estimate 50 ÷ 0.007 ≈ 7000. Exactly: scale both by 10000, 504000 ÷ 72 = 7000. Losing a power of ten gives 700 or 70000.

**47. (D)** 4.8 ÷ 0.6 = 8 (48 ÷ 6). 0.16 ÷ 0.02 = 8 (scale by 100: 16 ÷ 2 = 8). Then 8 ÷ 8 = 1. Mishandling a place shift gives 10 or 0.1.

**48. (D)** Pay = 25 × 7.5 = 187.50. Donates 0.2 × 187.50 = 37.50, keeps 187.50 − 37.50 = 150 (which is 0.8 × 187.50). Reporting the donation gives 37.50; reporting the full pay gives 187.50; misplacing the point gives 15.

**49. (B)** The number = 0.45 ÷ 0.6 = 0.75 (scale by 10: 4.5 ÷ 6 = 0.75). Then 0.4 × 0.75 = 0.30 = 0.3 (4 × 75 = 300, three decimal places → 0.300). Multiplying 0.4 × 0.45 directly gives 0.18; misplacing the point gives 3 or 0.03.

**50. (C)** (0.05)² = 0.0025 (four decimal places). Denominator 0.5 × 0.005 = 0.0025 (25 × ... gives 5 × 5 = 25 with 1 + 3 = 4 places → 0.0025). Then 0.0025 ÷ 0.0025 = 1. Any place-shift slip gives 10 or 0.1.

**51. (C)** 0.0144 ÷ 0.012 = 1.2 (scale by 10000: 144 ÷ 120 = 1.2). 0.0144 ÷ 0.0012 = 12 (scale by 10000: 144 ÷ 12 = 12). Sum 1.2 + 12 = 13.2. Mis-aligning place values gives 132, 1.32 or 13.02.

**52. (A)** Multiply by 100: 1.04 × 100 = 104, so 104%. Forgetting to shift gives 1.04%; one too few shifts gives 10.4%; one too many gives 1040%.

**53. (D)** After the point: tenths = 2, hundredths = 0, thousandths = 5, ten-thousandths = 9. Thousandths digit 5 × 10 = 50, plus tenths digit 2 = 52. Confusing the places gives 7 (2+5) or 25 (5 then 2).

**54. (C)** 7/8 = 0.875, and 0.875 × 100 = 87.5%. Forgetting one shift gives 8.75%; forgetting both gives 0.875%; one too many gives 875%.

**55. (D)** Round to 0.04 ÷ 0.002 = 20, so near 19. Exactly: scale both by 10000, 399 ÷ 21 = 19. Misplacing the point gives 1.9 or 190.

**56. (B)** 0.8 × 0.75 = 0.6 (8 × 75 = 600, three decimal places → 0.600), then × 0.5 = 0.3. Finally 0.3 ÷ 0.3 = 1. Losing a power of ten in the chain gives 10 or 0.1.

**57. (A)** 0.18 ÷ 0.015: scale both by 1000, 180 ÷ 15 = 12 exactly, so 12 full doses and 0 leftover (12 × 0.015 = 0.180). A miscount leaves 0.015 or a partial 0.003 litre.

**58. (A)** Compute each value: P = 0.6 × 0.5 = 0.30; Q = 0.6 ÷ 0.5 = 1.2 (scale by 10: 6 ÷ 5 = 1.2); R = 0.6 + 0.5 = 1.1; S = 0.6 − 0.5 = 0.1. In increasing order: S (0.1) < P (0.3) < R (1.1) < Q (1.2). The trap is assuming division gives a small number and addition the largest, which swaps R and Q.

**59. (A)** Rate = 400 ÷ 0.25 = 1600 per kg (scale by 100: 40000 ÷ 25 = 1600). Then 1600 × 0.6 = 960. Stopping at the rate gives 1600; multiplying 400 × 0.6 without finding the rate gives 240; misplacing the point gives 96.

**60. (D)** 5.8 ÷ 0.8: scale both by 10, 58 ÷ 8 = 7 remainder 2, i.e. 7 full pieces using 7 × 0.8 = 5.6 m, leaving 5.8 − 5.6 = 0.2 m. Claiming no leftover ignores the remainder; rounding the quotient up gives 8; under-counting gives 6.
