## Worked Solutions

**1. (D)** The धातु is the bare verb root पठ् ('to read'); every other form is BUILT from it. पठति is an inflected verb form (3rd-singular present), पठनम् is a derived noun ('reading'), and पठितुम् is the infinitive ('to read'). Only पठ् is the root itself.

**2. (A)** श्रु means 'to hear/listen', NOT 'to do' — 'to do' is कृ. The other three pairings are all correct: पठ् 'to read', अस् 'to be', भाष् 'to speak'. The trap mixes up श्रु with कृ.

**3. (B)** गम् ('to go') is a common Sanskrit root but is NOT among the eight tabled here. The appendix lists पठ्, अस्, कृ, श्रु, दा, ज्ञा, क्री, भाष् — so दा, ज्ञा and क्री all belong to the set; गम् does not.

**4. (C)** भाषते is built only from भाष् ('to speak'); its stem भाष- and -ते ending are specific to that root. ज्ञा ('to know') has a quite different stem and could never yield भाषते. The form is uniquely tied to भाष्, so it cannot come from ज्ञा.

**5. (A)** A root is the seed for CONJUGATION: from दा and ज्ञा one builds their tense/person forms. Roots are conjugated, not declined like nouns (declension and the seven cases belong to nominals). Counting syllables and 'converting to an adjective' are not what root-knowledge gives.

**6. (A)** The -ति ending marks 3rd-person singular present in parasmaipada — true of both पठति (from पठ्) and शृणोति (from श्रु). They are NOT from the same root (पठ् vs श्रु), are present not past, and -ति is third person, never first.

**7. (D)** लट्-लकार is the PRESENT tense, so पठति = 'he reads', not 'he read'. The imperfect past is लङ् (अपठत्), the future is लृट् (पठिष्यति), and the command/imperative is लोट् (पठतु). The student confused present with past.

**8. (D)** लट् is the present, लङ् the imperfect past, and लृट् the future — only that row is right. Every distractor swaps at least one pair (e.g. making लट् past or लृट् present), which contradicts the lakara meanings taught.

**9. (A)** The अ- augment plus the past ending make अपठत् लङ् (imperfect past, 'he read'). The -ष्य- future marker in पठिष्यति makes it लृट् (future, 'he will read'). The forms are not swapped, neither is present, and neither is an imperative.

**10. (B)** Sanskrit has TEN लकाराः (tense-moods); लट् (present) is only one of them. Seven is the count of NOUN cases, three is the genders, and eight is the tabled roots — none of these is the lakara count.

**11. (C)** लोट् is the imperative (a command, 'let him do') and विधिलिङ् the optative (advice/possibility, 'he should do'). Present is लट्, past लङ्, future लृट्; and the last option simply reverses the correct pairing.

**12. (B)** अकरोत् is लङ् (past, 'he did'), करोति is लट् (present, 'he does'), करिष्यति is लृट् (future, 'he will do'). Past-to-future therefore runs अकरोत् → करोति → करिष्यति. Each distractor misplaces at least one form on the timeline.

**13. (C)** Sanskrit verbs fall into ten गण; पठ् is class 1, but अस्, कृ and श्रु sit in other classes whose stem-formation differs (hence अस्ति, करोति, शृणोति). They ARE verbs, they DO have present forms, and they are NOT class 1 like पठ्.

**14. (A)** भ्वादि (class 1) is the largest, most regular class, making पठ् the natural template (पठति/पठतः/पठन्ति …). It is not the only class (there are ten), not rare, and class-1 verbs do take the normal person endings.

**15. (B)** The correct 3rd-singular of अस् is अस्ति (class 2), not the class-1-style 'असति'. The learner wrongly assumed all roots follow पठ्. अस् IS a verb with a present form, and it is a different root from पठ्.

**16. (D)** कृ is an eighth-class root whose stem alternates strong करो- / weak कुरु-,कुर्व- (करोति vs कुर्वन्ति). It is NOT first-class like पठ् (it would then be 'करति'), not tenth, and it certainly does conjugate.

**17. (B)** -ते is the atmanepada 3rd-singular marker, so भाष् conjugates भाषते/भाषेते/भाषन्ते — the whole row is atmanepada. It is not parasmaipada (which would give भाषन्ति in the plural — a wrong form here), not past, and -ते is third person not first.

**18. (B)** Parasmaipada 3rd-plural ends in -न्ति (पठन्ति); atmanepada ends in -न्ते (भाषन्ते). The first distractor swaps the labels, and the last two wrongly force both classes onto a single ending. Only पठन्ति / भाषन्ते is matched correctly.

**19. (D)** The parasmaipada 3rd-person row is -ति / -तः / -न्ति (singular/dual/plural): पठति, पठतः, पठन्ति. So the plural is पठन्ति. -ते and -न्ते are atmanepada endings (wrong pada), and पठामः is the first-person plural, not third.

**20. (A)** The atmanepada 3rd-person row is -ते / -ेते / -न्ते, giving भाषते (sg), भाषेते (dual), भाषन्ते (pl). So the dual is भाषेते. भाषतः uses the parasmaipada dual ending, भाषन्ते is the plural, and भाषन्ति is a wrong parasmaipada-style plural.

**21. (C)** Whether a root is parasmaipada (-ति) or atmanepada (-ते) is lexically fixed — listed with the root, like पठ् (P) vs भाष् (A). It is not a free politeness choice, not time-of-day dependent, and Sanskrit VERBS do not vary by gender.

**22. (D)** उभय means 'both', so an उभयपद root can conjugate in BOTH parasmaipada (-ति) and atmanepada (-ते). Restricting it to only one pada contradicts the term, and it certainly does have present forms.

**23. (A)** Both are present (same tense), singular (same number), third person (same person). The genuine difference is the pada-class — पठ् is parasmaipada, भाष् atmanepada — plus naturally being different roots. They do not differ in tense, number or person.

**24. (B)** मध्यमपुरुष (2nd person) parasmaipada present runs -सि / -थः / -थ: पठसि (sg), पठथः (dual), पठथ (pl). So 'you two read' is पठथः. पठतः is the THIRD-person dual, पठसि is 2nd singular, and पठथ is 2nd plural.

**25. (A)** प्रथमपुरुष ('first person' literally) is what English calls the THIRD person (he/she/it). The reversal is the whole trap: do NOT read प्रथम as English 'first' (I) or as 'second' (you), and the choice certainly matters for agreement.

**26. (C)** The -मि ending marks उत्तमपुरुष singular = English FIRST person, so करोमि = 'I do'. प्रथमपुरुष would be करोति ('he does'), मध्यमपुरुष करोषि ('you do'). The last distractor mislabels the पुरुष even while getting the English right.

**27. (B)** The -सि ending is मध्यमपुरुष singular = English second person, so करोषि = 'you (one person) do', matching त्वम्. सः 'he' needs करोति, अहम् 'I' needs करोमि, and ते 'they' need कुर्वन्ति.

**28. (D)** Despite the name, उत्तमपुरुष = English first person (अस्मि 'I am', कुर्मः 'we do'). English THIRD person is प्रथमपुरुष. So उत्तम is neither English third nor second, and Sanskrit certainly HAS a first person.

**29. (D)** अस् runs अस्ति / स्तः / सन्ति in the third person; स्तः is therefore the 3rd-person DUAL ('the two are'). 'He is' (singular) is अस्ति, 'we are' is स्मः, and 'you two are' is स्थः — none of which is स्तः.

**30. (C)** उत्तमपुरुष plural of कृ is कुर्मः = 'we do', matching वयम्. 'I do' (singular) is करोमि, 'they do' (3rd plural) is कुर्वन्ति, and 'we two do' (1st dual) is कुर्वः — so कुर्मः is specifically the first-person PLURAL.

**31. (C)** Third-person parasmaipada present is पठति / पठतः / पठन्ति; the DUAL is पठतः. पठन्ति is the 3rd plural, पठथः is the 2nd-person dual, and पठावः is the 1st-person dual ('we two read').

**32. (D)** उत्तमपुरुष parasmaipada present is -मि / -वः / -मः: पठामि, पठावः, पठामः. So 'we all read' = पठामः. पठन्ति is 3rd-plural, पठथ is 2nd-plural, and पठतः is 3rd-dual — none is first person.

**33. (B)** बालकौ is dual ('two boys'), 3rd person, so the verb must be the 3rd-DUAL पठतः. पठति is singular (one reader), पठन्ति is plural (three or more), and पठामि is 1st-singular — none agrees with a dual third-person subject.

**34. (B)** छात्राः is plural and third person, so it needs the 3rd-PLURAL पठन्ति. पठतः is dual (only two), पठति is singular (one), and पठथ is 2nd-person plural ('you all read') — wrong person.

**35. (B)** पठामि means 'I read' (उत्तमपुरुष), so it cannot follow त्वम् ('you', मध्यमपुरुष). The right form is पठसि ('you read'). त्वम् is correctly second person, and the error is the verb's PERSON, not its number.

**36. (C)** उत्तमपुरुष (first-person) singular of अस् is अस्मि = 'I am', so अहम् अस्मि. अस्ति is 'he is' (3rd sg), असि is 'you are' (2nd sg), and सन्ति is 'they are' (3rd pl) — none matches 'I'.

**37. (A)** ते ('they') is 3rd-plural, taking सन्ति, so ते सन्ति is correct. ते अस्ति wrongly uses the singular for a plural subject; अहम् अस्ति puts a 3rd-person verb with 'I' (should be अस्मि); त्वम् अस्मि puts a 1st-person verb with 'you' (should be असि).

**38. (C)** 'The two boys are' is dual third person → स्तः. सन्ति is the plural ('they all are'), अस्ति the singular ('he is'), and स्मः the first-person plural ('we are'). Only स्तः fits a dual third-person subject.

**39. (D)** वयम् ('we') is first-person plural, requiring स्मः: वयम् स्मः. अस्ति is 3rd-singular ('he is') — wrong person and number; अस्मि is 1st-SINGULAR ('I am') — wrong number; सन्ति is 3rd-plural ('they are') — wrong person.

**40. (A)** असि is the मध्यमपुरुष (second-person) SINGULAR of अस् = 'you are'. 'He is' is अस्ति (प्रथमपुरुष), 'I am' is अस्मि (उत्तमपुरुष), and the 2nd-PLURAL 'you all are' is स्थ — not असि.

**41. (C)** कृ takes the strong करो- in the singular (करोमि, करोषि, करोति) but the weak कुरु-/कुर्व- elsewhere — including the first-plural कुर्मः. The three distractors are all singular forms that keep the strong करो- stem.

**42. (B)** First-person dual of कृ is कुर्वः ('we two do'), on the weak stem कुर्व-. कुर्मः is the first-PLURAL ('we all do'), करोमि the first-SINGULAR ('I do'), and कुरुतः the THIRD-person dual ('the two do') — wrong person.

**43. (B)** Third-person dual of कृ is कुरुतः. करोति is the singular ('he does'), कुर्वन्ति the plural ('they do'), and कुर्मः the first-person plural ('we do') — none is the 3rd-dual.

**44. (B)** वयम् ('we', 1st plural) takes कुर्मः — correct. अहम् ('I') needs करोमि not करोति; त्वम् ('you') needs करोषि not करोमि; सः ('he', singular) needs करोति not the plural कुर्वन्ति. Only वयम् कुर्मः agrees in person and number.

**45. (C)** कृ does not conjugate like a class-1 verb; it forms करो- and yields करोति for 'he does'. 'करति' wrongly applies the पठति pattern. कृ certainly has a present, and करोमि means 'I do', not 'he does'.

**46. (D)** कुर्वन्ति is the 3rd-person PLURAL of कृ = 'they do'. 'We do' (1st pl) is कुर्मः, 'the two do' (3rd dual) is कुरुतः, and 'you all do' (2nd pl) is कुरुथ — so कुर्वन्ति is specifically 3rd plural.

**47. (D)** Atmanepada first-person singular ends in -ए: भाषे ('I speak'). भाषामि uses a parasmaipada-style first-person ending (wrong pada), भाषति is a wrong parasmaipada 3rd-singular, and भाषते is the atmanepada THIRD-singular ('he speaks').

**48. (C)** Atmanepada 3rd-person row is भाषते / भाषेते / भाषन्ते; भाषन्ते is the PLURAL 'they speak'. The singular is भाषते, the dual भाषेते, and the first-plural 'we speak' is भाषामहे — none of which is भाषन्ते.

**49. (B)** ते ('they') is 3rd-plural; the atmanepada plural is भाषन्ते, so ते भाषन्ते is right. भाषन्ति and भाषति apply parasmaipada -न्ति/-ति endings to an atmanepada root (wrong pada), and 'I speak' is भाषे, not भाषति.

**50. (A)** -ते is the atmanepada third-singular PRESENT ending; भाषते = 'he speaks'. Past would need the augment (अभाषत), future the -ष्य- marker, and the imperative is a different ending. The -ते says PADA (atmanepada), not tense.

**51. (C)** A finite verb agrees with its subject in PERSON and NUMBER (e.g. अहम् करोमि, ते कुर्वन्ति). Case and gender are properties of nouns, not of the agreement a verb shows; so 'case and gender', 'gender and number', and 'case only' are all wrong.

**52. (A)** सः is 3rd-person SINGULAR, so the verb must be पठति. The cleanest single fix keeps the stated subject ('he') and corrects the verb. Changing सः to ते alters the meaning to 'they', पठतः is dual (two, not one), and the sentence is not already correct.

**53. (D)** आवाम् ('we two') is 1st-person dual, matching पठावः ('we two read') — full agreement. अहम् needs पठामि (not the 3rd-dual पठतः); ते needs पठन्ति (not the singular पठति); त्वम् needs पठसि (not the 1st-person पठामि).

**54. (D)** यूयम् is 2nd-person PLURAL, so the verb is पठथ ('you all read'). पठसि is 2nd-singular ('you [one] read'), पठथः is 2nd-dual ('you two read'), and पठामः is 1st-plural ('we read') — wrong person.

**55. (C)** करोमि is उत्तमपुरुष singular = 'I do', so its subject is अहम्. सः ('he') takes करोति, वयम् ('we') takes कुर्मः, and त्वम् ('you') takes करोषि — only अहम् matches करोमि.

**56. (D)** लङ् (imperfect past) prefixes an अ- augment to the stem: अ + करोत् = अकरोत् ('he did'). It is NOT the negative prefix अ- (as in अधर्म), not a person marker, and not a case ending (verbs take no case).

**57. (A)** The verb अकरोत् carries the लङ् PAST AUGMENT अ-; the noun अधर्मः carries the NEGATIVE prefix अ- ('non-'). They look alike but do opposite jobs — one signals past tense, the other negation. Neither is a person marker.

**58. (A)** अधर्मः is a NOUN whose अ- negates धर्म ('non-righteousness'). अकरोत्, अपठत् and अभवत् are all लङ् PAST verb forms whose अ- is the past-tense augment — so only अधर्मः has the negative prefix.

**59. (C)** अकरोत् is the imperfect past of कृ = 'he did'; its अ- is the लङ् augment, NOT a negative. So 'he does not do' is wrong. Future would be करिष्यति and the imperative करोतु — neither matches अकरोत् either.

**60. (A)** Two errors: लट् is the PRESENT (future is लृट्), and कुर्वन्ति is 3rd-PLURAL 'they do' (whereas 'we do' is the first-plural कुर्मः). Only the correction that fixes BOTH is right; each distractor leaves at least one mistake standing.
