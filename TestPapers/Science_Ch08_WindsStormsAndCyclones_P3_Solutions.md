## Worked Solutions

**1. (C)** The air sealed inside is the same air at the same temperature, so the push it exerts on the walls does not change. What changes is outside: the mountain air is thinner and pushes inward less, so the balance shifts and the walls feel a net outward strain. 'Greater because high places have stronger pressure' is backwards — air pressure is lower at altitude. 'Zero' is false; the trapped air still has the same pressure. 'Less, because the trapped air thins out' is wrong — the jar is sealed, so the trapped air's amount and pressure cannot change just by moving it.

**2. (B)** Fast-moving air exerts lower pressure. Blowing across the top makes the pressure above the strip drop, so the higher-pressure still air underneath pushes the strip up. The student's claim is wrong because air blown OVER the top cannot push it up from above — that would push it down. 'Breath scoops under' is wrong; you blow across the top, not underneath. 'Warm breath floats it' is irrelevant — the effect works with any fast stream regardless of warmth. 'Removes the weight' is meaningless — weight is unchanged.

**3. (D)** Boiling fills the can with steam and pushes air out; sealing and cooling makes that steam condense to a few drops of liquid, leaving almost no gas inside. The inside pressure collapses while the outside air pressure stays high, so the atmosphere crushes the can. 'Cold air becomes heavier' is not the mechanism — the imbalance is between inside and outside pressure, not air weight. 'Metal folds itself' is wrong; metal contraction is far too small to crush a can. 'Extra water pushes the walls in' is backwards — water forming reduces the inside pressure, it does not push outward.

**4. (C)** Moving air has lower pressure than still air. The stream lowers the pressure in the gap, so the still air pressing on each sheet's outer face — now at higher pressure — pushes the two sheets toward each other. The 'fly apart' prediction wrongly assumes the moving air pushes outward. 'Breath glues them' is irrelevant to the pressure effect. 'Gap air pushes harder outward' is the exact opposite of what fast air does. 'Lighter so gravity pulls in' makes no sense — gravity acts downward, not inward.

**5. (B)** Air pressure pushes inward from all sides, but the fluids and gases inside our bodies push outward with an equal pressure, so the two balance and we feel nothing. 'No real pressure' is false — the same atmosphere crushes a sealed can. 'Skin is rigid and seals air out' is wrong; skin is flexible and the balance is about internal pressure, not sealing. 'Gravity pulls pressure away' confuses gravity with the all-directional push of air pressure.

**6. (B)** Hot water expanded the air and inflated the balloon. Ice water reverses this: the air contracts and its pressure falls below the outside atmosphere, so the balloon is pushed inward, even sucked into the neck. 'Inflates more' is wrong — cooling shrinks air, it does not expand it. 'Stays the same' ignores that the temperature changed. 'Pops off because cold air pushes harder' is backwards — cold air pushes LESS, not more.

**7. (C)** Air expands when heated and contracts when cooled. The cold shrank the trapped air so the balloon looked smaller; warming let it expand again — the same air throughout. 'Air leaked out and back in' contradicts 'no air leaked.' 'Cold destroys air' is false; matter is not destroyed by cooling. 'Only the rubber shrinks' is wrong — rubber's size change is tiny; the visible change is the air's volume responding to temperature.

**8. (A)** Heated air expands; the same air now spreads over more space, so each litre is lighter (less dense). The surrounding cooler, denser air sinks under it and pushes the warm bag up. 'Gains weight from smoke' is wrong — warm air becomes lighter, not heavier. 'Cooled by the flame' is contradictory; a flame heats. 'Loses all its air' is nonsense — the air is heated, not removed.

**9. (D)** The hot surface heats the air, which expands, grows less dense and rises; this leaves a region of lower pressure near the ground, so cooler surrounding air flows in — that inflow is the breeze. The other orderings put effects before their causes: a breeze cannot start before the heating that drives it; air does not rise 'for no reason'; and cooler air sinking onto the lot with 'no breeze' ignores that the rising hot air must be replaced.

**10. (D)** Wind blows from high to low pressure, and a bigger pressure difference drives faster wind. Since X is highest and Z is lowest, the X-to-Z gap is the largest, giving the strongest wind from X toward Z. 'Y toward X' blows from lower to higher pressure — impossible. 'Z toward Y' also blows from low to high — impossible. 'No wind' is wrong; differences in pressure are exactly what create wind.

**11. (B)** The larger and steeper the pressure difference, the faster the air rushes from high to low, so the wind is stronger today. 'High pressure holds air still' is wrong — high pressure is the source the air flows FROM. 'Wind does not depend on pressure' contradicts the core idea of the chapter. 'Air moves only when pressures are equal' is exactly backwards — equal pressures mean no wind.

**12. (B)** Rising warm air leaves a low-pressure gap; the surrounding cooler air is denser and at higher pressure, so it flows in to fill the gap — that inflow is wind. 'Cooler air is lighter' is false; cool air is denser and heavier than warm air. 'Sun pushes air sideways' is not the mechanism — the pressure difference moves the air. 'Warm air comes back down' contradicts the fact that warm air keeps rising.

**13. (C)** When air is heated it expands, so a given volume now holds less air than the same volume of cold air — it is less dense, hence 'lighter.' 'Fewer kinds of gases' is wrong; heating does not change which gases are present. 'Lost matter to the Sun' is false; heating adds energy, not removes matter. 'A special upward force only warm air feels' is a misconception — warm air rises because the denser cool air around it sinks and displaces it.

**14. (C)** Land warms faster than water under the same Sun. The air over the warmer land rises, leaving lower pressure there, so cooler higher-pressure air over the sea moves in — a sea breeze. 'Water heats faster' is the opposite of the truth. 'Same rate, random wind' is wrong — the different heating rates are exactly what set the direction. 'Sea always hotter' is false; by afternoon the land is hotter, which is why the breeze comes off the sea.

**15. (A)** Land loses heat faster than water, so at night the sea is the warmer surface. Air rises over the warmer sea, lowering pressure there, and cooler air flows out from the land — a land breeze, the reverse of daytime. 'Sea cools faster' is wrong; water holds heat longer. 'Reverse for no reason' ignores the cause — the temperature roles swap. 'Land stays warmer all night' is false; land cools quickly after sunset.

**16. (C)** The sea breeze depends on the land being hotter than the sea. If the land fails to heat and is cooler than the sea, the usual pressure difference vanishes or reverses, so the breeze weakens or flips toward the sea. 'Even stronger' is wrong — the driving difference is weaker, not stronger. 'Straight up' ignores that breezes are horizontal flows driven by horizontal pressure differences. 'No change, temperature doesn't matter' contradicts the whole mechanism of breezes.

**17. (B)** Water changes temperature slowly, so the nearby sea acts as a buffer; daytime sea breezes cool the coast and night-time land breezes are mild, smoothing out extremes. 'Sea has no effect / coincidence' ignores the breeze mechanism. 'Inland air has no weight' is false — all air has weight. 'Coast gets far less sunlight' is wrong; sunlight is similar at the same latitude, the difference is water's slow heating.

**18. (B)** In summer the land becomes hotter than the sea, so air over the land rises and lower pressure forms there; moisture-laden air flows in from the sea toward the land and its water falls as monsoon rain. 'Land to sea' describes the dry winter direction, not the rainy summer monsoon. 'Poles to equator, cold dry air' is not the monsoon and would not bring heavy rain. 'Straight downward' is not how winds blow.

**19. (D)** Winter winds blow from land to sea; having travelled over land, they carry little moisture and are mostly dry — the opposite of the moisture-laden summer monsoon. 'Even wetter, cold winds carry more rain' is wrong; land-origin winter winds are dry. 'Identical in moisture' ignores that direction (and origin over land vs sea) determines moisture. 'Straight up bringing snow' misdescribes the horizontal, dry winter flow.

**20. (D)** Because the Sun heats the equator far more than the poles, and land differently from sea, the resulting pressure differences set up the planet's wind patterns, including the monsoon. 'Moon's gravity' drives tides, not the global winds. 'Ocean currents alone' is wrong — the currents themselves and the winds both trace back to solar heating. 'Household fans' is absurd at a planetary scale.

**21. (A)** The equator's strong heating makes its air rise; cold dense polar air sinks, and air flows between them — giant currents that move heat across the globe and drive the prevailing winds. 'Poles hotter than equator' reverses reality. 'All wind stops' is wrong — the temperature difference is what CREATES wind. 'Air loses its weight' is false; air always has weight.

**22. (A)** Hot air rises quickly and humid air supplies the water vapour; as the rising air cools, that vapour condenses into the tall clouds of a thunderstorm. 'Too heavy to move' is wrong — warm air is light and rises. 'Contains no water' contradicts 'humid.' 'Cooler than the ground and sinks' is backwards — the storm air is warm and rises, it does not sink.

**23. (B)** A thunderstorm is driven by strong up-currents of warm moist air meeting droplets and ice falling down — these violent up-and-down motions build charge and turbulence. 'Cold dry sinking air' would suppress, not power, a storm. 'Lightning first, dragging air up' reverses cause and effect — the rising air comes first and helps make the lightning. 'Ground heating the cloud by touch' is not how the high cloud is energised.

**24. (B)** Light travels far faster than sound, so the flash arrives almost instantly while the slower sound (thunder) reaches us a few seconds later. 'Thunder created later' is wrong — both happen at the same instant. 'Sound faster than light' is the reverse of the truth. 'Eyes react seconds faster' is false; the delay comes from the travel speeds of light and sound, not reaction times.

**25. (A)** Lightning is an enormous electric spark released when charges built up in the storm cloud suddenly discharge. 'Reflected sunlight' is wrong — lightning happens in dark storm clouds and even at night. 'Burning gas' is not the mechanism; it is an electrical discharge. 'Sound turning into light' is impossible — sound and light are different things, and the flash is not made from thunder.

**26. (D)** Light arrives almost instantly, so the gap is set by how long the slower sound takes to travel — a shorter gap means a shorter distance, so the storm is closer. 'Moving away with a shorter gap' reverses the relationship. 'Lightning getting weaker' confuses brightness with distance; the gap measures distance. 'Speed of light increasing' is false — that speed is fixed.

**27. (B)** When water vapour condenses it releases heat; this extra warmth makes the air rise even more, drops the central pressure further and draws in faster wind — a self-feeding loop. 'Cools and halts' is wrong — condensation releases heat, it does not cool the storm. 'Makes air heavier and sinks' is backwards; the warmed air rises. 'Raises pressure and slows wind' is the opposite of what intensifies a cyclone.

**28. (D)** The cycle begins with the Sun warming the sea so moist air rises (ii); that vapour condenses, releasing heat and dropping the pressure further (iii); the lowered pressure then sucks in faster wind (i), which fuels more rising air. Orders starting with (i) or (iii) put effects before the heating and rising that cause them. (ii)→(i)→(iii) wrongly has wind rushing in before the condensation that lowers the pressure to pull it.

**29. (D)** A cyclone runs on the heat and moisture of warm sea air. On land that supply is cut off, so the self-feeding cycle starves and the storm weakens. 'Colder than outer space' is irrelevant and false. 'Blocked by buildings' is far too weak to stop a storm hundreds of kilometres wide. 'Loses its eye instantly' is not the mechanism — the energy starvation, not the eye, is the cause.

**30. (C)** The very centre (eye) is calm and low-pressure; surrounding it the eye wall has the fiercest winds and heaviest rain; beyond that lie outer spiralling cloud bands. 'Violent eye, calm eye wall' swaps the two. 'High-pressure eye with no clouds' is wrong — the eye is a LOW-pressure core. 'Calm everywhere' contradicts the destructive winds a cyclone is famous for.

**31. (C)** The eye wall hits first with fierce wind; then the calm eye passes overhead; then the eye wall on the far side brings fierce wind again, now from the opposite direction because the storm has moved past. 'Steady mild wind' ignores the violent eye wall. 'No wind at all' is impossible inside a cyclone. 'Permanent calm at the end' is wrong — the far eye wall brings the wind back.

**32. (C)** Intensifying means a deeper low at the centre: the central pressure drops further, and because wind rushes from high pressure toward that ever-lower centre, the winds blow faster. 'Pressure rises and winds faster' is contradictory — rising pressure weakens a cyclone. 'Pressure falls but winds slower' breaks the link between a deeper low and faster wind. 'Both constant' describes a steady, not intensifying, storm.

**33. (A)** Storms and cyclones are low-pressure systems, so a sharp pressure drop warns that one may be approaching. 'Clear settled weather' goes with high or steady pressure, not a sharp fall. 'Wind about to stop' is wrong — falling pressure usually means rising wind. 'Temperature below freezing' is unrelated; a barometer measures pressure, not temperature.

**34. (A)** Wind blows from high to low pressure; the extreme low at the cyclone's centre pulls in surrounding air at great speed, and that inrush IS the cyclone's wind. 'Keeps the air still' is the opposite — the low pressure drives the wind. 'Raises sea level only far out at sea' is wrong; the surge strikes the coast. 'Dries and warms so no clouds form' contradicts the towering clouds of a cyclone.

**35. (D)** Cyclone, hurricane and typhoon are regional names for the same kind of swirling tropical low-pressure storm. 'Completely different events' is wrong — they share the same structure and cause. 'Fixed different sizes' is false; the names depend on region, not size. 'Old names for tornadoes' confuses tropical cyclones with the much smaller, different tornado.

**36. (A)** Hundreds of kilometres across, with a calm low-pressure eye ringed by violent winds and swirling bands — those are the defining features of a cyclone. A tornado is far narrower (a slim column, not hundreds of km wide). A land breeze is a gentle one-way coastal flow, not a swirling system. A 'thunderstorm with no rotation' lacks the swirling, eye-centred structure described.

**37. (B)** The storm surge — sea water heaped up by the low pressure and fierce winds and driven far inland — causes the worst flooding and loss of life on low coasts. 'Loud thunder' is only sound and harms no one. 'Hailstones' are minor compared with a surge. 'Lightning' is dangerous but kills far fewer than the surge's flooding.

**38. (D)** The cyclone's low pressure lets the sea rise and its strong winds pile the water into a surge that is pushed far inland, flooding low coasts. 'Slow rainwater' understates it — the surge is sea water driven by the storm, not gradual rain. 'Sea level drops' is the opposite of a surge. 'Entirely fresh river water' is wrong — a surge is sea water pushed ashore.

**39. (C)** Early warnings from satellites and radar give people time to evacuate to shelters, which has hugely cut deaths. 'Cyclones have become weaker' is false — they are as strong as ever. 'Sea no longer floods' is wrong; surges still occur, but people now get out of the way. 'Nobody lives near the coast' is untrue — coasts remain densely populated.

**40. (B)** Moving promptly to a safe shelter and following official guidance keeps you out of the surge and wind. 'Watching the waves at the beach' puts you in the path of the deadly surge. 'Ignoring it until the wind is strong' wastes the warning time meant for escape. 'Driving toward the sea' heads straight into the danger.

**41. (C)** Storing clean water and keeping supplies and a radio ready means you can survive flooding and follow updates. Opening all doors and windows lets the wind in to damage the house. Going out to photograph the storm exposes you to deadly winds and surge. Sheltering under a lone tree is dangerous in lightning and high wind.

**42. (A)** A cyclone's fierce winds and towering waves can easily capsize or sink small boats, so going to sea is deadly. 'Fish swim away' is a fishing concern, not the safety reason. 'Water too cold to sail in' is false and irrelevant to the danger. 'Sea water vanishes' is impossible.

**43. (A)** Fast-moving air has lower pressure; the express drags air past, lowering the pressure in the gap, so the higher-pressure air on the person's far side pushes them toward the train — which is why you must stand back. 'Moving train blows air that pushes you toward it' wrongly has the wind pushing you in. 'Magnetically attracts' is false. 'Rails vibrate and drag you' is not a real mechanism.

**44. (D)** The fast air in the stream is at lower pressure than the still air around it; if the ball drifts to the edge, the higher outside pressure pushes it back into the low-pressure stream, so it self-centres. 'Sticky air glues it' is not real. 'Becomes weightless' is false — its weight is still balanced by the upward airflow, not removed. 'Heat lifts by magnetism' confuses heat, lift and magnetism, none of which is the pressure effect at work.

**45. (D)** Air pressure pushes up on the card with more force than the small weight of the water pushes down, so the card and water stay put. 'Glued by being wet' is not what holds the weight up. 'Water can't fall upside down' is false — it would fall without the air pressure. 'Magnetic force' has nothing to do with water and glass.

**46. (D)** Air pressure comes from the weight of the air above; high on a mountain there is less air overhead, so the outside pressure is lower than at the seashore. 'More air above on the mountain' is backwards — there is less. 'No pressure at all' is false; the air is thinner, not absent. 'Exactly the same everywhere' ignores that pressure falls with height.

**47. (B)** Heated air expands and becomes less dense; the surrounding cooler, denser air sinks beneath it and pushes the lighter warm air up. 'Cools, shrinks, becomes heavier, so it rises' is self-contradictory — heavier air sinks. 'Air turns into water' is not what happens to heated air. 'Gains weight and heavy air rises' is doubly wrong: heating lightens air, and heavy air sinks.

**48. (A)** Persistent warming makes the air expand and rise away, so less air sits over the region and its pressure falls — that is why hot regions become low-pressure zones. 'Higher pressure because warm air is heavier' is wrong; warm air is lighter and rises. 'No change' contradicts the link between heating and rising air. 'Zero pressure' is false — air remains, just less of it.

**49. (B)** Wind blows from high pressure to low pressure. By day the sea (cooler) is the higher-pressure side and the land (warm) is the lower-pressure side, so the breeze flows from sea to land. 'Land to sea' goes from low to high pressure, which is impossible. 'Straight upward only' ignores the horizontal pressure difference. 'In a circle with no net flow' contradicts the clear sea-to-land pressure gradient.

**50. (C)** As moist air rises and its vapour condenses, heat is released, the air rises more, and the central pressure drops further — so air keeps rushing in to the deepening low, a self-feeding loop. 'Wind manufactures low pressure with no heat or moisture' ignores the energy source that drives it. 'Vacuum cleaner unrelated to pressure' is wrong — it is precisely a pressure effect. 'Storm cools the sea and cool seas pull in wind' contradicts the warm-sea fuel a cyclone needs.

**51. (B)** Cooling makes the trapped air contract and its pressure fall; the outside air, still at higher pressure, then presses the bottle's sides inward, denting it. 'Freezer squeezed it mechanically' is not what a freezer does. 'Cold made the plastic expand and fold' is wrong — cold contracts plastic, and the effect here is about the air's pressure. 'Water soaked through' is false; a sealed bottle does not let freezer water in, and that would not dent it.

**52. (A)** Air moves from high pressure to low pressure, so the high-pressure region feeds air outward toward the low. 'Stays trapped' contradicts the pressure difference that drives wind. 'Flows inward from low to high' is the reverse of the rule. 'Rises straight up leaving the surface empty' is not what a high-pressure region does — high pressure is generally associated with sinking, not a surface emptied of air.

**53. (C)** When warm air rises and moves off, less air remains over the surface, so the weight pressing down — the pressure — falls. 'Leaves more air behind' is the opposite of air moving away. 'Sun adds pressure to cool surfaces' is unrelated to this mechanism. 'Turns into water that weighs the surface down' is wrong — the air rising away is what lowers the pressure.

**54. (A)** Fast-moving fluid has lower pressure; with low pressure between them and higher pressure outside, the objects are pushed together — the same reason close-sailing ships can be drawn in. 'Moving fluids exert higher pressure' is the reverse of the truth. 'Carries them by sticking' is not the pressure mechanism. 'Magnetic force' has nothing to do with moving air or water.

**55. (C)** A deeper central low means a bigger pressure difference between the surroundings and the centre, so air rushes in faster — faster winds. 'Lower pressure calms the air' is backwards. 'No wind' is wrong — the deep low drives strong inflow. 'Wind blows outward from the centre' reverses the direction; air rushes IN toward the low.

**56. (D)** Heating expands the air in the flask, which pushes on the water and lowers its level; cooling contracts the air and the water rises again — direct evidence that air expands on heating and contracts on cooling. 'Created and destroyed' is false; the same air just changes volume. 'Always pushes water up' ignores that the level fell when heated. 'No response to temperature' contradicts the observed movement.

**57. (D)** The true driver is pressure: wind flows from high to low. Because hot regions tend to become low-pressure, the flow often runs from cooler (higher-pressure) toward warmer (lower-pressure) regions — so the student's pattern is roughly right but for the wrong stated reason. 'Cold air attracted to heat' wrongly makes temperature the cause. 'Hot to cold in every case' is too absolute and ignores pressure. 'Nothing to do with pressure' denies the chapter's central idea.

**58. (A)** Boiling replaces the air with steam; on cooling the steam condenses to a few drops, so almost no gas is left and the inside pressure collapses far more than merely cooling trapped air would — giving a dramatic crush. 'Boiling softens the metal' is not the reason; the effect is about pressure. 'Room-temperature air cannot be cooled' is false. 'Boiling adds extra air' is wrong — it drives air OUT and replaces it with steam.

**59. (A)** Crushing needs a low inside pressure compared with outside; an open hole lets outside air rush in to equalise the pressure as the steam condenses, so no difference builds and the can stays whole. 'Crush even faster' is wrong — an open can cannot build the needed pressure difference. 'Metal melts' and 'steam catches fire' are not what happens when a can cools with a hole in it.

**60. (C)** As the cyclone moves over the town, the front eye wall hits with fierce wind, the calm eye passes over (the lull), then the rear eye wall brings fierce wind from the other direction. 'Stops and restarts itself' is not how a moving storm behaves. 'Two separate cyclones' is unnecessary — one storm's structure explains it. 'Changes its mind for no reason' ignores the eye-and-eye-wall structure.
