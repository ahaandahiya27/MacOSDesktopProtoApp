Worked Solutions

1. (C) Sum the moves with the start: 5 + (−8) + (−6) + 11 + (−4) + 9 + (−13). Group the negatives: −8 −6 −4 −13 = −31; group the positives with the start: 5 + 11 + 9 = 25. So 25 + (−31) = −6. A common slip drops the start value or mishandles a sign, giving −11 or −16; another reads the answer with the wrong sign to get +6.

2. (C) Positives: 34 + 8 + 6 = 48. Negatives: −27 −19 −15 = −61. Total: 48 + (−61) = −13. Adding all magnitudes ignoring signs gives 109; combining only the negatives gives −61; reporting the right size with the wrong sign gives +13.

3. (D) Since b − a is the opposite of a − b, if one is positive the other is negative; so the claim that both are positive is false. The other three are all true: subtracting a negative adds, integers are closed under subtraction but division can leave the integers, and subtraction is not associative.

4. (B) Work left to right: (−360) ÷ (−6) = 60; 60 ÷ (−5) = −12; (−12) ÷ 2 = −6; (−6) ÷ (−3) = 2. There are three negative divisors plus the negative dividend — four negatives in all gives a positive result. Stopping at −6 or mismanaging the parity of signs yields −2, +6, or −6.

5. (C) Do × and ÷ first, left to right. (−5) × (−6) = 30. For the last term, (−4) × (+3) = −12, then −12 ÷ (−2) = 6; it is subtracted: −6. So (−8) + 30 − 6 = 16. Treating the subtracted 6 as +6 gives 28; mishandling the first product's sign gives −2.

6. (B) An integer plus its own opposite is 0 by the additive-inverse property: (−83) + 83 = 0. Multiplying by 0 gives 0 but by the zero-product rule, not the inverse property. The third equals 166, and the fourth equals −83, neither being 0.

7. (C) There are five factors of (−1); an odd count of negatives makes the product negative, so the (−1) factors multiply to −1, giving a × (−1) = −a, the opposite of a. With an even count it would be a itself, but five is odd. It is not always negative (depends on a's sign) nor 0.

8. (D) Start −7, then −5, +9, −8, +3. Sum the changes: −5 +9 −8 +3 = −1. So −7 + (−1) = −8 °C. Forgetting the start and summing only changes gives −1; misreading a fall as a rise can give −2 or +6.

9. (A) Magnitudes: 2 × 3 × 1 × 4 × 5 × 1 = 120. Count the negative factors: −2, −1, −4, −5, −1 — that is five negatives (the +3 is positive). Five is odd, so the product is negative: −120. An even miscount gives +120; adding magnitudes mistakenly gives ±15.

10. (C) Changes: +18 −27 −14 +31 −9. Climbs: 18 + 31 = 49. Descents: 27 + 14 + 9 = 50. Net = 49 − 50 = −1 m. Twice this is −2 m. Forgetting to double gives −1; sign error on the doubling gives +2.

11. (D) An even number of negative factors gives a positive product, so four negatives multiply to a positive — the claim that it is negative is wrong. The others hold: opposite signs divide to negative, three negatives (odd) multiply to negative, and e.g. (−2) − (−9) = +7.

12. (B) Net = 6 − 9 + 4 − 11 + 8 − 7 + 2. Rights (with start): 6 + 4 + 8 + 2 = 20. Lefts: 9 + 11 + 7 = 27. So 20 − 27 = −7. Dropping a step or swapping a direction gives −5, +7, or −9.

13. (A) A positive product needs an EVEN number of negative factors. Among 0 through 9, the even options 0, 2, and 8 are all possible; 5 is odd and would force a negative product, so it is impossible.

14. (D) Originally 6 negatives (even) → positive product. Turning one positive factor negative raises the negative count to 7 (odd), so the product becomes negative. It is not zero (no factor is 0), and the sign does change.

15. (A) First term: (−108) ÷ (−9) = 12. Second: (−7) × (−4) = 28. Third: (−2) × 8 = −16, then −16 ÷ (−4) = 4, subtracted → −4. Total: 12 + 28 − 4 = 36. Adding the last term instead of subtracting gives 44; sign slips give 28 or 4.

16. (A) "How much greater" means (−17) − (−63) = −17 + 63 = 46. Adding the magnitudes gives 80; reversing the subtraction gives −46.

17. (B) (−3)⁴ = 81 (even power, positive), (−3)³ = −27, (−3)² = 9, (−3)¹ = −3. So 81 + (−27) − 9 + (−3) = 81 − 27 − 9 − 3 = 42. Treating odd powers as positive, or −(−3)² as +9, gives the larger wrong values.

18. (A) Left to right for × and ÷: 72 ÷ (−8) = −9, then −9 × 3 = −27, and it is subtracted: 40 − (−27) = 40 + 27 = 67. Last term: (−54) ÷ (−6) = 9. Total 67 + 9 = 76. Reading 40 − (−27) as 40 − 27 gives 22; other sign slips give 58 or 4.

19. (B) a + 0 = a and a × 1 = a, so 0 and 1 are the additive and multiplicative identities; the only integers that are their own reciprocals are 1 and −1. Most integers have no integer reciprocal (e.g. 3's reciprocal is 1/3), 1 is not the additive identity, and any integer × 0 = 0, not the integer.

20. (C) Magnitude is 1×2×3×4×5×6×7 = 5040. There are seven negative factors; seven is odd, so the product is negative: −5040. An even miscount gives +5040; summing instead of multiplying gives ±28.

21. (D) Total rise = (−80) − (−720) = 640 m over 8 steps, so each step is 640 ÷ 8 = +80 m. After 5 steps: −720 + 5 × 80 = −720 + 400 = −320 m. Using 640÷8 wrongly as 90, or stopping at −400 (the rise, not the depth), are the traps.

22. (A) Each bracket is −n, so the product is (−n) × (−n) × (−n) = (−1)³ × n³ = −n³ (three negatives → negative). It is not n³ nor −3n, and its sign follows n³, so it is not always positive.

23. (A) Nine copies of −14: 9 × (−14) = −126. Miscounting as 8 or 10 gives −112 or −140; sign error gives +126.

24. (B) First bracket: (−3) − 2 = −5, so (−150) ÷ (−5) = 30. Second bracket: (−4)×(−3) = 12, 12 + (−2) = 10, subtracted → −10. So 30 − 10 = 20. Forgetting to subtract the second bracket gives 30 then 40; sign slips give −10 or 0.

25. (A) Writing 97 = 100 + (−3) and multiplying each part by (−12) is distribution of multiplication over addition. The result is −1200 + 36 = −1164. The associative, commutative, and inverse properties describe different rearrangements, not this expansion.

39. (B) p×q×r×s is a product of four negatives (even) → positive. The sum p+q+r+s of four negatives is negative. Positive × negative = negative. It is never zero (no factor is 0), and the sign is always determined.
