## Worked Solutions

**1. (A)** A statistical statement reasons from data to a *likely* (not certain) conclusion. "A person 6 ft tall is more likely to be an adult than a toddler" is a likelihood claim from a pattern. The others are definitions/arithmetic facts — certain, not statistical.

**2. (D)** Shubman played 5 matches, Yashasvi 4. More innings give more chances to pile up runs, so the raw total is biased toward whoever batted more often. A fair comparison divides by the number of matches (the mean). The "bigger total = better" reflex is exactly the trap.

**3. (C)** Mean = (0 + 17 + 21 + 90) ÷ 4 = 128 ÷ 4 = 32. (128 is the total, not the mean — option D is the classic forgot-to-divide slip.)

**4. (A)** Sort: 0, 17, 21, 90. Even count (4), so the median is the average of the two middle values: (17 + 21) ÷ 2 = 19. (32 is the mean; don't confuse the two.)

**5. (C)** Count frequencies: 5 appears 3 times, 3 appears twice, 7 and 8 once each. The mode is 5.

**6. (D)** Range = largest − smallest = 90 − 33 = 57. (90 is just the maximum, a common mistake.)

**7. (A)** Total of five = mean × count = 20 × 5 = 100. Sum of the four known = 18 + 22 + 15 + 25 = 80. Fifth = 100 − 80 = 20.

**8. (C)** "Which size sells most often" asks for the most frequent value — the mode. Mean or median could land on a half-size nobody wears.

**9. (B)** The median is the middle of the *sorted* list, so you must sort first. You never need to add or divide for a median.

**10. (C)** Sort: 3, 4, 5, 7, 9, 11. Even count, two middle values 5 and 7, median = (5 + 7) ÷ 2 = 6.

**11. (A)** 1 cm = 10 runs, so 4.5 cm = 4.5 × 10 = 45 runs.

**12. (B)** 75 runs ÷ 10 runs-per-cm = 7.5 cm.

**13. (D)** Mean = (0 + 1 + 2 + 3 + 4) ÷ 5 = 10 ÷ 5 = 2. (10 is the total.)

**14. (A)** Sum = 12 + 15 + 18 + 11 + 14 = 70; mean = 70 ÷ 5 = 14.

**15. (C)** Frequencies: 4 appears 3 times (most), 7 twice, others once. Mode = 4.

**16. (A)** Both means are 30, so consistency is judged by spread. A's range = 32 − 28 = 4; B's range = 58 − 2 = 56. Smaller range = more consistent, so Player A. Equal means do NOT mean equal consistency.

**17. (B)** Frequencies: 30 appears 3 times — more than any other. Mode = 30.

**18. (D)** Sort: 30, 30, 30, 31, 32, 33, 35. Seven values, the 4th is the middle = 31. (The mode 30 is a tempting wrong pick.)

**19. (C)** Data 60, 60, 70, 80, 80: mean = 350 ÷ 5 = 70, median (middle of sorted) = 70 — both true, so C. There are two modes (60 and 80), so A is false; "mode is 70" is false (70 occurs once); range = 80 − 60 = 20, not 80.

**20. (A)** New total = old total + 45 = (4 × 35) + 45 = 140 + 45 = 185. New mean = 185 ÷ 5 = 37.

**21. (C)** Adding the same constant to every value raises the mean by that constant: 12 + 4 = 16. (Multiplying the count is a misread.)

**22. (B)** Total = 21 × 8 = 168. Remove 14: 168 − 14 = 154. New mean = 154 ÷ 7 = 22.

**23. (A)** Tallest bar = Banana (40 kg). Shortest = Mango (15 kg). Difference = 40 − 15 = 25 kg.

**24. (D)** Sort: 7, 9, 12, 20. Even count, median = (9 + 12) ÷ 2 = 10.5.

**25. (B)** For 5, 5, 5, 5: mean = 5, median = 5, mode = 5 — all equal. Check the others: 3, 4, 5, 7 has mean 4.75; 1, 5, 9 has mean 5 and median 5 but no mode; 4, 5, 6, 8 has mean 5.75 and no mode. Only the all-5 set has mean, median AND mode all equal to 5.

**26. (C)** The eye judges a bar's value by its height as if it grew from zero. With the axis truncated at 90, the visible part of the 96-bar is far longer than that of the 92-bar, so a 4-unit change looks huge. The real change is only about 4%.

**27. (C)** The mean uses every value, so the outlier 100 drags it far above the cluster around 4. The mode (4) and median (4) ignore the extreme. So the mean is most distorted.

**28. (A)** Sum = 8 + 6 + 7 + 9 + 6 + 10 + 5 = 51. Mean = 51 ÷ 7 = 7.285… ≈ 7.3.

**29. (A)** Total = 15 × 6 = 90. Known sum = 10 + 12 + 18 + 20 + 14 = 74. Sixth = 90 − 74 = 16.

**30. (D)** 1 symbol = 5 books, 6½ symbols = 6.5 × 5 = 32.5 books.

**31. (B)** Runs 30, 45, 45, 60, 20: 45 occurs twice, every other once. Mode = 45.

**32. (C)** Data 2, 2, 2, 5, 8, 100: mode = 2; median = (2 + 5) ÷ 2 = 3.5; mean = 119 ÷ 6 ≈ 19.8. So mode < median < mean — the outlier pulls the mean far right.

**33. (D)** Cricket gap = 18 − 12 = 6; Football gap = |10 − 22| = 12. Football's gap (12) is larger. (32 is the sum of the two football bars, a distractor.)

**34. (C)** A value was understated by 56 − 36 = 20, so the recorded total was 20 too low. Old total = 25 × 10 = 250; true total = 270; corrected mean = 270 ÷ 10 = 27.

**35. (B)** Combine totals: (30 × 140) + (20 × 150) = 4200 + 3000 = 7200; over 50 students = 144 cm. (Averaging 140 and 150 to 145 ignores the unequal group sizes — the trap.)

**36. (A)** Six sorted values; median = average of the 3rd and 4th = (x + 13) ÷ 2 = 11, so x + 13 = 22, x = 9.

**37. (D)** Each of 2, 3 and 5 appears exactly twice; no single value is most frequent, so there is no single mode.

**38. (B)** Mean = (80 + 120 + 100 + 60) ÷ 4 = 360 ÷ 4 = 90 mm.

**39. (A)** Data 5, 8, 8, 11, 13: mean = 45 ÷ 5 = 9; median (3rd value) = 8; mean − median = 9 − 8 = 1.

**40. (D)** First batter's range = 90 − 0 = 90; second batter's range = 67 − 18 = 49. Smaller range = more consistent, so the second batter (range 49). Option B attaches "range 49" to the wrong batter — a swap trap.

**41. (B)** Combined total = (9 × 30) + (3 × 42) = 270 + 126 = 396; over 12 = 33.

**42. (D)** Changing the 14 to 27 re-sorts to 10, 16, 16, 19, 21, 27. The mode stays 16 (still the only repeated value). The mean, range (was 11, now 17) and median (was 16, now (16+19)/2 = 17.5) all change. So the mode is unchanged.

**43. (B)** Ages 11, 12, 12, 13, 13, 13, 14, 16: 13 appears most → mode = 13. Eight values, median = average of 4th and 5th = (13 + 13) ÷ 2 = 13. So mode = 13, median = 13.

**44. (A)** Pets 0, 1, 0, 2, 1, 0, 3, 1, 0: 0 appears 4 times, 1 appears 3 times. Mode = 0. (Picking 1 because "1 pet" feels typical is the slip.)

**45. (C)** Five consecutive whole numbers with mean 12 are centred on 12: 10, 11, 12, 13, 14. The largest is 14. (For an odd run of consecutive numbers, the mean equals the middle value.)

**46. (B)** Mean daily saving = (20 + 0 + 40 + 30 + 10) ÷ 5 = 100 ÷ 5 = ₹20. The zero still counts as one of the 5 days — dividing by 4 is the trap.

**47. (D)** Sort: 7, 9, 9, 12, 14, 18, 21. Seven values, the 4th is the middle = 12.

**48. (A)** When the mean sits well above the median, a few large outliers are pulling the mean up while the median (the middle) stays near the cluster. Sorting doesn't change the mean, and equal modes/means aren't implied.

**49. (B)** Total = 8 × 5 = 40. Known sum = 6 + 9 + 7 + 11 = 33. Fifth = 40 − 33 = 7.

**50. (D)** X = 7 × 4 = 28 cars; Y = 5.5 × 4 = 22 cars. Difference = 28 − 22 = 6 cars. (Subtracting the symbols, 7 − 5.5 = 1.5, then forgetting to scale gives the C trap.)

**51. (A)** Data 3, 7, 7, 7, 9, 11, 12: mode = 7 (three times); median = 4th value = 7; mean = (3 + 7 + 7 + 7 + 9 + 11 + 12) ÷ 7 = 56 ÷ 7 = 8. So mode 7, median 7, mean 8 → "7, 7, 8", which is option A. (Option D, "7, 7, 7", wrongly takes the mean to be 7 too.)

**52. (B)** Equal means (60) but Section P's range is 8 versus Q's 40, so P's marks are bunched closely — more consistent / less spread. Larger range (Q) means more scatter, not tighter. Equal means never imply identical marks.

**53. (D)** Total = 13 × 5 = 65. Known sum = 6 + 10 + 14 + 20 = 50. x = 65 − 50 = 15.

**54. (C)** 1 unit = 2 students, 9 units = 9 × 2 = 18 students.

**55. (A)** Marks 10, 12, 12, 14, 15, 15, 15, 18, 19: mode = 15 (three times); nine values, median = 5th = 15. Mode − median = 15 − 15 = 0.

**56. (B)** Range = largest − smallest, so largest = smallest + range = 8 + 12 = 20. (The mean 14 is a distractor not needed here.)

**57. (C)** Total = (3 × 10) + (2 × 15) = 30 + 30 = 60; over 5 numbers = 60 ÷ 5 = 12. (Averaging 10 and 15 to 12.5 ignores the unequal counts — the trap.)

**58. (C)** Trees 30, 45, 60, 45: mode = 45 (appears twice). Sort 30, 45, 45, 60; median = (45 + 45) ÷ 2 = 45. Both the mode and the median equal 45.

**59. (B)** Mean 11 over 6 numbers → total = 66. Known sum = 5 + 9 + 11 + 11 + 14 = 50, so x = 66 − 50 = 16. The mode stays 11 (11 still appears twice; 16 once), consistent with the conditions.

**60. (D)** With the axis starting at 50, only the slivers above 50 are shown: 54 → 4 units, 52 → 2 units, so one bar looks twice the other even though the real values (54 vs 52) are almost equal. The honest reading: nearly the same sales; the truncated axis exaggerated a 2-unit gap.
