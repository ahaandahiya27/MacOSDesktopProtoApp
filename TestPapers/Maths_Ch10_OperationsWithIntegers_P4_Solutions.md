## Worked Solutions

**1. (A)** Add the signed steps: 9 + (−14) = −5; −5 + (−7) = −12; −12 + 5 = −7; −7 + (−1) = −8. Treating every step as a magnitude to add (9+14+7+5+1) gives 36; dropping a sign on the −1 leaves −7−1 mishandled as −12; flipping the final result's sign gives +8.

**2. (B)** Positives: 23 + 12 = 35. Negatives: 17 + 31 + 6 = 54. 35 − 54 = −19. Summing all magnitudes gives 89; adding only the three negatives gives −54 against +35 mis-paired as −45; mis-grouping +12 as a negative yields −7.

**3. (C)** Associativity fails for subtraction: take a=10, b=4, c=3 → (10−4)−3 = 3 but 10−(4−3) = 9, so the claim it 'always' holds is false. Closure holds (any integer minus an integer is an integer), non-commutativity is true, and a − b = a + (−b) is the defining identity.

**4. (B)** Left to right: (−120) ÷ (−5) = +24; +24 ÷ (−6) = −4; −4 ÷ 2 = −2. There are three negative signs among the divisions' inputs (the −5 and −6 give one positive, then dividing into −6 once more is already counted) — concretely the running quotient is +24, −4, −2; an even count of negatives would force +2.

**5. (B)** Multiply first: (−4)×(−5)=+20 and (−3)×(+2)=−6. Expression becomes (−6) + 20 − (−6) = −6 + 20 + 6 = 20. Adding left to right before multiplying gives wrong magnitudes; reading −(−6) as −6 gives 8; sign-slipping the +20 to −12 region comes from (−4)×(−5) taken as −20.

**6. (C)** An integer plus its opposite is 0 by the additive-inverse property: (−47) + 47 = 0. (−47)×0 = 0 but uses the zero-product (multiplicative) property, not additive inverse; 47 − (−47) = 94; (−47)+(−47) = −94.

**7. (C)** Three factors of −1 multiply to (−1)³ = −1, so a × (−1) = −a, the opposite of a. An even count of −1 factors would return a itself; the sign of −a depends on a (e.g. a=−5 gives +5), so it is not 'always negative'.

**8. (D)** Track signed changes from −11: −11 − 3 = −14; −14 + 8 = −6; −6 − 6 = −12. Reading the first 'fell 3' as a rise gives −10; adding every magnitude to −11 gives −28; ignoring the start and netting the changes (−3+8−6=−1) misplaces it.

**9. (A)** Magnitude: 2×3×1×4×5 = 120. There are five negative factors; an odd count makes the product negative, so −120. An even count would give +120; adding the magnitudes gives 15.

**10. (A)** Signed sum: 0 + 15 − 23 − 8 + 11 = −5. Adding all four magnitudes gives 57; taking only the descents (−23−8) against +15 mis-pairs to −16/−45; flipping the result sign gives +5.

**11. (D)** Negative ÷ negative is positive (e.g. (−12)÷(−4)=+3), so calling it negative is wrong. Negative × positive is negative; (−4)−(−9) = +5 is positive; an odd count of three negatives gives a negative product.

**12. (A)** Each 'left' subtracts, each 'right' adds: 4 − 6 − 9 + 7 − 8 + 3 = −9. Treating all moves as left (4−6−9−7−8−3) gives −29-type errors; mis-counting the rights as one more left lands at −15; sign flip gives +9.

**13. (A)** Inside brackets: (−5) + (−2) − (−4) = −5 − 2 + 4 = −3. Then (−3) × (−3) = +9; +9 + (−6) = +3. Reading −(−4) as −4 makes the bracket −11 and the answer −33-ish; forgetting to add the trailing −6 gives +9; sign error on the product gives −9.

**14. (D)** A product is negative exactly when an odd number of its factors are negative; this is independent of how many factors there are in total. So the count is odd (could be 1, 3, 5, or 7) — 'even' would force a positive product, and 'exactly seven' / 'exactly one' are only particular odd cases.

**15. (B)** Do ÷ and × first: (−84)÷(−7)=+12; (−5)×(−3)=+15; (−2)×(+4)=−8. Now 12 + 15 − (−8) = 12 + 15 + 8 = 35. Reading −(−8) as −8 gives 19; sign-slipping the +15 gives a smaller value; dropping a term gives 27.

**16. (C)** 'How much greater' is the difference (−13) − (−41) = −13 + 41 = 28. Subtracting the wrong way (−41) − (−13) gives −28; adding the magnitudes gives 54; both sign-and-order slips give −54.

**17. (C)** (−2)⁴ = +16 (four negatives, even); (−2)³ = −8 (odd); (−2)² = +4. So 16 + (−8) − (+4) = 16 − 8 − 4 = 4. Treating (−2)⁴ as −16 or (−2)³ as +8 flips terms; mishandling the final −(+4) as +4 gives 28.

**18. (A)** Do ÷ and × left to right: 48 ÷ (−6) = −8; −8 × 2 = −16; the term is −(−16) = +16. So 30 + 16 + (−5) = 30 + 16 − 5 = 41. Subtracting 16 instead of adding it (sign slip on ÷ by −6) gives 9; doing the subtraction before ×/÷ corrupts the rest.

**19. (A)** a + 0 = a (additive identity) and a × 1 = a (multiplicative identity) hold for every integer. Multiplying by 0 gives 0, not the integer back; 0 IS the additive identity, so the last claim is false; the first option swaps the two identities.

**20. (A)** Magnitude: 1×2×3×4×5×6 = 720. Six negative factors — an even count — give a positive product, so +720. An odd-count assumption gives −720; adding the magnitudes gives 21.

**21. (B)** Total rise = (−60) − (−540) = −60 + 540 = +480 m over 6 steps, so 480 ÷ 6 = +80 m each (a rise is positive). Dividing 540 ÷ 6 = 90 ignores that it stops at −60; dividing 600 ÷ 6 over-counts; a rise cannot be negative, ruling out −80.

**22. (B)** Two factors of −1 multiply to +1, leaving n × n = n². So the value is n², which is ≥ 0 for every integer (e.g. n=−3 gives 9). It is never −n²; 'always negative' is false since n² ≥ 0; 2n confuses multiplication with addition.

**23. (D)** Seven copies of −12: 7 × (−12) = −84. Counting six copies gives −72; counting eight gives −96; dropping the overall negative sign gives +84.

**24. (B)** Brackets first: (−4) + (−1) = −5. Then (−90) ÷ (−5) = +18; +18 − (−6) = +18 + 6 = +24. Stopping at +18 forgets the −(−6); reading −(−6) as −6 gives +12; a sign error on the division gives −24.

**25. (D)** Multiplying a single factor across a sum, a × (b + c) = a×b + a×c, is exactly the distributive property. Associativity regroups three multiplied factors; commutativity of addition only reorders the summands; the additive-inverse property is about a + (−a) = 0.

**26. (A)** Left to right for ×/÷: (−144)÷6 = −24; −24 × (−1) = +24; +24 ÷ (−3) = −8; then −8 + (−4) = −12. Dropping the final +(−4) gives −8 (then mis-rounded); a sign slip on the ÷(−3) gives +12; mishandling two negatives gives −20.

**27. (C)** Correct: 12 × 5 = 60. Wrong: 5 × (−3) = −15. Unattempted: 3 × (−1) = −3. Total: 60 − 15 − 3 = 42. Ignoring the unattempted penalty gives 45; ignoring both penalties gives 60; only counting the wrong penalty gives 45/48 variants.

**28. (D)** (−7)×x + 11 = −31 ⇒ (−7)x = −42 ⇒ x = (−42) ÷ (−7) = 6. Forgetting to subtract 11 first gives (−7)x = −31; a sign error in the final division (negative ÷ negative) gives −6; using +42 ÷ (−7) gives −6 as well.

**29. (C)** Inside brackets: (−8) − (−3) + (−2) = −8 + 3 − 2 = −7. Then (−5) − (−7) = −5 + 7 = +2. Reading the outer −(−7) as −7 gives −12; sign-slipping the bracket to +7 gives −12 the other way; mis-adding inside gives −2.

**30. (D)** First bracket: (−6) − (−10) = −6 + 10 = +4. Second bracket: (−3) − (+5) = −8. Product: (+4) × (−8) = −32. Reading the first bracket as −16 (sign error) or the product sign as positive gives +32; mis-multiplying gives ±16.

**31. (C)** An integer plus its opposite is 0, so we compute (−29) − 0 = −29. Confusing the zero result with the final answer gives 0; flipping the sign gives +29; doubling −29 gives −58.

**32. (D)** Evaluate each: (−3)×(−2)=+6; (−15)÷3=−5; (−4)+(−4)=−8; 6−9=−3. Ascending order of values −8, −5, −3, +6 corresponds to (−4)+(−4), (−15)÷3, 6−9, (−3)×(−2). Swapping −8 and −5 (treating −5 as smaller than −8) gives the first distractor; reversing gives the third.

**33. (B)** Multiply first: (−4)×(−4)=+16 and 4×(−4)=−16. Expression: 16 − (−4) − (−16) = 16 + 4 + 16 = 36. Reading −(−16) as −16 gives 4; reading −(−4) as −4 gives 28; combining both sign slips gives −4.

**34. (A)** p×q×r is a product of three negatives, hence negative. p + q + r is a sum of three negatives, hence negative. Negative × negative = positive, so the whole expression is positive for all such p, q, r. The result is fully determined.

**35. (C)** Brackets: 14 − 30 = −16; (−16) × (−3) = +48. Division: (−56) ÷ (−7) = +8. Sum: 48 + 8 = 56. A sign error on the product gives −48 → −40; mishandling the negative ÷ negative gives 40; both slips give −56.

**36. (D)** There are five negative factors; an odd number of negatives makes the product negative. (No factor is 0, so it is non-zero.) The actual value is −3840, but the question only needs the sign, which is negative; an even-count assumption gives 'positive'.

**37. (D)** (−96) ÷ x = +8 ⇒ x = (−96) ÷ (+8) = −12 (negative ÷ positive = negative, and −12 × 8 = −96 ✓). Choosing +12 gives (−96)÷12 = −8, the wrong sign; subtracting instead of dividing gives ±88.

**38. (B)** 98 = 100 + (−2), so (−6)×98 = (−6)×100 + (−6)×(−2) = −600 + 12 = −588. Writing 98 as 100 − 2 needs (−6)×100 − (−6)×2 = −600 + 12 = −588 too, but the distractors mis-state the arithmetic as −612; using (−6)×(−2)=−12 is the classic two-negatives sign error.

**39. (A)** x × (−7) = +56 ⇒ x = (+56) ÷ (−7) = −8 (and (−8)×(−7) = +56 ✓). The product is positive with one factor negative, so the other must also be negative; +8 would give (+8)×(−7) = −56; multiplying instead of dividing gives ±392.

**43. (A)** Adding, subtracting, or multiplying two integers always yields an integer, so closure holds for those three. Division can leave the integers (e.g. (−7) ÷ 2 is not an integer), so they are NOT closed under division. Subtraction IS closed, ruling out the third option.
