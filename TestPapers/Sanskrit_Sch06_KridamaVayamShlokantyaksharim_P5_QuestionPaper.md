1. A player closes a verse on the word धैर्यम्. To answer legally the next opener must echo the actual final akṣara of धैर्यम्, which is:
   (A) धै — the first syllable, because the opener always copies how the closing word begins
   (B) म् — the bare final consonant alone, since word-final vowels are always dropped
   (C) धैर्य — the whole stem before the case-ending, since the ending म् is discarded
   (D) र्यम् (rya-m) — the syllable य bearing र् as a pre-vocalic conjunct plus म्, i.e. the next verse must open on the यर्/र्य-coloured sound, since म् is a bare nasal closing the last full syllable र्य

2. A verse ends on the word गुरुः. By the game's syllable-rule, which proposed opener is LEGAL, and why?
   (A) रुचिरम्... — गुरुः ends in the full syllable रु (the visarga ः being only the nominative ending after it), so the opener must carry रु, which रुचिरम् supplies
   (B) गगनम्... — because the opener echoes the first syllable गु of गुरुः, here softened to ग
   (C) विसर्गः... — because the very last mark heard is the visarga ः, and a word beginning विसर्ग- formally names it
   (D) रुग्णः... is illegal while गृहम्... is legal, because only ग-initial words may answer a गुरुः-ending verse

3. Player P ends on the syllable तू (त् + long ऊ). Opponent offers a verse beginning तुरगः.... Is this legal?
   (A) Legal — the consonant त matches, and the game never counts vowel length
   (B) Legal — a long vowel may always be answered by its short counterpart, since they share one varṇa
   (C) Illegal — तुरगः opens on तु (त् bearing SHORT उ), but the carried syllable तू holds a LONG ऊ; vowel-length is part of the akṣara and must be reproduced
   (D) Illegal — but only because तुरगः is the name of a horse-word and animal-words are barred

4. In a four-player round the carried syllable becomes the rare cluster स्त्री (a single akṣara: स्+त्+र्+ई). Why is this the HARDEST kind of hand-over to answer?
   (A) Because cluster-syllables are forbidden as endings, so the player who produced स्त्री automatically forfeits
   (B) Because a verse must open on that exact triple-conjunct-plus-ई syllable, and very few known verses begin with so dense a cluster, so the supply of legal openers is tiny
   (C) Because the next player may now open with any vowel, the cluster being unplayable and therefore waived
   (D) Because the next player need only match the final vowel ई, making it in fact one of the easiest carries

5. Two children dispute the carry after a verse whose written close is ...विद्यां (anusvāra) but whose recited close sounds ...विद्या followed by a nasal hum. For the next opener, the game keys on:
   (A) the anusvāra ं alone, treated as a detachable final 'letter' to be matched by a ं-initial word
   (B) the syllable as actually recited — the open syllable द्या (with its long आ) that the next verse must begin on, since antyakshari is an oral, sound-keyed game
   (C) the pre-recitation written form letter-by-letter, since spelling, not sound, is the authority
   (D) the first syllable वि, because anusvāra endings reset the carry to the word's opening

7. P1 ends on मा; P2 must open on मा and chooses to end again on मा; P3 then ends on मा too. What does the rule imply about such a chain?
   (A) After two repeats the chain auto-resets and P3 may choose any syllable freely

9. The compound निष्फल (from निः + फल) and the chapter's निर्गन्ध (from निः + गन्ध) both begin with निः, yet the visarga surfaces differently. The reason is:
   (A) the trigger is the FOLLOWING sound — before the voiceless फ the visarga hardens to ष् (निष्फल), while before the voiced ग it becomes र् (निर्गन्ध); same निः, different following voicing
   (B) the difference is random; निः may be written either way with no rule governing the choice
   (C) निष्फल uses र् and निर्गन्ध uses ष्, the chapter simply printing them in reverse
   (D) both should be र्, so निष्फल is a misprint that ought to read निर्फल

10. At the seam श्लोक + अन्त्य → श्लोकान्त्य and at the seam महा + ईश → महेश, the two vowel sandhis are:
   (A) the same rule, since both simply join two vowels into one long vowel आ
   (B) both visarga sandhis, since each crosses a word-seam where a visarga is implied
   (C) both guṇa sandhis, since every two-vowel junction in Sanskrit produces ए
   (D) different — श्लोक+अन्त्य fuses two LIKE vowels (अ+अ→आ, savarṇa-dīrgha), whereas महा+ईश fuses two UNLIKE vowels (आ+ई→ए, guṇa), so one lengthens and the other forms a new guṇa vowel

11. A student writes the title-seam as श्लोकःअन्त्याक्षरी, claiming a hidden visarga is lost. The correct reply is:
   (A) correct — श्लोकः is the true stem and its visarga is deleted, leaving श्लोकान्त्य by visarga sandhi
   (B) correct — the र् in many sandhis comes from exactly such a hidden visarga, including this one
   (C) there is no visarga at the seam — श्लोक ends in the vowel अ, so श्लोक+अन्त्य is a VOWEL sandhi (अ+अ→आ), and inventing a visarga misclassifies the junction
   (D) partly correct — the seam is half visarga and half vowel sandhi acting together

12. Both साधोः + विपरीतम् → साधोर्विपरीतम् and पुनः + अपि → पुनरपि show a visarga becoming र्. The CONDITION shared by both is that the visarga stands before:
