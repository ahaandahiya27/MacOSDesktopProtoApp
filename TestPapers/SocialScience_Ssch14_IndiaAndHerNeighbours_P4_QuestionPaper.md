# Olympiad Test Paper — Social Science (Class 7) — Paper 4
## Chapter 14: India and Her Neighbours

**Paper 4 — (progressively harder)**

| | |
|---|---|

| **Chapter** | 14 — India and Her Neighbours |
| **Total Questions** | 60 (Multiple Choice, single correct) |
| **Maximum Marks** | 240 |
| **Suggested Time** | 90 minutes |

**Marking scheme:** **+4** for each correct answer · **−1** for each wrong answer · **0** if unattempted.

**Instructions:** Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. Classic traps: a neighbour need NOT share a land border (Sri Lanka and the Maldives are maritime neighbours), an 'open border' like India-Nepal still IS a recognised border (only crossing is visa-free), Indian culture reached Thailand, Indonesia and Cambodia PEACEFULLY through trade, travel and religion rather than conquest, landlocked Nepal and Bhutan still depend on the sea via Indian ports, and direction/side mix-ups (Pakistan and Afghanistan lie west/north-west, Bangladesh and Myanmar east, Sri Lanka and the Maldives south). Do not write on this paper — mark answers on your answer sheet.

---

1. A teacher draws two columns, 'land neighbours' and 'maritime neighbours', and asks the class which single rule decides where each of India's neighbours belongs. The rule that correctly sorts them all is:
   (A) whether the country is larger or smaller than India in area
   (B) whether the country touches India by a land boundary (land neighbour) or is reached across the sea without any shared land border (maritime neighbour)
   (C) whether the country lies to the north or to the south of India
   (D) whether the country uses the same language and currency as India

2. Four countries are listed: Pakistan, Bhutan, Sri Lanka and Bangladesh. Three of them share a LAND border with India and one does not. The odd one out is:
   (A) Pakistan, which is reached only across the sea
   (B) Sri Lanka, which is separated from India by the sea
   (C) Bhutan, which is a maritime island neighbour
   (D) Bangladesh, which has no border with India at all

3. A student says, 'A maritime neighbour is less of a real neighbour than a land one because only land can join two countries.' The best correction is that:
   (A) land neighbours are indeed the only true neighbours and the sea merely separates lands
   (B) maritime neighbours matter only to countries that have no land neighbours at all
   (C) a sea can join countries only if a bridge has first been built across it
   (D) a shared sea has long carried trade, travel and culture between coasts, so a maritime link is just as genuine a neighbourly bond as a land boundary

4. The single best reason India can have maritime neighbours at all is that:
   (A) India is landlocked and ringed entirely by high mountains
   (B) India shares a land border with every island in the region
   (C) the Indian Ocean has no other countries touching its shores
   (D) India has a long coastline and sits centrally in the Indian Ocean, so other coastal lands lie across the water from it

5. Which pairing of a neighbour with the correct TYPE of link is right?
   (A) Nepal — maritime neighbour; Sri Lanka — land neighbour
   (B) China — maritime neighbour; Myanmar — island neighbour
   (C) Bangladesh — land neighbour to the east; the Maldives — maritime neighbour to the south
   (D) Bhutan — coastal neighbour; Pakistan — landlocked neighbour

6. A list claims to give 'only maritime neighbours of India'. Which list is actually correct?
   (A) Nepal and Bhutan
   (B) Pakistan and Afghanistan
   (C) Myanmar and Bangladesh
   (D) Sri Lanka and the Maldives

7. A child argues, 'The Maldives is too small and too far out in the sea to count as India's neighbour.' The strongest reply is that:
   (A) the Maldives must first build a bridge to India before it can count
   (B) only the largest country in a region is ever counted as a neighbour
   (C) distance and size do not decide neighbourliness; the Maldives shares the Indian Ocean with India and is a close maritime neighbour
   (D) the Maldives is in fact one of India's land neighbours to the north

8. Both Sri Lanka and the Maldives are maritime neighbours, yet they differ in form. The most accurate comparison is that:
   (A) Sri Lanka is an archipelago of many islets while the Maldives is one large island
   (B) both are landlocked Himalayan kingdoms with no coastline
   (C) the Maldives is an archipelago of many small islands while Sri Lanka is a single large island
   (D) both share a long land border with India to the south

9. If you sailed in a straight line due SOUTH from India's southern tip, the FIRST neighbouring country you would reach is:
   (A) China, which lies far to the north across the Himalayas
   (B) Sri Lanka, lying just across a narrow strait to the south
   (C) Afghanistan, which lies to the north-west by land
   (D) Bhutan, the landlocked Himalayan kingdom to the north

10. A correct list of India's neighbours that lie to the WEST and NORTH-WEST would be:
   (A) Sri Lanka and the Maldives
   (B) Pakistan and Afghanistan
   (C) Myanmar and Bangladesh
   (D) Bhutan and Nepal

11. Which neighbour lies to India's EAST, sharing a long land border and many transboundary rivers?
   (A) Bangladesh
   (B) Afghanistan, lying to the north-west
   (C) the Maldives, lying to the south-west across the sea
   (D) Pakistan, lying to the west

12. Reading the map in words: China lies to India's ____, while Myanmar lies to India's ____.
   (A) south; west
   (B) west; north-west
   (C) east; south
   (D) north; east

13. A neighbour reached neither by a shared land border nor purely across open sea, but historically by an OVERLAND route through the mountains to the north-west, is:
   (A) the Maldives, reached only across the Indian Ocean
   (B) Sri Lanka, reached only by sailing south
   (C) Indonesia, reached far across the eastern seas
   (D) Afghanistan, linked to India by the ancient northern overland route

14. Going clockwise around India starting from the north-west, the correct order of these neighbours by direction is:
   (A) China (north-west), Afghanistan (north), Sri Lanka (east), Myanmar (south)
   (B) Myanmar (north-west), Sri Lanka (north), China (east), Afghanistan (south)
   (C) Afghanistan (north-west), China (north), Myanmar (east), Sri Lanka (south)
   (D) Sri Lanka (north-west), Myanmar (north), Afghanistan (east), China (south)

15. Two neighbours, Afghanistan and the Maldives, have connected with India in different ways through history. The key difference is that:
   (A) Afghanistan linked to India by overland routes through the north-west, while the Maldives links across the sea to the south
   (B) Afghanistan links across the sea while the Maldives links overland
   (C) both linked only through the northern Himalayas as a pair
   (D) both linked only across the southern Indian Ocean

16. The India-Nepal border is famously an 'open border'. The most accurate meaning is that:
   (A) there is no boundary line between the two countries at all
   (B) Nepal is simply a part of India with no separate government
   (C) the border is sealed with a high wall that cannot be crossed
   (D) the boundary exists and is recognised, but people may cross it freely without a visa or passport

17. Aria says, 'An open border means Nepal and India are really one country.' The best correction is that:
   (A) she is right, because openness legally merges the two states
   (B) she is right, because borders only exist where walls stand
   (C) Nepal and India in fact share a maritime border, not a land one
   (D) an open border still separates two sovereign states; only the crossing rules are relaxed, so the two remain different countries

18. Compared with the open India-Nepal border, the India-China border in the Himalayas is best described as:
   (A) also a land border, but one that is not visa-free and is shaped by high mountains
   (B) an open, visa-free border exactly like the India-Nepal one
   (C) a maritime border running across the Indian Ocean
   (D) not a border at all, since the mountains separate the two countries

19. That the Himalayas form much of the boundary between India and China best illustrates the idea that:
   (A) natural features of the land, like a great mountain range, can mark the border between countries
   (B) every border in the world is drawn along a coastline
   (C) mountains make any contact between neighbours impossible forever
   (D) the position of borders has nothing to do with geography

20. Pakistan and China are compared as India's neighbours. The truth shared by BOTH is that they:
   (A) are maritime neighbours reached only across the sea
   (B) share a land border with India
   (C) are landlocked countries with no coastline of their own
   (D) share an open, visa-free border with India like Nepal's

21. The phrase 'a shared heritage but a divided history', used for India and Pakistan, means the two countries:
   (A) grew from common cultural roots yet were later separated into distinct nations
   (B) have absolutely nothing in common in culture or past
   (C) are today a single country under one government
   (D) were never connected to each other at any point in history

22. A claim says, 'Nepal is landlocked, so it has no use for the sea.' The best refutation is that:
   (A) Nepal has a long ocean coastline of its own to use
   (B) landlocked countries never trade with anyone abroad
   (C) even landlocked Nepal needs the sea for overseas trade, and its goods usually reach the ocean through Indian ports
   (D) Nepal reaches the sea only through Chinese ports across the high Himalayas

23. Nepal and Bhutan share the same key geographic feature shaping their economies. That feature is that they are:
   (A) island nations scattered across the Indian Ocean
   (B) landlocked Himalayan countries with no coastline, depending on India for access to the sea
   (C) long-coastline maritime neighbours of India
   (D) desert countries with no mountains at all

24. The single best reason India's ports matter so much to Nepal and Bhutan is that:
   (A) India's ports give Nepal and Bhutan beaches and coastlines of their own
   (B) a coastline is useless to any country that does not legally own it
   (C) the ports let the two countries turn themselves into island nations
   (D) as landlocked countries they have no coast, so their overseas goods pass through Indian ports, making India their gateway to the sea

25. Bhutan generates much of its electricity from fast-flowing Himalayan rivers chiefly because Bhutan:
   (A) has a flat coastline ideal for tidal power
   (B) imports all of its electricity from distant oceans
   (C) burns sea water to produce its power
   (D) is a mountainous country where water falling from a height can turn turbines (hydroelectricity)

26. Bhutan measures progress through 'Gross National Happiness' rather than money alone. Compared with a country judging success only by wealth, Bhutan's approach gives extra weight to:
   (A) the wellbeing of people, care for the environment, good governance and culture
   (B) the size of its army and its number of weapons
   (C) the total number of islands it controls
   (D) how many tall city skyscrapers it can build

27. A landlocked neighbour and a maritime neighbour differ most clearly in that:
   (A) the landlocked one has the longer coastline of the two
   (B) the maritime one can never trade by sea
   (C) the landlocked one has no coast and must borrow a sea route, while the maritime one is itself reached across the sea
   (D) both have exactly the same access to the ocean

28. Among India's neighbours, the one whose territory lies largely 'in the lap of the Himalayas' and has no coastline is:
   (A) Sri Lanka, a southern island with a long coast
   (B) the Maldives, a chain of low islands in the sea
   (C) Bangladesh, a low-lying delta beside the sea
   (D) Nepal

29. A 'transboundary river' shared by India and Bangladesh creates a need for the two countries to:
   (A) build dams to stop the river crossing the border entirely
   (B) pretend the river does not flow between them at all
   (C) cooperate on how the shared waters are used and managed
   (D) stop speaking to each other about the river forever

30. The Ganga and the Brahmaputra both flow from India into Bangladesh before reaching the sea. The best lesson this teaches about neighbours is that:
   (A) rivers stop flowing the moment they reach a border
   (B) each country can use a shared river without affecting the other
   (C) rivers ignore political borders, so upstream and downstream neighbours are tied together and must manage water jointly
   (D) borders completely separate the two countries' water supplies

31. Which statement about the Himalayas as a shared geographic feature is most accurate?
   (A) the Himalayas are shared by several neighbours and feed great rivers that flow into India and beyond
   (B) the Himalayas lie wholly within India and touch no neighbour
   (C) the Himalayas are a desert range that blocks all rivers
   (D) the Himalayas are a maritime feature out in the Indian Ocean

32. That India shares both the Himalayas and major rivers with several neighbours best shows that the neighbourhood is bound together by:
   (A) a single shared government ruling over all the countries
   (B) a common physical geography that crosses political borders
   (C) one common currency used by every neighbour
   (D) borders that keep each country's geography fully separate

33. Why does shared geography often push neighbours toward cooperation rather than conflict?
   (A) shared geography lets one country fully control everyone's resources
   (B) features like rivers and mountains span borders, so managing them well requires neighbours to work together
   (C) shared rivers and ranges belong to no one, so no one need discuss them
   (D) geography is purely decorative and has no practical effect on neighbours

34. A river beginning in the Himalayas, flowing through India, and emptying into the sea only after passing into Bangladesh best illustrates the concept of a:
   (A) transboundary river crossing the borders of more than one country
   (B) landlocked lake with no outlet to the sea
   (C) strait separating two large landmasses
   (D) river that flows entirely within one country

35. The shared use of Himalayan-fed rivers by India and its northern and eastern neighbours best supports the conclusion that:
   (A) each country's rivers are entirely its own and affect no one else
   (B) neighbours' fortunes are linked by the same waters, making cooperation over rivers a regional necessity
   (C) the rivers vanish at every international border
   (D) water is too unimportant for neighbours to ever discuss

36. Thai kings have taken the name 'Rama' and an old Thai capital was called 'Ayutthaya', echoing Ayodhya. The best explanation is that:
   (A) Indian culture and stories like the Ramayana spread peacefully to Thailand through trade, travel and religion
   (B) India conquered Thailand and forced these names upon it

   (D) the resemblance is pure coincidence with no real connection

37. A claim states, 'India became influential in Indonesia and Cambodia because it conquered them with armies.' The best correction is that:
   (A) yes, large Indian armies marched in and ruled these lands
   (B) the influence appeared only in the last few decades
   (C) those lands show no traces of Indian culture at all
   (D) Indian culture spread there mainly through trade, travel and religion, not by military conquest
