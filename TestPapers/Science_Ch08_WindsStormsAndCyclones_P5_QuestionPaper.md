**Instructions:** Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. This is the hardest tier: every item chains air pressure to temperature to density to wind across several steps, and the distractors are engineered from the deepest misconceptions — treating warm air as heavier or as lifted by a special force, reversing Bernoulli so fast air seems to push outward, reading the can-crush as metal folding itself rather than an inside-versus-outside pressure imbalance, flipping wind to go from low toward high pressure, swapping the heating rates of land and sea, calling the calm eye the deadliest zone or a high-pressure core, ranking a tornado above a cyclone in size, mistaking the shorter light-then-thunder gap for a retreating storm, and reducing the storm surge to ordinary rainwater. 1. A student weighs a rigid sealed glass globe of air on a sensitive balance, then carefully heats only the trapped air inside (the glass does not melt or leak).    (A) The reading falls, because heating genuinely destroys some of the air's weight
   (B) The reading is unchanged, because no air entered or left the sealed globe; warm air is only 'lighter' in the sense of lower density (same amount of air spread over more space), which matters for floating, not for the total weight on a balance
   (C) The reading rises, because warming adds energy that counts as extra weight on the balance
   (D) The reading falls, because warm air pushes up on the balance pan from inside the sealed globe
