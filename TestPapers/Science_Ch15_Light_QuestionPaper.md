# Olympiad Test Paper — Science (Class 7)
## Chapter 15: Light

| | |
|---|---|
| **Subject** | Science (NCERT Class 7) |
| **Chapter** | 15 — Light |
| **Total Questions** | 60 (Multiple Choice, single correct) |
| **Maximum Marks** | 240 |
| **Suggested Time** | 90 minutes |

**Marking scheme:** **+4** for each correct answer · **−1** for each wrong answer · **0** if unattempted.

**Instructions:** Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. All questions stay within the Class 7 chapter on Light: straight-line travel, reflection, plane and spherical mirrors, lenses, and the dispersion of white light. No calculator is needed. Most questions need two to four reasoning steps; watch for traps — for example, a convex mirror never magnifies, and a virtual image can never be caught on a screen. Do not write on this paper — mark answers on your answer sheet.

---

1. A torch is switched on in a dusty room and a sharp straight beam is seen. This is the best everyday evidence that light:
   (A) bends around corners
   (B) travels in a straight line
   (C) is made of seven colours
   (D) always slows down in air

2. A small bright lamp is placed in front of a large cardboard sheet that has a tiny hole. The patch of light on a wall behind the hole, and the sharp-edged shadow of the cardboard, both happen because light:
   (A) spreads equally in all directions through solids
   (B) is reflected by the cardboard
   (C) is refracted by the hole
   (D) travels in straight lines

3. An opaque ball is held between a single small lamp and a screen. As the ball is moved **closer to the lamp** (away from the screen), its shadow on the screen will:
   (A) become smaller
   (B) disappear completely
   (C) become larger
   (D) change colour

4. A shadow is always:
   (A) dark and shows only the outline of the object
   (B) coloured like the object
   (C) larger than the object
   (D) the same shape and colour as the object

5. Three opaque cards have holes punched in them. Light from a lamp reaches your eye only when the three holes are:
   (A) of exactly equal size
   (B) placed in a curve
   (C) coloured the same
   (D) arranged in a straight line with your eye and the lamp

6. Light falls on a smooth polished mirror and on a sheet of white paper. Compared with the mirror, the paper:
   (A) absorbs all the light and reflects none
   (B) reflects a sharp clear image like the mirror
   (C) reflects light in many scattered directions (diffuse reflection)
   (D) lets all the light pass straight through

7. You can see a clear image of yourself in a still pond but not in a pond rippled by wind. The reason is that ripples cause:
   (A) refraction of the image
   (B) regular reflection
   (C) diffuse (irregular) reflection
   (D) total absorption of light

8. A ray of light strikes a plane mirror and the angle between the incoming ray and the mirror surface is 30°. The angle of reflection (measured from the normal) is:
   (A) 30°
   (B) 60°
   (C) 90°
   (D) 15°

9. Which statement about the reflection of light from any surface is **always** true?
   (A) Only shiny metals can reflect light.
   (B) Reflection only happens with white light.
   (C) Light is destroyed when it is reflected.
   (D) The angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection.

10. We are able to read the words printed on this paper because the paper produces:
    (A) regular reflection that forms an image
    (B) diffuse reflection that scatters light to our eyes from every part
    (C) its own light
    (D) refraction of the room light

11. The image of your face in a flat bathroom mirror is:
    (A) real and inverted
    (B) real and magnified
    (C) virtual, erect and the same size as you
    (D) virtual and diminished

12. Which one of these can a plane-mirror image **never** be?
    (A) caught on a screen
    (B) erect
    (C) the same size as the object
    (D) laterally inverted

13. You stand 1.5 m in front of a plane mirror. The distance between you and your image is:
    (A) 1.5 m
    (B) 3 m
    (C) 0.75 m
    (D) 0 m

14. A plane mirror forms an image that is the same size as the object. If you walk **towards** the mirror, your image:
    (A) grows larger than you
    (B) shrinks
    (C) turns upside down
    (D) stays the same size as you

15. A boy stands 2 m from a plane mirror and then steps back to 5 m from it. By how much does his image move away from the mirror?
    (A) 3 m
    (B) 6 m
    (C) 1.5 m
    (D) 10 m

16. In a plane-mirror image the part that appears swapped (changed) is:
    (A) top and bottom
    (B) near and far
    (C) left and right
    (D) nothing is swapped

17. Held up to a plane mirror, the printed word **AMBULANCE** would look correct. This is why the word is written reversed on the front of an ambulance, so that:
    (A) a driver ahead reads it correctly in the rear-view mirror
    (B) it looks artistic
    (C) it reflects more light
    (D) it can be read in the dark

18. The capital letters H, I, O, X look unchanged in a plane mirror, but the letter R does not. The reason H, I, O, X are unchanged is that:
    (A) they contain only curves
    (B) they are the brightest letters
    (C) they are written in red
    (D) they are symmetric about a vertical line down the middle

19. A wall clock shows the time 3:00. Seen in a plane mirror, the reflected clock appears to read:
    (A) 3:00
    (B) 6:00
    (C) 9:00
    (D) 12:00

20. The number **1881** is held up to a plane mirror. Its reflection reads:
    (A) 8118
    (B) 1881
    (C) 1188
    (D) 8811

21. An object is placed 4 cm in front of a plane mirror. The total distance from the object to its image is:
    (A) 4 cm
    (B) 8 cm
    (C) 2 cm
    (D) 16 cm

22. A candle is placed 10 cm in front of a plane mirror. Where would you have to put a screen to catch the candle's image?
    (A) 10 cm behind the mirror
    (B) at the surface of the mirror
    (C) nowhere — the image cannot be caught on a screen
    (D) 10 cm in front of the mirror

23. A man walks towards a plane mirror at 1 m/s. His image approaches the mirror at:
    (A) 1 m/s
    (B) 0.5 m/s
    (C) 2 m/s
    (D) it stays still

24. A reflecting surface curved **inwards** (like the inside of a spoon's bowl) is a:
    (A) plane mirror
    (B) convex mirror
    (C) convex lens
    (D) concave mirror

25. A concave mirror is sometimes called a **converging** mirror because it:
    (A) spreads parallel rays apart
    (B) brings parallel rays together to a point
    (C) lets light pass through it
    (D) splits white light into colours

26. Which mirror can form a **real, inverted** image of a distant object?
    (A) a plane mirror
    (B) a convex mirror
    (C) a concave mirror
    (D) no mirror can

27. When you hold a concave (shaving) mirror **very close** to your face, the image you see is:
    (A) real and inverted
    (B) diminished
    (C) exactly the same size
    (D) virtual, erect and magnified

28. A dentist uses a concave mirror mainly because, held close to a tooth, it gives:
    (A) an enlarged, erect image of the tooth
    (B) a wider view of the whole mouth
    (C) a smaller image so more fits in
    (D) a coloured image

29. Why is a concave mirror, and not a plane or convex one, placed behind the bulb in a torch or car headlight?
    (A) it makes the bulb look bigger
    (B) it spreads the light in all directions
    (C) it changes the colour of the light
    (D) it gathers the light and sends out a strong parallel beam

30. A student claims a concave mirror "always makes things look bigger." This is:
    (A) wrong — a distant object gives a small, inverted image
    (B) correct for every position of the object
    (C) correct only for red objects
    (D) wrong — a concave mirror always shrinks things

31. A reflecting surface curved **outwards** (bulging towards you, like the back of a spoon) is a:
    (A) concave mirror
    (B) plane mirror
    (C) convex mirror
    (D) concave lens

32. Whatever the position of the object, the image formed by a convex mirror is always:
    (A) real and inverted
    (B) virtual, erect and diminished
    (C) virtual, erect and magnified
    (D) real and the same size

33. A convex mirror is preferred as a vehicle's rear-view mirror chiefly because it:
    (A) gives a wider field of view
    (B) magnifies cars behind so they look close
    (C) forms a real image on the road
    (D) shows the true distance exactly

34. A salesperson says a security mirror in a shop "magnifies thieves so they look bigger." For a convex security mirror this claim is:
    (A) correct
    (B) correct only in bright light
    (C) wrong — a convex mirror always gives a diminished image
    (D) wrong — it inverts the image instead

35. In a convex mirror, a car that is actually quite close behind looks small and far away. This happens because the convex mirror always forms a:
    (A) magnified image
    (B) real image
    (C) laterally upright image
    (D) diminished image

36. Which single property is shared by a plane mirror **and** a convex mirror but **not** by a concave mirror used for a distant object?
    (A) they magnify the object
    (B) they form a virtual, erect image
    (C) they form a real image
    (D) they invert the image

37. Match the use to the correct mirror: a large mirror used to focus sunlight in a **solar concentrator** is:
    (A) plane
    (B) a concave lens
    (C) convex
    (D) concave

38. At a blind T-junction a large mirror is fixed high on a wall so drivers can see a **wide stretch** of the crossing road. The right mirror for this is:
    (A) concave, because it magnifies
    (B) convex, because it gives a wide field of view
    (C) plane, because it shows the true size
    (D) concave, because it gives a real image

39. Four mirrors are described: (i) gives a wide view, (ii) used in a torch reflector, (iii) gives a same-size image, (iv) can form a real image. The pair that are **both concave** is:
    (A) (i) and (iii)
    (B) (ii) and (iv)
    (C) (i) and (ii)
    (D) (iii) and (iv)

40. A shopkeeper needs a mirror that lets a small camera watch the **whole** shop floor. The best choice is a:
    (A) convex mirror
    (B) concave mirror
    (C) plane mirror
    (D) convex lens

41. A lens that is **thicker in the middle** than at the edges is a:
    (A) convex lens
    (B) concave lens

    (D) convex mirror

42. A magnifying glass used to read tiny print is a:
    (A) concave lens
    (B) concave mirror
    (C) plane mirror

43. A convex lens is called a **converging** lens because rays passing through it are:
    (A) spread apart
    (B) brought together towards a point
    (C) reflected back
    (D) split into colours

44. Which of these can be obtained on a screen (i.e., is a **real** image)?
    (A) the image in a plane mirror
    (B) the image in a convex mirror
    (C) the image of a distant tree formed by a convex lens
    (D) the magnified image seen through a magnifying glass held close to print

45. Sunlight passing through a convex lens can be focused to a tiny bright hot spot on a sheet of paper. This shows that a convex lens:
    (A) spreads light out
    (B) only works with coloured light
    (C) converges light to a point

46. A lens that is **thinner in the middle** than at the edges is a:
    (A) convex lens
    (B) concave mirror
    (C) convex mirror
    (D) concave lens

47. Whatever the position of the object, a concave (diverging) lens always forms an image that is:
    (A) real and inverted
    (B) virtual, erect and diminished

    (A) a concave lens
    (B) a convex lens

    (A) larger and upside down

    (A) just three colours
    (B) seven colours

    (A) violet, indigo, blue, green, yellow, orange, red

    (A) reflection

53. A rainbow in the sky is formed when sunlight is dispersed by:
    (A) clouds reflecting white light
    (B) tiny water droplets acting like little prisms

54. A glass prism splits white light into seven colours. A disc painted with the seven colours and spun very fast (Newton's disc) appears:
    (A) black

55. The fact that a prism can split white light into colours, **and** a spinning seven-colour disc looks white, together show that:
    (A) white light is a single pure colour

56. A prism produces a band of colours but a flat sheet of window glass does not. The key difference is that the prism's faces are:
