**Marking scheme:** **+4** for each correct answer · **−1** for each wrong answer · **0** if unattempted.

**Instructions:** Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. Remember the two big ideas of this chapter: air flows from a region of higher pressure to a region of lower pressure, and faster-moving air exerts lower pressure. No calculator is needed. Most questions need two to four reasoning steps; watch for traps. Do not write on this paper — mark answers on your answer sheet.

1. Which statement about air is correct?
   (A) Air has no weight and exerts no pressure.
   (B) Air exerts pressure only downward.
   (C) Air exerts pressure only at sea level.
   (D) Air has weight and exerts pressure in all directions.

2. Wind is best described as:
   (A) air that is being heated by the Sun
   (B) water vapour rising into the sky
   (C) air moving from a region of higher pressure to a region of lower pressure
   (D) air moving from a region of lower pressure to a region of higher pressure

3. A little water is boiled in a tin can, the cap is screwed on tightly, and the can is then cooled. The can gets crushed because:
   (A) the cold water outside pushes the walls in
   (B) the metal shrinks and tears
   (C) the air and steam inside cool, their pressure drops, and the higher outside air pressure crushes it
   (D) cooling creates extra air inside that pushes the walls

4. At a beach on a hot afternoon you feel a cool wind blowing from the sea toward the land. This sea breeze happens because:
   (A) the sea heats up faster than the land
   (B) the land heats up faster than the sea, so warm air over the land rises and cooler sea air moves in
   (C) the land is always cooler than the sea
   (D) sea water evaporates and pushes the air toward land

5. You hold two inflated balloons hanging side by side a few centimetres apart and blow a stream of air through the gap between them. The balloons will:
   (A) move apart
   (B) stay exactly where they are
   (C) both rise upward

6. Inside the towering clouds of a thunderstorm, the chief reason for the violent activity is:

9. Wind blows fast over the curved top of a roof while the air inside the house is still. During a storm a poorly tied roof tends to be lifted off because:

    (A) the land stays warmer than the sea all night
