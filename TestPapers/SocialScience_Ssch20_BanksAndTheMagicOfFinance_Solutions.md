## Worked Solutions

**1. (C)** Financial infrastructure is banks, payment systems, stock markets and institutions that move and manage money. Roads are physical infrastructure, schools social, and the army state machinery.

**2. (B)** Financial infrastructure complements physical infrastructure, moving money rather than goods. It does not replace, destroy, or stand apart from it.

**3. (C)** Banks and the stock market are financial infrastructure. Highways and ports are transport, schools and hospitals social, and grids and pipelines physical-utility infrastructure.

**4. (C)** A bank collects deposits and lends loans. Printing currency is the RBI's job, road-building is not a bank's, and selling shares is the stock market.

**5. (B)** A bank lends most deposits out as loans, keeping some as reserves - connecting savers and borrowers. It does not keep every rupee idle, refuse to lend, or give money away.

**6. (C)** A savings account keeps money safe, earns interest and allows easy withdrawals. Heavy daily business use is a current account, locking money for a period is a fixed deposit, and buying shares is the stock market.

**7. (B)** A fixed deposit locks money for a set period at higher interest. It is not for unlimited withdrawals, interest-free, or a company share.

**8. (C)** A current account suits businesses with frequent daily transactions. Slow saving suits a savings account, locking money a fixed deposit, and share-buying the stock market.

**9. (A)** A deposit is money placed in a bank, withdrawable and often interest-earning. Borrowed money is a loan, a profit share is a dividend, and the original sum is the principal.

**10. (B)** Interest is the extra money paid or charged over time for the use of money. The original sum is the principal, a profit share a dividend, and a cheque a payment instrument.

**11. (A)** Compounding is earning interest on interest, speeding growth. It is not fast spending, idle money, or losing to inflation.

**12. (B)** The doubling rice grains show how compounding grows amounts surprisingly fast. It is not about idle money, cheques, or printing currency.

**13. (C)** The principal (Latin principalis, 'chief') is the original sum before interest. Interest, a dividend and the benchmark rate are different.

**14. (B)** Compounding (interest on interest) makes money grow faster over time than a single simple-interest payment. It does not shrink, stay fixed, or become a share.

**15. (D)** A loan is borrowed money repaid later with interest. A deposit is money kept safe, a share is company ownership, and the benchmark rate is set by the RBI.

**16. (B)** Credit (Latin credere, 'to trust') is money lent in trust of repayment. It is not printed currency, a share, or a cheque.

**17. (D)** A borrower repays the loan with interest. They do not repay nothing, repay less, or become the bank's owner.

**18. (C)** Borrowing to build a house is a loan, repaid with interest. A deposit earns interest for the saver, a dividend is a profit share, and a share is company ownership.

**19. (B)** Banks earn from charging borrowers more interest than they pay depositors. They do not print currency, charge nothing, or give free loans.

**20. (C)** The bank lends most of your deposit out, keeping your savings working in the economy. It does not lock it away, lose it, or print money.

**21. (D)** Deposited savings are lent out to fund homes, businesses and farms. They are not idle, burned, or sent abroad.

**22. (B)** The bank earns the 5% gap between what it charges and what it pays. It does not print the difference, charge depositors 9%, or get an RBI dividend.

**23. (D)** Banks connect savers and borrowers, channelling deposits into loans. They are not for the rich only, foreign-only, or the government alone.

**24. (D)** The Jan Dhan Yojana (2014) made banking inclusive, helping everyone open accounts. It was not for the wealthy only, illegal, or a return to barter.

**25. (D)** The Jan Dhan Yojana was launched in 2014. 1935 is the RBI's founding, 1950 the Constitution, and 1853 the railways.

**26. (C)** Inclusive banking brings savings, payments and credit to everyone, not just the well-off. It does not exclude the poor, remove banks, or give free money.

**27. (A)** The RBI is India's central bank, the banker to banks. It is not an ordinary people's bank, the stock market, or a private lender.

**28. (B)** The RBI was established in 1935. 2014 is Jan Dhan, 1950 the Constitution, and 1853 the railways.

**29. (A)** The RBI is the central bank - commercial banks keep accounts there, not ordinary people. It is central to banking, not a people's bank, and not foreign.

**30. (C)** The RBI prints the country's currency (and supervises banking). Selling shares is the stock market, and highways and schools are other infrastructure.

**31. (B)** As 'banker to banks', the RBI holds accounts for and lends to commercial banks. It does not serve schoolchildren's accounts, sell groceries, or build dams.

**32. (D)** The RBI regulates and supervises banks to keep the system safe. It does not ignore banks, compete for retail customers, or print shares.

**33. (A)** The benchmark rate is the RBI's base rate for lending to commercial banks. It is not a share price, a cheque fee, or a dividend.

**34. (D)** Changing the benchmark rate affects how cheaply banks lend, shaping borrowing across the economy. It does not set building heights, airport counts, or one share's price.

**35. (A)** Inflation (Latin inflare, 'to blow up') is a general rise in prices, reducing money's buying power; the RBI tries to control it. It is not falling prices, cheques, or a dividend.

**36. (C)** Keeping inflation in check protects the value of money. Runaway rising prices erode savings, money's value does matter, and unlimited inflation is harmful.

**37. (A)** A cheque orders a bank to pay a named person from your account. A company unit is a share, the central bank is the RBI, and interest is a separate thing.

**38. (D)** Electronic transfers move money between accounts almost instantly, faster than a cheque. They are not slower, impossible, or money-printing.

**39. (B)** Cheques and electronic transfers move the same rupees between accounts. They do not create a new currency, move shares, or require paper notes.

**40. (D)** UPI is the Unified Payments Interface, an instant smartphone payment system. A company unit is a share, the central bank is the RBI, and a cheque is paper.

**41. (A)** UPI is called 'India's gift to the world'. It is not a foreign copy, a paper cheque, or the central bank.

**42. (B)** A phone-code instant payment is UPI, moving rupees between accounts. It is not a cheque, a share, or a fixed deposit.

**43. (A)** UPI and electronic transfers move the same rupees faster - new methods, not new money. They are not a new currency, barter, or shares.

**44. (C)** A share is a unit of ownership in a company, making the buyer a part-owner. It is not a deposit, a loan, or the benchmark rate.

**45. (A)** The stock market is where shares in companies are bought and sold. Safe deposits are at banks, currency is printed by the RBI, and home loans come from banks.

**46. (A)** Buying a share makes you a part-owner of the company. It does not make you a bank lender, an employee, or the sole owner.

**47. (A)** A dividend (Latin dividendum, 'a thing to be divided') is a shareholder's share of company profit. Deposit interest, the principal and a cheque are different.

**48. (B)** A shareholder earns through dividends from profit or a rising share price. Bankruptcy, printing money and cheques are not how shareholders earn.

**49. (C)** The stock market is financial infrastructure. It is not transport, social, or energy infrastructure.

**50. (C)** Shares can rise or fall, carrying real risk, unlike a fixed-interest savings deposit. They are not risk-free, can lose value, and are riskier than savings, not less.

**51. (A)** A savings deposit is safe with fixed interest; a share can gain or lose value. The other options reverse or confuse the two.

**52. (C)** Shares are a risky investment, not a guaranteed saving. They are not risk-free, the same as a fixed deposit, or locker money.

**53. (D)** A genuine bank never asks for your OTP, PIN or password; anyone who does is a fraudster. Sharing it endangers your money, and fraud is not foreigner-only.

**54. (D)** Staying safe means never sharing your OTP, PIN or password. Sharing them with callers, writing them on cards, or posting them online invites fraud.

**55. (A)** Refuse to share the OTP and report the fraud attempt on helpline 1930. Giving the OTP or PIN, or sending money, would help the fraudster.

**56. (B)** The cyber-fraud helpline is 1930. 1935 is the RBI's founding year, 2014 Jan Dhan, and 1853 the railways.

**57. (D)** Bank is from Italian banca ('bench'); a failed moneylender had his banca rotta ('broken bench'), giving 'bankrupt'. It is not from cheque, currency, or company.

**58. (D)** Bankrupt comes from banca rotta ('broken bench'), a failed moneylender. It is not a gold vault, a share, or a note.

**59. (A)** Finance (Latin finis, 'end') now means managing money - saving, lending and investing. It is not road-building, aviation, or farming.

**60. (D)** A bank lends most deposits out, the RBI is the banker to banks (not for ordinary accounts), and shares carry real risk - correcting all three errors. The other options keep one or more misconceptions.
