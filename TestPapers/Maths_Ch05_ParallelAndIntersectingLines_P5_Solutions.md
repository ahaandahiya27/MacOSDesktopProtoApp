**1. (C)** A linear pair sums to 180, so (3x+14)+(2x+16)=180, giving 5x+30=180 and x=30. Then angle AOC = 3(30)+14 = 104. Angle EOC = 52 degrees.

**2. (C)** Vertically opposite angles are equal: 4x-6 = 2x+30, so 2x = 36 and x = 18. The angle itself is 4(18)-6 = 66. Its linear-pair neighbour is its supplement, 180 - 66 = 114 degrees. Stopping at the vertical angle gives 66; 18 reports x; 33 halves the original. The neighbour is 114 degrees.

**3. (D)** Opposite angles are equal and the four total 360: 2(2x)+2(x+30)=360, so 6x+60=360 and x=50. The two distinct angles are 2x=100 and x+30=80. Angle to the nearer arm is 50 degrees.

**4. (D)** Linear pair: (5x+8)+(4x+10)=180, so 9x+18=180 and x=18. The angles are 5(18)+8 = 98 and 4(18)+10 = 82. **5. (B)** Vertically opposite angles are equal, so 7x-5 = 3x+47, giving 4x = 52 and x = 13. Choosing 23 mis-handles the constants; 26 doubles x; 6.5 halves it. x = 13.

**52. (D)** Equal alternate interior angles are exactly the condition that guarantees two lines cut by a transversal are parallel. Since both are 63°, the lines are parallel. The other options ignore this converse test. The lines are parallel.

**53. (C)** Corresponding angles are equal only when the lines are parallel. Here 78 ≠ 82, so the lines are not parallel. The '4°' option wrongly treats the difference as a crossing angle; perpendicular is irrelevant. The lines are not parallel.

**54. (B)** For parallelism, co-interior angles must be SUPPLEMENTARY (sum 180°), not equal; equal co-interior angles do not in general prove parallelism. Equal alternate interior angles, equal corresponding angles, and co-interior summing to 180° each guarantee parallelism. So the equal-co-interior condition is the one that does not by itself guarantee parallel lines.

**55. (D)** On parallel lines, alternate interior angles are EQUAL, not supplementary, so that statement is false. The false statement claims alternate interior angles are supplementary.
