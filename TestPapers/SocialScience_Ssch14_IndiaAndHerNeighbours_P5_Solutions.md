## Worked Solutions

**1. (D)** The deciding test is whether a country touches India by a land boundary; if it does not but lies across the sea, it is a maritime neighbour. Sri Lanka is a large island that does have land, so the student's 'pure ocean' rule would refuse to file it under maritime, yet it shares no land border, so it cannot be a land neighbour either, leaving it absurdly uncategorised. The fix is to define maritime by the absence of a shared land border plus a sea link, which places Sri Lanka correctly. The option calling the rule a perfect match ignores that Sri Lanka is land, not pure ocean. Filing it under land neighbours is false because no land boundary is shared. Deleting it as a non-neighbour wrongly assumes only land borders make neighbours, the central misconception of the chapter.

**2. (A)** A boundary's length and the count of neighbours along it are independent: a long coast might face only a couple of maritime neighbours while a shorter land boundary could be split among several countries, so the comparison of kilometres tells us nothing about how many neighbours of each kind exist. The 'sound' option assumes length translates directly into neighbour count, which is the error. Claiming the coastline is longer contradicts the given figures (15,100 km land exceeds 11,100 km coast). Saying India has no maritime neighbours is plainly false, since Sri Lanka and the Maldives are maritime neighbours.

**3. (D)** Nepal sits in the lap of the Himalayas with no coastline, so its overseas trade travels out through Indian ports, while the Maldives is a nation of islets, all coast yet tiny and dispersed, leaning on regional cooperation. The contrast is land-locked versus island. Calling Bhutan a maritime archipelago is false; Bhutan is landlocked. Saying Sri Lanka is landlocked and the Maldives sits in the Himalayas reverses every fact, both are southern sea neighbours. Calling Pakistan landlocked and Afghanistan an island chain is doubly wrong: Pakistan has a coast and Afghanistan is a landlocked overland neighbour.

**4. (B)** The Uttarapatha was the ancient northern overland route linking the Ganga plains toward Central Asia through the north-west, passing through Afghanistan, and the Buddhist remains along it prove sustained contact, refuting any claim of isolation. The Dakshinapatha was the southern route, not a path to Afghanistan, and a southern sea route cannot describe a north-west mountain crossing. Myanmar lies east and is a land gateway to South-East Asia, not a north-west overland link. Bhutan is a Himalayan neighbour, not the route to Central Asia, and the description does not match it.

**5. (C)** A transboundary river crosses one or more borders, so upstream actions change downstream flow; this links India and Bangladesh and makes joint management by agreement the sound principle. Claiming India may take all the water assumes a shared river belongs to the upstream country alone, which the transboundary idea denies. A wall cannot stop a river and would only cause harm, not cooperation. Saying the rivers stop at the border ignores that water keeps flowing across it, the very point of 'transboundary'.

**6. (D)** Gross National Happiness adds wellbeing, environmental care, good governance and culture to, not instead of, economic life, and hydroelectricity from fast-flowing Himalayan rivers is a clean income source that fits an environment-minded outlook, so the two facts sit together. Saying GNH forbids all earning misreads it; it values money alongside other goods. Saying electricity sales prove a money-only focus ignores that GNH weighs wellbeing too. Saying GNH measures only the army is false; the army is not what GNH tracks at all.

**7. (D)** An open border is visa-free yet remains a real boundary between two sovereign states, and the very freedom of movement is what makes cooperation on safety and order necessary, so Vir's reasoning holds. Aria's first claim treats 'open' as 'absent', which the chapter rejects. Her merger claim is false, Nepal keeps its own government. The last option reaches Vir's conclusion through a false premise: India and Nepal share a land border, not a maritime one.

**8. (C)** Cultural influence can travel without armies, and the Thai names and the Ramakien reflect ideas carried by traders, travellers and teachers, with the Ramakien being a distinctly Thai retelling rather than a copy. The 'right' option wrongly equates resemblance with conquest. Calling the echoes coincidence denies the genuine cultural link the chapter affirms. Claiming Thailand conquered India reverses the direction of a peaceful, India-originating spread.

**9. (C)** Hindu-themed Angkor Wat, Buddhist Borobudur and the name Suvarnabhumi together point to ideas, faiths and art carried by sea trade and travel and reshaped by local cultures, a peaceful exchange. The colonial-rule option contradicts the chapter's insistence that the spread was not by conquest. Claiming no contact until modern times ignores ancient evidence like Suvarnabhumi. Calling them coincidences denies the consistent Indian cultural origin the three examples share.

**10. (C)** The Maldives and Sri Lanka are maritime neighbours to the south, China is the Himalayan neighbour to the north, and Pakistan lies to the west and north-west, so the southward-to-north-west ordering is correct. Placing Pakistan and China to the south contradicts their northern/north-western positions. Putting Sri Lanka and the Maldives to the north across the Himalayas is impossible, as they are southern sea neighbours. The third wrong option scrambles every direction.

**11. (A)** Maritime neighbours are reached across the sea without a shared land border; Sri Lanka and the Maldives qualify, but Nepal is a landlocked land neighbour and China is a Himalayan land neighbour, so those two are misplaced. Calling Sri Lanka and the Maldives wrong falsely claims they share land borders. The third option inverts the truth, Nepal is not maritime and Sri Lanka is not a land neighbour. The fourth wrongly labels China maritime and the Maldives landlocked, both reversed.

**12. (A)** The India-Pakistan phrase shows a border slicing through a shared heritage, while the India-China case shows a border tracing a mountain range, together revealing that political lines may cut across culture or follow physical geography, the two need not align. The claim that borders always follow shared culture is contradicted by the Himalayan, geography-drawn India-China border. Calling both borders maritime is false, both are land borders. Saying India shares nothing with either ignores the explicit 'shared heritage' with Pakistan.

**13. (B)** Myanmar being a gateway to South-East Asia means it links India by land toward that region's countries, whereas Indian ports serve as Nepal's gateway to the sea, giving a landlocked country ocean access; the first is overland regional access, the second is sea access. Calling them the same as ocean-via-coastline ignores that Myanmar's role is overland regional linkage. Calling both sea routes bypassing land is false for Myanmar. The last option reverses the facts, Myanmar is not landlocked and Nepal has no coast.

**14. (C)** The Uttarapatha is an ancient trade route, Bangladesh became a separate nation relatively recently (the 'newborn neighbour'), and SAARC was founded in 1985, so oldest to newest runs Uttarapatha, then Bangladesh, then SAARC. Placing SAARC first puts a 1985 body before an ancient route, which is impossible. Placing the Uttarapatha after Bangladesh denies its antiquity. Placing Bangladesh after 1985 SAARC is wrong because Bangladesh predates SAARC's founding.

**15. (B)** Theravada means 'the teaching of the elders', Mahayana 'the great vehicle', and Vajrayana the 'diamond vehicle'; so labelling Vajrayana 'the great vehicle' confuses it with Mahayana and is the error. The other three statements correctly state each meaning, so they are not the mistake.

**16. (A)** Including Iran and Oman in the west and Malaysia, Singapore and Indonesia in the east shows that sea links and trade make distant coastal lands neighbours in many directions, beyond immediate land borders. The land-border-only option contradicts the whole idea of maritime neighbours. Calling them Himalayan land neighbours is geographically false. Saying the neighbourhood is purely western ignores the eastern maritime group named alongside Iran and Oman.

**17. (C)** The river's Himalayan source shared by several neighbours shows a common physical geography crossing borders, and its flow into a neighbour's delta makes it transboundary, tying upstream and downstream together, so both concepts apply. A strait and an archipelago describe sea features, not a Himalayan river. An open border and a landlocked gateway concern crossings and ports, not the river's shared source. The Silk Route and Buddhist schools are trade and religion, unrelated to this river's geography.

**18. (B)** Sea routes carried goods, people and culture, building deep ties such as those with Sri Lanka and South-East Asia, so a maritime bond is fully genuine and important, rebutting the 'weaker' claim. Agreeing that the sea only separates ignores this connecting history. The bridge condition is false, sea links work without bridges. The 'only if no land neighbours' option wrongly makes maritime ties conditional, when India has both kinds at once.

**19. (D)** China lies north across the Himalayas, Pakistan to the west, Bangladesh to the east with its river delta, and Sri Lanka to the south across the narrow strait, so that mapping is correct. The other three options scramble these directions, for instance placing Sri Lanka to the north or Bangladesh to the west, which contradict the chapter's geography.

**20. (A)** The Uttarapatha, Dakshinapatha and spice routes are ancient and carried ideas, faith and culture as well as goods, so the ties are old and far broader than economics, refuting both halves of the claim. Calling the ties recent and economic-only repeats the error. Calling the routes modern military roads is false, they were ancient trade routes. Conceding only a direction error leaves the false 'recent' claim standing, which the chapter contradicts.

**21. (D)** Hydroelectricity is an energy resource and has nothing to do with sea access; both Nepal and Bhutan are landlocked and depend on Indian ports for overseas trade, so their need for the sea is the same. Saying hydroelectricity gives Bhutan a coastline is nonsense, electricity does not create coast. Calling Nepal coastal is false, it is landlocked. The Chinese-ports claim contradicts the chapter, which names Indian ports as their gateway to the sea.

**22. (C)** SAARC promotes cooperation and shared progress among South Asian countries that remain separate sovereign states, so neither merger nor rule by force fits, that is what regionalism means. The merger option misreads cooperation as union. The rule-by-force option contradicts the equal-cooperation idea. Ending all trade is the opposite of a cooperative body's aim.

**23. (C)** Each item is a trace of peaceful cultural spread, traders, travellers and teachers carrying ideas and faith that locals adapted, not of conquest, so none supports the student's claim. The 'all prove conquest' option repeats the central misconception. Singling out Angkor Wat as conquest while calling the rest coincidence still misreads peaceful adaptation. Claiming a name proves ownership is false, Suvarnabhumi was a name born of trade knowledge, not rule.

**24. (D)** An open border lets people cross and a transboundary river lets water cross, and in each case what crosses the boundary ties the two countries together and demands cooperation, the shared lesson. Saying borders fully separate ignores that both cases involve things crossing. Saying one country can act alone contradicts the interdependence of crossing flows. Claiming they form one country misreads cooperation between sovereign states as merger.

**25. (B)** The Maldives does matter to India, and the stated reason, that regional security and friendship rest on every member including small islands, is exactly why, so both are true and the reason explains the assertion. Calling the reason false denies that small members affect security. Calling the assertion false repeats the size-based misconception the chapter rejects. Calling both false ignores that even small neighbours count.

**26. (B)** A strait is a narrow sea channel linking larger waters and often separating two lands (as between India and Sri Lanka), whereas an archipelago is a group or chain of many islands (like the Maldives), so the two terms differ. Equating both with a chain of islands ignores the strait's meaning. Equating both with a sea channel ignores the archipelago's meaning. Calling a strait a mountain pass and an archipelago a desert is wrong on both counts.

**27. (D)** Mountains and rivers that span borders link the countries' resources, so they cannot be treated as wholly private and must be managed cooperatively, which refutes the conclusion. Agreeing each country fully owns its slice ignores the cross-border linkage. Conceding only the mountains leaves the false private-rivers claim, yet rivers are transboundary too. Saying they belong to no one wrongly removes any need to manage them, when shared use is exactly why cooperation matters.

**28. (A)** Afghanistan was linked by the north-west overland route (the Uttarapatha), Bangladesh shares a land border and transboundary rivers to the east, and Indonesia lies across the eastern seas, so that mapping is right. Calling Afghanistan a sea connection is false, it was overland. Saying Indonesia connected by overland mountain routes ignores that it lies across the sea. Saying all three connect only from the south misplaces all three.

**29. (C)** China is a land neighbour across the Himalayas, not maritime; Nepal is landlocked with no coastline; and Myanmar is a land neighbour to the east, the gateway to South-East Asia, not a Pacific island. All three statements are false, so none is correct. Any option claiming one, two, or three correct overcounts these errors.

**30. (D)** Both Sri Lanka and Indonesia absorbed Indian culture and religion brought peacefully by sea, the difference being only the distance of the crossing, near versus far. Saying Indonesia had none ignores Borobudur and Suvarnabhumi. Saying Sri Lanka had none ignores its long shared religious history. Claiming Sri Lanka's influence came by conquest contradicts the chapter's peaceful-spread theme.

**31. (D)** A diaspora is people settled away from their homeland; a bilateral agreement is a deal between two countries, so one is a population and the other a pact. Equating both with a two-country agreement erases the diaspora's human meaning. Equating both with a settled community erases the bilateral's meaning. The last option misdefines both, a bilateral deal is between two countries, not within one.

**32. (B)** Sailing south from India's tip reaches Sri Lanka across the strait, and sailing west reaches the western sea-trade neighbours Iran and Oman, so that pairing is correct. Afghanistan is reached overland to the north-west, not by sailing south. China is northern and reached over the Himalayas, not south by sea. Bangladesh and Myanmar lie to the east, not south or west.

**33. (A)** The Himalayas mark the India-China boundary but a natural barrier shapes contact rather than ending it entirely, so claiming no contact ever is an overreach. Agreeing that mountains make contact impossible forever repeats the error. Calling the Himalayas a sea is factually wrong. Saying the two share no border denies the well-known Himalayan land border.

**34. (C)** Bhutan is a landlocked Himalayan land neighbour, the Maldives is a maritime island neighbour, and Oman is a western neighbour linked by sea and old trade, so the assignment is correct. Calling Bhutan maritime and the Maldives a land neighbour reverses both. Calling the Maldives landlocked and Oman Himalayan is doubly false. Calling all three land neighbours ignores that the Maldives and Oman are reached by sea.

**35. (C)** The two cases differ: India offers a sea gateway to landlocked Nepal and was itself the origin of culture that travelled to Indonesia, so India both links neighbours and gives ideas of its own. The 'only forwards' option ignores India as a cultural source. The conquest claim about Indonesia contradicts the peaceful-spread theme. Saying Nepal has its own port is false, Nepal is landlocked and uses Indian ports.

**36. (D)** An open border is indeed a real border, and the reason, that the recognised boundary stays even while crossing is visa-free, is exactly why, so both are true and the reason explains the assertion. Saying the reason fails to explain ignores that the persisting boundary is the very point. Calling the reason false denies the visa-free-yet-recognised fact. Calling the assertion false repeats the 'open means no border' misconception.

**37. (B)** A long coastline at the centre of an ocean means many coastal lands lie across the water and can be reached by sea, enabling the trade and cultural links that create maritime neighbours, so it does far more than help fishing. Limiting it to fishing ignores this connecting role. A country cannot be both coastal and landlocked, so that option is incoherent. A coastline serves sea links and thus maritime neighbours, not land ones, making the last option backwards.

**38. (B)** The Ganga and Brahmaputra crossing from India into Bangladesh make them upstream-downstream partners needing water cooperation, a valid cause and effect. Bangladesh is not landlocked; it is a delta with its own coast, so the port-dependence claim is false. It shares land and rivers with India, not a distant maritime-only border. It is a 'newborn neighbour', recently formed, not an ancient unchanged state.

**39. (B)** The Ramakien is Thailand's distinct retelling of the Ramayana, reshaped by Thai culture, which is creative adaptation, not copying. Calling it an exact copy is wrong because it was adapted locally. Calling it a trade route confuses an epic with a route. Calling it a school of Buddhism confuses a story with Theravada, Mahayana or Vajrayana.

**40. (A)** The Nepal (open land), China (restricted mountain land) and Sri Lanka (maritime) cases show India's borders come in several forms, illustrating varied connection types. Saying all are open like Nepal's ignores the restricted China border. Saying all are maritime ignores the two land borders. Saying India has one identical border denies the clear differences among these three.

**41. (A)** The wider maritime neighbourhood reaches across the seas to Malaysia, Singapore and Indonesia as well as the nearby islands, so excluding them is too narrow. Limiting it to the nearest neighbours repeats the error. Calling Malaysia and Singapore Himalayan land neighbours is false, they lie across the seas to the east. Denying that Sri Lanka and the Maldives are maritime neighbours contradicts the chapter.

**42. (D)** From the south clockwise: Sri Lanka lies south, Pakistan and Afghanistan west and north-west, China north, and Bangladesh and Myanmar east, which is correct. The other options misplace directions, for example putting Sri Lanka north or China south or Bangladesh south, all contradicting the chapter's map.

**43. (B)** Buddhism originated in India and spread outward to neighbours in its three schools, so the claim reverses the flow by calling India a borrower. Saying India imported it repeats the reversal. Quibbling over the number ignores that there were three schools and leaves the direction error. Saying Buddhism never left India contradicts the very spread the question describes.

**44. (C)** Pakistan and China both have land borders, yet one relationship rests on a shared heritage and the other on a mountain divide, showing the same border type can carry very different relationships. Saying land borders mean identical relationships ignores this contrast. Calling the borders maritime is false, both are land borders. Denying any border contradicts the premise that both share land borders.

**45. (A)** Nepal is landlocked, borrowing sea access via Indian ports, whereas the Maldives is itself a sea-surrounded archipelago, a sharp difference in access. Saying both own coastlines is false for landlocked Nepal. Reversing the labels misstates both. Saying neither can trade by sea ignores that the Maldives is maritime and Nepal trades via Indian ports.

**46. (C)** Shared physical geography, ancient routes and the peaceful spread of religion and culture have bound these countries over centuries, so the neighbourhood rests on geographic, historical and cultural ties beyond mere borders. The aeroplane option wrongly makes the ties recent. There is no single government over all neighbours. There is no common currency across them; these last two invent bonds the chapter never claims.

**47. (A)** Indian culture truly is found across those lands, but the reason is false because the spread was peaceful through trade, travel and teaching, not conquest, so the assertion is true and the reason false. Saying both are true endorses the false conquest reason. Saying the assertion is false denies the well-attested cultural presence. Saying both are false wrongly rejects the genuine cultural spread.

**48. (B)** A feature of flowing fresh water crossing borders is a transboundary river; a strait is a narrow sea channel, so the term is misapplied. Calling anything connecting two lands a strait ignores that a strait is specifically sea. An archipelago is a group of islands, not a river. A landlocked lake has no sea outlet, but this river does reach the sea through the delta.

**49. (A)** The Iran-Oman sea trade is described as ancient, predating the 1985 founding of SAARC, so the western ties are older, making the student's ordering backwards. Saying the western ties began after 1985 repeats the error. Iran and Oman are western neighbours, so that 'eastern' correction is itself wrong. SAARC dates to 1985, not ancient times, so the last option misstates a clear fact.

**50. (A)** Borobudur stands in Indonesia, Angkor Wat in Cambodia, and the Ramakien is Thailand's retelling of the Ramayana, each evidence of peaceful cultural spread, so this set is correct. Placing Borobudur in Bhutan and Angkor Wat in Sri Lanka misplaces both. Calling them a trade route, a school of Buddhism and a strait misidentifies all three. Putting Angkor Wat in Afghanistan and calling the Ramakien an unchanged Indian epic is wrong on both counts.

**51. (D)** Shared rivers, mountains and history make the countries interdependent, so acting alone can damage common resources, and cooperation through regionalism serves everyone, the opposite of the student's policy. Saying acting alone is best ignores this interdependence. Sealing borders contradicts the cooperative ties the chapter stresses. Merging into one country misreads regionalism, which keeps members separate while cooperating.

**52. (A)** An archipelago is an extensive group or chain of islands, so that pair is correct. A strait is a narrow sea channel, not a chain of islands. A diaspora is a community settled far from its homeland, not a sea channel. A bilateral agreement is between two countries, not among all of them.

**53. (A)** The clues, eastern land border, transboundary rivers, recent nationhood and a low coastal delta, point to Bangladesh, and the likely confusion is the southern island Maldives, which is maritime, not a land or river neighbour. Nepal is landlocked with no delta or coast, so it does not fit. Sri Lanka is a southern island without a shared land border. Myanmar lies east but is the gateway to South-East Asia, not described as a recently formed delta nation.

**54. (B)** India's coastline and ocean position open sea links to maritime neighbours while its land borders and old routes connected it to land neighbours, so geography and history together make it central. Calling India landlocked is false, it has a long coast. There is no common currency across the neighbours. The 'only modern' claim ignores the ancient routes and cultural ties the chapter describes.

**55. (C)** Bhutan, the Land of the Thunder Dragon, earns from hydroelectricity and uses GNH to weigh wellbeing and environment alongside income, so it does not reject money. Saying GNH refuses all trade misreads it. Denying the Thunder Dragon name contradicts the chapter. **57. (B)** An open border, shared rivers and SAARC all show interconnection and the value of cooperation, the shared idea. Keeping neighbours separate contradicts these cooperative links. **59. The first wrong option swaps the routes' meanings and wrongly limits trade to the sea. The second reverses the north-south orientation of the two named routes. **60. (B)** Diverse, deep traces such as monuments, Rama-named kings, the Ramakien and the name Suvarnabhumi reflect repeated contact built up over centuries of trade and travel, not a one-off event.
