Worked Solutions

1. (C) The tip moves along a circle, so the path is circular, not straight (rectilinear) or back-and-forth (oscillatory). Because it returns to the same spot after a fixed 60 s interval and repeats, the motion is periodic. It is not random and not oscillatory, so 'circular and periodic' is the only fit.

2. (B) Speed = distance / time = 240 / 16 = 15 m/s. The option 16 m/s wrongly reports the time as the speed; 0.067 m/s divides time by distance (16/240) the wrong way; 3840 multiplies distance by time. So 15 m/s is correct.

3. (B) To go from km/h to m/s, multiply by 5/18: 108 x 5/18 = 540/18 = 30 m/s. The value 388.8 multiplies by 3.6 (the wrong direction); 60 doubles the answer; 10.8 just shifts a decimal. So 30 m/s is correct.

4. (D) Total distance = 12 + 12 = 24 km. Total time = 0.5 h + 0.5 h (rest) + 1 h = 2 h. Average speed = 24 / 2 = 12 km/h. Forgetting the rest gives 24/1.5 = 16 km/h; averaging the two leg-speeds (24 and 12) gives 18 km/h; ignoring all stops gives 24. The rest must be counted, so 12 km/h is correct.

5. (D) Take each half as 60 km. First half time = 60/30 = 2 h; second half time = 60/60 = 1 h. Total distance = 120 km, total time = 3 h, average = 120/3 = 40 km/h. The plain mean (30+60)/2 = 45 km/h is the classic trap; 50 and 90 are unrelated. Equal distances mean more time is spent slow, pulling the average below 45, so 40 km/h is correct.

6. (B) The time period of a simple pendulum depends only on the length of the string, not on the mass of the bob and not on the amplitude (release angle). Same length means the same period regardless of iron vs wood, so the two are equal. Mass and amplitude do not change the period, ruling out the other three.

7. (C) A speedometer shows the instantaneous speed at that moment (the '80'), while an odometer accumulates and shows the total distance travelled (the number that climbed by 45 km). The pairing must be speedometer-then-odometer; swapping them is the trap, and a clock measures time, not either of these readings.

8. (C) To go from m/s to km/h, multiply by 3.6: 25 x 3.6 = 90 km/h. Dividing by 3.6 (25/3.6 = 6.94) goes the wrong way; 75 multiplies by 3; 45 uses 5/18 (the km/h-to-m/s factor). So 90 km/h is correct.

9. (A) Total distance = 400 + 100 = 500 m. Total time = 50 + 100 = 150 s. Average speed = 500 / 150 = 3.33 m/s. Averaging the two leg-speeds (8 m/s and 1 m/s) gives 4.5 m/s, a trap; 5 and 1 are individual hints, not the whole-trip average. So about 3.3 m/s is correct.

10. (D) A horizontal line on a distance-time graph means the distance from the start stays at 40 m while time passes, so the object is stationary (speed = 0). A flat line never means faster; the value 40 is a position, not a speed; and the distance never decreases, so it is not moving backward. Hence it is at rest.

11. (C) Distance = 80 x 1 + 40 x 3 = 80 + 120 = 200 km. Total time = 1 + 3 = 4 h. Average = 200 / 4 = 50 km/h. The plain mean (80+40)/2 = 60 km/h ignores that 3 hours were spent slow; 55 and 120 are unrelated. So 50 km/h is correct.

12. (B) Spinning about a fixed axis while staying in place is rotational motion. Rectilinear and translational motion mean moving along a straight path, which the bit does not; oscillatory means swinging to-and-fro, which it also does not. Hence rotational motion.

13. (D) Speed = slope = distance / time. P: 24/4 = 6 m/s; Q: 16/4 = 4 m/s. The steeper line P is faster, by 6 - 4 = 2 m/s. Reaching t = 4 s together does not make them equal in speed (different distances), and the difference is 2 m/s, not 8. So P at 6 m/s vs Q at 4 m/s is correct.

14. (B) Any repeating (periodic) event marks equal intervals of time, which is exactly how clocks work. The clicks are evenly spaced only because the turning is periodic and steady; non-uniform or random motion would give uneven gaps, and an event that repeats at fixed intervals is by definition periodic. So 'periodic' is correct.

15. (B) First convert: 90 km/h x 5/18 = 25 m/s. Then distance = speed x time = 25 x 8 = 200 m. Using 90 m/s by mistake gives 720 m; 25 m forgets to multiply by the 8 s; 180 multiplies 90 km/h's number badly. So 200 m is correct.

16. (A) For a simple pendulum the time period depends only on the string length, not on how far back it is pulled (the amplitude). The bigger arc is covered at higher speed, so the period stays the same. Bigger swing does not lengthen or shorten the period, and extra energy does not change it either. Hence 'the same'.

17. (D) Speed = slope = change in distance / change in time = (35 - 5) / (6 - 1) = 30 / 5 = 6 m/s. Reading only the first point (5 m at 1 s = 5 m/s) ignores the slope; 35/6 (about 5.8, near 6) tempts a guess of 7; 30 is just the distance change. So 6 m/s is correct.

18. (C) Each hour covers a different distance: 36 + 54 + 72 = 162 km in 3 h. Average = 162 / 3 = 54 km/h. Because the segments are equal in time, the average is simply the mean of the speeds here, 54 km/h. The value 162 is the total distance, not a speed; 48 and 60 are unrelated. So 54 km/h is correct.

19. (A) Time = distance / speed = 360 / 20 = 18 s. Convert speed: 20 x 3.6 = 72 km/h. The pairing 18 s with 72 km/h is the only fully correct one; 56 km/h misconverts, 20 s misreads time, and 7200 s multiplies instead of dividing. So 18 s and 72 km/h.

20. (D) First convert distance: 9 cm = 0.09 m. Speed = 0.09 / 3 = 0.03 m/s. Forgetting to convert cm to m gives 3 (cm/s) mislabelled; 0.3 misplaces a decimal; 27 multiplies instead of dividing. So 0.03 m/s is correct.

21. (B) On a distance-time graph the slope (steepness) equals speed, so the steeper line, X, has the greater speed. A steeper line means more distance per unit time, not less, so X is not slower and has not necessarily travelled less; both start at the origin, so neither started later. Hence X is faster.

22. (A) 5 minutes = 300 s. Number of oscillations = total time / time period = 300 / 2 = 150. Using 5 minutes directly without converting (5/2 = 2.5) is a trap; forgetting to divide by 2 gives 300; multiplying wrongly gives 75. So 150 is correct.

23. (A) The lines meeting at (4 s, 20 m) means at t = 4 s both objects are 20 m from the start, i.e. at the same position. Crossing says nothing about their speeds (their slopes can differ), and neither stops nor reverses just because the lines meet. So they are at the same distance from the start.

24. (C) Time period = total time / number of oscillations. Before: 36 / 24 = 1.5 s. After: 18 / 24 = 0.75 s. So the period halved (1.5 to 0.75 s), which fits the shorter string giving a shorter period. It did not double or stay the same, and 24 is a count, not a period. So it halved to 0.75 s.

25. (A) km/h to m/s means multiply by 5/18: 18 x 5/18 = 5 m/s. Multiplying by 3.6 (18 x 3.6 = 64.8) goes the wrong way; 0.5 and 50 just shift the decimal. So 5 m/s is correct.

26. (C) Whole trip should take 300 / 75 = 4 h. First half took 150 / 50 = 3 h, leaving only 1 h for the last 150 km. Required speed = 150 / 1 = 150 km/h. Simply averaging the deficit (100) or repeating 75 ignores the time already used up. So 150 km/h is needed.

27. (B) For the same distance, speed is inversely proportional to time, so the car with less time (A, 4 h) is faster. Speed ratio A : B = (1/4) : (1/5) = 5 : 4. Same distance does not mean same speed when times differ, and B (more time) is slower. So A is faster, 5 : 4.

28. (C) A straight slanted line means equal distances in equal times, i.e. uniform motion. Speed = 12 / 3 = 4 m/s. A straight line is never non-uniform; 12 is the distance, not the speed; and a sloped line is not at rest. So uniform motion at 4 m/s.

29. (A) The distance from the start only ever increases (or pauses); it never comes back down, so the final reading, 70 m, is the total distance travelled. Adding all the segment values (40 + 0 + 30 again over the same path) double-counts; 110 and 40 misread the curve. So 70 m.

30. (C) Steepness on a distance-time graph means speed, so a line that keeps getting steeper means the speed keeps increasing, i.e. speeding up. A bending-up curve is not slowing down; a constant speed would be a straight line; and a rising line is not at rest. So it is speeding up.

31. (A) Sprinter: 150 / 15 = 10 m/s. Horse: 600 m in 60 s = 600 / 60 = 10 m/s. Both are 10 m/s, so they are equally fast. Comparing 150 directly with 600 (ignoring the different times) wrongly favours the horse; the 590 figure subtracts raw distances. So they are equal at 10 m/s.

32. (A) Average speed = total distance / total time, so you need the distance from the odometer and the time from a clock. A speedometer only shows the speed at one instant, which is not the trip average; a clock alone gives no distance. So the odometer plus a clock is correct.

33. (B) The distances covered each second fall: 6, then 4, then 2 m. Unequal distances in equal times means non-uniform motion, and since the distance per second is decreasing, the car is slowing down. Continuing forward does not make it uniform, the distances are not rising (not speeding up), and a moving toy is not at rest. So non-uniform, slowing down.

34. (C) Distance = speed x time = 300,000 x 4 = 1,200,000 km. Dividing (300,000 / 4 = 75,000) is the wrong operation; 300,000 ignores the 4 s; 1,200 drops three zeros. So 1,200,000 km.

35. (B) A longer pendulum has a longer time period, so it takes more time per swing and fits fewer swings into a minute. The shorter pendulum A has the smaller period, so it oscillates more often in the same minute. Length, not mass or energy, sets the count, and the longer one is slower. So A makes more.

36. (A) Speed A = 100 / 10 = 10 m/s; Speed B = 60 / 10 = 6 m/s. Ratio A : B = 10 : 6 = 5 : 3. The reciprocal 3 : 5 reverses it; equal times do not mean equal speeds; and 100 : 60 does simplify (to 5 : 3). So 5 : 3.

37. (B) Equal distances (60 km) in equal times (1 h each) is the definition of uniform motion; the speed is a steady 60 km/h. A long total distance does not make it non-uniform, the speed never changes (not speeding up), and a straight highway is rectilinear, not circular. So uniform.

38. (D) To pass a pole, the whole train length must go by, so distance = 150 m in 6 s. Speed = 150 / 6 = 25 m/s. The value 6 reports the time as speed; 900 multiplies 150 x 6; 0.04 divides time by distance. So 25 m/s.

39. (A) Swinging to-and-fro is oscillatory, and because each beat repeats at a fixed time interval it is also periodic. Every oscillatory motion that repeats regularly is periodic, so 'oscillatory but not periodic' is wrong; it is not pure rotation (it does not spin all the way round). So both oscillatory and periodic.

40. (C) The whole trip should take 120 / 60 = 2 h. But the first 80 km already used 2 h, leaving 0 h for the last 40 km. Covering 40 km in 0 time is impossible, so she cannot meet the average. Any positive time (1 h, 40 min, 30 min) would push the total over 2 h. So it is impossible.

41. (D) A steeper distance-time line means a higher speed, which means more distance in the same time. So M, the steeper line, covers more distance than N. Equal time intervals do not force equal distance when slopes differ, and a sloped line is not at rest. So M covers more.

42. (A) Total distance = 100 + 100 = 200 km. Total time = 2 h + 1 h (traffic) + 2 h = 5 h. Average = 200 / 5 = 40 km/h. Leaving out the traffic hour gives 200/4 = 50 km/h, the trap; 200 is the distance; 60 is unrelated. The idle time counts, so 40 km/h.

43. (B) The line keeps rising, so distance keeps increasing and the object moves forward. But the slope is getting smaller, and a smaller slope means a lower speed, so it is slowing down. A rising line is not moving backward, a flattening slope is not speeding up, and the line is not horizontal (not at rest). So forward but slowing down.

44. (B) Time period = 50 / 40 = 1.25 s per oscillation. In 2 minutes = 120 s, number of oscillations = 120 / 1.25 = 96. Using 100 s instead of 120 gives 80; doubling 80 gives 160; halving 96 gives 48. So 96.

45. (B) Put all in m/s. P = 6 m/s. Q = 1800 m / 300 s = 6 m/s. R = 25.2 x 5/18 = 126/18 = 7 m/s. R at 7 m/s is the fastest; P and Q tie at 6 m/s but neither leads. So R is fastest.

46. (A) An hourglass works because the sand falls through its narrow neck at an almost constant rate, so a fixed amount of sand always takes the same time to run through. The grains do not pulse, the bulbs do not swing, and no sunlight is involved. So the steady trickle of sand is the reason.

47. (D) Times: 60/60 = 1 h; 60/30 = 2 h; 120/40 = 3 h. Total distance = 60 + 60 + 120 = 240 km; total time = 1 + 2 + 3 = 6 h. Average = 240 / 6 = 40 km/h. The plain mean of the leg speeds, (60 + 30 + 40)/3 = 43.3 km/h, is the trap; 45 and 60 are unrelated. So 40 km/h.

48. (D) A horizontal distance-time line means the distance does not change as time passes, so the object covers no ground: its speed is zero. A flat line never means maximum or high speed, and the speed is perfectly well-defined as 0. So zero.

49. (D) Convert: 180 x 5/18 = 50 m/s. Distance = 50 x 30 = 1500 m. Using 180 m/s gives 5400 m; dividing 180/30 gives 6; 5000 is a slip. So 1500 m.

50. (C) Times: 60/20 = 3 h; 60/30 = 2 h; 60/60 = 1 h. Total distance = 180 km; total time = 3 + 2 + 1 = 6 h. Average = 180 / 6 = 30 km/h. The plain mean (20+30+60)/3 = 36.7 km/h is the trap; equal distances spend more time at the slow speeds, pulling the average down to 30 km/h.

51. (D) A horizontal line means the distance is unchanging, so the object is at rest (speed zero), not moving fast — that statement is false. The other three are all true: steeper means faster, a straight slant means uniform speed, and the slope equals speed. So the false one is the 'horizontal = high speed' claim.

52. (D) Convert: 1.2 km = 1200 m; 20 min = 1200 s. Speed = 1200 / 1200 = 1 m/s. Forgetting to convert minutes to seconds (1200 m / 20) gives 60; misplacing the decimal gives 0.06; 3.6 is the km/h conversion of 1 m/s, not the answer asked. So 1 m/s.

53. (A) Distance falling back to zero means the object's distance from the start became 0 again, i.e. it came back to where it began. A line that rises then falls is not continuous speeding up, says nothing about a circular path, and the object kept moving after the peak (it did not stop). So it returned to its starting point.

54. (B) Going: 2.4 / 6 = 0.4 h. Returning: 2.4 / 4 = 0.6 h. Total distance = 4.8 km, total time = 1.0 h, average = 4.8 / 1.0 = 4.8 km/h. The plain mean (6+4)/2 = 5 km/h is the trap; 2.4 is one leg's distance; 10 adds the speeds. So 4.8 km/h.

55. (C) A shorter pendulum has a shorter time period, so it ticks more often than it should and the clock gains time (runs fast). A shorter pendulum actually swings more often, not less; the period depends on length, not the bob's mass; and a short pendulum still swings fine. So it runs fast.

56. (D) Both lines start together at the origin; the steeper line (A) gains distance faster, so at every later moment A is ahead of B and the gap keeps widening. A gentler line is slower (not farther), the gap is not constant, and from the origin A is never behind B. So A is always farther.

57. (C) To cross completely the train travels its own length plus the bridge: 120 + 480 = 600 m in 24 s. Speed = 600 / 24 = 25 m/s. Using only the bridge (480/24 = 20) forgets the train length; using only the train (120/24 = 5) forgets the bridge; 30 is a slip. So 25 m/s.

59. (A) Distance = 12,700 - 12,460 = 240 km. Average speed = total distance / total time = 240 / 4 = 60 km/h. Dividing the final reading by 4 (12,700/4 ≈ 3175) ignores the start; 240 forgets to divide by time; 50 is a slip. So 60 km/h.
