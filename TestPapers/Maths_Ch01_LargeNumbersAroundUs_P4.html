Worked Solutions

1. (C) Lakhs place has value 1,00,000 and thousands place has value 1,000, so the number is 8 x 1,00,000 + 8 x 1,000 = 8,00,000 + 8,000 = 8,08,000, a 6-digit number. Writing the two 8s side by side as 88,000 is only a 5-digit number and ignores the empty places between them. Putting the second 8 in the hundreds place gives 8,00,800. Reading the first 8 as ten-lakhs gives 80,08,000.

2. (B) Grouped 4,86,30,517: crores 4, ten-lakhs 8, lakhs 6, ten-thousands 3, thousands 0, hundreds 5, tens 1, ones 7. Ten-lakhs 8 plus lakhs 6 is 14. Adding crores and lakhs (4 + 6) gives 10; adding lakhs and ten-thousands doubled to 6 + 6 gives 12; misreading the lakhs digit as 3 (the ten-thousands digit) and adding 6 + 3 gives 9.

3. (D) Drop commas to get 60700050 (eight digits). Grouping in threes from the right gives 60,700,050. Keeping the Indian commas is no conversion at all; grouping the spare digits on the right as 607,000,50 breaks the 3-3-3 rule; 60,70,0050 uses a 2-2-4 grouping that belongs to neither system.

4. (B) The leftmost digit 9 is in the crores place: place value 9,00,00,000. The 8 is in the lakhs place: place value 8,00,000. The difference is 9,00,00,000 − 8,00,000 = 8,92,00,000. Subtracting face values (9 − 8) gives 1; reading the 8 as ten-lakhs (80,00,000) and subtracting gives 8,20,00,000; mistaking the 8's place value for 80,000 and subtracting gives 8,99,20,000.

5. (B) 3 crore = 3,00,00,000. One ten-thousand = 10,000, so the count is 3,00,00,000 ÷ 10,000 = 3,000. Dividing by one lakh instead gives 300; dividing by one thousand gives 30,000; treating crore as ten lakh and dividing wrongly gives 3,00,000.

6. (D) Crores 4, then lakhs part 'four lakh forty thousand' = 4,40,000, then four ones. Building it: 4,04,40,004. Reading 'four lakh' into the ten-lakh slot inflates it to 4,44,40,004; writing 'forty thousand' as 04 thousand gives 4,04,04,004; dropping the empty ten-lakh place collapses it to the 7-digit 44,40,004.

7. (D) 1 million = 10,00,000, so 250 million = 250 × 10,00,000 = 25,00,00,000 (twenty-five crore). Treating million as one lakh gives 250 × 1,00,000 = 2,50,00,000; treating it as ten thousand gives 25,00,000; adding an extra zero (2,500 lakh as 2,50,00,00,000) is ten times too big.

8. (B) An even number ends in 0 or 2. To be largest, put the biggest digits in the highest places, so try ending in 0: the remaining 9,7,5,2 go in descending order 9,7,5,2 giving 97,520. Ending in 2 forces 0 lower and a smaller leading arrangement (97,5_2 needs 0 inside: 97,502 < 97,520). 97,250 puts 2 before 5 in the tens/ones region and is smaller; 97,502 ends in 2 and is smaller; 75,920 wastes the lead digit.

9. (B) The 6 sits in the ten-lakhs place, so its place value is 60,00,000 and its face value is 6. Difference 60,00,000 − 6 = 59,99,994. Subtracting place from face the wrong way still needs the 6 (giving 6 only if you compare face values); reading the 6 as lakhs (6,00,000) and subtracting gives 59,99,940-type slips; forgetting to subtract the face value leaves the place value 60,00,000.

10. (A) From the right: 789, then 56, then 34, then 12 — giving 12,34,56,789 (twelve crore...). 123,456,789 is the International 3-3-3 grouping; 1,23,45,6789 and 1,2345,6789 leave a 4-digit block which the Indian system never allows.

11. (D) Compare from the ten-thousands place: 7,90,000 has 9, 7,09,000 has 0, 7,00,900 has 0. So 7,90,000 is largest. Between the other two, 7,09,000 has 9 thousands while 7,00,900 has 0 thousands, so 7,09,000 > 7,00,900. Order: 7,90,000 > 7,09,000 > 7,00,900. The other lists either rank 7,09,000 above 7,90,000 or swap the smallest two.

12. (C) Nearest hundred: the tens digit is 6 (≥5), so 7,49,962 → 7,50,000. Rounding that to the nearest thousand leaves 7,50,000 (it is already a multiple of 1,000). Rounding down by truncating gives 7,49,000; stopping at the hundred step alone gives 7,49,900 wrongly (in fact the hundred-round is already 7,50,000); over-rounding to the nearest lakh gives 8,00,000.

13. (A) 19 rounds to 20, so the estimate is 4,12,000 × 20 = 82,40,000. Multiplying by 2 only (forgetting a zero) gives 8,24,000; adding instead of multiplying gives 4,12,019; using the unrounded 19 (4,12,000 × 19 = 78,28,000) is the exact product, not the asked estimate.

14. (C) 3 crore + 5 lakh + 7 thousand + 2 tens = 3,00,00,000 + 5,00,000 + 7,000 + 20 = 3,05,07,020. Collapsing all the empty places gives 3,57,200; reading the first term as 10 crore gives 30,50,07,020; placing the 5 in the ten-lakh slot gives 3,50,07,020.

15. (C) 9,999 × 9,999 is just under 10,000 × 10,000 = 10,00,00,000 (a 9-digit number). Exactly, 9,999² = 99,980,001 = 9,99,80,001, which has 8 digits. A product of two 4-digit numbers has 7 or 8 digits, and here the leading digits make it the maximum 8 — not 9, since it stays below 10 crore. The estimate 7 undercounts; 9 wrongly assumes it reaches 10,00,00,000.

16. (A) Five crore = 5,00,00,000; plus five thousand = 5,000; plus five = 5; total 5,00,05,005. Dropping the empty lakh places gives the 6-digit 5,05,005; reading only 'five thousand five' gives 55,005; placing the thousands as ten-thousands gives 5,00,50,005.

17. (B) 30 days × 4,800 = 1,44,000 copies. Rounding 1,44,000 to the nearest lakh: the ten-thousands digit is 4 (<5), so it rounds down to 1,00,000. Misplacing a zero gives 10,00,000; rounding 1,44,000 down to nothing gives 0; rounding up wrongly gives 2,00,000.

18. (C) Exact need is 9 × 1,250 = 11,250 sacks. To never run short you must round UP to a safe round figure, 12,000. Rounding down to 11,000 or 11,200 leaves you short; stocking exactly 11,250 is not a 'round figure' and leaves no margin.

19. (B) One day is 24 × 60 × 60 = 86,400 seconds, so one lakh (1,00,000) seconds is a bit more than one day (about 1 day 4 hours). An hour is only 3,600 seconds (far too few); a week of 6,04,800 seconds is far more than a lakh; a month of about 26 lakh seconds is much more still.

20. (D) 18 lakh = 18,00,000 and 144 crore = 1,44,00,00,000. The fraction is 18,00,000 ÷ 1,44,00,00,000 = 18 ÷ 14,400 = 1/800. Dropping the crore-to-lakh factor of 100 gives 1/8 (treating crore as lakh) or 1/80; adding an extra factor of 10 gives 1/8000.

21. (A) Group 90,90,090: ninety lakh, ninety thousand, ninety. Reading the first 90 as crores gives the wrong 'nine crore...'; reading the middle 90 as 9 thousand gives 'nine thousand'; merging the lakh and ten-thousand blocks gives 'ninety-nine lakh'.

22. (C) 1 crore = 100 lakh, so 12 crore = 12 × 100 = 1,200 lakh. Multiplying by 10 instead of 100 gives 120; by 1,000 gives 12,000; forgetting the conversion entirely gives 12.

23. (A) Groups: 408 million, 090 thousand, 007. So 'four hundred eight million ninety thousand seven'. Reading 408 as 480 and 090 as 9 gives the first wrong option; mixing in Indian units (crore/lakh) is the wrong system; reading 090 thousand as 'nine hundred thousand' inflates it.

24. (C) 2 crore 5 lakh = 2,00,00,000 + 5,00,000 = 2,05,00,000 = 20500000. Grouping in threes from the right: 20,500,000. The option 2,05,00,000 is still Indian commas; 2,500,000 is only 25 lakh (a tenfold error); 205,000,00 breaks the 3-3-3 grouping.

25. (C) Each place to the right is ten times smaller, so two places to the right is 10 × 10 = 100 times smaller. Hence the given place value is 100 times larger. Counting one place gives 10; counting the steps instead of the factor gives 2; counting three places gives 1,000.

26. (B) Convert all to plain numbers: 8 crore = 8,00,00,000; 79 million = 7,90,00,000; 7,95,00,000 is itself; 790 lakh = 7,90,00,000. The largest is 8 crore (8,00,00,000), which beats 7,95,00,000 and the two equal 7,90,00,000 values. Mistaking million for crore would wrongly crown 79 million.

27. (A) Rounding to the nearest crore looks at the ten-lakhs (next) digit. Here the part after the crores is 49,52,000, whose leading digit 4 is below 50,00,000, so round DOWN to 5,00,00,000. Rounding up gives 6,00,00,000; rounding to the nearest fifty-lakh is not a place; rounding to the nearest lakh gives 5,49,00,000-type figures, not crore.

28. (C) Round each: 3,87,000 → 4,00,000; 6,12,000 → 6,00,000; 2,49,000 → 2,00,000. Sum 4 + 6 + 2 = 12 lakh = 12,00,000. Rounding 2,49,000 up to 3,00,000 gives 13,00,000; rounding 3,87,000 down to 3,00,000 gives 11,00,000; the exact sum 12,48,000 is not the rounded estimate asked.

29. (D) 25,000 × 8,000 = 25 × 8 × 1,000 × 1,000 = 200 × 10,00,000 = 20,00,00,000, which has 9 digits. Counting only the eight original trailing structure or losing the carry into a new digit gives 8; further undercounting gives 7; adding a spurious zero gives 10.

30. (B) 50 million = 50,000,000; 500 thousand = 5,00,000; fifty = 50; total 50,500,050. Reading 500 thousand as 5 thousand gives 50,005,050; reading the final 'fifty' as 'five hundred' gives 50,500,500; reading fifty million as five hundred million gives 500,500,050.

31. (D) ₹2 crore 40 lakh = 2,40,00,000. Dividing by 16: 2,40,00,000 ÷ 16 = 15,00,000. Misplacing one zero gives ₹1,50,000 or ₹1,50,00,000; dividing only the lakh part gives ₹15,000.

32. (A) A 6-digit palindrome has the form abccba. The lakhs digit (1st, 'a') is 4, so the ones digit is also 4: 4bccb4. The tens digit (2nd from right, 'b') is 7, so the ten-thousands digit is also 7: 47cc74. The thousands and hundreds digits are the middle pair 'cc'; smallest means c = 0, so the thousands digit is 0 (number 470074). Choosing c = 7 to match the tens, or c = 4 to match the lakhs, or c = 1 are larger or unjustified.

33. (A) 60 lakh × 5 = 60,00,000 × 5 = 3,00,00,000 = 3 crore. So they are equal. Reading 60 lakh × 5 as 30 lakh wrongly makes 3 crore look bigger; mis-scaling by ten produces the 'ten times' claim.

34. (D) Digit string 7030500 (seven digits). Indian grouping 3-2-2 from the right: 500, then 30, then 70 → 70,30,500 (seventy lakh thirty thousand five hundred). The options 7,03,05,00 and 70,30,50,0 mis-split the digits; 7,03,0500 leaves a 4-digit block, which Indian commas forbid.

35. (A) Indian commas fall after 3 digits, then every 2 digits, reading from the right: 890, then 67, then 45, then 23, then 1 — giving 1,23,45,67,890 (one arab twenty-three crore...). The other layouts leave a forbidden 4-digit group (7890) or mix in International 3-3 blocks.

36. (A) The largest place value is the 8 in the crores place: 8,00,00,000. In 8,07,60,400 the digit 6 sits in the ten-thousands place, so its place value is 60,000.
