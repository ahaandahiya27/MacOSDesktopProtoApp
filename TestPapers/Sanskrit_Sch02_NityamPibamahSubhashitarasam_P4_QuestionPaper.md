1. नित्यं पिबामः सुभाषितरसम् uses 'पिबामः' (we drink) for an act of the mind, not the throat. The verb-choice 'drink' rather than 'read' is meant to suggest that wise sayings should be:
   (A) swallowed once quickly and never returned to
   (B) consumed only when one is physically thirsty
   (C) tasted but spat out because their flavour is bitter
   (D) taken in regularly and absorbed as inner nourishment, the way a drink sustains the body

2. In 'पिबामः', the ending '-आमः' identifies the verb as:
   (A) third person singular present — 'he drinks'
   (B) first person plural present — 'we drink'
   (C) second person plural imperative — 'you all drink!'
   (D) first person singular present — 'I drink'

3. A pupil writes the plural of सुभाषितम् as 'सुभाषिताः', reasoning that नराः is the plural of नरः so the same '-आः' must apply. The precise flaw is that:
   (A) नरः is masculine and takes '-आः', but सुभाषितम् is neuter and its nominative plural is सुभाषितानि ('-आनि')
   (B) सुभाषितम् is feminine, so its plural is सुभाषिताः after all
   (C) all -अ-stem nouns take '-आः' regardless of gender, so the pupil is right
   (D) सुभाषितम् has no plural because abstract sayings cannot be counted

4. The title-word सुभाषितरसम् packs three pieces: सु + भाषित + रस. Read as a whole, its structure is best described as:
   (A) 'the rasa OF the well-spoken (saying)' — सुभाषित (well-spoken saying) qualifies रस in a genitive (tatpurusha) relation
   (B) 'a rasa that is both bad and spoken' — सु here means 'bad'
   (C) 'rasa AND a saying' — two equal, unrelated nouns joined as a pair
   (D) 'O sweet saying!' — a word used purely to address the verses

5. नरः पञ्चभिः वकारैः पूजितः. A reader who renders this as 'the man worships by five va-things' has misread the grammar because पूजितः is:
   (A) an active present verb meaning 'he worships', so the man is the worshipper
   (B) an imperative, ordering the man to worship the five things
   (C) a past passive participle — the man is the one BEING honoured, not the one doing the honouring
   (D) a future tense, predicting that the man will worship later

6. Exactly five qualities honour a person and exactly six faults are to be shed. A student lists honouring qualities as विद्या, वपुस्, वाणी, वस्त्र, विनय, सत्य. The single defect is:
   (A) सत्य is not one of the five 'va' qualities — it is a purification MEANS (मनः सत्येन शुध्यति); the five are विद्या, वपुस्, वाणी, वस्त्र, विनय
   (B) nothing is wrong; there are six 'va' qualities
   (C) वस्त्र should be removed, leaving only four genuine qualities
   (D) विनय is the intruder; the real fifth quality is सत्य

7. The pot fills 'जलबिन्दुनिपातेन क्रमशः' (drop by falling drop, gradually). Transferred to character-building, the deepest implication is that one small undesirable act, repeated, also:

   (B) accumulates gradually into a large result, so habits (good or bad) build the same drop-by-drop way
   (C) instantly creates a finished character with no further effort

8. षड्दोषाः splits into षड् + दोषाः. The first member षड् here means:

9. नित्यम् + पिबामः joins to नित्यं पिबामः. The change of final म् to the anusvāra ं before प is an instance of:

10. विद्यया, वपुषा, वाचा, वस्त्रेण, विनयेन are the five qualities as they appear in the verse. Why are these the forms used rather than the dictionary forms विद्या, वपुस्, etc.?
   (A) because they are the subjects performing the honouring, hence nominative
   (B) because the qualities are being directly addressed, hence vocative
   (C) because the verse says one is honoured BY them — the means of honouring takes the instrumental (तृतीया)

12. By the purification verse, which single change to this set of claims would make ALL four correct?
'गात्राणि जलेन; मनः ज्ञानेन; आत्मा तपसा; बुद्धिः सत्येन.'
   (A) swap the means of गात्राणि and आत्मा, giving गात्राणि तपसा and आत्मा जलेन
   (B) change गात्राणि जलेन to गात्राणि सत्येन
   (C) swap the means of मनः and बुद्धिः, giving मनः सत्येन and बुद्धिः ज्ञानेन
   (D) leave it unchanged; the set is already fully correct

13. बुद्धिः केन शुध्यति? A learner answers जलेन because 'water cleans, so it must clean the intellect too.' The flaw in this reasoning is that it:
   (A) is correct — water does clean the intellect in the verse
   (B) wrongly assumes the intellect needs cleaning at all
   (C) should have answered तपसा, the means for the intellect
   (D) over-generalises water's role: water cleans the physical body (गात्राणि), but the intellect is cleaned by knowledge (ज्ञानेन)

14. षड् दोषाः हातव्याः भूतिम् इच्छता. The reason 'भूतिम् इच्छता' is instrumental rather than nominative भूतिम् इच्छन् is that:

   (B) instrumental here means 'O desirer of prosperity!', a form of address
   (C) the phrase means prosperity is desired BY the six faults

15. A reader counts the faults as 'निद्रा/तन्द्रा = one sleepy fault; आलस्यम्/दीर्घसूत्रता = one lazy fault; भयम्; क्रोधः', concluding there are FOUR faults. The correct rebuttal is:
   (A) the reader is right; there really are only four faults
   (B) there are five faults, because भयम् and क्रोधः are also one fault
   (C) there are eight faults once each pair is split into three
   (D) the verse names six DISTINCT faults: निद्रा≠तन्द्रा (full sleep vs drowsy half-sleep) and आलस्यम्≠दीर्घसूत्रता (general laziness vs delaying tasks), so merging undercounts to four

   (A) निद्रा = doing nothing; तन्द्रा = delaying tasks

   (D) दीर्घसूत्रता = doing nothing; आलस्यम् = delaying tasks (the reverse)

21. गात्राणि जलेन शुध्यन्ति uses शुध्यन्ति (plural), but बुद्धिः ... शुध्यति uses शुध्यति (singular). The reason for the different verb-endings is:
