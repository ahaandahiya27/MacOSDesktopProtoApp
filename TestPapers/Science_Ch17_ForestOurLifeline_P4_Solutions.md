## Worked Solutions

**1. (B)** A seedling with limited stored food cannot make enough food in 2 percent light, and it cannot climb to the top without leaves; the herb-level claim wrongly treats dim light as ideal. Many forest-floor seeds stay dormant and shoot up only when a treefall gap floods the spot with light, matching the real light gradient.

**2. (A)** The owl here eats only the mouse, and grass appearing as the shared base does not make a crossing among consumers; the mouse eats only grass, not the hare. The fox feeding on both the hare-chain and the mouse-chain is what knits the two chains into a web.

**3. (D)** Losing the fox frees both prey from one predator, but the hare had only the fox while the mouse still faces the owl that keeps it partly in check, so equal rises or a steeper mouse rise are unlikely; the claim that predator loss has no effect contradicts how webs respond.

**4. (A)** The spider was only one of the warbler's foods, so the warbler does not starve; leaves are eaten by caterpillars, not protected by spiders; spiders did not hunt warblers, so the hawk gains no direct food. Removing the spider lifts predation on caterpillars (they rise) and pushes the warbler to rely more on caterpillars.

**5. (B)** Tigers do breed, deer being many does not stop them being prey, and tigers always need food; the real ceiling is that energy shrinks sharply at each trophic step, so the producer-herbivore base fixes how much predator mass the forest can carry.

**6. (B)** Wind removal and trees ceasing leaf-fall are invented causes, and a piling, un-recycled litter mat signals nutrients trapped rather than richer soil. The contrast shows decomposers normally match litter breakdown to leaf fall (plot X); removing them stalls the cycle (plot Y).

**7. (B)** The eagle eats snakes and foxes, not frogs directly, so it does not starve; rabbits sit in the other chain and did not share the frog's predators; frogs do not fertilise clover. Fewer frogs starve the snakes (decline), shift the eagle toward foxes, and release grasshoppers to rise.

**8. (A)** Trees do not drink floods to feed springs, baked soil holds less not more water, and cut-wood steam is trivial. One change, loss of the root-and-litter sponge, simultaneously speeds runoff (erosion and flash floods), empties the slow store that fed the spring, and cuts the transpiration that moistened the air.

**9. (D)** The floor is the darkest layer, not the brightest; dead matter collects on the ground, not in the canopy; and the layers are clearly not uniform. Light is captured high (feeding canopy animals) while fallen dead matter accumulates low (feeding floor decomposers), placing the two riches in different layers.

**10. (A)** Plants do not make mineral nutrients from sunlight, decomposing litter enriches rather than poisons soil, and rain does not resupply nutrients daily. The forest stays fertile only because decomposers return litter and dead wood to humus; hauling that matter away breaks the loop and drains the soil.

**11. (D)** Plants do respire at night, so the observation is real, not a mistake, and they do not stop respiring by day. Because daytime photosynthesis takes up much more carbon dioxide and gives out much more oxygen than night respiration consumes, the net effect over a day is to enrich the air with oxygen.

**12. (B)** The sequence took sixty years, not one season, and it unfolded on its own without a planting plan, so it was neither fixed from the start nor instantly complete. The gradual, self-driven progression from herbs to a layered community shows the forest is a dynamic, self-renewing system.

**13. (B)** Herbivores are not the humus-making link, rain does not move nutrients into roots from dead matter, and sunlight powers photosynthesis but is not the return step to soil. Only the decomposers convert dung and dead bodies back into humus, so cutting them severs the nutrients' road back to the soil.

**14. (A)** Ants were protectors of aphids, not their food, so losing ants does not feed-starve aphids upward; ants ate aphid honeydew, not leaves; and spiders ate ladybirds, not ants. Removing the protecting ants exposes aphids to ladybirds, so aphids fall and the better-fed ladybirds offer spiders more prey.

**15. (D)** A rainless soil is not what makes it fertile, hooves alone do not enrich soil, and darkness does not create nutrients. Forest soil stays rich because decomposers continually add humus while roots and litter protect it from being washed away, two effects the bare field loses at once.

**16. (A)** Pollination and dispersal both matter, and losing the bird does not stop the surviving moth from pollinating, so neither 'unaffected' nor 'dies at once' fits; seeds dropped only under the parent compete in its shade and rarely spread the species. Without its disperser the tree sets fruit but cannot scatter offspring, so it renews poorly, showing how interdependent the partners are.

**17. (A)** Clean air and water clearly do have value to people, products and services are not the same (cut timber gives no oxygen or water recharge), and limiting the worth to wildlife ignores human benefit. The living forest supplies many simultaneous services that felled timber cannot, so its value is far more than the wood.

**18. (C)** Rain did not become intrinsically heavier or dirtier, the listed effects are all forest-linked rather than unrelated, and deforestation worsens rather than improves soil and climate. One cause, the lost tree-and-litter cover, simultaneously speeds runoff, exposes soil to erosion, and removes the transpiration and shade that cooled the air.

**19. (C)** Deer and leopard never ate the fish, so they do not collapse; the shared decomposer link shows the chains are not wholly separate and do not keep private recyclers. Losing the fish starves the kingfisher (decline) and frees the phytoplankton, while the single decomposer community keeps recycling the dead of both chains into one soil.

**20. (D)** Removing decomposers would halt the nutrient recycling the forest depends on, clearing the canopy exposes and harms the lower layers, and a single-crop conversion destroys the many-layered community. Protecting the existing forest and replanting lost areas keeps the whole system, and its services, intact and renewing.

**21. (D)** Bats survive and keep pollinating, so flowering continues and the beetle loss does not disable the bats; whole un-decayed fruit does not feed roots directly, and both pollination and decay do matter. Without the beetle grubs the fallen fruit breaks down more slowly, delaying the nutrients' return to the soil near the tree.

**22. (C)** Body size does not set the energy share, the middle of a chain is not the minimum, and the grass as producer captures the most, not the least. Since a fraction is lost at each step, the organism furthest up the chain, the hawk, depends on the smallest remaining share of the Sun's energy.

**23. (A)** Energy does not return to the Sun and is steadily lost as heat, both clearly do move through the forest, and it is energy, not nutrients, that flows one way. Energy makes a one-way trip and is lost, whereas nutrients are recycled back to the soil by decomposers, so the two arrows differ.

**24. (C)** The thrush ate caterpillars too, so it does not starve; caterpillars still graze the leaves, so they are not stripped of grazers; and hawks ate thrushes, not weevils. Losing weevils pushes the thrush onto caterpillars more, which can press caterpillar numbers down even as the leaves keep one grazer.

**25. (C)** Sowing seeds does not yield a grown forest in a season, a cleared patch generally does regrow given time, and no predator is needed to start regrowth. A forest is a slowly assembled, layered community whose soil and species rebuild over many years, so it cannot reappear complete within months.

**26. (B)** Meat-eaters trace their energy back through prey to the plants, so they are not exempt, and the effect reaches far below the top predator alone; animals cannot make their own food. Because producers are the energy base every chain rests on, destroying them starves the whole web, not just the herbivores.

**27. (C)** Size is not the defining difference, a forest need not contain a particular predator, and forests are full of animals rather than free of them. What sets a forest apart is being a diverse, layered, self-renewing community lasting years, unlike a uniform crop sown and harvested in a single season.

**28. (A)** Floor plants face the least sun, not the harshest, so water storage for sun is misplaced; plants cannot make food without light by drinking humus; and the floor is shaded, not flooded with sun. Broad, thin leaves maximise capture of the scarce filtered light, letting the shade fern still photosynthesise enough to live.

**29. (A)** A forest does far more than cast shade or shift one gas, and even one forest measurably affects its surroundings, so the single-factor and 'nothing' options fail. Forests jointly govern temperature, moisture, rainfall, soil, water flow and carbon dioxide, so removing one disturbs all of these together.

**30. (D)** Coming first does not make an organism a producer, feeding on a dead leaf is decomposing rather than herbivory of living plants, and the fungus is the one being eaten, not a flesh-eater. The fungus lives by breaking down dead matter, the defining work of a decomposer.

**31. (C)** Meat-eaters trace their food through the zebra back to the grass, so they are not spared, and the effect reaches beyond the lion alone; predators cannot make their own food. Since the grass is the producer underpinning the whole web, its loss starves every animal that depends on it directly or indirectly.

**32. (B)** Burning consumes oxygen rather than leaving it to rise, the released gas is mainly carbon dioxide not nitrogen, and carbon does not vanish but is freed as gas. Burning the tract both releases the trees' stored carbon as carbon dioxide and removes the living trees that had been absorbing it, so the gas climbs from both effects.

**33. (B)** Mice still eat seeds, so seeds are not left unconsumed; owl and fox each had two prey, so neither starves; and sparrows were not the mice's food. With fewer sparrows, both predators turn more to mice, pressing mouse numbers and shifting grazing pressure on the seeds.

**34. (C)** A forest is not a mere timber stock, nor a set of unrelated trees, nor a yearly human rebuild. Its many services arise precisely because it is one living, interlinked system in which plants, animals, soil and microbes work together, each part enabling the rest.

**35. (C)** Plants do not manufacture mineral nutrients from sunlight, the nutrients really are taken up into plant tissue rather than just borrowed in place, and rain does not resupply them daily. The soil stays stocked because decomposers continually recycle nutrients back from litter, dung and dead matter, balancing what plants remove.

**36. (D)** The filtered light is helpful, not harmful, it does feed plants and not merely warm animals, and it does not remove a plant's need for water and nutrients. Shade-adapted lower plants depend on exactly this weak filtered light to photosynthesise enough to live under the canopy.

**37. (B)** The snake still eats lizards, so it does not collapse; beetles ate clover rather than controlling grasshoppers; and lizards do not eat clover. Losing beetles pushes the lizard onto grasshoppers (pressing them down) while the snake, still supplied with lizards, feels only a mild effect.

**38. (D)** Trees do not pull clouds down, do not seed clouds with smoke, and do not absorb all solar heat. They moisten the air through transpiration and cool the ground with shade and cover, and these together favour rain and temper the local climate, both lost when the forest goes.

**39. (A)** Erosion is not soil 'floating off' for lack of tree-drinking, baked-hard soil is not what resists washing away, and rain temperature does not govern erosion. The forest holds soil because roots bind it and litter slows the rain into the ground, protection the bare, churned slope simply lacks.

**40. (B)** The owl also ate warblers, so it does not starve; shrews grazed caterpillars, not leaves, so leaves are not stripped; and caterpillars were eaten by, not dependent on, shrews. Removing shrews relieves warbler eggs (warblers may rise) and caterpillars (one fewer predator), and pushes the owl toward warblers.

**41. (C)** A forest is not a single-species place, its living diversity is not merely a product of soil minerals, and its animals could not thrive on bare cleared land. The forest's layered, varied habitats house countless interdependent species, so protecting the forest protects them all.

**42. (D)** The leopard had three prey, so halving one does not starve it; deer did not control hares; and hares and birds did not eat deer. With fewer deer the leopard hunts hares and birds more (pressing them) and grass formerly grazed by deer is spared somewhat, a typical predator-switching ripple.

**43. (D)** Plants cannot create mineral nutrients from sunlight, the forest is described as needing little outside input so rain-import is not the basis, and daily sea imports by animals is invented. The forest sustains itself because decomposers keep recycling the same nutrients from dead matter back into the soil for reuse.

**44. (B)** Carbon taken from the air is not simply repacked into the air between trees, nor mainly carried off in streams, nor lodged in animal fur. Photosynthesis builds it into wood, leaves and, through litter, into soil humus, which is where a growing forest holds most of its captured carbon.

**45. (A)** A burnt patch is neither doomed to stay bare nor able to become a full forest overnight, and it is not permanently locked as grassland. Regrowth proceeds in stages over years, herbs to shrubs to trees, as soil and community rebuild, showing the forest's slow, dynamic recovery.

**46. (B)** Losing pollinating bees does affect seed-set, plants do not gain by keeping nectar (they need the pollination), and snakes gain no benefit from fewer bees. The bees' loss starves birds (decline), reaches snakes a step later, and cuts pollination so the plants set fewer seeds, a ripple running both up and down.

**47. (D)** Forests do not convert rain to oxygen, do not seal the soil (litter and roots open it), and do not feed a well by sending water to the sea. They recharge groundwater by letting rain percolate slowly into the ground; cleared land sheds rain too fast to recharge, so the water table and well fall.

**48. (C)** Extra dung does not instantly create forest, forest species clearly do affect one another, and deer do not hunt tigers. Without the tiger the deer multiply and strip the young plants by overgrazing, hampering the forest's renewal, the classic many-step ripple from removing one keystone species.

**49. (D)** Least light does not force least life, the floor is genuinely the darkest layer, and floor organisms do not make their own light. The dead matter and moisture that collect on the floor provide abundant food and shelter for decomposers and ground dwellers, so life is dense there despite the dimness.

**50. (C)** The heron does not starve because the litter chain still feeds the fish; the fish is not wholly unaffected, since one of its two food routes is hit; and algae do not produce leaf litter. Killing the algae starves tadpoles (decline) but leaves the fish a second, litter-fed food source, so the upper levels are squeezed, not eliminated.

**51. (A)** It is not about a geometrically equal circle, nor a three-organism limit, and nutrients are reused rather than destroyed-and-remade. The nutrient loop is a cycle because the same nutrients keep returning to the soil for reuse, whereas the food chain runs one way with energy progressively lost.

**52. (C)** The paired contrast is not chance, the differences are not limited to flooding, and the cleared hill is plainly worse off. One cause, keeping versus losing the forest, jointly governs water flow, local climate and soil fertility, so the forested village gains all these services together while the cleared one loses them.

**53. (C)** Plastic and petrol are manufactured or fossil products, not gathered from a living forest, so any list containing them is wrong. Honey, gum, medicinal herbs and timber all come directly from forest plants and animals, making them the genuine forest products.

**54. (A)** Snakes did not protect grass, the eagle ate snakes rather than competing with them for frogs, and mid-chain removals do ripple outward. Killing snakes starves the eagle (decline) and releases frogs to rise, which then press grasshoppers down and ease the grazing pressure on the grass, ripples in both directions.

**55. (D)** Reducing the forest to saleable wood, to wildlife only, or to a mere stand of trees each ignores most of what it does. A forest is a lifeline because it delivers oxygen, nutrient recycling, soil and water protection, climate balance and shelter for many species, all together.

**56. (C)** A single tree's branchy top is its crown, not a 'forest crown,' and the meeting of many crowns overhead is the canopy, not the understorey or floor. Meera describes the canopy formed by many interlocking crowns; Vikram describes one tree's crown.

**57. (B)** Foxes did not keep rabbits off the grass nor feed the owl or the voles. Losing foxes frees rabbits (their only predator gone, so they rise) and removes a competitor for voles, leaving the owl more vole prey, a two-step ripple in two directions.

**58. (D)** A single-species stand, the mere presence of one predator, or a grass carpet each miss the essence of a forest. The decisive evidence is a diverse, layered, self-renewing community of plants, animals and microbes persisting over the years, which is what makes a place a forest.

**59. (A)** A tiger is a carnivore not a producer, grass is a producer not a carnivore, and a fungus is a decomposer not a herbivore. Only the mushroom-as-decomposer pairing is right, since the fungus breaks dead matter into humus.

**60. (B)** Removing predators does not improve the forest, decomposers are essential, and the two unrelated losses do not cancel. Losing the predator lets herbivores overgraze the plants while losing decomposers stalls nutrient recycling and starves the soil, so the forest suffers on both fronts together.
