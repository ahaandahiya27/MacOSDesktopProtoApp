Instructions: Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. This is the hardest tier: expect multi-step chains that combine isosceles base-angle reasoning with Pythagoras, congruence criteria layered on top of CPCT, self-correspondence arguments, and symmetry counts woven into angle work. The recurring traps are SSA disguised as SAS, AAA or equal-area/equal-perimeter mistaken for congruence, the wrong base angle in nested isosceles figures, mixing up the order of rotational symmetry with the count of mirror lines, and reading a correspondence's letter order incorrectly. Worked Solutions

1. (B) Drop the altitude from the apex A to the base; because AB = AC, its foot M is the midpoint of BC, so BM = 30/2 = 15 cm and angle AMB = 90°. In right triangle ABM, AM = √(AB² − BM²) = √(17² − 15²) = √(289 − 225) = √64 = 8 cm. Since P is on BC with BP = 9 cm and BM = 15 cm, P lies between B and M with PM = BM − BP = 15 − 9 = 6 cm, and AM is perpendicular to BC so triangle AMP is right-angled at M. Hence AP = √(AM² + PM²) = √(8² + 6²) = √(64 + 36) = √100 = 10 cm. The value 8 cm is only the altitude AM itself and ignores the horizontal offset PM; √145 cm comes from using BP = 9 directly as the horizontal leg (√(8² + 9²)) instead of the correct PM = 6; 12 cm comes from mis-taking the altitude foot as lying 9 cm from B and then mis-adding, and in any case the shortest distance from A to a point on BC is the altitude 8 cm, so AP just past the foot is only slightly more than 8.

2. (A) Angle B = 90° sits opposite side AC, so AC is the hypotenuse (12 cm) and AB is a leg (9 cm). Equal right angle, equal hypotenuse and one equal leg is exactly RHS, which is a valid criterion, so they are congruent. The SSA-objection fails because RHS is precisely the safe special case of side-side-right-angle. SAS is wrong because the right angle at B is NOT included between AB and AC (AC does not start at B). 'Only similar' ignores that all three pairs of parts are equal in length.

3. (A) Base angles: angle ABC = angle ACB = (180−36)/2 = 72°. BP bisects angle B, so angle ABP = angle PBC = 36° each. (Indeed BP = BC is consistent: triangle BPC has angle PBC = 36° and angle BCP = 72°, so angle BPC = 72°, making BP = BC.) angle ABP = 36°. The trap answer 72° is the whole base angle (not the bisected half); 18° halves the apex instead of the base angle; 24° splits 72° into three.

4. (B) Composing two reflections in lines that meet at angle θ gives a rotation through 2θ; perpendicular lines (θ = 90°) compose to a 180° rotation, forcing at least order 2. It is not forced to order 4 — that would need a third pair of mirror lines. A non-square rectangle is exactly such a figure (2 mirror lines, order 2), so it need NOT be a square, and any regular polygon with 5+ sides has 5+ mirror lines, contradicting 'exactly two'. Order 1 is wrong because the 180° rotation is genuine.

5. (C) angle B = angle C makes AB = AC = 13 cm (isosceles), and the altitude from the apex to the base lands at the midpoint, with right angle at M. In right triangle ABM, AM² = AB² − BM² = 169 − 25 = 144, so AM = 12 cm. The 8 comes from 13 − 5; 18 from 13 + 5; √194 from wrongly adding 169 + 25.

6. (C) Chaining the correspondences A↔D↔P, B↔E↔Q, C↔F↔R. So angle A = angle D = angle P = 50°, and angle Q = angle E = angle B = 70°. The angle sum gives angle C = 180 − 50 − 70 = 60°. Picking 50° or 70° just echoes a given angle for the wrong vertex; 'cannot be determined' ignores that the correspondences pin down both other angles.

7. (A) Base angles are (180−100)/2 = 40° each. The bisectors halve them to 20° each, so in triangle BIC the angles at B and C are 20° each and angle BIC = 180 − 20 − 20 = 140°. (Equivalently angle BIC = 90 + A/2 = 90 + 50 = 140°.) 120° uses 90 + 30; 100° just copies the apex; 130° comes from 90 + 40.

8. (C) Because the right angle is at A, the side opposite it, BC, is the hypotenuse. We therefore have equal hypotenuse (8 cm), one equal leg (6 cm) and the right angle — that is RHS, which does guarantee congruence. SAS is wrong because the right angle at A is included between AB and AC, not between AB and BC. It is not the unsafe SSA pattern precisely because the equal angle is the right angle (RHS is the legitimate special case). 'Only similar' is wrong since the third side is then forced equal too.

9. (B) A regular n-gon has smallest rotation 360°/n, so 360/n = 24 gives n = 15. A regular polygon has exactly n lines of symmetry, hence 15. Answer 24 confuses the angle with a count; 12 halves n; 30 doubles it.

10. (A) The self-correspondence A↔C, B↔B, C↔A says, by CPCT, side BA = side BC and angle A = angle C. That makes the triangle isosceles with the apex at B; nothing forces all three sides equal, so it need not be equilateral. The apex is at B (the fixed letter), not A, so 'AB = AC' is the wrong pairing. No right angle is implied.

11. (D) M and N are midpoints of two sides, so MN is the midsegment parallel to the third side AC and equal to half of it: MN = AC/2 = 10/2 = 5 cm. The distractor 6 = BC/2 uses the wrong side; 8 is the altitude AM (√(100−36)); 4 is an unrelated guess. (The numbers 10, 12 are there to tempt a Pythagorean detour that is not needed.)

12. (D) Triangles ABE and ACD share angle A; AB = AC and AE = AD are given, so SAS proves them congruent, giving BE = CD and angle ABE = angle ACD. Since angle ABC = angle ACB (base angles) we get angle OBC = angle OCB, so triangle OBC is isosceles with OB = OC. SSS is wrong (no third pair of sides given directly); ASA is wrong because the common angle A is included between the two equal sides, making it SAS not ASA; AAA never yields congruence and the relation OB = OC genuinely holds.

13. (C) The angles are 40°, 60°, 80° — all different, so the triangle is scalene and has 0 lines of symmetry; reflection does not change that. A reflection is a rigid motion: it preserves every length and angle, so the image is congruent to the original (congruence allows a flip). 'Only similar' wrongly treats a flip as a loss of congruence; '3 lines' is the rule for equilateral, not scalene; the orientation reversal does not break congruence.

14. (B) With angle A = 90° and AB = AC, the triangle is an isosceles right triangle; the altitude from the right angle to the hypotenuse lands at its midpoint and equals half the hypotenuse: AD = BC/2 = 8/2 = 4 cm. (In a right triangle the median to the hypotenuse equals half it, and here the altitude coincides with that median.) 4√2 wrongly applies a leg formula; 8 forgets to halve; 2 quarters it.

15. (A) Corresponding angles are equal, so 2x + 15 = 3x − 5, giving x = 20 and angle A = angle D = 55°. In triangle ABC the angle sum gives angle C = 180 − angle A − angle B = 180 − 55 − 50 = 75°. Since angle F corresponds to angle C, angle F = 75°. The trap 55° is angle A/angle C confusion (it is angle A's value, not angle C's); 50° just echoes angle B; 65° comes from a wrong angle sum.

16. (C) Equal angles give similarity with some scale factor k, so each side of the second is k times the matching side of the first; the perimeter scales by the same k. Equal perimeters force k = 1, hence equal corresponding sides and congruence. The blanket 'AAA never gives congruence' ignores the extra equal-perimeter condition. The 'differently shaped' objection contradicts the given equal angles (same shape). It holds for all triangles, not just equilateral ones.

17. (D) Since AB = AC, angle B = angle C. Let angle A = a; then angle B = angle C = 2a. Sum: a + 2a + 2a = 180, so 5a = 180, a = 36°, and angle C = 2a = 72°. The trap 36° is angle A (the apex), not angle C; 45° and 54° come from wrong split assumptions.

18. (C) N maps onto itself under a 180° turn (order-2 rotational symmetry) yet has neither a vertical nor horizontal mirror line — its diagonal stroke breaks both. H and X each have BOTH a vertical and horizontal line of symmetry (so they do have mirror lines), and T has only a vertical mirror line with no order-2 rotation. So N is the unique fit.

19. (D) BD = DC means D is the midpoint of BC, and AD = BD = DC means D is equidistant from A, B, C, so D is the circumcentre lying on BC — i.e. BC is a diameter and A is on the circle, making angle BAC the angle in a semicircle = 90°. More elementarily: triangles ABD and ACD are isosceles, so angle ABD = angle BAD and angle ACD = angle CAD; their sum 2(angle BAD + angle CAD) = 180° gives angle BAC = angle BAD + angle CAD = 90°. 60° assumes equilateral; 45° halves wrongly; it is fully determined.

20. (D) Two angles and the side BETWEEN them are equal: BC lies between angle B and angle C, the textbook ASA pattern, which proves congruence. SAS requires two SIDES and the included angle, but only one side is given here, so calling it SAS is wrong. It is not SSA (no two sides given) and not mere AAA (the equal side BC fixes the size).
