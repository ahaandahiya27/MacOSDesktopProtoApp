Game logic: the NEXT verse opens with the LAST heard अक्षर (full consonant+vowel syllable, e.g. ते not त्), never the last bare consonant or the previous opener; a player who ends on a long vowel (...या) hands over या not य. Sandhi at seams: श्लोक+अन्त्य is savarṇa-dīrgha (अ+अ→आ), निः+गन्ध and साधोः+विपरीतम् are visarga→र् before voiced sounds — distinct mechanisms, do not conflate. The तादर्थ्ये चतुर्थी -आय endings (विवादाय/दानाय/रक्षणाय) are DATIVE OF PURPOSE, not genitive/locative, and they line up विद्या-धन-शक्ति → विवाद-मद-परिपीडन (wicked) AGAINST ज्ञान-दान-रक्षण (good) — same gift, विपरीत use; never swap the triple's order. परेषाम् = 'of OTHERS' (gen. pl. of पर). खल = a fault of WILL (moral), मूर्ख = a fault of intellect; विद्या = studied learning, ज्ञान = realised insight; worth rests on शीलम् (=वृत्तम्/आचरणम्), so the शीलम्-less are भारभूताः (=भारकराः). किंशुक = the bright but SCENTLESS Flame of the Forest; the simile dies if the scentlessness is dropped. क्रीडाम वयम् is a cohortative 'let us play' (1st-person plural, आत्मनेपद-sense exhortation), not a statement or command to others. Worked Solutions

1. (C) The game hands over the last whole syllable that is heard. In शोभन्ते that syllable is the consonant त् married to its vowel ए, i.e. ते — Sanskrit has no bare word-final consonants here, the opening must echo the first syllable of the next verse not the last word's first syllable, and ते is a genuine syllable of the verb, not a detachable ending.

2. (D) The handed-over syllable is the full final syllable द्या, not just the consonant व. विद्यार्थी opens on द्या (vi-dyā-...), reproducing that exact syllable, so it is legal. Merely sharing an initial व is insufficient — the vowel ā is part of the carried syllable — and there is no rule forbidding reuse of a stem.

3. (B) The difficulty is one of supply: a frequent, easily-pronounced open syllable such as नī is the opening of many known verses, while an awkward final cluster is the opening of very few, so finding a legal continuation is harder. Clusters are not banned, the last full syllable (not merely its vowel) is what must be matched, and a hard ending does not relax the rule to a free choice.

4. (D) क्रीडाम is the first-person PLURAL exhortative agreeing with वयम्; to make it first-person singular both the person-number of the verb and the pronoun must change together, giving क्रीडानि अहम्. The verb cannot stay plural with a singular pronoun, a third-person command (क्रीडतु) means 'let HIM play', and क्रीडसि is an ordinary 'you play', not an exhortation.

5. (D) क्रीडाम is a let-mood (exhortative/leṭ-type) form, an invitation that includes the speaker; क्रीडामः merely reports that 'we play'. The difference is mood, not tense, and an exhortation with वयम् includes the speaker rather than commanding others, nor does it pose a question.

6. (C) श्लोक ends in अ and अन्त्य begins in अ, so two like vowels lengthen to आ — a vowel sandhi. निः ends in a visarga which, standing before the voiced consonant ग, turns to र् — a visarga sandhi. Two distinct mechanisms; they are not both of one type and Sanskrit junctions are governed by several rules, not one.

7. (C) The rule is conditioned by voicing: a visarga before a voiced consonant (व, ग, and the like) becomes र्. It is precisely NOT before voiceless consonants (where it behaves differently), it has nothing to do with attraction to र, and before a vowel a visarga's behaviour is again different — the common trigger here is voicing.

8. (C) The र् is the regular, correct output of visarga sandhi at the seam साधोः + विपरीतम्, the visarga changing to र् before the voiced व. Resolved sandhi is normal in continuous text, साधोः is not exempt from sandhi, and no doubling of र is involved.

9. (B) हीन ('devoid of') takes its complement in the instrumental/separation sense: विद्यया हीनः, 'lacking by means of / separated from knowledge'. It is a dependent (तत्पुरुष) compound, not a coordinate द्वन्द्व, not a comparative 'exceeding', and हीन does not govern an accusative object here.

10. (C) In an उपमा the उपमेय is the subject under discussion — here the विद्याहीनाः — and the उपमान is what they are held up against, the किंशुकाः. The flowers are the yardstick, not the thing measured; the verb and the particle इव are not the compared item.

11. (A) An उपमा turns on the property common to both terms. Here it is 'splendid outside, empty inside': the किंशुक blazes with colour yet has no scent, the unlearned looks fine yet lacks the inner worth knowledge gives. It is the abstract pattern, not literal redness or a forest habitat, and the whole point is the LACK of scent/worth, not its presence.

12. (D) The verse criticises a LACK. If the flower is described as wholly lovely with no missing quality, then the person it stands for would also seem to lack nothing, and the criticism evaporates. The error is not a reversal, not an identification of knowledge with scent, and not a literal transformation — it is the loss of the 'something missing' that the simile needs.

13. (A) निर्- (from निः) here carries the privative sense 'without', yielding निर्गन्ध = 'lacking scent'. It is not an intensifier ('very fragrant'), not a marker of fragrance pouring out, and not an affirmation — all of which would make the flower fragrant, contradicting the simile.

14. (A) The same privative निः- that strips fragrance from गन्ध strips fruit/result from फल, giving 'fruitless'. (The visarga here meets a voiceless फ and surfaces as ष्, but the privative meaning is identical.) It does not mean abundance, imminence, or excellence of fruit.

15. (C) The -आय ending here is the dative of purpose (तादर्थ्ये चतुर्थी): the wicked person's knowledge, wealth and power exist 'for the sake of' quarrel, pride and oppression. They are not genitives ('of'), not locatives ('in'), and not nominative subjects — the gifts are the subjects, these three are their purposes.

16. (A) The wicked turn knowledge to quarrelling, wealth to arrogant pride, and power to the oppression of others — विद्या→विवाद, धन→मद, शक्ति→परिपीडन, in that order. The other rotations scramble the pairing, and दान/रक्षण/ज्ञान are the GOOD uses, not the wicked ones.

17. (C) Each gift's wicked and good use must be matched within the SAME gift: knowledge → quarrel vs sharing; wealth → pride vs giving; power → oppression vs protection. The other pairings cross gifts, and a single good use cannot answer all three different gifts.

18. (C) पर means 'other', and परेषाम् is its genitive PLURAL, 'of others'; the verse says power becomes the oppression OF OTHERS. It is not self-directed, not singular ('one rival'), and पर here is the ordinary 'other', not a name of the Supreme.

19. (B) दानाय and सञ्चयाय are datives of purpose, exactly like the verse's विवादाय/दानाय, so the line contrasts two aims of wealth: giving versus hoarding. They are not genitives 'of', not locatives 'in', and not accusative objects.

20. (A) Turning knowledge to quarrel or power to oppression is a deliberate, ill-willed act — the signature of the खल (a moral failing of will). The मूर्ख's problem is lack of wisdom, not malice; the मूर्ख may well possess gifts, खल and मूर्ख are not synonyms, and the मूर्ख is certainly not the good person.

21. (D) He has विद्या in abundance, so he is no मूर्ख; but he chooses to use it to harm, the mark of the खल, and because he lacks शीलम् his learning serves quarrel and his worth is low. Bad conduct is not the same as ignorance, learning does not auto-confer character, and विद्या misused does not confer worth.

22. (A) A's shortfall is purely intellectual with no ill will — a मूर्ख; B knowingly uses his eloquence to harm — a खल. The learned can certainly be wicked (that is exactly the खल), and the two faults are distinct kinds (will vs intellect), so they are not both of one label.

23. (B) विद्या is studied, acquired learning; ज्ञान is direct, realised insight — so one can exist without the other, exactly as the sage-versus-scholar case shows. Degrees do not guarantee insight, ज्ञान is not confined to a school, and the two terms are not interchangeable.

24. (A) विद्या ends in आ and आलय begins in आ; like vowels merge to a single long आ (savarṇa-dīrgha), and the dependent compound means 'abode (आलय) of learning (विद्या)', i.e. a school. It is not guṇa (अ+इ), there IS sandhi, and it is a place-noun तत्पुरुष, not a द्वन्द्व list or an अव्ययीभाव.

25. (C) The possessive -वत्/-विन् suffix on तेजस् with the feminine -ई gives तेजस्विनी, 'a woman full of lustre', exactly parallel to यशस्विनी. अतेजस् and तेजःहीना mean the OPPOSITE (lacking lustre), and a dative तेजाय means 'for lustre', not 'possessing it'.

26. (B) लक्ष्मी is a feminine ī-stem (a goddess-name), declined with feminine endings and concord, the visarga in लक्ष्मीः being merely the nominative-singular ending — not a gender marker. Visarga-final words are not all masculine, abstractions are not all neuter, and the gender follows the noun's class, not a verbal origin.

27. (D) विद्याहीनाः is nominative plural and शोभन्ते is third-person plural present, so they agree as 3rd-person plural — 'they (the unlearned) do not shine'. There is no first-person वयम् here, the plural is correct (not a singular error), and it is a statement about them, not an address to 'you'.

28. (D) शोभन्ते means the subjects themselves shine or look beautiful — an intransitive state, needing no object; the unlearned just do not shine. It is not making the flowers shine (transitive), not 'are shone upon' (passive), and not causing others to shine (causative).

29. (C) भारभूत = 'having become a burden' and भारकर = 'burden-making'; both pick out the same person — one who is, or imposes, a burden. भूत here is the past participle 'become' (not 'ghost' or 'earth') and कर is the agentive 'making' (not 'hand' or 'tax'), so the glosses genuinely agree.

30. (B) Worth, for the chapter, rests on शीलम्; a man without it is a भारभूतः regardless of wealth, looks or birth. The other options reverse the doctrine — wealth/looks/birth do NOT outweigh character, high birth does not guarantee character, and handsomeness is exactly the empty 'colour without scent' the किंशुक warns against.

31. (D) Where wealth and looks are EQUAL, they cannot be what distinguishes the two; only the differing quality — शीलम् — can. Equal traits cannot 'tip' anything, and birth is not even mentioned as differing in this case.

32. (D) The verse says the knowledge-less do not truly shine despite looks — i.e. 'no विद्या ⇒ looks alone do not make one shine'. The other conditionals invert or misstate it: beauty does not remove the need for knowledge, knowledge does not guarantee beauty, and the verse never says lacking beauty cancels the value of knowledge.

33. (D) Possessing विद्या but using it for quarrel marks the खल, and lacking शीलम् he is of low worth — each link holds. The others break: merely having विद्या does not make one a साधु if it is misused, wealth and looks do not produce शीलम्, and being scentless-like-a-किंशुक signals EMPTINESS, not wisdom.

34. (A) The verse is built on identical-source-but-opposite-use; दान (giving) and मद (pride) both spring from धन yet pull in contrary directions, which refutes 'treated alike'. The premise that both stem from धन is actually correct (so denying it is wrong), and neither belongs to विद्या or शक्ति.

35. (A) The किंशुक verse condemns splendour-without-substance, the शीलम् verse roots worth in character; together they make inner quality and right use decisive. They do not exalt outward splendour, do not restrict the lesson to the unattractive, and neither verse praises wealth or beauty as the ideal.

36. (C) The handed-over unit is the last pronounceable syllable, मī; the visarga ः is merely the nominative ending and cannot itself start a word. The opener matches the END of the previous verse (not its first syllable ल), and the medial cluster क्ष् is not the final syllable.

37. (C) क्रीडाम ends in the syllable म, so a legal continuation must open on म, which मधुरम् does. Sharing a root is irrelevant, an internal syllable (ड) is not the final one, and ending on a verb does not free the next player from the rule.

38. (A) An akṣara is a syllable — a consonant plus vowel — and verses open on syllables, so the rule matches syllables to ensure the next opener is a real, pronounceable verse-start. Letters are pronounceable in principle, the last letter is not always a vowel, and varṣa/akṣara are related concepts, not random alternatives.

39. (D) Being stuck is a supply problem: the rule demands a त्व-opening verse, and the player knows only vowel-initial ones, none of which qualifies. Vowel-initial verses are perfectly legal, a vowel cannot be substituted for the required consonant-syllable, and the previous player's legal verse does not end the game.

40. (B) The rule only ever looks at the LAST syllable of the verse just heard; if that is again मा, P3 must again start on मा. A repeat does not reset the chain, does not switch to first-syllables, and does not end the round — repetition of a syllable is allowed.

41. (A) To play legally a player must correctly hear and reproduce the precise final syllable (ते not त, या not य), which trains phonetic accuracy. The game rewards correctness over speed, does not require translation, and uses recalled verses rather than freshly composed ones.

42. (B) Antyakshari is an oral game; players match the syllable as it is heard in recitation. The pre-sandhi spelling is not the operative authority, the choice is not left to preference, and a sandhi-altered ending does not void the turn — it simply fixes which sound to match.

43. (C) The किंशुक is a WARNING, not a model, and it is precisely scent-LESS — so the claim that its fragrance proves inner worth is doubly false. The other three statements accurately report the chapter: knowledge→quarrel in the wicked, शीलम् glossed as conduct, and विपरीतम् marking the opposite uses.

44. (D) Both words use a possessive suffix on a quality-stem (रूप, यशस्) to mean 'having' that quality — endowed with beauty / fame. They are not privatives ('without'), not datives ('for'), and not comparatives ('more than').

45. (B) खलस्य and साधोः are genitives identifying whose handling of the gifts is in view — the wicked's use versus the good's. They are not datives ('to'), not ablatives ('from'), and not vocatives ('O ...!').

46. (B) विपरीतम् is the hinge that flips each gift's use between wicked and good; replacing it with समानम् ('same') would collapse the contrast and falsely equate the two. समान does not intensify a contrast (it erases it), it is the opposite of विपरीत, and the subject remains people, not flowers.

47. (B) वि- in विपरीत conveys turning-the-opposite-way (reversal), whereas निर्- in निर्गन्ध conveys lack (privation). They are not both privative, not both intensive, and their senses are as stated, not swapped.

48. (C) Using power to crush and humiliate others is परिपीडन — power's wicked use — marking him a खल. It is not protection (रक्षण), the fault is deliberate ill-will (not mere foolishness), and the किंशुक image is about empty beauty, not about doing harm.

49. (A) Giving wealth to help others is दान, wealth's good use, evidencing शीलम्. Quiet generosity is the opposite of मद (pride), the verse explicitly treats wealth (धन→दान/मद), and giving makes one worthy, not a burden.

50. (B) Worth needs शीलम् and right use; a विद्वान् who lacks character or turns learning to quarrel remains a burden despite his learning. Learning does not guarantee worth, it does not destroy character, and the chapter roots worth in character, not wealth.

51. (D) Same gift (wealth), विपरीत use: दान is other-directed giving, मद is self-directed pride — the difference is intent and direction, not quantity. Amount, source, and 'colour' are all beside the verse's moral point.

52. (D) Across both verses the deciding factor is inner quality plus right use, not the bare possession of gifts. The chapter explicitly denies that more gifts, or beauty-and-wealth alone, or even misused knowledge, confer worth.

53. (D) Both halves of the claim are wrong: किंशुक is a particular tree whose scentlessness is the whole point of the simile, and खल (wickedness of will) is not the same as मूर्ख (foolishness of intellect). The correction must fix BOTH errors, which only this option does.

54. (B) इव is an indeclinable comparison-particle; it does not assign a case, so किंशुकाः simply matches विद्याहीनाः in nominative plural. इव governs neither an accusative object, nor an instrumental of means, nor a genitive.

55. (A) Because identical gifts produce opposite outcomes depending on the holder, the gifts themselves are neutral and the user's character/intent decides their worth. No gift is intrinsically evil or good, the wicked really do possess the gifts (they misuse them), and gifts do not automatically reform their holders.

56. (C) Power used to protect is रक्षण, the good use, marking him a साधु. Merely possessing power is not oppression, the protective use is the opposite of being a burden, and protection of the weak is substantive good, not empty show.

57. (A) The chapter ranks inner goods — character and rightly-used knowledge — above outward ones (beauty, wealth, birth). It does not put wealth/looks or birth on top, and the किंशुक's colour is criticised, not celebrated, so beauty cannot head the list.

58. (A) The chapter grounds worth in well-used knowledge plus character, expressly subordinating beauty, wealth, birth and fame. The alternatives each elevate exactly the outward goods the chapter demotes.

59. (B) The handed-over syllable या carries a long आ, so a legal opener must reproduce यā, not the short या-of-यमुना (ya). Vowel length IS part of the syllable that must be matched, a long vowel is not freely interchangeable with its short counterpart, and proper names are not barred — the flaw is purely the mismatched vowel-length.

60. (B) क्रीडाम agrees with वयम् as first-person plural, a self-inclusive 'let us play' that draws speaker and audience into the game — unlike क्रीडन्तु, a third-person exhortation 'let them play' that excludes the speaker. It is neither third person (which क्रीडन्तु is), nor second person, nor singular.
