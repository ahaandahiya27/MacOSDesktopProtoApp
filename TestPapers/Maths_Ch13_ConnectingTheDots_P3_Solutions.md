## Worked Solutions

**1. (C)** Six tests at mean 72 give a total of 6 x 72 = 432. Seven tests at mean 74 give a total of 7 x 74 = 518. The seventh score is 518 - 432 = 86. Picking the new mean 74 ignores that the seventh score must pull the average up. The gap 74 - 72 = 2 is only the change in mean, not the score, and 76 = 72 + 4 also confuses the rise in mean with the actual mark.

**2. (C)** Every one of the 5 members ages by 4 years, so the total rises by 5 x 4 = 20 years and the mean rises by 20 ÷ 5 = 4. New mean = 28 + 4 = 32. The mean does not stay 28 because time passes for all. Adding 4 x 5 = 20 to the mean itself gives 48, which wrongly adds the whole extra total to one average. 30 would be only a 2-year increase.

**3. (C)** Originally 9 appears three times and 4 twice. Adding another 4 makes 4 appear three times too, so 4 and 9 are tied as the most frequent — the data is bimodal. Choosing 4 only ignores that 9 still appears three times; choosing 9 only ignores the new third 4. The mode is about frequency, not the middle position, so 7 (the median) is not the mode.

**4. (B)** Original mean = (3+5+6+8+10) ÷ 5 = 32 ÷ 5 = 6.4. New mean = (3+5+6+8+50) ÷ 5 = 72 ÷ 5 = 14.4, an increase of 8. The sorted lists are 3,5,6,8,10 and 3,5,6,8,50; the middle (3rd) value is 6 in both, so the median is unchanged. The median resists a single extreme value, so it does not jump with the mean, and replacing a value certainly does change the mean.

**5. (D)** Total of all 12 = 12 x 9 = 108. First 7 total = 7 x 8 = 56; last 4 total = 4 x 11 = 44. Those two groups cover 11 numbers totalling 56 + 44 = 100, so the one leftover number = 108 - 100 = 8. Answer 9 just echoes the overall mean. 12 and 20 come from miscounting how many numbers each group covers.

**6. (C)** Five numbers with mean 18 have a total of 5 x 18 = 90. The four known numbers add to 12 + 25 + 19 + 14 = 70, so the fifth is 90 - 70 = 20. Picking 18 just copies the mean; 70 is the sum of the four known numbers, not the missing one; 90 is the required total of all five, not a single value.

**7. (A)** Range = highest - lowest, so highest = range + lowest = 64 + 6 = 70. Taking 64 alone forgets to add back the lowest value. 58 = 64 - 6 subtracts instead of adds. 6 is the lowest score, not the highest.

**8. (B)** With 8 values the median is the average of the 4th and 5th values, p and q, so (p + q) ÷ 2 = 15 means p + q = 30. The pair 14 and 16 sums to 30 and keeps order (11 < 14 < 16 < 20), so it works. 13 and 18 sum to 31, giving median 15.5, not 15. The median is not forced to be 15, 15; many pairs summing to 30 work. It uses two middle values, not the 4th alone.

**9. (A)** Total of 9 boxes = 9 x 40 = 360 kg. Removing the 60 kg box leaves 360 - 60 = 300 kg over 8 boxes, so the mean is 300 ÷ 8 = 37.5 kg. The mean does not stay 40 because the box removed was above average, pulling the average down. 20 = 60 ÷ 3 is a stray division and 45 wrongly raises the mean.

**10. (D)** Class A total = 25 x 70 = 1750; Class B total = 15 x 78 = 1170. Combined total = 2920 over 40 students, mean = 2920 ÷ 40 = 73. The simple average of 70 and 78 is 74, but that wrongly ignores the larger size of Class A, which weights the result toward 70. Adding the two means gives 148, and 76 over-weights Class B.

**11. (B)** Initially 12 appears four times and 15 appears twice. Adding two more 15s makes 15 appear four times as well, tying 12 and 15 as the most frequent values, so the data is bimodal. Choosing 12 only ignores the two added 15s; choosing 15 only ignores that 12 still appears four times; the mode is about frequency of values, not the mean.

**12. (C)** Original mean = 42 ÷ 6 = 7. New mean = (42 - 12 + 120) ÷ 6 = 150 ÷ 6 = 25, an increase of 18. The median of six values averages the 3rd and 4th (6 and 8), giving 7 both before and after, so it is unchanged. The median is unaffected because only the largest value moved; the mean rose by the extra 108 spread over 6 values = 18, not the full 108.

**13. (D)** Five numbers with mean 6 have total 5 x 6 = 30. The four known values add to 0 + 6 + 6 + 8 = 20, so x = 30 - 20 = 10. Choosing 6 just copies the mean; 30 is the required total of all five; 20 is the sum of the four known numbers, not the missing fifth.

**14. (A)** Mean = total ÷ count, so count = total ÷ mean = 135 ÷ 15 = 9. Choosing 15 confuses the count with the mean; 135 is the total, not the count; 120 = 135 - 15 wrongly subtracts.

**15. (B)** X totals 40+50+30 = 120; Y totals 20+70+30 = 120 — equal. X's scores range from 30 to 50, a range of 20; Y's range from 20 to 70, a range of 50, so X is the more consistent (less spread) batter. Winning more matches does not raise the season total here, the 70 is a sign of more spread not less, and the ranges are clearly different.

**16. (B)** With seven values the median is the 4th value, which is m, so m = 12 (and 9 < 12 < 15 keeps the order). Choosing 9 or 15 takes a neighbour of the middle, not the middle itself; 11 is a plausible-looking but unjustified guess for the 4th position.

**17. (A)** The recorded total = 10 x 50 = 500. The entry was 50 too low (75 - 25 = 50), so the true total = 500 + 50 = 550 and the corrected mean = 550 ÷ 10 = 55. The mean cannot stay 50 once a value is corrected upward. 60 adds the full 100 difference wrongly; 52 underestimates the correction (the error of 50 spread over 10 is 5, giving 55, not 2).

**18. (D)** Equal means with the same student count give equal totals, so neither has a higher total. A smaller range means the values are bunched closer together, so Section P (range 12) is more consistent than Section Q (range 48). Equal means do not imply identical marks — the same average can arise from very different spreads.

**19. (B)** The value 15 appears twice (Mar and Apr), so it is the mode. Sorting gives 12, 15, 15, 18; the median averages the two middle values (15 and 15) = 15. So 15 is both. The tallest bar (18) is the maximum, not the median or mode; 16.5 is the mean; 12 is the minimum.

**20. (D)** Group A total = 4 x 10 = 40; group B total = 6 x 20 = 120. Combined = 160 over 10 numbers = 16. The plain average of 10 and 20 is 15, but B has more numbers so the combined mean leans toward 20. 30 adds the means; 200 is the combined total, not the mean.

**21. (A)** The value 1 appears five times — more than any other — so the mode is 1. Sorting: 0,0,1,1,1,1,1,2,2,3; with 10 values the median averages the 5th and 6th (both 1) = 1. So both are 1. The median is 1, not 1.5, because the 5th and 6th values are both 1; mode is about frequency, so 0 (two times) and 3 (the largest) are not the mode.

**22. (D)** Six numbers total 6 x 25 = 150; seven numbers total 7 x 27 = 189. The 7th number = 189 - 150 = 39. Picking 27 copies the new mean; 29 wrongly assumes the score equals the mean plus the count of extra; 2 is just the change in mean.

**23. (A)** Each unit represents 5 litres, so 7.4 units = 7.4 x 5 = 37 litres. Reading the height as the answer (7.4) ignores the scale; 12.4 = 7.4 + 5 adds the scale instead of multiplying; 74 multiplies by 10 rather than 5.

**24. (A)** The median of five numbers is the 3rd value when sorted. Only the two smallest and the two largest were altered; the middle value was untouched and the order is preserved, so the median stays 11. Subtracting 3 (giving 8) or adding 3 (giving 14) wrongly changes the middle value, and there is no fractional shift to give 11.5.

**25. (C)** Adding a value equal to the current mean does not change the mean: total goes from 5 x 14 = 70 to 70 + 14 = 84 over 6 numbers, and 84 ÷ 6 = 14. The mean only moves if the added value differs from it. 16.8 = 84 ÷ 5 uses the wrong count; the other options misapply the change.

**26. (C)** Because the axis begins at 80, the visible bar heights measure only the part above 80 (4 units vs 2 units), making A look much taller. The true values 84 and 82 differ by only 2, so the brands sold almost the same. A did not sell three times as much, B clearly sold 82, and the graph can be read once the axis start is noticed.

**27. (B)** The two groups overlap at c. Sum of first group = 60, sum of second = 90, but adding them counts c twice, so a+b+c+d+e = 60 + 90 - c = 150 - c. The five-number mean = (150 - c) ÷ 5, which needs c. It is not simply 25 (that would require no overlap); knowing a or d alone does not remove the double-counted c.

**28. (D)** Total of six = 6 x 30 = 180. Total of the five largest = 5 x 33 = 165. The smallest = 180 - 165 = 15. The smallest is not 30 (the overall mean) nor 33 (a group mean); 3 is just the difference of the two means, not the value removed.

**29. (C)** With nine values the median is the 5th. Increasing only the four largest (positions 6 to 9) leaves the 5th value untouched and order preserved, so the median stays 40. Adding 100 to the median (giving 140) is wrong because the median value itself was not among those changed; 65 and 60 are unfounded shifts.

**30. (A)** The value 35 appears twice, more than any other, so the mode is 35. The mean = (20+35+0+35+30+50) ÷ 6 = 170 ÷ 6 ≈ 28.3. Sorting: 0,20,30,35,35,50; median = (30+35) ÷ 2 = 32.5. Only the mode equals 35, so the other choices that claim the mean, median, or all three equal 35 are wrong.

**31. (B)** Subtracting 4 from each of the 5 numbers lowers the total by 5 x 4 = 20, so the mean drops by 20 ÷ 5 = 4, giving 16 - 4 = 12. The mean does change when every value changes. 80 = 5 x 16 is the original total; 60 = 80 - 20 is the new total, not the new mean.

**32. (A)** Dance difference = |8 - 22| = 14; Sports difference = |24 - 12| = 12. Dance has the larger gap, 14. The 36 mistakenly adds boys and girls for sports; 30 adds the dance values; 12 is the smaller (sports) gap, not the larger one.

**33. (A)** Total of seven = 7 x 10 = 70. The six known values add to 7+9+11+13+8+12 = 60, so the seventh = 70 - 60 = 10. Here the missing value happens to equal the mean. 60 is the sum of the six known numbers; 70 is the required total; 12 is just one of the listed numbers.

**34. (B)** Mode = 4 (appears twice); sorted 4,4,5,6,100 gives median = 5 (middle value); mean = 119 ÷ 5 = 23.8. So 4 < 5 < 23.8, i.e. mode < median < mean. The large outlier 100 drags the mean far above the others, so the mean cannot be smallest, and the three are clearly not equal.

**35. (C)** Total of 8 = 8 x 16 = 128; total of 11 = 11 x 18 = 198. The three added numbers total 198 - 128 = 70, so their mean = 70 ÷ 3 ≈ 23.3. The added group must average above 18 to pull the mean up. 18 is the overall new mean, 2 is just the change in mean, and 70 is the added total, not its mean.

**36. (D)** Convert heights to marks: 5x8=40, 6x8=48, 4x8=32, 5x8=40. Mean of marks = (40+48+32+40) ÷ 4 = 160 ÷ 4 = 40. Averaging the heights first (20 cm ÷ 4 = 5 cm) then forgetting to apply the scale gives 5; multiplying that 5 by 4 gives 20; 160 is the total marks, not the mean.

**37. (D)** Range = largest - smallest, so smallest = largest - range = 38 - 30 = 8. The smallest is not the mean (20), nor the range (30); 68 = 38 + 30 adds instead of subtracting.

**38. (D)** Sorted the marks are 6, 7, 8, 8, 8, 8, 9, 10. Mean = (6+7+8+8+8+8+9+10) ÷ 8 = 64 ÷ 8 = 8. With 8 values the median averages the 4th and 5th (both 8) = 8. So mean - median = 8 - 8 = 0. The difference is not a whole 1 or 0.5 (the mean and median coincide here), and 8 is the value of each, not their difference.

**39. (A)** First group total = 3 x 12 = 36; second group total = 7 x 22 = 154. Combined = 190 over 10 numbers = 19. The plain average of 12 and 22 is 17, but the seven-number group is larger so the combined mean leans toward 22. 34 adds the means; 190 is the total, not the mean.

**40. (A)** Class P = 4.5 x 6 = 27 students; Class Q = 3 x 6 = 18 students; difference = 27 - 18 = 9. Subtracting the symbol counts (4.5 - 3 = 1.5) forgets each symbol is 6 students; 6 is the value of one symbol; 45 is the total of both classes, not the difference.

**41. (B)** Changing 7 to 27 raises the total by 20, so the mean rises by 20 ÷ 10 = 2, giving 12 + 2 = 14. The mean does change because the total changed. 32 = 12 + 20 adds the whole difference to the mean; 13 adds only half the correct shift.

**42. (C)** Original six values: median = (12+14) ÷ 2 = 13. After removing 8, the five values are 10,12,14,16,18 with median = 14 (the middle value). So the median rises from 13 to 14. It does not stay 13 because the count changed from even to odd; it does not fall, since a small value was removed; 15 is not a middle value of either set.

**43. (B)** Total = 5 x 138 = 690 cm. The four known heights add to 130+135+140+145 = 550, so the unknown = 690 - 550 = 140 cm. 138 just copies the mean; 550 is the sum of the four known heights; 690 is the required total of all five.

**44. (B)** Increasing the largest value raises the total, so the mean goes up. The median of seven numbers is the 4th value; the largest value sits at position 7, so moving it further out does not touch the 4th value — the median stays 18. The mean must change because the total changed, and the median is shielded from a single extreme.

**45. (D)** 5 appears 4 times. 8 appeared once and gains three more, reaching 1 + 3 = 4. Now 5 and 8 are tied at 4 each, so the data is bimodal — both are modes. 8 alone is not the mode because its count only reaches 4, equalling 5; 5 is not the unique mode because 8 has caught up; and a tie still means there are modes, not none.

**46. (B)** Graph 1: 6 x 10 = 60 mm. Graph 2: 3 x 25 = 75 mm. The taller-looking 6 cm bar actually represents less than the 3 cm bar because the scales differ. Reading heights as values gives 6 and 3; using graph 1's scale for both gives 30; and bar height alone is meaningless without matching the scale.

**47. (A)** Mean 30 over 6 innings needs a total of 180. The five known scores add to 30+0+45+30+30 = 135, so x = 180 - 135 = 45. This keeps 30 as the most frequent value (three times), so the mode condition still holds. 30 would make the total 165, not 180; 180 is the required total; 0 gives a total of 135.

**48. (D)** Mean, median and mode measure different things, so they need not agree. A frequent low value (mode 30) can coexist with a symmetric balance that keeps the mean and median both at 50. There is no rule forcing all three to be equal, the most common value is 30 not 50, and the mode need not lie between the other two.

**49. (D)** Four numbers total 4 x 12.5 = 50; five numbers total 5 x 13 = 65. The fifth number = 65 - 50 = 15. Picking 13 copies the new mean; 0.5 is just the change in mean; 50 is the four-number total, not the fifth value.

**50. (D)** Village P = 5 x 8 = 40; Village Q = 3.5 x 8 = 28; total = 40 + 28 = 68. Adding the symbol counts (5 + 3.5 = 8.5) ignores the scale; 40 and 28 are the separate village totals, not the combined total.

**51. (C)** The mode is 16 (appears three times). With 9 values the median is the 5th, which is 16. So mode - median = 16 - 16 = 0. Taking 14 as the median (the 4th value) gives 2; mixing up counts gives 3; 16 is the value of each, not their difference.

**52. (C)** Averaging 55 and 95 to get 75 ignores that Section A has four times as many students, so the combined mean must lean toward 55. True total = 40x55 + 10x95 = 2200 + 950 = 3150 over 50 students = 63. So 75 is wrong, 50 is below both means (impossible), and 150 is the sum of the two means.

**53. (A)** Original median is 10 (middle of five). Adding a 6th value makes the median the average of the 3rd and 4th sorted values. To get median 11, those two must be 10 and 12, which happens when the new value is at least 12 (it slots above 12 or equals it, leaving 10 and 12 in the middle). A value of 11 would itself become a middle value and give median (10+11)/2 = 10.5, not 11; 22 works but is only one of many; a value of 10 or less keeps the middles at 8 and 10.

**54. (B)** Five numbers with mean 21 total 5 x 21 = 105. The four known values add to 12+18+24+30 = 84, so x = 105 - 84 = 21. Here 105 is the required total and 84 is the sum of the known four — not x itself; 20 is an unfounded guess.

**55. (C)** Cycles = 6.5 x 4 = 26 children. The bus bar is twice as tall, so 13 units, giving 13 x 4 = 52 children (or simply double 26). Doubling the unit height (6.5 x 2 = 13) without applying the scale gives 13; 26 is the cycling count, not the bus count; 104 doubles too far.

**56. (C)** Doubling every value doubles the total, so the mean also doubles: 9 x 2 = 18. The mean does not stay 9 since all values changed; adding 2 (giving 11) confuses doubling with adding; 54 = 6 x 9 is the original total, not the new mean.

**57. (C)** Mean 12 over 5 numbers needs total 60. The four known values add to 6+10+14+14 = 44, so x = 60 - 44 = 16. The mode 14 is already secured by the two 14s, and 16 keeps 14 the most frequent. 14 would make the total 58, not 60; 12 copies the mean; 60 is the required total.

**58. (B)** Park P went 120 → 150, a rise of 30; Park Q went 200 → 180, a fall of 20. So P grew while Q declined, yet Q's 2024 total (180) still exceeds P's (150). Tall bars do not mean both grew; P does not overtake Q in 2024; and Q shrank, so it did not grow faster.

**59. (D)** Sort 4, 7, 9, 9, 11 with y inserted. For the median (average of the 3rd and 4th of six values) to be 9, both middle values must be 9, which needs y >= 9. For the mode to stay uniquely 9, y must not create a rival pair — y = 11 would tie 11 with 9, and y = 9 makes 9 appear three times. The clean value satisfying both is y = 9: list becomes 4, 7, 9, 9, 9, 11, mode 9 and median (9+9)/2 = 9. With y = 8 the middles are 8 and 9, median 8.5; with y = 5 the median drops to 8; 'exactly 18' is an arbitrary single value with no special role.

**60. (A)** Visible bar heights measure only the part above 100 (4 vs 1), so X looks four times Y, but the real values 104 and 101 differ by just 3. The honest reading notes the truncated axis exaggerates a small difference. X is not four times Y, Y is 101 (not near zero), and the graph misleads rather than proves a big gap.
