Worked Solutions

1. (B) Work left to right, or multiply all the fractions first: 2/3 × 3/4 × 5/6 = (2×3×5)/(3×4×6) = 30/72 = 5/12. Then 5/12 of 48 = 48 ÷ 12 × 5 = 4 × 5 = 20. A reader who computes only 5/6 of 48 = 40 then halves carelessly might land on 24 or 16; multiplying the wrong combination of numerators gives 30. The chained 'of' simply means multiply everything, so 20 is correct.

2. (C) If 1/3 of the water is used, then 2/3 of the water remains. The water present is 2/3 of 5/8 = (2×5)/(3×8) = 10/24 = 5/12 of the full capacity. Subtracting 1/3 from 5/8 directly (a common slip) wrongly treats the 1/3 as a fraction of the whole tank rather than of the water; multiplying 1/3 × 5/8 gives the amount removed (5/24), not what remains. So 5/12 stays.

3. (D) We need x with 4/9 × x = 8/3, so x = (8/3) ÷ (4/9) = 8/3 × 9/4 = 72/12 = 6. Multiplying instead of dividing gives 8/3 × 4/9 = 32/27; flipping the wrong fraction gives 8/3 × 9/4 ... only the correct flip of the divisor 4/9 to 9/4 yields 6. So the required multiplier is 6.

4. (B) Each fraction is just below 1; the largest is the one whose 'missing piece' from 1 is smallest. The gaps are 1/8, 1/12, 1/6 and 1/10. The smallest gap is 1/12, so 11/12 is closest to 1 and therefore largest. Picking 9/10 confuses a small denominator-numerator gap; 1/10 is bigger than 1/12, so 9/10 is not the largest. Hence 11/12.

5. (D) Per loaf the flour is 2 1/4 ÷ 3 = 9/4 ÷ 3 = 9/12 = 3/4 cup. For 5 loaves: 3/4 × 5 = 15/4 = 3 3/4 cups. Multiplying 2 1/4 by 5 without dividing by 3 gives 11 1/4; stopping at the per-loaf amount gives 3/4. The two-step rate reasoning gives 3 3/4 cups.

6. (D) Each step keeps the complement: after the first, 1/2 remains; after the second, 2/3 of that = 1/3; after the third, 3/4 of that = 1/4. So 1/2 × 2/3 × 3/4 = 6/24 = 1/4 remains. Adding the fractions given away (1/2 + 1/3 + 1/4) is wrong because each acts on a smaller leftover. Multiplying the given-away fractions (1/2 × 1/3 × 1/4 = 1/24) is also wrong. The fraction kept is 1/4.

7. (B) Convert: 2 1/2 = 5/2, 1 3/5 = 8/5, 1 1/3 = 4/3. First 5/2 × 8/5 = 40/10 = 4. Then 4 ÷ 4/3 = 4 × 3/4 = 3. Multiplying by 4/3 instead of dividing gives 16/3; forgetting to convert mixed numbers throws off the rest. The value is 3.

8. (C) Divide total by bottle size: 7 1/2 ÷ 2/3 = 15/2 × 3/2 = 45/4 = 11 1/4. Only complete bottles count, so 11 bottles can be filled (with 1/4 of a bottle's worth left over). Multiplying 15/2 × 2/3 = 5 comes from forgetting to invert; rounding 11 1/4 up to 12 over-counts since the last bottle is not full. So 11 complete bottles.

9. (A) Dividing by a fraction less than 1 always makes a positive number bigger, because you are asking how many 5/6-sized pieces fit in 24, and each piece is smaller than 1. Indeed 24 ÷ 5/6 = 24 × 6/5 = 144/5 = 28.8, which exceeds 24. The trap is treating division like multiplication (which would shrink it). So the result is greater than 24.

10. (A) Use a common denominator of 40: 3/5 = 24/40, 5/8 = 25/40, 7/10 = 28/40. The smallest is 24/40 = 3/5. Judging by numerator alone (7 is biggest) or denominator alone misleads. So 3/5 is smallest.

11. (D) Convert: 2 2/5 = 12/5, 3 1/3 = 10/3. Distance = 12/5 × 10/3 = 120/15 = 8 km. Adding the mixed numbers (≈5 11/15) confuses 'per minute × minutes' with addition; multiplying only the fraction parts gives 12/25. The distance is 8 km.

12. (B) Wheat uses 1/3 of 3/4 = 1/4 acre, leaving 3/4 − 1/4 = 1/2 acre. Rice is 2/5 of that remaining 1/2 acre = 2/5 × 1/2 = 1/5 acre. Taking 2/5 of the whole 3/4 (= 3/10) ignores 'of the remaining'; taking 2/5 of the original field after wheat without recomputing the leftover slips too. Rice covers 1/5 acre.

13. (A) After spending 2/5, the fraction left is 3/5. So 3/5 of savings = 360, giving savings = 360 ÷ 3/5 = 360 × 5/3 = 600. Treating 360 as the 2/5 part gives 360 × 5/2 = 900; taking 2/5 of 360 gives 144. The savings are Rs 600.

14. (D) Do multiplication and division left to right. 5/6 × 3/10 = 15/60 = 1/4. Then 1/4 ÷ 1/4 = 1/4 × 4 = 1. Multiplying by 1/4 instead of dividing gives 1/16; mishandling the order also misleads. The value is 1.

15. (B) Common denominator 24: 5/12 = 10/24, 1/2 = 12/24, 3/8 = 9/24. Ascending: 9/24, 10/24, 12/24, i.e. 3/8 < 5/12 < 1/2. Comparing numerators or denominators alone scrambles the order. The correct order is 3/8 < 5/12 < 1/2.

16. (C) 4 1/2 ÷ 3/8 = 9/2 × 8/3 = 72/6 = 12 exactly. So 12 full pieces with nothing left over. Multiplying 9/2 × 3/8 = 27/16 (forgetting to invert) gives a meaningless count; the exact quotient 12 means no remainder. Answer: 12 pieces, 0 m left.

17. (A) 10 ÷ 2/5 = 10 × 5/2 = 25, while 10 ÷ 5/2 = 10 × 2/5 = 4; 25 > 4, so dividing by the smaller fraction gives the larger result — true. Multiplying 10 by 2/5 (a fraction below 1) shrinks it to 4, not larger. 10 ÷ 5/2 = 4, not 25. And dividing is never the same as multiplying by the same fraction. The first statement is correct.

18. (B) Rate = buckets ÷ time = 2 1/4 ÷ 3/4 = 9/4 × 4/3 = 36/12 = 3 buckets per hour. Multiplying 9/4 × 3/4 = 27/16 (no inversion) is wrong; 2 1/4 itself is the amount in only 3/4 hour, not a full hour. The rate is 3 buckets per hour.

19. (A) Express both groups as a fraction of the class out of 32. Boys = 3/8 = 12/32, and footballers among boys = 2/3 × 12/32 = 8/32. Girls = 5/8 = 20/32, and footballers among girls = 1/4 × 20/32 = 5/32. Total footballers = 8/32 + 5/32 = 13/32 of the class. Adding 2/3 + 1/4 directly (=11/12) ignores that each acts on a different group; counting only the boys' footballers gives 1/4. The fraction is 13/32.

20. (C) After selling 3/7, then 4/7 remain; selling half of that remainder leaves 1/2 × 4/7 = 2/7 of the original. So 2/7 of the start = 20, giving start = 20 ÷ 2/7 = 20 × 7/2 = 70. Treating 20 as 4/7 (the first remainder) gives 35; doubling once more gives 140. He started with 70 apples.

21. (D) Convert: 3 1/3 = 10/3, 2 2/5 = 12/5. Then 10/3 × 12/5 = 120/15 = 8, and 8 × 1/4 = 2. Forgetting the final ×1/4 leaves 8; multiplying only fraction parts (1/3 × 2/5 × 1/4) gives 1/30 which is far off. The product is 2.

22. (B) Fastest means the smallest time. Comparing 1/2, 1/3, 1/6: the smallest is 1/6 (since 1/6 < 1/3 < 1/2). A common trap is to think the bigger-looking fraction 1/2 is fastest, but more time means slower. The 1/6-hour pipe is fastest.

23. (B) Remaining before the painter starts = 1 − 4/5 = 1/5. He paints 1/4 of that 1/5 = 1/20, leaving 1/5 − 1/20 = 4/20 − 1/20 = 3/20 unpainted. Taking 1/4 of the whole wall, or forgetting to subtract from the 1/5 leftover, gives 1/4 or 1/5. Unpainted = 3/20.

24. (C) Half plus a third of the number is (1/2 + 1/3) = 5/6 of the number. So 5/6 of n = 25, giving n = 25 ÷ 5/6 = 25 × 6/5 = 30. Adding 1/2 + 1/3 as 2/5 (denominator slip) misleads; treating 25 as the answer directly is wrong. The number is 30.

25. (B) First bracket: 4/5 ÷ 2/3 = 4/5 × 3/2 = 12/10 = 6/5. Second bracket: 5/6 ÷ 1/4 = 5/6 × 4 = 20/6 = 10/3. Product = 6/5 × 10/3 = 60/15 = 4. Multiplying without inverting either divisor gives small wrong values like 8/45. The answer is 4.

26. (D) Day 1 reads 3/8 of 320 = 120, leaving 200. Day 2 reads 2/5 of 200 = 80, leaving 200 − 80 = 120 unread. Taking 2/5 of the whole 320 (=128) ignores 'of the remaining'; stopping at 200 forgets day 2. Unread pages = 120.

27. (D) Distance from 1 is |1 − 8/9| = 1/9 and |9/8 − 1| = 1/8. Since 1/9 < 1/8, the fraction 8/9 is closer to 1. Being larger than 1 (as 9/8 is) does not make it closer. So 8/9 is closer, by exactly 1/9 versus 1/8.

28. (C) We need x with 15/2 ÷ x = 2/3, so x = (15/2) ÷ (2/3) = 15/2 × 3/2 = 45/4 = 11 1/4. A reader who computes 15/2 × 2/3 = 5 has inverted the wrong fraction; the dividend stays put, the divisor side is rearranged. So x = 45/4.

29. (A) Square's area = (4/5)² = 16/25 m². Garden = 7/8 of 16/25 = (7×16)/(8×25) = 112/200 = 14/25 m². Forgetting to square the side (using 4/5 directly) gives 7/10; stopping at 16/25 forgets the 7/8 factor. The garden area is 14/25 m².

30. (C) Multiplying 18/19 by 20/21 — a factor less than 1 — must shrink it below 18/19. Likewise the product is below 20/21. Since both factors are under 1, the product is under 1, not greater. So it is less than 18/19.

31. (C) Below ground = 2/5, so above ground = 3/5 of the pole = 5 m. Pole = 5 ÷ 3/5 = 5 × 5/3 = 25/3 = 8 1/3 m. Treating 5 m as the 2/5 part gives 5 × 5/2 = 12 1/2; taking 2/5 of 5 gives 2. The pole is 25/3 m long.

32. (D) First the bracket: 4/5 × 5/8 = 20/40 = 1/2. Then 2/3 ÷ 1/2 = 2/3 × 2 = 4/3. Dividing before doing the bracket, or multiplying by 1/2 instead of dividing, gives 1/3. The value is 4/3.

33. (B) After the elder takes 3/5, the remainder is 2/5 of the jar. The younger takes 2/3 of that = 2/3 × 2/5 = 4/15 of the jar = 8 sweets. So jar = 8 ÷ 4/15 = 8 × 15/4 = 30. Treating 8 as 2/5 of the jar (=20) skips the elder's share interaction. The jar had 30 sweets.

34. (A) Compute each: 10 × 4/5 = 8; 10 ÷ 2/3 = 10 × 3/2 = 15; 10 × 5/4 = 12.5; 10 ÷ 5/4 = 10 × 4/5 = 8. The largest is 15, from 10 ÷ 2/3 — dividing by the smallest fraction enlarges the most. Multiplying by 5/4 only reaches 12.5, and the others are 8. The largest value is 15 from 10 ÷ 2/3.

35. (D) Sugar per cup of flour = 3/4 ÷ 2/3 = 3/4 × 3/2 = 9/8 cup. For 2 cups flour: 9/8 × 2 = 18/8 = 9/4 = 2 1/4 cups. Multiplying 3/4 × 2/3 (=1/2) confuses the ratio direction. Sugar needed = 9/4 cups.

36. (A) Half of a third of 24 = 1/2 × 1/3 × 24 = 1/6 × 24 = 4. This equals 1/4 of the second, so 1/4 of second = 4, giving second = 4 × 4 = 16. Computing 1/6 of 24 wrong, or dividing instead of multiplying at the end, gives 12 or 8. The second quantity is 16.

37. (B) 2 1/2 ÷ 5/8 = 5/2 × 8/5 = 40/10 = 4 exactly. So 4 full packets with nothing left. Multiplying 5/2 × 5/8 = 25/16 (no inversion) is wrong; the clean quotient 4 means no leftover. Answer: 4 packets.

38. (B) The midpoint is the average: (1/3 + 1/2) ÷ 2 = (2/6 + 3/6) ÷ 2 = (5/6) ÷ 2 = 5/12. Averaging numerators and denominators separately gives 2/5, a classic error that is not the true midpoint. The midpoint is 5/12.

39. (D) In 2 days he does 2 × 1/6 = 1/3, leaving 2/3. On day 3 he does 1/4 of the remaining 2/3 = 1/4 × 2/3 = 1/6. Total done = 1/3 + 1/6 = 2/6 + 1/6 = 3/6 = 1/2. Taking 1/4 of the whole job on day 3 (=1/4) instead of of the remainder gives 7/12. By day 3's end, 1/2 is done.

40. (C) Left to right: 1 1/5 = 6/5; 6/5 ÷ 3/10 = 6/5 × 10/3 = 60/15 = 4; then 4 ÷ 2 = 2. Multiplying by 2 at the end (instead of dividing) gives 8; mishandling the first division gives 9/25. The value is 2.

41. (A) Each hour 7/8 of the current water remains. After 2 hours: 7/8 × 7/8 = 49/64 of the original. Subtracting 1/8 twice (giving 3/4) is wrong because the second hour's loss is 1/8 of a smaller amount, not of the original. Remaining = 49/64.

42. (C) First find the whole: sum = 250 ÷ 5/6 = 250 × 6/5 = 300. Then 2/3 of 300 = 200. Taking 2/3 of 250 directly (≈166.67) ignores that 250 is only 5/6 of the sum. The answer is Rs 200.

43. (C) Bracket first: 3/4 − 1/6 = 9/12 − 2/12 = 7/12. Then 7/12 ÷ 7/12 = 1. Multiplying 7/12 × 7/12 (no inversion) gives 49/144; subtracting incorrectly throws off the bracket. The value is 1.
