## Worked Solutions

**1. (C)** Leg 1 time = 60/40 = 1.5 h; leg 2 time = 90/60 = 1.5 h. Total 150 km in 3 h, so average = 150/3 = 50 km/h. The plain mean of 40 and 60 gives 52.5-type values; a mis-weighted blend gives 48; 100 is just the sum of the two speeds.

**2. (A)** Take the path as 5 units. Time for unit 1 = 1/6; time for the last 4 units = 4/24 = 1/6. Total time = 1/3 h-unit for 5 units, so average = 5/(1/3) = 15 km/h. Mis-weighting the fifths gives 12 or 18; over-counting the fast portion gives 20.

**3. (C)** Multiply km/h by 5/18: 270 x 5/18 = 1350/18 = 75 m/s. Multiplying by the inverted 18/5 gives 972; dividing by 36 gives 7.5; multiplying by 18/50 gives 97.2.

**4. (B)** Multiply m/s by 3.6: 35 x 3.6 = 126 km/h. Dividing by 3.6 gives about 9.72; halving gives 63; multiplying by 5 gives 175.

**5. (D)** Distance = 2x5 + 1x9 = 19 km. Total time including the rest = 2 + 1 + 1 = 4 h. Average = 19/4 = 4.75 km/h. The plain mean of 5 and 9 gives 7; dropping the rest hour gives 19/3 = 6.33; 9.5 is half the distance misread as a speed.

**6. (A)** Period depends only on length, with T proportional to the square root of L; mass and amplitude do not matter. So T_P : T_Q = sqrt(4) : sqrt(1) = 2 : 1. Using lengths directly gives 4 : 1; inverting gives 1 : 2; squaring the length ratio gives 1 : 4.

**7. (A)** The climbing-and-falling needle is the speedometer (current 54 km/h); the only-increasing digits are the odometer (162 km). Time = distance/speed = 162/54 = 3 h. Multiplying gives 108; 162/162 gives 1; 54/162 gives about 0.33.

**8. (D)** 25 m/s x 3.6 = 90 km/h, beating the train's 81 km/h. Dividing 25 by 3.6 gives about 6.9 and wrongly favours the train; the train is 81 not 90, so no tie; 25 x 3.6 is 90, not 108 (108 would be 30 m/s).

**9. (A)** Total distance = 30 + 45 = 75 cm = 0.75 m. Total time including the pause = 5 + 6 + 10 = 21 s, so average = 0.75/21 = 0.0357 m/s. Dropping the pause gives 0.75/15 = 0.05; a wrong leg total gives near 0.075; 0.75/12 gives 0.0625.

**10. (C)** Slope = rise/run. First segment 36/3 = 12 m/s; flat segment 0; last segment (90-36)/(12-9) = 54/3 = 18 m/s, the steepest. 12 is the gentler first slope; 36 is a y-value not a slope; 7.5 is the whole-trip average 90/12.

**11. (D)** Equal 0.5 h intervals but growing distances 30, 45, 60 km mean speed rises, so the motion is non-uniform and speeding up. Equal intervals alone do not make it uniform when distances differ; the distances grow not shrink; straight track rules out circular.

**12. (B)** Up-and-down repeating motion is oscillatory and, repeating at a fixed rate, periodic. 240 strokes in 60 s means one stroke every 60/240 = 0.25 s. The 4 s inverts the ratio; the needle does not go in circles; a fixed 240/min rate is periodic, not non-periodic.

**13. (D)** Speed P = 84/6 = 14 m/s; speed Q = 48/6 = 8 m/s; difference = 14 - 8 = 6 m/s. 36 is the raw distance gap, not a speed; 2 is a wrong subtraction; 14 is P's full speed, not the excess.

**14. (C)** Total time allowed = 360/90 = 4 h. The first leg took 270/108 = 2.5 h, leaving 1.5 h for the last 90 km, so required speed = 90/1.5 = 60 km/h. Averaging 108 and the target gives 72; 90 just repeats the trip average; using a wrong remaining time gives 120.

**15. (A)** Original period = 75/30 = 2.5 s. New period = 2.5 x 2/5 = 1.0 s. In 75 s it now makes 75/1.0 = 75 oscillations. Keeping 30 ignores the change; multiplying the period instead gives the 12-style error; using three-fifths gives 60.

**16. (C)** Convert to m/s: 30 m/s stays 30; 100 km/h = 100 x 5/18 = 27.8 m/s; 1620 m/min = 1620/60 = 27 m/s; 0.4 km/min = 400/60 = 6.7 m/s. The greatest is 30 m/s, clear of all the others.

**17. (C)** Since T is proportional to the square root of L, multiplying L by 9 multiplies T by sqrt(9) = 3, so the period triples. Taking the factor directly gives nine times; inverting gives one-third; length does affect the period, so 'unchanged' is wrong.

**18. (A)** Distance from the start (a cumulative graph) rises to 50 m, stays, then the descent shows the object returning over those same 50 m. Path length = 50 m out + 50 m back = 100 m. 50 m counts only the outward trip; 0 m confuses path with displacement; 150 m wrongly adds the flat segment as travel.

**19. (B)** Speed = slope = (41 - 14)/(10 - 4) = 27/6 = 4.5 m/s. 3.5 comes from 41/10 minus a slip; 5.5 from a wrong rise; 4.1 from reading 41/10 directly instead of using both points.

**20. (D)** Total distance = 48 + 60 + 72 = 180 km in 3 h, so average = 60 km/h. Because the hourly distances differ (48, 60, 72), the motion is non-uniform, not uniform. 180 is the total distance, not a speed; 55 is a miscalculated mean.

**21. (C)** Total distance = 48 + 48 = 96 km; total time = 2 + 4 = 6 h; average = 96/6 = 16 km/h. The downstream speed is 24 and upstream 12; their plain mean 18 is the trap; 12 is only the upstream leg; 24 is only the downstream leg.

**22. (B)** The tip traces a circle (circular) and repeats the same path every hour (periodic). It does not swing to-and-fro, so not oscillatory; it does not move in a straight line, so not rectilinear; the motion is regular, not random.

**23. (B)** Since T is proportional to the square root of L, dividing T by 3 (from 6 s to 2 s) requires dividing L by 3-squared = 9. So the new length is one-ninth. One-third confuses T with L directly; one-sixth halves wrongly; three times would lengthen, not shorten.

**24. (A)** 75 km/h x 5/18 = 20.83 m/s, not 25, so that one is wrong. Checking the rest: 54 x 5/18 = 15 (correct); 90 x 5/18 = 25 (correct); 126 x 5/18 = 35 (correct). Note 90 km/h is the one that truly equals 25 m/s, so 75 km/h cannot also be 25.

**25. (B)** A crossing point means both lines share the same coordinates, so the objects are at the same distance from the start at that time. The slopes (speeds) generally differ, so they need not be moving at the same speed; nothing forces them to stop or reverse.

**26. (B)** 90 ticks in 60 s gives an interval of 60/90 = 0.67 s between ticks. Inverting to 90/60 gives 1.5; 90 is the tick count, not a time; 0.015 misplaces a factor of 60.

**27. (C)** Distance = 120 + 80 = 200 km. Total time including the 0.5 h park = 2 + 0.5 + 1 = 3.5 h. Average = 200/3.5 = 57.1 km/h. Dropping the park gives 200/3 = 66.7; the plain mean of 60 and 80 km/h leg speeds gives 60-ish; 40 is the slower leg's speed alone.

**28. (D)** An hourglass keeps time because sand falls through the neck at a steady rate, taking the same total time to empty. The grains do not pulse; the bulbs do not swing; it needs no sunlight, working in the dark.

**29. (B)** 108 km/h x 5/18 = 30 m/s. Distance = 30 x 75 = 2250 m. Using 108 m/s gives 8100; using 20 m/s gives 1500; dropping a factor of ten gives 225.

**30. (A)** A rising line means forward motion; an increasing steepness means the speed is growing, so the object is speeding up. Flattening (not steepening) would mean slowing down; a falling line would mean returning; a flat line would mean at rest.

**31. (A)** Frequency = number of oscillations / total time = 48/72 = 0.67 per second. Inverting (time/oscillations) gives the period 1.5 s, not the frequency; 48 is the count; 72 is the time.

**32. (B)** Times: 40/40 = 1 h, 40/20 = 2 h, 40/60 = 0.67 h; total = 3.67 h for 120 km, so average = 120/3.67 = 32.7 km/h. The plain mean of 40, 20, 60 gives 40; a slightly different wrong mean gives 36; 30 underestimates by mis-adding the times.

**33. (B)** Distance = speed x time = 300000 x 600 = 180000000 km = 180 million km. 500 mistakes time for distance; 18 million drops a factor of ten; 1.8 billion adds one.

**34. (C)** B begins with a gentler slope (slower) but, as its upward bend steepens, its speed grows and it can overtake A. A straight line does not automatically stay ahead, nor does a curve automatically win; starting together does not keep them together once speeds differ.

**35. (A)** Total distance 600 m; total time = 30 + 90 = 120 s; average = 600/120 = 5 m/s. The plain mean of the leg speeds (8 m/s and 4 m/s) gives 6; 4 is only the slow leg; 8 is only the fast leg.

**36. (C)** Speed A = 150/10 = 15 m/s, speed B = 90/10 = 9 m/s; ratio 15 : 9 = 5 : 3. Inverting gives 3 : 5; 150 : 90 does simplify (divide by 30); equal times do not make speeds equal when distances differ.

**37. (B)** A horizontal line means the distance stays at 60 m, so no distance is covered and the speed is zero. 60 is a y-value not a slope; 8 is the time width, not a speed; a flat line means stopped, not fast.

**38. (A)** Period is proportional to the square root of length, so the 16 m pendulum's period is sqrt(16) = 4 times the 1 m one's. A four-times-longer period means four times fewer oscillations, so the short pendulum makes four times as many. Longer strings swing more slowly; mass is irrelevant; the factor is 4, not 16.

**39. (C)** The flag moves to-and-fro (oscillatory in style) but the intervals are irregular, so it is non-periodic. A clock pendulum repeats at fixed intervals, unlike this; the flag does not trace a circle; the straight pole does not make the flag's motion rectilinear.

**40. (D)** Distance = 150 + 90 = 240 km. Total time including the 0.5 h idle = 1.5 + 0.5 + 1 = 3 h. Average = 240/3 = 80 km/h. Dropping the idle gives 240/2.5 = 96; the plain mean of the leg speeds gives near 60-95; 120 ignores the idle and a leg.

**41. (C)** Out time = 4/8 = 0.5 h; back time = 4/24 = 0.167 h; total = 0.667 h for 8 km, so average = 8/0.667 = 12 km/h. The plain mean of 8 and 24 gives 16; 10 and 14 come from wrong weightings of the two legs.

**42. (A)** 84 mm = 0.084 m; speed = 0.084/7 = 0.012 m/s. Forgetting the mm-to-m conversion gives 12; a one-place slip gives 0.12 or 0.0012.

**43. (C)** Period = 50/40 = 1.25 s. 4 minutes = 240 s, so oscillations = 240/1.25 = 192. Using 50 s as a minute gives 160; reading 40 per minute gives 160 or 200; treating 240 s as the answer gives 240.

**44. (C)** First slope 40/4 = 10 m/s; flat 0; last slope (100-40)/(13-10) = 60/3 = 20 m/s, the fastest. 10 is the slower first segment; 40 is a y-value not a slope; the segments are not equal.

**45. (B)** A water clock depends on dripping water, not the Sun, so clouds, night, and a low Sun do not stop it — those are precisely the conditions that defeat a sundial. None of the listed items disables the water clock.

**46. (B)** Equal distance means the quickest is fastest. Times are Q 30 min, R 36 min, P 45 min, so fastest to slowest is Q, R, P. Equal distance does not make speeds equal; the other orderings reverse or scramble the times.

**47. (D)** Times: 60/60 = 1 h, 60/30 = 2 h, 60/90 = 0.67 h; total = 3.67 h for 180 km, so average = 180/3.67 = 49 km/h. The plain mean of 60, 30, 90 gives 60; 55 and 45 are other mis-weighted means.

**48. (A)** 216 km/h x 5/18 = 60 m/s. Distance = 60 x 35 = 2100 m. Using 216 m/s gives 7560; using 30 m/s gives 1050; dropping a factor of ten gives 210.

**49. (B)** A horizontal line means distance is unchanging, so the object is at rest, not moving fast — that statement is false. The other three are all true facts about distance-time graphs.

**50. (A)** 2.7 km = 2700 m; 45 min = 2700 s; speed = 2700/2700 = 1 m/s. Multiplying by 3.6 wrongly gives 3.6; using 45 s instead of 2700 s gives 60; a factor slip gives 0.06.

**51. (D)** Shortening the pendulum shortens its period (T proportional to the square root of length), so it ticks more frequently and the clock gains time, running fast. A shorter pendulum swings more often, not less; period does not depend on mass; a short pendulum still swings.

**52. (B)** On a distance-from-start graph, a rise then a fall back to zero means the object went out to 72 m and then came back to where it began. The descent shows return, not continued speeding up, no circle, and no permanent stop.

**53. (D)** Speed = slope = (52 - 16)/(8 - 2) = 36/6 = 6 m/s. 8 comes from 16/2; 5 from a wrong rise; 6.5 from 52/8.

**54. (D)** By 11:00 the walker is 2 h x 5 = 10 km ahead. The cyclist closes the 10 km gap at 15 - 5 = 10 km/h, taking 1 h, so they meet at 12:00, by which time the cyclist has gone 15 km/h x 1 h = 15 km. The 1:00 and 11:30 options use wrong closing speeds; they do meet.

**55. (D)** To pass the post, the train moves its own length 120 m in 6 s, so speed = 120/6 = 20 m/s. Multiplying gives 720; inverting parts gives 5 or 0.05.

**56. (C)** Both start at the origin; a steeper line means greater speed, so A pulls ahead and stays ahead at every later time. A gentler line is slower, not farther; the gap grows rather than staying fixed; since both start together, A is never behind.

**57. (A)** 117 km/h x 5/18 = 585/18 = 32.5 m/s, not 30, so that conversion is wrong. The others check out: 36 x 5/18 = 10, 72 x 5/18 = 20, 144 x 5/18 = 40, all correct. (30 m/s would correspond to 108 km/h, not 117.)

**58. (D)** Distance = 36470 - 36200 = 270 km. The break is part of the elapsed time, so total time = 4 h. Average = 270/4 = 67.5 km/h. Removing the 0.5 h break gives 270/3.5 = 77.1; 60 and 54 come from wrong distance or time figures.

**59. (D)** Speed = slope = (77 - 21)/(11 - 3) = 56/8 = 7 m/s. 7.7 comes from 77/10; 6 and 8 from wrong rise or run values.

**60. (D)** From 0 to 4 s the distance rises 12 m every 2 s (6 m/s, uniform). From 4 to 6 s it rises only 6 m (3 m/s, slower). From 6 to 8 s the distance stays at 30 m, so the object is at rest. It is not uniform across all 8 s, not speeding up, and certainly not at rest the whole time since it clearly advanced first.
