**Marking scheme:** **+4** for each correct answer · **−1** for each wrong answer · **0** if unattempted.

**Instructions:** Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. Classic traps: confusing weather with climate, assuming pressure rises with altitude (it falls), swapping the jobs of thermometer/barometer/rain gauge/wind vane/anemometer/hygrometer, treating high humidity as a guarantee of rain, and mixing up the units mb, degrees, percent and mm; many stems need multi-step reasoning, data reading or a full cause-and-effect chain. Do not write on this paper — mark answers on your answer sheet.

1. A diary keeps two records of Darjeeling: a line for 12 March, '6 degrees, foggy, light drizzle since noon', and a paragraph beginning 'Decade after decade, the spring stays cool and misty here.' A student must label each correctly. The right labels are:
   (A) The dated 12 March line is climate because it gives an exact temperature, and the decade paragraph is weather because it has no number
   (B) The dated 12 March line is weather (the state of the air at one time) and the decade-after-decade paragraph is climate (the long-term average pattern)
   (C) Both records are weather because both name Darjeeling and mention mist
   (D) Both records are climate because both describe cool, misty conditions
