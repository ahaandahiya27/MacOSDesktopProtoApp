**Marking scheme:** **+4** for each correct answer · **−1** for each wrong answer · **0** if unattempted.

**Instructions:** Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. Classic P4 traps: confusing the colour an indicator shows with the family it names (china rose magenta=acid not base, methyl orange red=acid, turmeric red=base, phenolphthalein pink=base); reading one indicator as conclusive when a second contradicts or merely fails to distinguish (phenolphthalein/turmeric staying unchanged cannot separate acid from neutral); treating 'no litmus change' as proof of water rather than mere neutrality; over- and under-shooting an endpoint during drop-by-drop neutralisation; choosing the wrong member of the acid-base pair to fix a sting, soil, effluent or stomach; assuming the salt from every neutralisation is common salt; and confusing strength or amount of taste with chemical family. Do not write on this paper — mark answers on your answer sheet.
