Worked Solutions

1. (D) Lakhs place value = 4 × 1,00,000 = 4,00,000. Ten-thousands = 7 × 10,000 = 70,000. Thousands = 2 × 1,000 = 2,000. Sum = 4,00,000 + 70,000 + 2,000 = 4,72,000. Reading 4 lakh, 0 ten-thousand, 7 thousand gives 4,07,200 — that swaps the 7 and the empty ten-thousands slot. Dropping a zero and writing 47,200 makes it a 5-digit number, losing the lakhs digit. Writing 4,27,000 puts the 2 in the ten-thousands and 7 in the thousands — the two digits are exchanged.

2. (B) Each press adds 1,000, so presses = 4,08,000 ÷ 1,000 = 408. Reading the number as 'forty-eight thousand' and dividing gives 48, which only reaches 48,000. Multiplying instead of placing the comma right gives 4080, which reaches 40,80,000. The number 4,08,000 itself counts +1 presses, not +1000 presses.

3. (B) The digits are 80540000 — eight crore five lakh forty thousand. International grouping is 3-3-3 from the right: 80,540,000 (eighty million, five hundred forty thousand). Grouping as 8,054,0000 leaves a 4-digit final block, which the 3-3-3 rule forbids. Keeping the Indian 2-2 groups on the left (805,40,000) mixes the two systems. Writing 80,54,0000 again leaves a 4-digit block at the end.

4. (A) Nearest thousand: look at the hundreds digit of 7,46,892 — it is 8 (≥5), so round the thousands up: 7,46,892 → 7,47,000. Nearest ten-thousand: look at the thousands digit of 7,46,892 — it is 6 (≥5), so round the ten-thousands up: 7,46,892 → 7,50,000. Rounding both down (7,46,000 and 7,40,000) ignores the 8 and the 6. The pair 7,47,000 and 7,40,000 rounds the first correctly but the second wrongly down. The pair 7,46,000 and 7,50,000 rounds the first wrongly down.

5. (C) Digit 3 sits in the ten-lakhs place: place value = 3 × 10,00,000 = 30,00,000. Digit 4 sits in the lakhs place: place value = 4 × 1,00,000 = 4,00,000. Sum = 30,00,000 + 4,00,000 = 34,00,000. Adding the face values 3 + 4 gives 7 — that ignores place. Writing 30,40,000 misplaces the 4 one position too low (ten-thousands). Writing 3,40,000 drops one zero from each place value.

6. (C) Convert all to plain numbers: 8 lakh = 8,00,000; 0.9 million = 9,00,000; 70,000 = 70,000; 1.2 million = 12,00,000. Order: 70,000 < 8,00,000 < 9,00,000 < 12,00,000. So 70,000 < 8 lakh < 0.9 million < 1.2 million. Placing 0.9 million (9,00,000) before 8 lakh (8,00,000) reverses those two. Placing 8 lakh before 70,000 ignores that 70,000 is smallest. Ending with 0.9 million after 1.2 million reverses the largest pair.

7. (C) Total = 25 × 4,000. Use 25 × 4 = 100, then attach the three zeros of 4,000: 100 × 1,000 = 1,00,000 — exactly one lakh. Getting 10,000 forgets a factor of 10 in the multiplication. Getting 10,00,000 (ten lakh) attaches one zero too many. Getting 1,00,00,000 (one crore) attaches two extra zeros.

8. (D) Three crore = 3,00,00,000; five lakh = 5,00,000; sixty thousand = 60,000. Sum = 3,00,00,000 + 5,00,000 + 60,000 = 3,05,60,000. Estu's 3,56,000 (three lakh fifty-six thousand) collapsed the crore and the lakh-zero into the thousands — far too small. Writing 30,56,000 reads it as 'thirty lakh fifty-six thousand', still missing the crore scale. Writing 3,50,60,000 reads the 5 lakh as 50 lakh.

9. (B) Five crore is 5,00,00,000. Subtract 2,75,000: 5,00,00,000 − 2,75,000 = 4,97,25,000. Check by adding back: 4,97,25,000 + 2,75,000 = 5,00,00,000. Getting 4,97,75,000 subtracts only 2,25,000 by mishandling the borrow. Getting 2,25,00,000 wrongly subtracts 2,75,00,000 (a lakh too large). Getting 4,72,25,000 borrows from the wrong place, dropping 25 lakh.

10. (B) Smallest 8-digit number = 1,00,00,000 (one crore). Largest 7-digit number = 99,99,999. Their difference = 1,00,00,000 − 99,99,999 = 1. Consecutive whole numbers always differ by 1. Answering 10 confuses this with a place jump. Answering 0 wrongly treats them as equal. Answering 1,00,00,000 just repeats the smaller boundary instead of subtracting.

11. (D) Rounding to the nearest lakh keeps the lakhs digit and looks at the ten-thousands digit. A number rounds to 10,00,000 when it is in [9,50,000 , 10,49,999]. Among the options, 9,51,000 lies in that band (its ten-thousands digit 5 rounds up). It also sits between 9,80,000? No — re-read: the band that rounds to 10,00,000 is from 9,50,000 to 10,49,999, and 9,51,000 is inside it. 9,40,000 rounds to 9,00,000 (ten-thousands digit 4). 10,60,000 rounds to 11,00,000 (digit 6). 9,49,000 rounds to 9,00,000 (digit 4).

12. (C) 6 × 7 = 42. Count the zeros: 6,000 has 3 and 7,000 has 3, so 6 zeros in all. 42 followed by 6 zeros = 42000000 = 4,20,00,000 (four crore twenty lakh). Writing 42,00,000 uses only 5 zeros. Writing 4,20,000 uses only 4 zeros. Writing 42,00,00,000 uses 7 zeros.

13. (A) Digit 8 is in the ten-lakhs place: 8 × 10,00,000 = 80,00,000. Digit 6 is in the ten-thousands place: 6 × 10,000 = 60,000. Difference = 80,00,000 − 60,000 = 79,40,000. Subtracting face values 8 − 6 = 2 ignores place. Writing 80,06,000 simply joins the two place values instead of subtracting. Writing 7,94,000 drops a zero from the answer.

14. (B) Nearest hundred: tens digit is 8 (≥5), so round the hundreds up — 2,45,680 → 2,45,700. Nearest thousand: hundreds digit is 6 (≥5), so round the thousands up — 2,45,680 → 2,46,000. Rounding both down (2,45,600 and 2,45,000) ignores the 8 and the 6. The pair 2,45,700 and 2,45,000 rounds the first up but the second wrongly down. The pair 2,45,600 and 2,46,000 rounds the first wrongly down.

15. (D) Convert to plain numbers: 3 crore = 3,00,00,000 and 25 million = 2,50,00,000, so 3 crore > 25 million is true. 1 lakh = 1,00,000 while 1 million = 10,00,000, so 1 lakh is the smaller, not larger. 50 lakh = 50,00,000 while 6 million = 60,00,000, so 50 lakh is smaller. 9 crore = 9,00,00,000 while 80 million = 8,00,00,000, so 9 crore is larger, making '9 crore < 80 million' false.

16. (C) Two crore = 2,00,00,000; three lakh = 3,00,000; nine thousand = 9,000; five = 5. Sum = 2,03,09,005. Writing 2,30,90,005 reads three lakh as thirty lakh and nine thousand as ninety thousand. Writing 2,03,90,005 reads nine thousand as ninety thousand. Writing 2,03,09,500 puts the 5 in the hundreds place (five hundred) instead of the ones.

17. (D) 5 × 10,00,000 = 50,00,000; 2 × 1,00,000 = 2,00,000; 8 × 1,000 = 8,000; 4 × 10 = 40. The hundred-thousands and ten-thousands and hundreds and ones are 0. Sum = 50,00,000 + 2,00,000 + 8,000 + 40 = 52,08,040. Writing 5,28,040 collapses the 5 ten-lakhs into the lakhs place, losing a place. Writing 52,80,040 reads 8 thousand as 8 ten-thousand. Writing 52,08,400 reads 4 tens as 4 hundreds.

18. (A) Total = 1,25,000 × 8. Since 125 × 8 = 1,000, attach the three trailing zeros: 1,000 × 1,000 = 10,00,000 litres (ten lakh). Getting 1,00,000 multiplies 125 × 8 but drops the thousands zeros. Getting 1,00,00,000 (one crore) attaches one zero too many. Getting 8,25,000 adds 8 lakh to the daily figure instead of multiplying.

19. (A) One crore = 100 lakh. So 5 crore = 5 × 100 = 500 lakh. Check: 500 lakh = 500 × 1,00,000 = 5,00,00,000 = 5 crore. Answering 50 uses 1 crore = 10 lakh (wrong). Answering 5,000 uses 1 crore = 1,000 lakh. Answering 5 treats crore and lakh as the same scale.

20. (D) Indian grouping from the right: a group of 3 (678), then groups of 2 (45, 23, 1): 1,23,45,678. The first comma (rightmost) separates the last three digits 678 from 45 — i.e. after the thousands. Writing 12,345,678 uses International 3-3-3 grouping. Writing 1234,5678 uses 4-4 grouping, which neither system allows. Writing 1,2345,678 mixes a 4-digit middle block in.

21. (C) One million = 10,00,000 and one crore = 1,00,00,000. Fraction = 10,00,000 ÷ 1,00,00,000 = 1/10. Check: 10 million = 1 crore, so one million is a tenth of a crore. Answering 1/100 treats one million as one lakh. Answering 1/1000 is off by a further factor of 10. Answering 10 inverts the ratio.

22. (B) Nearest lakh keeps everything down to the lakhs digit and looks at the ten-thousands digit. In 8,49,99,500 the ten-thousands digit is 9 (≥5), so round the lakhs up: 8,49,xx,xxx becomes 8,50,00,000. Rounding down to 8,49,00,000 ignores the 9. Jumping to 9,00,00,000 rounds to the nearest crore instead. Writing 8,49,99,000 rounds only to the nearest thousand.

23. (B) Total = 60,000 × 50. Since 6 × 5 = 30, and there are 4 + 1 = 5 trailing zeros (four in 60,000, one in 50, but count: 60,000 has 4 zeros, 50 has 1 zero → 5 zeros), 30 followed by 5 zeros = 30,00,000 (thirty lakh). Writing 3,00,000 (three lakh) uses one fewer zero. Writing 3,00,00,000 (three crore) uses one extra zero. Writing 30,000 ignores the matches entirely.

24. (A) International 3-3-3 reads 305 | 407 | 000 as three hundred five million, four hundred seven thousand, zero — i.e. three hundred five million four hundred seven thousand. Reading it with Indian words (crore/lakh) is the wrong system and also miscounts the digits. 'Three hundred fifty million forty-seven thousand' is 350,047,000, a different number. 'Thirty-five million four hundred seven thousand' is 35,407,000, missing a digit.

25. (D) Convert: 1 crore = 1,00,00,000; 95 lakh = 95,00,000; 9 million = 90,00,000; 99,00,000 = 99 lakh. Comparing: 1,00,00,000 > 99,00,000 > 95,00,000 > 90,00,000. So 1 crore is the largest. 95 lakh and 99,00,000 are both under a crore. 9 million equals 90 lakh, the smallest here.

26. (B) Total = 144 × 2,500. Compute 144 × 25 = 3,600, then attach the two trailing zeros of 2,500: 3,600 × 100 = 3,60,000 (three lakh sixty thousand). Writing 36,000 uses one fewer zero. Writing 36,00,000 uses one extra zero. Writing 3,60,00,000 uses two extra zeros.

27. (A) 99,99,994 + 9 = 1,00,00,003. The count crosses from the largest 7-digit number (99,99,999) into the 8-digit range. Counting the digits of 1,00,00,003: 1-0-0-0-0-0-0-3 = 8 digits (one crore three). Saying 7 digits forgets that adding past 99,99,999 pushes into 8 digits. The figure 99,99,903 mis-adds (subtracting instead of carrying). Saying 9 digits over-counts by one place.

28. (B) 48 × 125 = 48 × 1000 ÷ 8 = 48,000 ÷ 8 = 6,000. Check: 6,000 ÷ 125 = 48. Getting 600 divides 48,000 by 80 instead of 8. Getting 60,000 forgets to divide by 8 and just multiplies 48 × 1000 then keeps a zero. Getting 384 computes 48 × 8 instead.

29. (C) Convert: 7 lakh = 7,00,000; 0.6 million = 6,00,000; 8,00,000 = 8,00,000; 1 million = 10,00,000. Decreasing: 10,00,000 > 8,00,000 > 7,00,000 > 6,00,000, i.e. 1 million > 8,00,000 > 7 lakh > 0.6 million. Placing 8,00,000 above 1 million reverses the top pair.
