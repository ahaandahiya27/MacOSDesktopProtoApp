## Worked Solutions

**1. (D)** Because च conjoins the two adjectives, the verse points to the intersection of useful-speech and pleasing-speech — the one cell where both are true at once — and calls only that combination rare. It does not speak of speech in general, nor of the merely-one-quality cases, nor of the speech that is neither.

**2. (B)** A conjunction demands that both conditions hold; a disjunction is satisfied by either condition alone. Swapping the 'and' for an 'or' therefore drops the demand from two simultaneous qualities to a single one — loosening, not tightening or erasing, the standard.

**3. (C)** The line has one substantive, वचः, and the other three forms qualify it — they share its neuter singular and so behave as predicate adjectives. Reading them as separate nouns, as verbs, or as adverbs all misses the single-subject-with-three-attributes structure.

**4. (A)** Sanskrit adjectives agree with their noun in gender, number and case; matching the neuter singular वचः forces neuter singular qualifiers. Were the noun masculine, the qualifiers would shift to masculine. They are not gender-frozen, not pluralised by their own count, and they remain adjectives.

**5. (D)** The ideal demands usefulness and pleasantness together. The sweet lie pleases but does not benefit (and is untrue); the harsh rebuke benefits but does not please; silence is neither. Only the true, kind, helpful correction satisfies both halves at once.

**6. (A)** दुर्लभम् sits between 'easy to get' and 'cannot be got': it means rare and hard, yet reachable with effort. The verse therefore does not place ideal speech among the common, nor at the impossible extreme, nor off any frequency scale.

**7. (C)** दुर्- signals difficulty, the contrary of सु- ('easy'); it negates ease, not possibility. अशक्यम् negates possibility outright. So 'hard to obtain' is a softer claim than 'cannot be obtained' — not its synonym, not its opposite, and certainly not stronger than impossibility.

**8. (C)** A goal worth striving for must be attainable; दुर्लभ keeps it attainable-though-rare, so persistence makes sense. अशक्यम् declares it unattainable, which guts the exhortation to persist. The two words are not interchangeable, दुर्लभ does not mean common, and an impossible goal cannot justify effort.

**9. (A)** The first member is मनः (मनस्); before the voiced ह the visarga turns to the 'o' vowel, yielding मनो-, joined to हारि ('stealing/captivating'). It is not मन + अहारि, not मनः + आहारि, and the स्/visarga does change — so 'mind-feeding' and 'not-stealing' are both wrong resolutions.

**10. (C)** हारि here is 'one who steals/captivates', so the compound literally 'steals the heart' — i.e. delights and pleases. It does not mean harming, instructing, or hiding; those readings confuse हारि with unrelated senses.

**11. (D)** हितम् is about benefit to one's welfare; मनोहारि (heart-stealing) is about appeal to one's feelings. So usefulness targets welfare and pleasantness targets the heart — not the reverse, and neither concerns wealth or character here.

**12. (B)** सत्य means 'true' and its negative अनृत means 'untrue/false'. हित is 'beneficial' and प्रिय is 'pleasing/dear' — so the option swapping हित with प्रिय is wrong, as are the options that tie truth-words to pleasing/benefit.

**13. (B)** प्रियम् ('dear/pleasing') overlaps with मनोहारि ('heart-stealing'), since both pick out the agreeable, heart-winning side of speech. It is not the useful side (हितम्), not rarity (दुर्लभम्), and not speech itself (वचः).

**14. (C)** Vowel-sandhi needs a vowel ending meeting a vowel beginning; च opens with the consonant च्, so no such merger arises and the particle is simply written after. च is not a verb, मनोहारि ends in a vowel (not a consonant), and the explanation has nothing to do with emphasis.

**15. (B)** An enclitic च placed after a word ties that word back to the preceding one, so मनोहारि च links 'pleasing' with the earlier 'useful'. It does not bind the later वचः/दुर्लभम् pair, does not attach only forward, and is not meaningless.

**16. (B)** वा is the disjunctive 'or', so replacing च would relax the conjunction to an alternative. वा is not another 'and', not the interrogative किम्/का, and not a negation.

**17. (B)** The verse predicts rarity from the both-and quality, not the reverse; affirming the consequent (rare ⇒ both) is a fallacy, since many things may be rare for other reasons. So rarity alone licenses neither 'it is both' nor 'it is neither' nor 'it is common'.

**18. (C)** The subject is not bare वचः but वचः qualified as both useful and pleasing; rarity attaches only to that qualified subject. The pupil drops the qualifiers and over-generalises. दुर्लभम् does not mean 'all', and the restriction comes from the adjectives, not from वचः itself.

**19. (A)** वा drops the demand to a single quality, and सुलभम् ('easy to get') replaces 'rare' with 'common'. Both edits loosen the claim. वा and च are not synonyms, nor are सुलभ and दुर्लभ, so neither 'unchanged' nor a split effect fits.

**20. (D)** Negating the 'pleasing' half leaves speech that helps yet grates; lacking one of the two required qualities, it fails the both-and ideal. It is neither 'pleasing but useless', nor still 'both', nor 'neither'.

**21. (D)** -शस् makes 'by units of' distributive adverbs, and adverbs are indeclinable — invariant across gender, number and case. They are not datives, not agreeing adjectives, and not conjugating verbs.

**22. (B)** क्षणशः ('moment by moment') fits the time-based growth of learning; कणशः ('grain by grain') fits the unit-by-unit saving of wealth. The match is principled, so the adverbs are not freely swappable, are not literal descriptions of each noun's substance, and do not mean the same thing.

**23. (C)** बिन्दु + -शस् mirrors क्षण/कण + -शस्, giving a 'drop by drop' distributive adverb that does not decline. It is not a dative noun, not a verb form, and not an agreeing adjective.

**24. (D)** Daily moments of study are units of time, matching क्षणशः ('moment by moment'). कणशः belongs to grain/coin accumulation, the growth was incremental rather than instantaneous, and these distributive adverbs are precisely meant to describe such gradual processes.

**25. (B)** -वान् adds 'possessing', and क्रिया (from कृ, 'to do') is real action, so क्रियावान् is a possessor-of-action, a doer. The suffix is not privative (so not 'idle'), the word concerns doing not merely describing, and -वान् does not mean 'opposing'.

**26. (C)** The praised doer embodies action; the criticised boaster embodies empty talk, so the chapter prizes deeds over talk. It is not the reverse, not a wealth-over-knowledge claim, and not a counsel of total silence.

**27. (A)** The doer is the one whose completed action speaks for itself, namely the quiet finisher; the boaster is the one whose talk outruns his unfinished deeds, namely the loud announcer. Confident announcement is not doing, producing work does not make one a boaster, and describing work aloud is not the mark of a doer.

**28. (C)** The two teachings share one spine — value substance over show. Good speech must really help and please (not merely sound nice), just as a worthy person must really do (not merely boast). They neither contradict, since the verse never prizes empty talk, nor are they unrelated or jointly pro-silence.

**29. (B)** Two similar simple vowels (उ + उ) coalesce into the corresponding long vowel ऊ, so सु + उक्तिः → सूक्तिः. They do not yield the diphthong ओ, do not become the semivowel व्, and do not stay separated.

**30. (C)** Only सूक्तिः embeds उक्ति, anchoring it to 'saying'; सूक्ष्म lacks that element and means 'subtle/fine'. So they are not both speech-words, सूक्ष्म is not a kind of saying, and a shared opening syllable does not make synonyms.

**31. (D)** Like सु+उक्ति, सुभाषितम् is सु ('well') + भाषितम् ('spoken'), i.e. a well-spoken saying — matching सूक्तिः. It is not 'six faults', not 'hidden light', and not merely a proper name.

**32. (B)** कुतूहलम्, कौतुकम् and जिज्ञासा all name curiosity, the desire to learn, which the chapter treats as the engine of knowledge. They concern neither boasting, nor rest, nor greed for wealth.

**33. (A)** Both a storehouse and क्षणशः-knowledge grow by incremental additions over time, so the metaphor fits the gradual gathering of learning. A storehouse does not fill instantly, the metaphor is meant earnestly (not ironic about coins), and it is not an emptiness image.

**34. (A)** Eloquence, useful advice and riches are outward gifts; dishonesty shows the inner deficiency the chapter prizes most — शीलम्. He plainly has speech and wealth, and curiosity is not the relevant lack.

**35. (B)** Ideal speech is दुर्लभम् (rare-but-reachable) and knowledge/wealth grow bit by bit, so both verses point to patient, incremental effort toward hard-but-attainable goods. They are not easy/instant, not impossible, and not won by boasting.

**36. (A)** The two words differ in force: difficulty versus impossibility. The verse's choice of दुर्लभम् keeps the goal reachable, refuting the 'never exist' claim. दुर्लभम् is not 'impossible', अशक्यम् is not 'rare', and the verse does use दुर्लभम्.

**37. (D)** Matching neuter singular वचः requires the neuter singular हितम्. The masculine हितः and feminine हिता disagree in gender, and a plural हितानि disagrees in number — the count of separate qualities does not pluralise an adjective bound to one noun.

**38. (B)** वचः is a neuter sibilant (-स्) stem, parallel to मनस् → मनः; the visarga is the stem-final -स् at word end. It is neither a feminine -आ stem, nor a masculine -अ stem, nor an indeclinable.

**39. (C)** मनः in मनोहारि is the same -स् stem as वचः/मनस्, and its visarga turns to 'o' before the voiced ह. क्रियावान्'s -वान् is a possessive suffix, दुर्- is a prefix, and क्षणशः's -शः is the distributive -शस् suffix — none are neuter -स् noun stems.

**40. (C)** सु+उक्ति is a 'well-spoken saying', so a सूक्तिः is a pithy maxim. It is not a boast (विकत्थन), not a storehouse inventory (भाण्डागारः), and not a meaningless subtle riddle (a confusion with सूक्ष्म).

**41. (A)** Eager questioning and seeking answers is curiosity, कुतूहलम्. Asking aloud is not boasting, her questions are answerable (not अशक्यम्), and she herself is a curious learner, not a 'storehouse'.

**42. (B)** दुर्लभम् is 'rare', च is 'and', क्रियावान् is 'doer', सूक्तिः is 'good saying' — all four right. The other sets each smuggle in at least one classic error: 'impossible/or/boaster/subtle', 'common', or 'or/boaster'.

**43. (C)** Knowledge is gathered moment by moment (क्षणशः) and wealth grain by grain (कणशः); the adverbs must not swap and must not collapse to one. The other three options either reverse the pair or wrongly use a single adverb for both.

**44. (A)** च is 'and', दुर्लभम् is 'rare' not impossible, क्रियावान् is a doer not a talker, and क्षणशः/कणशः are distributive 'unit by unit' adverbs, not 'all at once' — every one of the four is mistaken.

**45. (D)** सूक्तिः (wise saying), कुतूहलम् (curiosity), भाण्डागारः (treasured store of knowledge) and शीलम् (character) map onto wise speech, curiosity, valued learning and good character. The negative lists reverse the chapter's values.

**46. (D)** Calling the ideal rare-but-reachable invites perseverance toward it. अशक्यम् would invite giving up (resignation), 'common' would invite complacency, and neither word implies that speech is unimportant.

**47. (A)** Only the respectful-yet-honest approach satisfies both halves. Vague praise drops the हितम् (no real benefit), the humiliating rebuke drops the मनोहारि (no pleasantness), and silence drops both.

**48. (B)** Multiple adjectives commonly add separate information, and here 'beneficial' and 'pleasing' are genuinely different — their rare co-occurrence is the verse's whole point. They are not forced synonyms, not both 'pleasing', and a noun may take several adjectives.

**49. (A)** वचः ('speech') is the substantive; the other three describe it (useful, pleasing, rare). दुर्लभम् and हितम् are adjectives, not the head noun, and the line is not four parallel nouns.

**50. (D)** By predicating rarity of the qualified speech, the verse locates the difficulty precisely in fusing the two qualities, which is a sharper and more useful claim than a blanket 'speech is rare'. The attachment is grammatical, meaningful, and does not turn दुर्लभम् into a verb.

**51. (C)** Both clusters elevate the harder, genuine good — speech that truly helps and pleases, and learning/wealth/deeds patiently earned — over the cheap or hollow option. They do not exalt boasting or instant ease, and they are unified, not opposed.

**52. (C)** Letting action outshine talk is exactly the doer-over-boaster lesson (कर्म over कथन). It is not about incremental learning, not the साइंग-versus-subtle vocabulary point, and not the rarity-versus-impossibility distinction.

**53. (D)** Blunt, wounding truth helps (हितम्) yet does not please (not मनोहारि), satisfying only one of the two demands — so it is not the ideal, and truth is not inherently pleasing. It is not the reverse (it is useful), and not 'neither' (it is useful).

**54. (A)** Flattery may delight (मनोहारि) yet, lacking truth or benefit, it misses हितम्; the both-and standard rejects it. Pleasantness is not by itself sufficient, मनोहारि is not condemned (it is half the ideal), and the issue is ethical, not grammatical.

**55. (A)** Requiring both criteria narrows the field to their intersection, which is smaller than either single set — so such items are rarer, matching the verse's दुर्लभम्. They are not more common, not equally common, and not impossible (दुर्लभम्, not अशक्यम्).

**56. (D)** The faithful restatement keeps the conjunction (both), the correct sense of दुर्लभम् (rare-but-attainable), and the implied encouragement to strive. The distractors variously swap 'and' for 'or', make सुलभम् of दुर्लभम्, declare impossibility, or condemn pleasing speech.

**57. (D)** The chapter weaves together both-and speech, patient accumulation (क्षणशः/कणशः), deeds over boasting (क्रियावान्/विकत्थन), and शीलम् as the crowning value — exactly the first option. The others invert one or more of these.

**58. (A)** दुर्- adds difficulty: hard-to-obtain, hard-to-traverse, and so on, modifying the base's action with 'hard'. It is not a doubling prefix, not the laudatory सु-, and not a directional 'away'.

**59. (B)** Mirroring हितं मनोहारि च, the new line would have सत्यम् and प्रियम् as neuter-singular adjectives conjoined by च ('and') and agreeing with वचः. They would not be 'or'-linked nouns, not adverbs on दुर्लभम्, and not a subject-verb pair.

**60. (D)** Naming the both-and speech as a rare yet reachable ideal frames it as something worth striving for, which is an ethical nudge, not mere parsing. The verse is not value-neutral description, not a grammar exercise, and not about wealth.
