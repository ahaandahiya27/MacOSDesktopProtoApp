## Worked Solutions

**1. (C)** The flaw is not ignorance of the number ten, nor a moving boy, nor double-counting from haste. It is structural self-reference: the very one who counts is also a member to be counted, but a subject cannot turn himself into the object of his own counting glance, so he omits precisely himself.

**2. (D)** Each counter omits exactly himself, regardless of group size, so each independently reports one fewer than the true total: nineteen for twenty. The error neither cancels with more counters nor doubles because of the traveller, nor is it frozen at any fixed number.

**3. (A)** Across the ten counts the omitted boy is always the current counter, so it shifts each time; over all ten counts every boy does get counted by someone. No single fixed boy (first, tenth, or middle) is the perpetually missed one.

**4. (A)** Consistency does not make the count true; it shows the error is systematic, not a random slip. Repeating the same inside-vantage reproduces the same self-omission every time. No new boy drowns per recount, and the boys, not the river, are doing the counting.

**5. (D)** The decisive factor is position, not arithmetic skill or any vow: only someone outside the counted set can include every member of it. The cleverest insider is still inside the set and so cannot make himself the object of his own count.

**6. (D)** Mutual counting does not help: each still counts the other plus the remaining members but omits himself, so each says nine. One does not 'fill in' the other's gap, because each gap is the counter's own self, which the other's count of his subgroup does not address as the first counter's total.

**7. (A)** No boy is actually absent (so not ontic — neither a real loss-then-rescue nor 'only nine ever existed'). The gap is epistemic: a blind spot in counting. And the forgotten item is a person — the self — not the river's name.

**8. (A)** The mapping is precise: the counter overlooks the one nearest of all — himself — just as a person overlooks the Self, the very ground of seeing. It is not about forgetting others' names, miscounting coins, or mere arithmetic.

**9. (B)** Merely saying 'ten' supplies a number; 'त्वम्' relocates the sought tenth into the listener himself, turning an external count into a first-person recognition. It does not identify the traveller as tenth, issue a command, or ask about banks.

**10. (B)** In तत्त्वमसि, तत् points to the supreme reality (Brahman), which in the parable corresponds to the long-sought 'tenth' — the very thing being looked for — not the river, the traveller, or the act of counting.

**11. (D)** Double-checking does not touch the deeper points: the error is built into counting from inside oneself, and the sought Self is the seeker. Reducing the chapter to careful arithmetic misses both its structural and its metaphysical teaching.

**12. (C)** 'The ninth is not drowned' denies a loss but never locates the uncounted self, so the boys could miscount again on the next attempt. It is therefore weaker, not stronger or equal; the weakness is conceptual, not a mere ordinal mismatch.

**13. (A)** The surface is a counting puzzle solved by an outsider; the depth is that the Self is the seeker it seeks. The race, flood, and market readings do not fit either layer of this parable.

**14. (C)** Both hold because what was missing was only self-awareness, not the boy: he was present and active throughout as the counter. There was no hidden bank, no stand-in, no newly created tenth.

**15. (A)** The general principle is that any agent trying to make itself its own object generates a blind spot at itself — the inherent limit of self-reference. It is not about multilingualism, only-the-first-counter erring, or water.

**16. (B)** Reordering changes nothing: whoever counts omits himself and gets nine. The result depends on the role of counter, not on sequence or position, so no arrangement yields a different total.

**17. (D)** A name-list is an external, fixed reference that already includes the counter as a named entry, so the count no longer depends on who reads it. The fix comes from externalizing the reference, not from counting past nine or removing a boy.

**18. (C)** Their own repeated count had certified nine, so a credible 'ten' contradicts what they were sure of: surprise must precede relief. It is not a language barrier, a prior self-discovery, or a refusal to speak.

**19. (C)** The traveller functions as a guru-figure who dispels ignorance by turning the seeker's attention back upon himself, not as a physical rescuer, a judge, or a king ordering a census.

**20. (C)** The deepest one-line statement is that what you seek as the missing tenth is yourself, the seeker. The others reduce it to arithmetic prudence, an insider/outsider truism, or an absurd rule about rivers.

**21. (D)** The last boy is the tenth (ordinal दशमः), while the total is ten (cardinal दश). Swapping them, or using one word for both, conflates order with quantity.

**22. (D)** Ordinals agree in gender, so with the feminine कन्या the form becomes दशमी (feminine -ī). It does not stay masculine, switch to a cardinal, or take a neuter ending.

**23. (B)** The correct ascending ordinal run is द्वितीयः, चतुर्थः, षष्ठः, अष्टमः, दशमः. The first option lists cardinals, the second is out of order, and the last descends.

**24. (C)** Leaving himself out, each counter stops one ordinal short — he never reaches the topmost ordinal his own count would have had (the tenth slot, his own). It is not the first, the middle, or a random ordinal.

**25. (C)** The particle तु marks contrast/distinction, setting the tenth apart right after the ninth. It is not causal, not a double naming of one boy, and not temporal sequencing.

**26. (C)** Both are masculine nominative singular, so कः asks the identity of the very person दशमः names. Neither is accusative, and दशमः is a noun, not a verb.

**27. (B)** The understood verb is अस्ति, third person singular, agreeing with the singular subject दशमः/कः. It is not the second-person असि, the plural सन्ति, or a past tense, since the question is a present 'who is the tenth?'.

**28. (A)** नदीम् is the object crossed by the gerund तीर्त्वा, so it takes the accusative (कर्मणि द्वितीया). It is not the agent, gerunds do not put their object in the dative, and rivers are not inherently accusative.

**29. (D)** A gerund shares the main clause's subject, so the boys are the doers of both crossing and going. The river, an unnamed separate agent, or the bank are not the agents.

**30. (D)** Gerunds mark prior, completed actions in sequence: cross, then go to the forest, then eat. The finite verb (अखादन्) is last in time, not first, and the actions are not simultaneous or reordered.

**31. (B)** Only 'सः नदीं तीर्त्वा गृहम् अगच्छत्' uses the gerund correctly as a prior completed action before the finite verb. The others treat तीर्त्वा as a main predicate, double the crossing, or inflect it like a finite verb.

**32. (C)** तरन् is a present participle ('while crossing'), not the gerund तीर्त्वा ('having crossed'), so the completed-prior meaning is lost. The object नदीम् and the plural गताः are both correct as they stand.

**33. (B)** The initial अ is the augment (आगम) that marks the लङ्-लकार (imperfect past). It is not a negation, a future marker, or an imperative.

**34. (C)** Only गणयति → अगणयत् keeps the same root (गण्) from present to past. The others jump roots: गण्→गा, गा→गण्, and तृ→मस्ज्.

**35. (A)** अमज्जत् ('he sank') comes from the root मस्ज्, the verbal counterpart of the adjective मग्नः ('drowned'). The other roots give counted, crossed, and sang.

**36. (B)** Turning 'sang' into 'counted' requires changing the root गा to गण् (अगायत् → अगणयत्). It is not a prefix, not a final-letter swap, and the words are related through the contrast of roots, not unrelated.

**37. (A)** Crossing = तृ, singing = गा, counting = गण्, drowning = मस्ज्, giving तृ / गा / गण् / मस्ज्. The other sets mismatch a root (ज्ञा is 'know') or scramble the order.

**38. (A)** With ten boys (more than two), the precise word is कतमः, 'which of many'; कतरः is reserved for a choice of two. The generic कः loses the which-of-set nuance, and किम् asks about a thing.

**39. (B)** With exactly two paths, the right form is कतरेण ('by which of two') in the instrumental. कतमेन is for many, plain केन drops the two-way sense, and कतिभिः asks 'by how many'.

**40. (B)** कति asks 'how many', कदा 'when', कतरः 'which of two', कतमः 'which of many'. The other mappings scramble these or misread कति/कदा as who/what.

**41. (A)** कतरः ('which of two') is correct only when the choice is narrowed to two suspects. For ten boys कतमः is needed; for a quantity, कति; and कतरः asks neither 'when' nor a compound question.

**42. (D)** कतरः asks 'which of two', but there are ten boys, so the many-set word कतमः is required. कतरः is not 'who drowned', not a verb, and not a quantity word.

**43. (D)** The suffix -इकः here forms a word meaning 'one connected with / who travels the road' — a traveller. It is neither a diminutive, an action-noun, nor a negative.

**44. (A)** अ + अ coalesce into long आ (सवर्णदीर्घ), giving नवापि. The other forms wrongly insert a glide, apply guṇa/visarga sandhi, or elide incorrectly.

**45. (B)** Final अ + initial आ merge into a single long आ (सवर्णदीर्घ), yielding दशागताः. The distractors insert a glide, apply guṇa, or vṛddhi incorrectly.

**46. (A)** The final त् of तत् abuts the त् of त्वम् and the cluster stands as तत्त्वम् (a consonant junction). The त् is not dropped, not softened to द्, and no vowel is inserted.

**47. (B)** The anusvāra represents the म् of नदीम् before the following consonant त्. It is not an added long vowel, a meaningless letter, or a fused gerund ending.

**48. (C)** विषण्णाः is masculine nominative plural, agreeing with the plural subject बालकाः. It is not an accusative object, not singular, and not feminine.

**49. (B)** ततः here signals sequence — 'thereafter/then' — the joy follows the sadness in time. It is not a cause-from-joy, a place ('from there'), or a negation.

**50. (D)** दुःखिताः → सन्तुष्टाः runs negative → positive, like sad → delighted. The first option reverses direction, the second stays negative, and the third stays within surprise.

**51. (D)** विस्मय (wonder/surprise) is the intermediate reaction — the shock at hearing 'ten' — felt before the joy settles. It is not a synonym for joy, the starting state, or the cause of the feared drowning.

**52. (A)** Only the set with traveller / he-counted / drowned / overjoyed is fully correct. The others wrongly gloss पथिकः as 'road', अगणयत् as 'sang', or flip मग्नः/प्रहृष्टाः to their opposites.

**53. (C)** The correct set: तीर्त्वा is an indeclinable gerund, अगणयत् is imperfect past, नदीम् is the accusative object, दशमः is an ordinal. The others mislabel तीर्त्वा as future, अगणयत् as present, नदीम् as nominative/dative, or दशमः as cardinal.

**54. (C)** The root error is that no one was ever lost: the 'tenth' was the uncounted self, and the traveller revealed it rather than performing a rescue. River-vs-lake and walking-vs-diving are trivial details, not the core.

**55. (B)** The fully correct chain: having crossed, each counted nine, until the traveller showed 'दशमः त्वम् असि' — the Self was never lost. The others misgloss तीर्त्वा as future, अगणयत् as 'sang', wrongly make the count ten, or wrongly make the traveller the missing tenth.

**56. (D)** The grammar tools are the very means of telling and probing the Self-parable: ordinals place the tenth, the gerund sequences crossing-then-counting, the past narrates, कतमः asks which-of-many. The grammar is neither unrelated padding, nor the sole point, nor proof of a real loss.

**57. (A)** Adding 'and myself one' at the end re-includes himself as an explicitly counted item, so he reaches the true ten without a traveller. He does not stay stuck at nine, double himself to eleven, or make counting impossible.

**58. (C)** The same self-reference blind spot recurs with the eye: it sees everything but cannot directly see itself — the seer of seeing is unseen, just as the counter of counting is uncounted. Mirrors, intrinsic arithmetic, and water-distortion are not the structural analogue.

**59. (D)** Because the traveller directly addresses the listener with त्वम्, the verb agrees as second-person singular असि. It is not the third-person अस्ति (the subject is 'you'), not a plural, and not a past form.

**60. (B)** अगणयम् is first-person singular past ('I counted'), while अगणयत् is third-person singular past ('he counted'); they differ in person, not number, tense being the same imperfect past.
