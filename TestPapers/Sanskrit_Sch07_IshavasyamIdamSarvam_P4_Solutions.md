## Worked Solutions

**1. (C)** The first line establishes that the Lord pervades the whole world; the second draws the ethical conclusion that one should enjoy it in a spirit of renunciation, not grasping it as private property. It neither forbids all use nor permits unlimited grabbing, and it does not restrict use to priests.

**2. (A)** The long final आ on ईशा is the instrumental singular ending (ईश + आ). It conveys 'by the Lord', the means/agent of the pervading, exactly as the gloss ईश्वरेण व्याप्तम् shows with its instrumental ईश्वरेण.

**3. (D)** ईशा already ends in the long instrumental आ and वास्यम् begins with the consonant व, so no vowel-merger occurs at the seam; the long आ is the case ending, not the product of two short vowels combining into ए.

**4. (B)** Word-final म् before a consonant in the same breath-group is regularly written as anusvara, so इदम् सर्वम् appears as इदं सर्वम्. It does not turn into a visarga, nor double, nor vanish.

**5. (C)** त्यक्त is the past participle of त्यज् ('to abandon'); त्यक्तेन adds the instrumental ending to mean 'by/through renunciation'. It is not a finite verb of any tense or mood.

**6. (A)** भुञ्जीथाः is the optative (विधिलिङ्) 2nd-singular ātmanepada of भुज्, carrying the sense 'you should enjoy / let you enjoy'. It is neither a present, future, nor past form.

**7. (A)** Sanskrit forbids an action with मा plus an aorist stem that drops the past augment अ-; so मा गृधः means 'do not covet'. The ordinary negative न negates statements, not commands, and no future marker is present.

**8. (D)** The defining feature of the prohibitive is मा + an aorist form stripped of its augment अ-; this is a grammatical rule, not a mere metrical licence, and मा does not supply the augment.

**9. (C)** The particle स्वित् attaches to interrogatives to make them indefinite; कस्य ('of whom') + स्वित् yields 'of anyone whatever', so मा गृधः कस्यस्विद्धनम् means 'do not covet anyone's wealth'.

**10. (B)** By voicing-assimilation, an unvoiced stop like त् becomes its voiced counterpart द् before a voiced sound such as ध, giving कस्यस्विद्धनम्. It neither doubles, becomes a visarga, nor disappears.

**11. (A)** The instrumental त्यक्तेन names the inner attitude of detachment as the MEANS of right enjoyment; one enjoys the world while holding nothing as one's own. It is not about scavenging others' leavings, nor a total ban on enjoyment, nor a deferral.

**12. (A)** Because everything is pervaded by the Lord, no part of it is anyone's absolute possession; this is why one enjoys with non-attachment and refrains from coveting. The verse neither threatens users, bans all enjoyment, nor declares the world unreal.

**13. (D)** यत्किञ्च is an indefinite expression 'whatever, whatsoever', reinforcing सर्वम् so that absolutely everything that moves in the world is included. It neither restricts to one thing nor poses a question.

**14. (D)** जगत्याम् is locative ('in the world'), naming the container, while जगत् here names that which moves within it; the repetition contrasts the encompassing world with each transient thing in it. Neither form is a verb or a vocative.

**15. (B)** Joining 'enjoy' with 'by renunciation' yields a paradox resolved as full engagement without attachment, not withdrawal, not indifference, and not unrestrained consumption.

**16. (B)** Because all wealth is pervaded by the Lord, the verse targets the attitude of possessive grasping itself, not just the act of acquiring more. It does not demand instant dispossession, nor forbid earning, nor confine itself to others' goods alone.

**17. (A)** The optative (विधिलिङ्) expresses what one ought to do, fitting the verse's tone of wise counsel; it is gentler than a direct imperative, not stronger, and is neither past nor a statement of doubt.

**18. (C)** The -एन ending on a-stems is the instrumental singular; both words name the means by which something is done. They are not genitives, locatives, or accusatives.

**19. (C)** Keeping the optative middle but shifting from singular to 2nd-plural gives भुञ्जीध्वम् ('you all should enjoy'). भुञ्जीत is 3rd-singular, भोक्ष्यथे is future, and अभुङ्क्थाः is past.

**20. (D)** Adding the nominative त्वम् preserves the prohibitive मा + aorist गृधः. The other options shift to a present statement, a future, or a passive — none of which is a prohibition.

**21. (C)** The instrumental ईशा is exactly what gives the verse its sense 'pervaded by the Lord' (cf. gloss ईश्वरेण व्याप्तम्); Reader X's अवास्यम् would negate the very dwelling the verse asserts. The readings are not equivalent.

**22. (D)** वास्यम् is a gerundive ('to be enveloped/dwelt-in'); combined with the instrumental ईशा it yields 'to be pervaded by the Lord'. It is neither an agent noun, a past verb, nor a place-noun.

**23. (B)** The instrumental case on ईशा fixes the Lord as the agent and the nominative इदं सर्वम् as the patient; the directionality cannot be reversed. 'All this' is not instrumental, and ईशा is not nominative.

**24. (A)** The verse's ideal is detached enjoyment and non-coveting; its opposite is the grasping hoarder. The other options all describe the ideal, not its contrary.

**25. (A)** 'By means of X' is precisely the instrumental's job, so त्यक्तेन is correct. An accusative would make renunciation the thing enjoyed, which is not the sense.

**26. (C)** भुञ्जीथाः is the optative of counsel and मा गृधः is the prohibitive of forbidding; they are different but complementary moods. Neither is a plain indicative, and the labels in the other options are misassigned.

**27. (C)** The verse affirms right enjoyment (भुञ्जीथाः) and forbids covetous grasping (मा गृधः) — a single coherent ethic of detached engagement. It neither bans enjoyment outright nor endorses hoarding.

**28. (B)** Classical prohibition uses मा with the augmentless aorist (गृधः); मा गृह्णीहि mixes in the present-imperative stem, which the strict construction avoids. The required stem is the aorist, not the future, optative, or perfect.

**29. (D)** The rhetorical 'whose is wealth?' answers itself: ultimately no one's, since all is the Lord's; this undercuts the very idea of coveting another's property. It neither legitimises seizure nor reserves wealth for priests, nor denies that wealth exists.

**30. (D)** 'You should enjoy' assumes one is actively living and using the world; detachment governs the ATTITUDE, not whether one acts. It implies neither idleness, nor accumulation, nor withdrawal.

**31. (B)** All-pervasion plus omnipotence makes the Lord the real owner of all, dissolving the basis for possessive grasping. The verse places the duty of non-greed on the person, not on a wealth-seizing or temple-bound deity.

**32. (A)** The optative marker -ई- (not the future -स्य-) is what shapes भुञ्जीथाः, giving 'you should enjoy'. The mistake is in the MOOD/tense, not merely person or voice.

**33. (C)** ईश्वरेण and ईशा are both instrumentals of the Lord; that shared case is why the gloss and the verse-word mean the same thing. The Lord is not genitive or vocative here, and the world is not the instrument.

**34. (B)** The command to enjoy presupposes a real world worth living in; detachment qualifies how one holds it, not whether it exists. भुञ्जीथाः means 'enjoy', not 'renounce' or 'flee'.

**35. (B)** Just as मा गृधः uses मा + augmentless aorist, a prohibition of 'enjoy' would use मा + the aorist of भुज् (मा भुक्थाः). The plain न negates statements, not commands, and no future or present form fits a prohibition.

**36. (A)** ईशा and त्यक्तेन are both instrumentals (a noun and a participle), भुञ्जीथाः is an optative finite verb, and गृधः is the aorist used in the prohibitive. The other rows misclassify one or more of these.

**37. (B)** The bridge from 'the Lord pervades all' to 'do not covet' is the realisation that nothing is truly one's own; remove it and the conclusion no longer follows. The temple, pleasantness, and motion premises are not load-bearing for this inference.

**38. (D)** तेन is the instrumental of तद् ('that'), agreeing with the instrumental त्यक्तेन; together 'by that renunciation'. They are not dative, genitive, or a subject-verb pair.

**39. (C)** Continuing rightful work while releasing attachment to gain is exactly 'enjoyment through renunciation'. The verse does not demand abandoning livelihood, the merchant is not disgracing his family, and सर्वशक्तिमान् describes the Lord, not him.

**40. (B)** मा is the particle of prohibition (don't!), while न negates declarative statements; they are not interchangeable. The roles are not reversed, and neither marks number.

**41. (A)** The verse counsels detached enjoyment plus non-coveting, grounded in the Lord's pervasion of all. It endorses neither total renunciation, nor accumulation, nor indifference.

**42. (D)** ईशा is ईश plus the instrumental ending आ, giving 'by the Lord'; it is not the unmodified root ईश्, not an '-ish'-style adjective, and not a vocative.

**43. (A)** मा गृधः commands the addressee not to act; सः धनं न इच्छति simply describes someone's lack of desire. One is a prohibition, the other a report — not the reverse, and not both reports.

**44. (C)** The universality of सर्वम् is what makes the non-greed conclusion apply to all wealth without exception; a partial claim would leave room for coveting the unpervaded remainder. The phrase exempts nothing and is logically load-bearing, not mere decoration.

**45. (C)** The correct row pairs the right meaning, the instrumental ईशा, and the detachment/non-greed ethic. Each wrong row contains at least one false meaning, case, or ethical inversion.

**46. (B)** Because the verse says to ENJOY by renunciation, the renunciation is inward (an attitude), compatible with still using things; it is not the physical disposal, donation, or destruction of objects.

**47. (B)** The root sense of गृध् is craving/coveting, an inner state; forbidding it covers more than the single act of theft. गृधः does not mean 'give', and the prohibition is not restricted to priests' wealth or to permitting trade-over-theft.

**48. (B)** The instrumental covers both agent and means; here ईशा is the divine agent of pervading, while त्यक्तेन is the means of right enjoyment. The roles are not reversed, and the instrumental is not limited to one function.

**49. (C)** Coveting and hoarding against the verse's ethic exemplifies disgraceful conduct, captured by कुलकलङ्कः. सर्वशक्तिमान् describes the Lord, गन्धर्वः is a semi-divine being, and बुद्धिमान् denotes the wise — none fits the greedy hoarder.

**50. (D)** The gerundive भोक्तव्यम् ('should be enjoyed') matches the optative's sense of what ought to be done. The past, future, and present forms each lose the 'should' force.

**51. (D)** Line 1 states what IS the case (the Lord pervades all) and line 2 derives what one OUGHT to do; the fact grounds the duty. The lines are related, the order is fact-then-duty, and line 1 is not itself a command.

**52. (C)** सर्वम् here is the nominative subject ('all this [is pervaded]'); reading it as a vocative would turn an assertion into an exclamation, breaking the verse's factual claim. It is neither instrumental nor a motion-object.

**53. (B)** Optative (counsel) and prohibitive (forbidding) both concern duty — what ought and ought not to be done — distinguishing them from the indicative's statements of fact. They do not assert past, present, or future facts.

**54. (A)** Making 'whose' indefinite (anyone's) closes every loophole, so no one's wealth is exempt from the ban on coveting. It neither narrows to one person, turns the command into a mere question, nor weakens it.

**55. (C)** The first line removes the very basis of private ownership, which is why coveting another's wealth is baseless and prohibited in the second. The verse neither promises divine redistribution, sanctifies hoarding, nor denies the existence of wealth.

**56. (A)** Only the first row gets every element right: the instrumental ईशा, the anusvara sandhi इदं सर्वम्, the optative भुञ्जीथाः, the prohibitive मा गृधः, and the detached-enjoyment/non-greed ethic. Each other row contains at least one false case, sandhi, mood, or ethical claim.

**57. (A)** गृध् denotes craving or greed, so मा गृधः forbids covetousness. It is the opposite of generosity, and has nothing to do with forgetting or revering.

**58. (D)** भुञ्जीथाः is the middle (ātmanepada) optative meaning 'you should enjoy' for oneself, with active sense, not a passive 'be enjoyed'. Q misreads the voice; the readings are not equivalent, and the form is not 3rd-person past.

**59. (D)** Detached enjoyment-as-trusteeship — using and benefiting without claiming absolute ownership — is the verse's direct application. It rules out possessive seizure, total abdication, and negligent indifference alike.

**60. (D)** Both the call to enjoy and the ban on coveting flow from one root virtue: non-attachment, which engages life without grasping. It is neither world-rejecting asceticism, nor acquisitiveness, nor indifference.
