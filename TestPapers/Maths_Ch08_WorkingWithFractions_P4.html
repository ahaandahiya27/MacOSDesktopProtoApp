Worked Solutions

1. (A) Convert all: 5/4 × 12/5 × 7/6. Multiply: (5×12×7)/(4×5×6) = 420/120 = 7/2 = 3 1/2. A common slip is keeping the whole-number parts separate (1×2×1 = 2 plus fractions) which loses the cross terms; the only correct route is converting to improper fractions first.

2. (C) Start 4/5. After removing 1/4: remaining = 4/5 × 3/4 = 3/5. After removing 1/3 of that: 3/5 × 2/3 = 2/5. Each 'of what remains' must multiply the leftover, not the original 4/5; subtracting the fractions of the whole instead gives the wrong leftover.

3. (A) 15/4 ÷ 3/2 ÷ 5/2. Left to right: 15/4 × 2/3 = 30/12 = 5/2; then 5/2 × 2/5 = 10/10 = 1. Only the divisor is flipped at each step; flipping the dividend or chaining the division in the wrong order produces a different value.

4. (B) After rent, 3/5 left. After food, 2/3 of that remains: 3/5 × 2/3 = 2/5 of salary = Rs 4800. So salary = 4800 × 5/2 = Rs 12000. Treating the 1/3 as 1/3 of the whole salary (not of the remainder) misvalues what is left.

5. (D) Use denominator 90: 7/9 = 70/90, 5/6 = 75/90, 11/15 = 66/90, 13/18 = 65/90. The largest numerator over 90 is 75/90 = 5/6. Comparing only numerators or only denominators without a common base misranks them.

6. (B) 5/6 − 1/4 = 10/12 − 3/12 = 7/12. 2/3 + 1/2 = 4/6 + 3/6 = 7/6. Product: 7/12 × 7/6 = 49/72, already in lowest terms. Adding numerators and denominators straight across when summing the bracket (e.g. 7/13) is the classic error to avoid.

7. (A) 9/4 × 4/3 = 36/12 = 3; then 3 ÷ 6/5 = 3 × 5/6 = 15/6 = 5/2 = 2 1/2. Multiplying by 1 1/5 instead of dividing (i.e. not flipping the divisor) gives 3 × 6/5 = 18/5 = 3 3/5, the trap value.

8. (A) Distance = 5/8 − 1/4 = 5/8 − 2/8 = 3/8. One-third of it = 3/8 × 1/3 = 1/8. Point = 1/4 + 1/8 = 2/8 + 1/8 = 3/8. Taking the midpoint (1/2 the gap) instead of one-third gives 7/16, and one-third of the way being confused with one-third of 5/8 gives wrong values.

9. (B) Cows brown: 3/8 × 2/3 = 6/24 = 1/4 of herd = 8/32. Non-cows = 5/8; their brown share: 5/8 × 1/4 = 5/32 of herd. Total brown = 8/32 + 5/32 = 13/32. Simply averaging 2/3 and 1/4 ignores the unequal group sizes.

10. (D) Dividing by a fraction less than 1 enlarges the number, so 30 ÷ 6/7 = 30 × 7/6 = 35 > 30. Confusing division by a proper fraction with multiplication by it (which would shrink to under 30) gives the wrong direction.

11. (C) Fraction left = 4/7 × 3/4 × 2/3 = (4×3×2)/(7×4×3) = 24/84 = 2/7. So 2/7 of start = 16, start = 16 × 7/2 = 56. Each 'of the remainder' must keep multiplying the running leftover; subtracting the named fractions from 1 separately mis-states the leftover.

12. (B) LCM of 12, 8, 18 is 72: 7/12 = 42/72, 5/8 = 45/72, 11/18 = 44/72. Ascending: 42 < 44 < 45, i.e. 7/12 < 11/18 < 5/8. Ranking by numerator size (7 > 5) or denominator size alone gives a wrong order.

13. (D) Rise = 13/15 − 3/5 = 13/15 − 9/15 = 4/15 of capacity = 8 litres. Capacity = 8 × 15/4 = 30 litres. Subtracting the fractions wrongly (e.g. 13/15 − 3/5 read as 13/15 − 3/15) gives 10/15 and a wrong capacity.

14. (A) 2 2/5 = 12/5, 1 1/3 = 4/3. Divide: 12/5 × 3/4 = 36/20 = 9/5. Then 5/6 of 9/5 = 5/6 × 9/5 = 45/30 = 3/2 = 1 1/2. Multiplying instead of dividing inside the bracket (12/5 × 4/3) breaks the result.

15. (C) 3/4 + 5/6 = 9/12 + 10/12 = 19/12. 2/3 − 1/4 = 8/12 − 3/12 = 5/12. Difference: 19/12 − 5/12 = 14/12 = 7/6 = 1 1/6. Dropping the brackets and combining left-to-right with sign errors gives a different total.

16. (B) Filled = 5/12 + 3/8 = 10/24 + 9/24 = 19/24. Drained 1/3, so 2/3 remains: 19/24 × 2/3 = 38/72 = 19/36. Draining 1/3 of the full tank (not of the water present) over-removes water.

17. (D) We need 9/4 ÷ x = 3/5, so x = (9/4) ÷ (3/5) = 9/4 × 5/3 = 45/12 = 15/4 = 3 3/4. Multiplying 9/4 by 3/5 instead of dividing gives 27/20, the trap; the unknown divisor is found by dividing dividend by quotient.

18. (D) 5 1/4 = 21/4 m, each piece 7/8 m. 21/4 ÷ 7/8 = 21/4 × 8/7 = 168/28 = 6 exactly. So 6 full pieces and nothing left. Reading 21/4 ÷ 7/8 as 21/4 × 7/8 (no flip) gives a fraction under 6 and a false leftover.

19. (C) Half of 1/5 of 90 = 1/2 × 1/5 × 90 = 90/10 = 9. This equals 1/3 of the second, so second = 9 × 3 = 27. Forgetting the 'half' (using only 1/5 of 90 = 18, then ×3 = 54) is the trap.

20. (A) 4/3 ÷ 2/5 = 4/3 × 5/2 = 20/6 = 10/3; then 10/3 ÷ 5/3 = 10/3 × 3/5 = 30/15 = 2. Each division flips only its own divisor; collapsing both divisors at once or skipping a flip changes the answer.

21. (B) Combined rate = 2/7 + 5/14 = 4/14 + 5/14 = 9/14 of the tank per hour. Time = 1 ÷ 9/14 = 14/9 hours. Adding the times instead of the rates, or forgetting to take the reciprocal of the combined rate, gives 9/14.

22. (B) 9/2 × 8/3 = 72/6 = 12; then 12 ÷ 18/5 = 12 × 5/18 = 60/18 = 10/3 = 3 1/3. Multiplying by 3 3/5 instead of dividing (12 × 18/5) gives 43 1/5, the trap.

23. (D) Field = 6/7. Maize = 1/2 × 6/7 = 3/7; remaining = 3/7. Cotton = 3/5 × 3/7 = 9/35 acre. Applying 3/5 to the whole 6/7 (not to the 3/7 remaining) gives 18/35, the trap.

24. (C) Need 5/8 × x = 15/8, so x = 15/8 ÷ 5/8 = 15/8 × 8/5 = 120/40 = 3. Multiplying 5/8 by 15/8 instead of dividing gives 75/64, the trap; the missing factor is target divided by the known factor.

25. (B) After first: 3/4 left. After second (1/3 of that): 3/4 × 2/3 = 1/2 left. Third takes 2/5 of 1/2 = 1/5 of jar = 8 nuts, so jar = 8 × 5 = 40. Treating each fraction as of the whole jar mis-sizes the third child's share.

26. (B) 2/3 ÷ 4/9 = 2/3 × 9/4 = 18/12 = 3/2. 3/10 ÷ 9/5 = 3/10 × 5/9 = 15/90 = 1/6. Product: 3/2 × 1/6 = 3/12 = 1/4. Flipping the wrong term in either division reverses the size of each factor.

27. (A) 'of' means multiply: 7/8 × 16/21 × 3/4 × 56. Step by step: 7/8 × 56 = 49; 49 × 16/21 = 784/21 = 112/3; 112/3 × 3/4 = 336/12 = 28. Reordering correctly: the exact value is 28. Mistaking any 'of' for division would shrink or grow the chain wrongly.

28. (A) 5 1/3 = 16/3, 1 1/9 = 10/9. Divide: 16/3 × 9/10 = 144/30 = 24/5 = 4 4/5. Forgetting to convert the mixed numbers (e.g. 5 ÷ 1 dealt with separately) destroys the result.

29. (A) Hidden fraction = 2/9 + 1/3 = 2/9 + 3/9 = 5/9. Above = 1 − 5/9 = 4/9 of pole = 20 m. Pole = 20 × 9/4 = 180/4 = 45 m. Adding the buried fractions with mismatched denominators (e.g. 3/12) mis-states the above-water fraction.

30. (C) Bracket first: 3/4 × 2/5 = 6/20 = 3/10. Then 9/10 ÷ 3/10 = 9/10 × 10/3 = 90/30 = 3. Dividing before doing the bracket, or flipping 9/10 instead of the divisor, gives a wrong value.

31. (D) Day 1: 5/12 × 480 = 200 read, 280 left. Day 2: 2/7 × 280 = 80 read, 280 − 80 = 200 unread. Taking 2/7 of the whole 480 (not of the 280 remaining) over-counts the second day's reading.

32. (D) 1/2 × 2/5 × N = 24, so 1/5 × N = 24, N = 120. Then 3/4 of 120 = 90. Forgetting the 'half' (using 2/5 × N = 24, N = 60) gives a wrong N and a trap of 45.

33. (B) Read 'of' as multiply, evaluate the products first: (1/3 of 6) ÷ (1/2 of 8) = 2 ÷ 4 = 1/2. Treating 'of' as a plain ÷ chain left-to-right, e.g. 1/3 × 6 ÷ 1/2 × 8 = 32, is the classic precedence trap.

34. (C) 3 1/3 = 10/3 kg; 10/3 ÷ 5/12 = 10/3 × 12/5 = 120/15 = 8 exactly. So 8 full pouches, nothing left. Dividing without flipping (10/3 × 5/12) gives a small fraction and a false leftover.

35. (C) 4/5 of 7/8 = 4/5 × 7/8 = 28/40 = 7/10. 7/8 ÷ 4/5 = 7/8 × 5/4 = 35/32, which is more than 1 and so larger than 7/10. Dividing by a proper fraction (4/5) enlarges, while multiplying by it shrinks; assuming both give the same value is the trap.

36. (D) Full 9/4 L. After 1/3 out, 2/3 remain: 9/4 × 2/3 = 18/12 = 3/2. After 2/5 out, 3/5 remain: 3/2 × 3/5 = 9/10 L. Subtracting 1/3 and 2/5 as fractions of the whole bottle over-removes liquid.

37. (A) Sum: 5/2 + 4/3 = 15/6 + 8/6 = 23/6. Difference: 7/6 − 2/3 = 7/6 − 4/6 = 3/6 = 1/2. Divide: 23/6 ÷ 1/2 = 23/6 × 2/1 = 46/6 = 23/3 = 7 2/3. Not flipping the 1/2 divisor (multiplying by 1/2) halves the result instead.

38. (B) After day 1: 7/9 left. Day 2 does 1/3 of 7/9, leaving 2/3 × 7/9 = 14/27. Day 3 does 1/2 of that, leaving 1/2 × 14/27 = 7/27 undone. Applying each fraction to the whole job rather than the running remainder mis-tracks the leftover.

39. (A) New width = 5/6 × 9/4 = 45/24 = 15/8 m. Excess over 3/2 = 15/8 − 12/8 = 3/8 m. Forgetting to subtract the 1 1/2 (just giving 15/8) or comparing to the original width is the trap.

40. (C) 15/8 ÷ 3/4 = 15/8 × 4/3 = 60/24 = 5/2; then 5/2 ÷ 5/4 = 5/2 × 4/5 = 20/10 = 2. Flipping the dividend instead of each divisor, or chaining out of order, gives a wrong product.

41. (B) Mangoes + guavas = 4/9 + 1/3 = 4/9 + 3/9 = 7/9. Bananas = 2/9 of crate = 60, so crate = 60 × 9/2 = 270. Adding denominators (4/9 + 1/3 read as 5/12) mis-states the banana fraction.

42. (C) Each hour 4/5 of the current water remains. After 3 hours: (4/5)^3 = 64/125. Subtracting 1/5 three times from the whole (1 − 3/5 = 2/5) wrongly treats the leak as a fixed fraction of the original.

43. (D) 16/5 × 15/8 × 5/12 = (16×15×5)/(5×8×12) = 1200/480 = 5/2 = 2 1/2. Keeping whole parts separate (3×1 = 3 plus fraction bits) loses the cross products and misses the answer.

44. (D) Oats per cup of honey = (3/4) ÷ (2/5) = 3/4 × 5/2 = 15/8 cups. For 3/2 cups honey: 15/8 × 3/2 = 45/16 = 2 13/16 cups. Multiplying 3/4 by 2/5 (instead of dividing) to get the rate understates the oats needed.

45. (B) Net fill rate = 5/9 − 1/6 = 10/18 − 3/18 = 7/18 per hour. Adding the rates (A plus B) instead of subtracting the drain over-counts the fill.

46. (C) Paint per whole room = (9/2) ÷ (3/5) = 9/2 × 5/3 = 45/6 = 15/2 = 7 1/2 L. Multiplying 9/2 by 3/5 (instead of dividing) gives 27/10 = 2 7/10, the trap.

47. (A) 3/8 N − 1/4 N = 6. 3/8 − 2/8 = 1/8, so 1/8 N = 6, N = 48. Subtracting the fractions wrongly (3/8 − 1/4 read as 2/4) or dividing 6 by the wrong fraction gives the trap values.

48. (A) 7/8 − 1/3 = 21/24 − 8/24 = 13/24. 5/6 − 1/4 = 10/12 − 3/12 = 7/12 = 14/24. Divide: 13/24 ÷ 14/24 = 13/24 × 24/14 = 13/14. Flipping the dividend instead of the divisor inverts the answer to 14/13.

49. (A) 63/4 m. After selling 4/9, remaining = 5/9 × 63/4 = 315/36 = 35/4 = 8 3/4. After selling 2/5 of that, remaining = 3/5 × 35/4 = 105/20 = 21/4 = 5 1/4 m. Taking 2/5 of the whole 63/4 m, not of the remainder, over-sells.

50. (C) 6 ÷ 2/5 = 6 × 5/2 = 15; 15 ÷ 3 = 5; 5 ÷ 5/4 = 5 × 4/5 = 4. Each division flips only its own divisor and is taken left to right; collapsing the chain or skipping a flip changes the value.

51. (D) Seniors = 7/12 × 720 = 420. Music = 3/7 × 420 = 180. Both = 1/2 × 180 = 90. Forgetting the final 1/2 (stopping at 180) or applying the 3/7 to all 720 mis-counts the group.

52. (D) 12 × 3/8 = 9/2 = 4.5; 12 ÷ 8/3 = 12 × 3/8 = 9/2 = 4.5; 12 × 7/4 = 21; 12 ÷ 3/8 = 12 × 8/3 = 32. The largest is 32 from 12 ÷ 3/8: dividing by a small proper fraction enlarges the most. Treating ÷ 3/8 as × 3/8 would instead shrink it to 4.5, the trap.

53. (B) AB = 11/6 − 2/3 = 11/6 − 4/6 = 7/6. AP = 2/5 × 7/6 = 14/30 = 7/15. P = 2/3 + 7/15 = 10/15 + 7/15 = 17/15. Taking the midpoint (1/2 of AB) instead of 2/5, or forgetting to add A's position back, gives the trap values.

54. (B) Bracket: 3/2 ÷ 2/3 = 3/2 × 3/2 = 9/4. Then 9/4 ÷ 9/4 = 1. Evaluating left-to-right without the bracket (2 1/4 ÷ 1 1/2 first) gives a different value; the bracketed division must be done first.

55. (C) Dividing by a fraction GREATER than 1 (e.g. ÷ 5/4) actually shrinks the number, so that claim is false. The other three are true: dividing by a proper fraction enlarges, multiplying by a proper fraction shrinks, and the reciprocal of an improper fraction is a proper fraction.

56. (A) Rise = 5/8 − 3/8 = 2/8 = 1/4 of capacity = 14 L. Capacity = 14 × 4 = 56 L. Subtracting the fractions wrongly, or dividing 14 by the wrong fraction, mis-sizes the rise.

57. (D) 8/3 ÷ 6/5 = 8/3 × 5/6 = 40/18 = 20/9; then 20/9 ÷ 5/9 = 20/9 × 9/5 = 180/45 = 4. Flipping the wrong fraction at either step, or not converting the mixed numbers, breaks the chain.

58. (C) Used = 1/4 + 1/6 + 1/3 = 3/12 + 2/12 + 4/12 = 9/12 = 3/4. Rest = 1 − 3/4 = 1/4. Adding denominators or using a wrong common denominator gives a false used-fraction and a wrong remainder.

59. (C) Pulp fraction = 2/9 + 1/6 = 4/18 + 3/18 = 7/18. Water = 11/18 of mix = 110 mL, so mix = 110 × 18/11 = 180 mL. Adding the pulp fractions with mismatched denominators (e.g. 3/15) mis-sizes the water share.

60. (D) All 'of' mean multiply: 2/3 × 9/5 × 5/6 × 9/4 = (2·9·5·9)/(3·5·6·4) = 810/360 = 9/4 = 2 1/4. Mistaking any 'of' for division, or keeping the whole-number parts separate, would change the chain.
