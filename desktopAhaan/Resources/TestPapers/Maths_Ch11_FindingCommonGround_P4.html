Worked Solutions

1. (C) HCF uses the lowest power of each COMMON prime. Common primes: 2 (min of 2², 2²) → 2²; 3 (min of 3², 3¹) → 3; 7 (min of 7², 7²) → 7². The prime 5 is in only one number, so it is excluded. HCF = 4 × 3 × 49 = 588. Taking highest powers and including 5 gives the LCM 8820; dropping the common 3 gives 196; lowering the 7 to 7¹ gives 84.

2. (D) LCM uses the highest power of EVERY prime present: 2² (both) → 2²; 3 (max of 3², 3¹) → 3²; 5 (only in 2940) → 5; 7 (both 7²) → 7². LCM = 4 × 9 × 5 × 49 = 8820. Using lowest powers gives the HCF 588; raising 5 to 5² invents a power not present; adding exponents instead of taking the maximum gives the bloated last form.

3. (C) HCF (lowest powers) = 2³ × 3² × 5; LCM (highest powers) = 2⁴ × 3³ × 5². The ratio LCM:HCF = 2^(4−3) × 3^(3−2) × 5^(2−1) = 2 × 3 × 5 = 30. Forgetting the 5 gives 6; squaring the 5 gives 150; using only the 5 gives 5.

4. (B) 720 = 2⁴ × 3² × 5. A perfect-square factor needs even exponents: for 2⁴ choose 0, 2 or 4 (3 ways); for 3² choose 0 or 2 (2 ways); for 5¹ only 0 (1 way). Total = 3 × 2 × 1 = 6 (they are 1, 4, 16, 9, 36, 144). Using 2 × 2 = 4 forgets a choice; allowing 5 gives 8; counting only 1, 4, 9 gives 3.

5. (D) 5400 = 2³ × 3³ × 5². Number of factors = (3+1)(3+1)(2+1) = 4 × 4 × 3 = 48. Mis-adding (4 × 3 × 2 = 24) or using the exponents themselves (3 × 3 × 2 = 18) gives the traps.

6. (C) 600 = 2³ × 3 × 5². Total factors = (3+1)(1+1)(2+1) = 24. The ODD factors use 2⁰ only: 1 × 2 × 3 = 6. Even factors = 24 − 6 = 18. Forgetting to subtract the odd factors gives 24; counting only the odd ones gives 6.

7. (B) A multiple of 12 contains at least 2² × 3. Try 2² × 3 × 5 = 60: factor count (2+1)(1+1)(1+1) = 12 ✓. Smaller multiples of 12 — 12, 24, 36, 48 — have 6, 8, 9, 10 factors, none reaching 12. So 60 is smallest. 72 = 2³×3² also has 12 factors but is larger; 96 = 2⁵×3 has 12 factors but is larger; 36 has only 9 factors.

8. (C) 15 factors means (a+1)(b+1) = 15 = 5 × 3. With a > b take a+1 = 5, b+1 = 3, so a = 4, b = 2: N = 2⁴ × 3² = 16 × 9 = 144. Swapping to a = 2, b = 4 (which breaks a > b) gives 2² × 3⁴ = 324; using 15 = 15 × 1 forces a single prime, impossible here.

9. (D) 972 = 2² × 3⁵ = 4 × 243 = 972 ≤ 999 ✓. Nearby candidates: 2⁷ × 3² = 1152 and 2⁴ × 3⁴ = 1296 and 2 × 3⁶ = 1458 all exceed 999; 2⁵ × 3³ = 864 and 2⁸ × 3 = 768 and 2³ × 3⁴ = 648 are all smaller than 972. Hence 972 is the largest.

10. (D) 540 = 2² × 3³ × 5 and 1260 = 2² × 3² × 5 × 7. Lowest common powers: 2², 3² (min of 3³, 3²), 5; the 7 is not common. HCF = 4 × 9 × 5 = 180. Taking 3¹ gives 60; including 7 and highest powers gives the LCM 3780; dropping a 2 gives 90.

11. (B) 540 = 2² × 3³ × 5, 1260 = 2² × 3² × 5 × 7. Highest powers: 2², 3³, 5, 7. LCM = 4 × 27 × 5 × 7 = 3780. Lowest powers give the HCF 180; raising 5 to 5² gives 11340; halving (using 3² × … wrongly) gives 1890.

12. (D) The common factors of two numbers are exactly the factors of their HCF. 360 = 2³ × 3² × 5, 540 = 2² × 3³ × 5, so HCF = 2² × 3² × 5 = 180. Factors of 180 = (2+1)(2+1)(1+1) = 3 × 3 × 2 = 18. Counting factors of the LCM (1080) gives 24; using exponents directly (3 × 3 × 2 mis-done) gives smaller wrong counts.

13. (A) HCF × LCM = product of the two numbers, so LCM = 7560 ÷ 6 = 1260. Multiplying (7560 × 6 = 45360) instead of dividing, or adding 6 (1266), are the slips; 210 ignores the relation.

14. (C) Product of the numbers = HCF × LCM = 14 × 1764 = 24696. Other = 24696 ÷ 98 = 252. Check: 98 = 2 × 7², 252 = 2² × 3² × 7; HCF = 2 × 7 = 14 ✓, LCM = 2² × 3² × 7² = 1764 ✓. Dividing the LCM by 14 (1764 ÷ 14 = 126) instead of the product by 98 is the classic mistake.

15. (A) Product = HCF × LCM, and product is given as 18 × HCF². So HCF × LCM = 18 × HCF², giving LCM = 18 × HCF = 18 × 12 = 216. Using HCF² alone (144) or 18 × HCF² (2592) misreads the algebra; 18 ignores the HCF.

16. (A) LCM = 14 × 15 = 210, and product = HCF × LCM = 15 × 210 = 3150. Stopping at the LCM gives 210; squaring the HCF gives 225; an extra factor of 10 gives 31500.

17. (C) 8 and 15 are co-prime, so the numbers are 9 × 8 = 72 and 9 × 15 = 135. LCM = HCF × 8 × 15 = 9 × 120 = 1080. Check: 72 = 2³×3², 135 = 3³×5; LCM = 2³ × 3³ × 5 = 1080 ✓. Forgetting the HCF (8 × 15 = 120), or reading the larger number as the LCM (135), are the traps; multiplying both numbers gives 9720, near 8640.

18. (C) With co-prime ratio 5 : 8, the numbers are 5h and 8h, and LCM = 5 × 8 × h = 40h. So 40h = 360 ⟹ h = 9. (The numbers are 45 and 72.) Reading LCM ÷ 8 = 45 or LCM ÷ 5 = 72 gives the numbers, not the HCF; 40 forgets to divide.

19. (B) Write a = 12m, b = 12n with HCF(m, n) = 1; then 12 × m × n = 720 so m × n = 60 = 2² × 3 × 5, which has 3 distinct primes. Each prime's whole power goes to m or n, giving 2³ = 8 ordered co-prime pairs, hence 8 ÷ 2 = 4 unordered pairs: (1,60),(3,20),(4,15),(5,12) → (12,720),(36,240),(48,180),(60,144). The rule is 2^(k−1) with k = 3 distinct primes = 4.

20. (D) a = 15m, b = 15n, HCF(m,n) = 1, so m × n = 3150 ÷ 15 = 210 = 2 × 3 × 5 × 7 (k = 4 distinct primes). Number of unordered co-prime pairs = 2^(k−1) = 2³ = 8. Using k = 3 gives 4; forgetting to halve the 2⁴ ordered pairs gives 16.

21. (C) a = 7m, b = 7n, HCF(m,n) = 1, m × n = LCM ÷ HCF = 11 × 13 × 17, which has 3 distinct primes. Unordered co-prime pairs = 2^(3−1) = 4. (The shared 7 is already the HCF and is not a free prime.) Counting the 7 as a fourth free prime wrongly gives 8.

22. (A) Write the numbers as 48a and 48b with HCF(a, b) = 1; then 48(a + b) = 384 so a + b = 8. Co-prime pairs (a, b) with a + b = 8, a < b: (1,7) and (3,5) — note (2,6) and (4,4) are not co-prime. So 2 pairs: (48,336) and (144,240). Counting (2,6) as valid gives 3 or 4.

23. (B) For any two numbers, (their product) = HCF x LCM, so n x 36 = 12 x 360 = 4320, giving n = 4320 / 36 = 120. Check: 120 = 2^3 x 3 x 5 and 36 = 2^2 x 3^2, so HCF = 2^2 x 3 = 12 and LCM = 2^3 x 3^2 x 5 = 360, both correct. Dividing the LCM by the HCF (360 / 12 = 30) is not the relationship being tested; 60 = HCF x 5 gives HCF(60,36) = 12 but LCM(60,36) = 180, not 360; 24 and 48 also fail the LCM check.

24. (D) For any valid pair the HCF must divide the LCM. Test: 144 ÷ 12 = 12 ✓; 270 ÷ 9 = 30 ✓; 300 ÷ 15 = 20 ✓; but 204 ÷ 14 = 14.57… is not a whole number, so HCF 14 does not divide LCM 204 — impossible. The refuting check is 'does the HCF divide the LCM exactly?'

25. (C) The number of bouquets must divide all three counts, so it is the HCF. 252 = 2²×3²×7, 378 = 2×3³×7, 630 = 2×3²×5×7; lowest powers = 2 × 3² × 7 = 126. Taking 3¹ gives 42; missing the 2 gives 63; dropping a 3 and the 7 gives 18.

26. (B) Roses per bouquet = 252 ÷ 126 = 2. (Lilies 378 ÷ 126 = 3, tulips 630 ÷ 126 = 5.) Dividing the wrong flower count, e.g. 378 ÷ 126 = 3 or 630 ÷ 126 = 5, gives the lily/tulip counts instead of the roses.

27. (D) Convert: 792 cm and 540 cm. 792 = 2³ × 3² × 11, 540 = 2² × 3³ × 5; HCF (lowest powers) = 2² × 3² = 36 cm. Taking 3¹ gives 12; missing a 2 gives 18; taking highest powers gives a non-divisor like 108 (which does not divide 792).

28. (B) Tiles along length = 792 ÷ 36 = 22; along width = 540 ÷ 36 = 15. Total = 22 × 15 = 330. Using a wrong tile side or computing 22 + 15 wrongly, or 12 × 11, gives the distractors; 300 comes from rounding the room area carelessly.

29. (A) Convert to cm: 900, 1350, 1890. 900 = 2²×3²×5², 1350 = 2×3³×5², 1890 = 2×3³×5×7; lowest powers = 2 × 3² × 5 = 90 cm. Taking 3¹ × 5 gives 30; missing a 5 gives 45; taking highest powers gives 270 (not a common divisor).

30. (C) The can size is the HCF. 437 = 19 × 23, 551 = 19 × 29, 703 = 19 × 37; the only common prime factor is 19, so HCF = 19 L. 23, 29 and 37 each divide only one of the volumes, so picking 23 (a factor of 437 alone) or 17 fails on the others.

31. (C) Row size = HCF(1080, 1320). 1080 = 2³ × 3³ × 5, 1320 = 2³ × 3 × 5 × 11; lowest powers = 2³ × 3 × 5 = 120. Taking 3⁰ wrongly gives 40-type errors; using 2² gives 60; taking the LCM-style highest powers gives 240.

32. (B) Marigold rows = 1080 ÷ 120 = 9; rose rows = 1320 ÷ 120 = 11; total = 9 + 11 = 20. Reporting only one kind (9 or 11) or doubling (22) are the slips.

33. (C) String size = HCF. 391 = 17 × 23, 425 = 5² × 17, 493 = 17 × 29; the common prime is 17, so HCF = 17. 23 divides only 391, 25 only 425, 29 only 493 — none is common.

34. (C) Plot side = HCF(1517, 1591). 1591 − 1517 = 74 = 2 × 37; 1517 = 37 × 41, 1591 = 37 × 43, so HCF = 37 m. Plots along 1591 m = 1591 ÷ 37 = 43. Computing along the other side (1517 ÷ 37 = 41) gives 41; mistaking 37 for the count gives 37.

35. (A) Bundle size = HCF. 1224 = 2³ × 3² × 17, 1632 = 2⁵ × 3 × 17, 2040 = 2³ × 3 × 5 × 17; lowest powers = 2³ × 3 × 17 = 408. Taking 2² gives 204; dropping the 3 gives 136; taking highest powers gives a non-divisor like 612.

36. (D) Bag size = HCF(84, 126, 210). 84 = 2²×3×7, 126 = 2×3²×7, 210 = 2×3×5×7; lowest powers = 2 × 3 × 7 = 42 kg. Bags = 84/42 + 126/42 + 210/42 = 2 + 3 + 5 = 10. Reporting the bag size (42) instead of the count, or doubling the 10, are the traps.

37. (A) Next together = LCM(18, 24, 32). 18 = 2×3², 24 = 2³×3, 32 = 2⁵; highest powers = 2⁵ × 3² = 32 × 9 = 288. Using 2⁴ gives 144; doubling gives 576; missing a 3 gives 96.

38. (D) LCM(12, 18, 21, 28): 12 = 2²×3, 18 = 2×3², 21 = 3×7, 28 = 2²×7; highest powers = 2² × 3² × 7 = 4 × 9 × 7 = 252. Doubling gives 504; taking 3¹ gives 84; using 2×3²×7 gives 126.

39. (A) LCM(12, 21, 28) = 2² × 3 × 7 = 84 minutes = 1 h 24 min. Adding to 9:00 am gives 10:24 am. Doubling to 168 min gives 11:48 am; 9:84 am is the impossible 'add minutes without carrying'; 10:00 (60 min) ignores the 84.

40. (D) They meet again after LCM(56, 70, 84). 56 = 2³×7, 70 = 2×5×7, 84 = 2²×3×7; highest powers = 2³ × 3 × 5 × 7 = 840 s. Fastest runner has the smallest lap time (56 s), so laps = 840 ÷ 56 = 15. Using 70 s gives 12; using 84 s gives 10.

41. (B) They meet after LCM(54, 72, 90). 54 = 2×3³, 72 = 2³×3², 90 = 2×3²×5; highest powers = 2³ × 3³ × 5 = 8 × 27 × 5 = 1080 s. The slowest cyclist has the LONGEST lap time (90 s), so laps = 1080 ÷ 90 = 12. Using 72 s gives 15; using 54 s gives 20.

42. (A) Alignment returns after a whole number of turns of each gear, i.e. after LCM(36, 48) teeth pass any meshing point. 36 = 2²×3², 48 = 2⁴×3; LCM = 2⁴ × 3² = 144. Since teeth pass at the same rate at the mesh, the smaller gear has 144 teeth pass (= 4 full turns). Using HCF (12), doubling (288), or multiplying 36×48 (1728) are the traps.

43. (A) Together every LCM(9, 12, 15) = 180 s = 3 min. In 30 minutes there are 30 ÷ 3 = 10 such moments after the start. Counting the start too gives 11; halving gives 5.

44. (B) LCM(6, 8, 12, 16): 6 = 2×3, 8 = 2³, 12 = 2²×3, 16 = 2⁴; highest powers = 2⁴ × 3 = 48 s. In 240 s the joint tolls are at 0, 48, 96, 144, 192, 240 → 6 tolls. Excluding the start gives 5; using 24 s gives 10.

45. (C) LCM(36, 48) = 2⁴ × 3² = 144 min = 2 h 24 min. Adding to 7:00 am gives 9:24 am. Using 84 min (a wrong LCM) gives 8:24 am; doubling 144 gives 11:48 am.

46. (A) LCM(48, 72, 108): 48 = 2⁴×3, 72 = 2³×3², 108 = 2²×3³; highest powers = 2⁴ × 3³ = 16 × 27 = 432. Using 3² gives 144; using 2³ gives 216; doubling gives 864.

47. (B) Such a number is LCM(15, 20, 35) × k + 8. LCM = 2² × 3 × 5 × 7 = 420; smallest (k = 1) = 420 + 8 = 428. Forgetting the +8 gives 420; subtracting 8 gives 412; doubling the LCM gives 848.
