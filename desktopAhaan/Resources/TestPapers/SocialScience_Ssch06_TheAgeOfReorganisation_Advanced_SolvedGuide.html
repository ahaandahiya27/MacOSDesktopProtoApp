Set 1 · Q1–Q10

Q1

The "Age of Reorganisation" is best understood as the centuries when:

  • Aa single empire reorganised the whole subcontinent under one law
  • BIndia was ruled entirely by foreign powers
  • Cthe political map fragmented and was repeatedly redrawn by many competing kingdoms✓
  • Dall kingdoms united into one Silk-Route confederation

Working

✓

The age is named for fragmentation: the political map broke into many competing kingdoms and was redrawn again and again. A single unifying empire or wholesale foreign rule is the opposite of what happened.

Q2

The deepest reason the Mauryan empire could not hold together was that:

  • Agoverning so vast an area from one centre, with the costs of a huge army and bureaucracy, strained its resources from within✓
  • Bit had no capital city
  • Cit never collected any taxes
  • Dit lacked any written script

Working

✓

The deepest cause was internal strain — governing a vast territory from one centre, with the heavy cost of a huge army and bureaucracy, stretched Mauryan resources. The empire had a capital, taxes and the Brahmi script, so those "explanations" are false.

Q3

Pushyamitra, who founded the Shunga dynasty around 185 BCE, had been the Mauryas':

  • Acourt poet
  • Bchief tax collector
  • CBuddhist royal chaplain
  • Dcommander-in-chief of the army✓

Working

✓

Pushyamitra had been the Mauryas' commander-in-chief before founding the Shunga dynasty around 185 BCE — power passing from the army's head, not from a poet, a tax collector or a chaplain.

Q4

The Heliodorus pillar at Besnagar is historically striking because it shows:

  • Athe first Buddhist stupa ever built
  • Ban Indo-Greek ambassador who had adopted the worship of Vasudeva (Vishnu)✓
  • CAshoka's lion capital in miniature
  • Da Roman emperor's victory over the Shakas

Working

✓

The Heliodorus pillar records an Indo-Greek ambassador who had taken up the worship of Vasudeva (Vishnu) — vivid proof that foreigners adopted Indian religion. It is not a stupa, a copy of Ashoka's capital or a Roman record.

Q5

The Satavahanas built their power chiefly in the:

  • AGangetic plain around Pataliputra
  • BDeccan, between the Godavari and Krishna rivers✓
  • Cfar northwest beyond the Indus
  • DTamil country south of the Kaveri

Working

✓

The Satavahanas rose in the Deccan, in the country between the Godavari and Krishna rivers — not the Gangetic plain, the far northwest or the Tamil south.

Q6

Most Satavahana inscriptions are in Prakrit rather than Sanskrit, which suggests that:

  • Athey could not read Sanskrit at all
  • BSanskrit had not yet been invented
  • Cthey were forbidden by law to use Sanskrit
  • Dthey used the everyday language of their people for public records✓

Working

✓

Writing inscriptions in Prakrit means the Satavahanas used the everyday spoken language for public records. It does not mean Sanskrit was uninvented or forbidden; it was a choice to reach ordinary people.

Q7

The Satavahana king remembered for rolling back Shaka power and styled "lord of the Deccan" was:

  • AGautamiputra Satakarni✓
  • BPushyamitra Shunga
  • CMenander
  • DKharavela

Working

✓

Gautamiputra Satakarni is the Satavahana king remembered for rolling back Shaka power and styled lord of the Deccan. Pushyamitra, Menander and Kharavela belonged to other lines.

Q8

A unique window into Gautamiputra Satakarni's reign comes from an inscription composed by:

  • Athe Greek ambassador Megasthenes
  • Bthe scholar Kauṭilya
  • Chis mother, the queen Gautami Balashri✓
  • Dthe Chinese pilgrim Faxian

Working

✓

A famous inscription about his reign was composed by his mother, the queen Gautami Balashri. Megasthenes, Kauṭilya and Faxian are from other times and places.

Q9

That a queen could compose a royal inscription praising her son's deeds tells us that, in this age, royal women:

  • Acould hold genuine prestige and a public political voice✓
  • Bwere never mentioned in any record
  • Cruled only when no male heir survived
  • Dwere barred from all religious ritual

Working

✓

A queen composing a royal inscription shows that royal women of this age could hold real prestige and a public political voice — not that they were invisible, ruled only as a last resort, or were barred from ritual.

Q10

The Indo-Greek king best remembered in Indian tradition, partly through a dialogue with the monk Nagasena, was:

  • ASeleucus
  • BAlexander
  • CMenander (Milinda)✓
  • DKanishka

Working

✓

Menander, known in Indian tradition as Milinda, is the best-remembered Indo-Greek king, partly through his recorded dialogue with the monk Nagasena. Seleucus, Alexander and Kanishka are different rulers.

Q11

The text recording King Milinda's questions to a Buddhist monk is the:

  • A*Arthaśhāstra*
  • B*Periplus of the Erythraean Sea*
  • C*Indica*
  • D*Milinda-pañha* ("Questions of Milinda")✓

Working

✓

That dialogue survives as the *Milinda-pañha*, the "Questions of Milinda." The *Arthaśhāstra*, the *Periplus* and the *Indica* are unrelated texts.

Q12

Indo-Greek coins are prized by historians above all because they:

  • Awere never actually used in trade
  • Bcarry realistic portraits of named kings, helping fix who ruled and when✓
  • Cwere all made of pure iron
  • Dbore no writing of any kind

Working

✓

Indo-Greek coins are prized because they carry realistic portraits of named kings, which lets historians fix who ruled and when. They were real currency, not iron, and they did bear writing.

Q13

The Shakas (Scythians) who entered India are described as originally a people of:

  • Athe Tamil coast
  • Bthe Gangetic delta
  • Cthe island of Sri Lanka
  • DCentral Asian steppe herders pushed south and west✓

Working

✓

The Shakas (Scythians) were originally Central Asian steppe herders pushed south and west — not people of the Tamil coast, the Gangetic delta or Sri Lanka.

Q14

  • Bone of the earliest long royal inscriptions written in Sanskrit✓

Working

✓

Rudradaman's Junagadh rock inscription is celebrated as one of the earliest long royal inscriptions written in *Sanskrit* — not English or Greek, and not a sea-voyage log.

The same inscription records that Rudradaman repaired the dam of the Sudarshana lake, first built in Mauryan times — not the Great Wall, a road or a stupa.

  • AKanishka✓

Kanishka was the greatest Kushana ruler and a noted patron of Buddhism. Pushyamitra, Rudradaman and Gautamiputra ruled other dynasties.

Q17

The Kushana empire was unusual among Indian powers of the age because it:

The Kushana empire was unusual because it straddled both Central Asia and northern India, linking the two — not confined to a Tamil port, the Deccan or the islands.

Q18

Kanishka's chief city Purushapura is modern Peshawar — not Patna, Mathura or Ujjain.

  • Bgold✓
  • Cclay
  • Dwood

The Kushanas are famous for fine *gold* coinage — not iron, clay or wood.

Q20

Kanishka is traditionally linked with the convening of a major Buddhist:

  • Ahorse-sacrifice

  • Dcouncil, which helped spread Mahayana Buddhism✓

Kanishka is traditionally linked with convening a major Buddhist council, which helped spread Mahayana Buddhism — not a horse-sacrifice, a sea expedition or a Satavahana coronation.

Set 3 · Q21–Q30

Q21

The Gandhara school of art is distinctive for its:

  • AGreco-Roman influence — wavy hair, flowing robes, life-like bodies✓
  • Bred sandstone and a wholly indigenous Indian style
  • Crefusal ever to depict the Buddha
  • Duse only of cave paintings

Working

✓

The Gandhara school shows strong Greco-Roman influence — wavy hair, flowing robes, life-like bodies. The red-sandstone indigenous style is Mathura's, and Gandhara certainly did depict the Buddha.

Q22

  • Ca Roman style in marble only
  • Dan indigenous Indian style working chiefly in red sandstone✓

Working

✓

The Mathura school is an indigenous Indian style working chiefly in red sandstone — not Greek schist, Chinese jade or Roman marble.

✓

Both schools mark a turning point as among the first to make images of the Buddha in human form. They did not ban imagery, invent Brahmi or mint coins.

Buddhism spread into Central Asia and China largely along the trade routes, carried by merchants and monks — not by sea to the Americas, only in the Tamil country, or by Roman armies.
