Set 1 · Q1–Q10

Q1

The word "empire" comes from the Latin word *imperium*. The chapter uses this origin mainly to make which point?

  • AEmpires were always ruled by Roman emperors
  • BAn empire rests on one ruler holding supreme power over many lands✓
  • CEmpires were named in Latin by their founders
  • DAn empire is simply any very wealthy kingdom

Working

✓

*Imperium* is Latin for "supreme power", and the chapter uses the word's origin to stress that an empire is built on one ruler holding supreme power over many lands. (A) and (C) over-read it into Rome; (D) confuses an empire with mere wealth — size/wealth alone is not the defining trait.

Q2

The clearest single difference between a kingdom and an empire is that an empire:

  • Ais always richer than a kingdom
  • Bhas a larger army than a kingdom
  • Clayers many kingdoms, with smaller kings paying tribute under one emperor✓
  • Dnever allows the smaller kings to keep their thrones

Working

✓

A kingdom is one ruler over one people/territory; an empire layers many kingdoms under one emperor, with smaller kings kept on as tributaries who pay tribute. (A)/(B) are just "more of the same" and not the defining difference. (D) is false — tributaries usually keep their thrones.

Q3

A defeated king is allowed to keep his throne but must send the emperor gold, grain and elephants each year. The chapter would call this defeated king a:

  • Asatrap
  • Bbureaucrat
  • Cguild-head
  • Dtributary (vassal)✓

Working

✓

A defeated ruler who keeps his throne but sends tribute (gold, grain, elephants) is a tributary, also called a vassal. A satrap (A) is an *appointed* provincial governor, not a king who pays tribute; (B) a bureaucrat is an official; (C) a guild-head leads traders.

Q4

Which of the following is NOT one of the six features the chapter says an empire must maintain?

  • AHolding regular elections to choose the emperor✓
  • BA strong army
  • CLaws and a common currency
  • DSupport for art and learning

Working

✓

The six features are: army, administration, laws and currency, control over resources, support for art and learning, and communication. Empires were NOT democracies, so "holding regular elections to choose the emperor" (A) is not among them. (B), (C), (D) are all genuine features.

Q5

Why does the chapter say an empire needs a "common currency, weights and measures"?

  • ATo make the emperor personally richer than the kings
  • BSo distant regions can trade and be taxed under one shared system✓
  • CBecause coins were the only form of art the Mauryas valued
  • DTo replace the need for an army

Working

✓

A common currency, weights and measures let distant regions trade and be taxed under one shared system — a unifying administrative feature. (A) misreads the purpose; (C) confuses coins with art; (D) is false — currency does not replace the need for an army.

Q6

An emperor "cannot personally rule every distant village." Which two features of an empire most directly solve this problem?

  • AArt and learning, and a common currency
  • BCommunication networks, and control over resources
  • CA strong army, and support for learning
  • DAn administration of officials, and communication networks✓

Working

✓

The administration (officials managing land, taxes and order) plus communication networks (to move messages and goods) are exactly the two features that let an emperor rule far-off villages he cannot visit. (B) names communication but pairs it with resources rather than administration; (A) and (C) miss administration entirely.

Q7

Maintaining a large army is described as extremely expensive. According to the chapter, the wealth to pay for it came mainly from:

  • Aa thriving economy, especially trade✓
  • Bgifts sent by foreign kings
  • Cgold dug personally by the emperor
  • Dselling the emperor's old weapons

Working

✓

Armies are hugely expensive (feeding, arming and paying soldiers, horses and elephants), and the chapter says the wealth came mainly from a thriving economy, especially trade. (B), (C) and (D) are not how empires actually funded their forces.

Q8

Why did a wise ancient ruler work hard to build and control trade routes?

  • ATrade routes let him avoid keeping any army
  • BRoutes were the only way to send edicts
  • CMore trade meant more goods, richer producers and more tax income✓
  • DControlling routes ended the need for guilds

Working

✓

A wise ruler built and controlled trade routes because more trade meant more goods, richer producers and more tax income for the treasury. (A) is false — trade does not remove the need for an army; (C)'s rivals (B)/(D) confuse routes with edicts or guilds.

Q9

India's two great trade routes named in the chapter were the:

  • AUttarapatha and the Dakṣhiṇapatha✓
  • BGanga route and the Son route
  • CSilk Road and the Spice Route
  • DRoyal Road and the King's Highway

Working

✓

India's two great routes were the Uttarapatha (northern) and the Dakṣhiṇapatha (southern). The Silk Road/Spice Route (C) and the invented "Royal Road" (D) are not the chapter's terms; the Ganga and Son (B) are rivers that aided Magadha, not the two named trade routes.

Q10

A śhrenī of weavers fixes fair prices, shares news of demand, elects an honest head and settles its members' disputes by its own rules. This best shows that guilds:

  • Aorganised and governed themselves, helping trade flourish✓
  • Bwere controlled day-to-day by the king's soldiers
  • Cexisted only to collect tribute for the emperor
  • Dwere small family workshops with no rules

Working

✓

A guild (śhreni) that fixes prices, shares market news, elects an honest head and settles disputes by its own rules is organising and governing itself — which helped trade flourish. (B) is the opposite (kings let guilds be); (C) confuses guilds with tribute; (D) ignores their written rules and elected leaders.

Set 2 · Q11–Q20

Q11

"An enlightened ancient ruler usually let guilds make and follow their own rules." The chapter suggests a king did this because:

  • Ahe had no power to stop them
  • Bself-governing guilds kept trade and tax revenue flowing smoothly✓
  • Cguilds paid no taxes at all
  • Dthe law forbade kings from touching trade

Working

✓

An enlightened king let guilds self-govern because self-regulating guilds kept trade — and thus tax revenue — flowing smoothly. (A) wrongly says he was powerless; (C) is false (trade was taxed); (D) invents a law.

Q12

Of the sixteen large kingdoms (mahājanapadas), the one that rose to become India's first great power was:

  • AGandhāra
  • BAvanti
  • CMagadha✓
  • DKalinga

Working

✓

Of the sixteen mahājanapadas, Magadha (in south Bihar) rose to become India's first great power. Gandhāra and Avanti were rivals that faded; Kalinga was the region Ashoka later conquered, not the empire's core.

Q13

Which combination BEST explains why Magadha grew more powerful than its rivals?

  • AFertile Ganga-plains land, iron ore, forests for elephants, and river trade✓
  • BSea ports, a great navy, and ocean trade alone
  • CSnowy mountains and a protective cold desert
  • DGold mines and control of the Silk Road

Working

✓

Magadha's strength came from fertile Ganga-plains land, iron ore, forests for timber and elephants, and Ganga/Son river trade. (B) wrongly stresses a navy and sea ports; (C) describes the opposite terrain; (D) invents gold mines and the Silk Road.

Q14

Iron from the nearby hills helped Magadha in two distinct ways at once. These were:

  • Abuilding ships and minting coins
  • Btrading iron abroad and paying tribute in iron
  • Ciron temples and iron edicts
  • Diron ploughs for more food and iron weapons for a strong army✓

Working

✓

Iron helped in two ways at once: iron ploughs grew more food, and iron weapons made the army strong. (A), (B), (C) describe uses (ships, temples, export tribute) that the chapter does not give for Magadhan iron.

Q15

The Ganga and Son rivers were a major advantage to Magadha chiefly because they:

  • Amarked the empire's only border
  • Bwere worshipped by the Nandas
  • Cmade it cheap and easy to move goods and troops✓
  • Dwere full of iron ore

Working

✓

The Ganga and Son rivers made it cheap and easy to move goods and troops — a transport advantage. (A) wrongly makes them a border; (B) invents worship; (D) confuses the rivers with the iron hills.

Q16

Who founded the Nanda dynasty in Magadha around the 5th century BCE?

  • AMahāpadma Nanda✓
  • BDhana Nanda
  • CAjātaśhatru
  • DChandragupta Maurya

Working

✓

Mahāpadma Nanda founded the Nanda dynasty around the 5th century BCE. Dhana Nanda (B) was its last king; Ajātaśhatru (C) was an earlier Magadhan king; Chandragupta Maurya (D) overthrew the Nandas later.

Q17

Mahāpadma Nanda showed his economic power and built features of imperial rule by:

  • Awriting the Arthaśhāstra
  • Bcarving edicts on rock pillars
  • Cunifying many kingdoms, issuing coins and keeping a huge army✓
  • Ddefeating Alexander in Punjab

Working

✓

Mahāpadma Nanda showed imperial power by unifying many kingdoms, issuing coins and keeping a huge army. The Arthaśhāstra (A) was Kauṭilya's; edicts (B) were Ashoka's; defeating Alexander (D) never happened — Alexander was in the northwest, not fighting the Nandas.

Q18

The last Nanda king, Dhana Nanda, though very rich, lost the people's loyalty because he:

  • Agave away the whole treasury
  • Boppressed and over-taxed his subjects✓
  • Crefused to issue any coins
  • Dhanded the throne to Alexander

Working

✓

Dhana Nanda, though very rich, was hated for oppressing and over-taxing his subjects, costing him their loyalty. (A), (C), (D) are inventions; his fault was oppression, not generosity or abdication.

Q19

Around 326 BCE the Greek king Alexander of Macedonia reached northwest India after first conquering which great empire?

  • Athe Persian Empire✓
  • Bthe Maurya Empire
  • Cthe Nanda Empire
  • Dthe Roman Empire

Working

✓

Alexander first conquered the mighty Persian Empire (to avenge earlier Persian attacks on Greece) before reaching northwest India around 326 BCE. The Maurya (B) and Nanda (C) empires were Indian and not conquered by him; the Roman Empire (D) came much later.

Q20

A student writes, "Alexander conquered the whole of India." The chapter shows this is wrong because Alexander:

  • Anever actually left Greece
  • Bwas defeated and killed by Porus
  • Cconquered only the far south of India
  • Dreached only the northwest and turned back when his soldiers refused to go further✓

Working

✓

Alexander reached only the northwest, defeated Porus, and turned back when his tired soldiers refused to advance toward the Ganga — so he did not conquer all of India. (A) and (B) are false; (C) reverses it — he never reached the far south.

Q21

Alexander defeated King Porus of the Pauravas, yet then turned homeward. The immediate reason given is that:

  • AChandragupta Maurya defeated him
  • Bhis tired soldiers refused to advance further✓
  • Che ran out of weapons
  • Dthe Nandas paid him tribute to leave

Working

✓

After defeating Porus, Alexander turned homeward chiefly because his exhausted soldiers refused to go further. (A) is false (Chandragupta rose only later); (C)/(D) are inventions.

Q22

The chapter says Alexander's real impact on India was mostly *indirect*. This is best explained by the fact that he:

  • Adirectly ruled India for fifty years
  • Bpersonally founded the Maurya dynasty
  • Ccarved edicts across India in Greek
  • Dleft behind weak Greek satraps and opened contacts, rather than holding Indian land✓

Working

✓

Alexander's impact was *indirect*: he left weak Greek satraps in the northwest and opened contacts, rather than holding Indian territory himself — which later helped Chandragupta. (A)/(B) are false; (C) confuses him with Ashoka and uses the wrong language.

Q23

Around 321 BCE, Chandragupta Maurya founded the Maurya empire largely by:

  • Aoverthrowing the unpopular Nandas with Kauṭilya's help✓
  • Binheriting the throne peacefully from Ashoka
  • Cbeing appointed emperor by Alexander
  • Dmarrying into the Persian royal family

Working

✓

Around 321 BCE Chandragupta Maurya founded the empire by overthrowing the unpopular Nandas with Kauṭilya's help, making Pāṭaliputra his capital. (B) reverses the generations (Ashoka was his grandson); (C)/(D) are inventions.

Q24

Chandragupta could build so vast an empire so quickly partly because of conditions left after Alexander's death. Which condition helped him most in the northwest?

  • Aa strong united Greek army guarding India
  • Ba peace treaty signed by Ashoka
  • Cweak Greek satraps he could defeat or absorb✓
  • Dthe loyalty of the Nanda soldiers to him from birth

Working

✓

After Alexander's death the northwest was held by weak Greek satraps Chandragupta could defeat or absorb. (A) is the opposite; (B) is anachronistic (Ashoka came later); (D) is invented.

Q25

Kauṭilya, the mentor of Chandragupta Maurya, is also known by which other name(s)?

  • AMegasthenes
  • BChāṇakya (Viṣhnugupta)✓
  • CPorus
  • DPāṇini

Working

✓

Kauṭilya is also called Chāṇakya (and Viṣhnugupta). Megasthenes (A) was a Greek visitor to the court; Porus (C) was the king Alexander fought; Pāṇini (D) was a grammarian, not Chandragupta's mentor.

Q26

The title of Kauṭilya's famous treatise, the *Arthaśhāstra*, is best described as:

  • Aa book of poems praising the emperor
  • Ba collection of Ashoka's edicts
  • Cthe science of governance and economics✓
  • Da Buddhist religious text

Working

✓

The *Arthaśhāstra* means "the science of governance and economics", covering defence, administration, justice and welfare. It is not poetry (A), Ashoka's edicts (B), or a religious text (D).

Q27

Which list correctly gives the seven parts (saptānga) of a state according to Kauṭilya?

  • AKing, queen, priests, merchants, farmers, soldiers, slaves
  • BArmy, navy, cavalry, forts, coins, roads, temples
  • CLand, sea, forests, mines, rivers, hills, cities
  • DKing, ministers, territory and people, forts, treasury, army and justice, allies✓

Working

✓

Kauṭilya's saptānga (seven limbs) are: king, ministers, territory and people, forts, treasury, army and justice, and allies. (A), (B), (C) are plausible-looking but invented lists that do not match the seven limbs.

Q28

Why would Kauṭilya insist a strong state needs ALL seven limbs working together, not just a powerful king?

  • ABecause a state is like a body — if any one limb is weak, the whole suffers✓
  • BBecause seven was a lucky number for the Mauryas
  • CBecause tradition forbade a king from keeping a treasury
  • DBecause the king alone was not allowed to make laws

Working

✓

Kauṭilya treated the state like a body whose limbs must all work together — if any one of the seven is weak, the whole state suffers. (B), (C), (D) are inventions; the reasoning is the body/limbs analogy.

Q29

In Kauṭilya's saptānga, the treasury and the army are listed as separate limbs. The best reason is that:

  • Athe treasury and army were never used together
  • Bthe army was paid in grain, never money
  • Cone funds the state's needs while the other defends and enforces — different jobs✓
  • Dthe treasury belonged to the guilds, not the king

Working

✓

The treasury funds the state's needs while the army defends and enforces — two different essential jobs, so each is a separate limb. (A), (B), (D) are false claims about how they were used or owned.

Q30

Match the figure to the role: Chandragupta Maurya, Kauṭilya, Megasthenes. The correct matching is:

  • AGreek visitor; founder of the empire; author of the Arthaśhāstra
  • Bfounder of the Maurya empire; mentor and author of the Arthaśhāstra; Greek visitor to the Mauryan court✓
  • Cauthor of the Arthaśhāstra; Greek visitor; founder of the empire
  • Dmentor; founder of the empire; the last Nanda king

Working

✓

Chandragupta Maurya → founder of the empire (c. 321 BCE); Kauṭilya → mentor and author of the Arthaśhāstra; Megasthenes → Greek visitor to the Mauryan court. The other options scramble these roles.

Set 4 · Q31–Q40

Q31

Ashoka, who ruled 268–232 BCE, was related to Chandragupta Maurya as his:

  • Afather
  • Bmentor
  • Cbrother
  • Dgrandson✓

Working

✓

Ashoka (ruled 268–232 BCE) was Chandragupta Maurya's grandson. He was not his father, brother or mentor — Kauṭilya was the mentor, and Bindusāra (Chandragupta's son) was the generation between them.

Q32

At the height of Ashoka's reign the empire covered almost the whole subcontinent EXCEPT:

  • Athe northwest, including parts of Afghanistan
  • Bthe Ganga plains
  • Cmodern Bangladesh
  • Dthe far south✓

Working

✓

Ashoka's empire covered almost the whole subcontinent EXCEPT the far south. It DID include the northwest/Afghanistan, the Ganga plains and modern Bangladesh, so those are not the exception.

Q33

The turning point in Ashoka's life was a terrible, bloody war fought against:

  • AKalinga (modern Odisha)✓
  • Bthe Persians in the northwest
  • CPorus in Punjab
  • Dthe Nandas of Magadha

Working

✓

The turning-point war was the bloody Kalinga war (modern Odisha). The northwest Persians (B) were Alexander's foe; Porus in Punjab (C) was Alexander's foe; the Nandas (D) were overthrown by Chandragupta, not Ashoka.

Q34

A student claims, "Ashoka attacked Kalinga in order to spread Buddhism." Why is this the wrong way round?

  • AAshoka never actually fought at Kalinga
  • BKalinga was already Buddhist before the war
  • CHe attacked to expand his empire; the bloodshed afterwards led him toward dhamma✓
  • DBuddhism forbade any king from going to war

Working

✓

The claim reverses cause and effect: Ashoka attacked Kalinga to *expand* his empire by conquest; the horror of the bloodshed afterwards led him toward dhamma and peace. (A)/(B) are false; (D) overstates a rule.

Q35

We know Ashoka was genuinely shaken by Kalinga largely because:

  • AMegasthenes wrote about it in Greek
  • Bhe openly admitted his remorse in his own edicts✓
  • CKauṭilya recorded it in the Arthaśhāstra
  • Da later king built a temple to mark it

Working

✓

We know Ashoka was genuinely shaken because he openly admitted his remorse in his own edicts — a rare, first-hand confession. Megasthenes (A) predates Kalinga's aftermath in this context; the Arthaśhāstra (C) is Kauṭilya's; (D) is invented.

Q36

Ashoka's idea of *dhamma* is best described as:

  • Aa new religion that replaced Buddhism
  • Ba tax collected from tributary kings
  • Cmoral and ethical duty — honesty, non-violence, doing one's duties truthfully✓
  • Dthe science of warfare

Working

✓

Dhamma is moral and ethical duty — honesty, non-violence and doing one's duties truthfully, living in harmony with the order of the universe. It is not a replacement religion (A), a tax (B), or warfare (D).

Q37

Ashoka is called a "great communicator" because he spread dhamma mainly by:

  • Asending soldiers to repeat his words in every village
  • Bprinting books for every household
  • Cbuilding one great central temple
  • Dengraving edicts on rocks and pillars across the empire✓

Working

✓

Ashoka is called a "great communicator" because he engraved edicts on rocks and pillars across the empire. (A), (B), (C) are inventions — soldiers, printing and a single temple were not his method.

Q38

Ashoka's edicts were mostly written in the ____ language using the ____ script.

  • ASanskrit; Devanagari
  • BGreek; Latin
  • CPrakrit; Brahmi✓
  • DTamil; Brahmi

Working

✓

Ashoka's edicts were mostly in the Prakrit language using the Brahmi script. Not Sanskrit/Devanagari (A), Greek/Latin (B), or Tamil (D) — Prakrit was the common spoken language, Brahmi the widely understood script.

Q39

"Prakrit and Brahmi are really the same thing." Identify the correct correction:

  • ABoth are scripts used only by the Greeks
  • BPrakrit is a language (what people spoke); Brahmi is a script (a way of writing)✓
  • CBrahmi is a language; Prakrit is a script
  • DBoth are languages, and neither is a script

Working

✓

The correction to the classic mix-up: Prakrit is a *language* (what people spoke); Brahmi is a *script* (a way of writing). (A) and (D) deny one is a script; (C) reverses the two.

Q40

Why might Ashoka have chosen the everyday Prakrit language rather than refined Sanskrit for his edicts?

  • ASanskrit had not yet been invented
  • BPrakrit was the only language that could be carved
  • CPrakrit was forbidden to commoners, making the message secret
  • DSo that ordinary people could understand the messages directly✓

Working

✓

Ashoka likely chose everyday Prakrit so ordinary people could understand his messages directly, rather than refined Sanskrit. (A) and (B) are false; (C) reverses the purpose (he wanted the message public, not secret).

Q42

The Brahmi script is historically important beyond Ashoka's edicts because it is:

  • Bthe ancestor of most of today's Indian scripts✓

Q43

The Lion Capital of the Sarnath pillar was adopted as India's national emblem. The Great Stūpa at Sanchi (A), the Dhauli rock elephant (B) and a terracotta figure (D) are other ancient artworks, not the emblem.

Q45

Ashoka set up the lion pillar at Sarnath because Sarnath was the place where:

Q46

At the centre of India's national flag is the *dharmachakra*, taken from Mauryan art. It symbolises:

  • Bthe wheel of a war chariot

The dharmachakra is the "wheel of dharma", symbolising the Buddha's teachings; it sits at the centre of India's national flag. It is not a chariot wheel (B), a sun-god (C), or trade routes (D).

Q47

The Maurya empire ended around 185 BCE, roughly how long after Ashoka's death (232 BCE)?

  • Aabout two centuries
  • Babout half a century✓
  • Cwithin a single year
  • Dover three hundred years

Working

✓

The Maurya empire ended around 185 BCE, roughly half a century after Ashoka's death (232 BCE) — successors could not hold it together. (A), (C), (D) misjudge the span.

Q48

Empires are described as "fragile" mainly because:

  • Athey were never actually very large
  • Bthey had no armies to defend themselves
  • Cthey rest on force, and distant regions break away when the centre weakens✓
  • Demperors always refused to collect any tribute

Working

✓

Empires are "fragile" because they rest mainly on force and the emperor's strength, so distant regions break away when the centre weakens. (A) is false (they were large); (B) is false (they had armies); (D) is false (tribute was collected).

Q49

Which situation, according to the chapter, would MOST tempt a distant province to break away from an empire?

  • Aa strong emperor following another strong emperor
  • Blower tribute demands and a long peace
  • Cthe building of better roads to the capital
  • Da weak emperor following a strong one, plus heavier tribute demands✓

Working

✓

A weak emperor following a strong one, combined with heavier tribute demands, most tempts a distant province to break away. (A), (B), (C) all describe *stabilising* conditions that would make breaking away less likely.

Q50

The chapter calls an empire a "paradox." Which statement captures the paradox best?

  • AIt can bring unity and prosperity, yet it rests on war and force and tends to fall apart✓
  • BIt is both very small and very large at once
  • CIt has an emperor but no kings beneath him
  • DIt collects tribute but spends nothing on an army

Working

✓

The paradox: an empire can bring unity and prosperity, yet because it rests on war and force and distant regions drift away, it is fragile and tends to decline. (B), (C), (D) misstate what an empire is.

Q51

Comparing the Nanda and Maurya states, which is the BEST single statement?

  • AThe Nandas carved edicts while the Mauryas did not
  • BThe Mauryas began the empire and the Nandas destroyed it
  • CThe Nandas first unified Magadha's lands; the Mauryas then took over and expanded much further✓

Q52

Put these in correct chronological order: (i) Mahāpadma Nanda founds the Nanda dynasty, (ii) Ashoka fights the Kalinga war, (iii) Chandragupta founds the Maurya empire, (iv) Alexander reaches northwest India.

  • A(iv), (i), (iii), (ii)
  • B(i), (iv), (iii), (ii)✓
  • C(iii), (i), (iv), (ii)
  • D(i), (iii), (iv), (ii)
