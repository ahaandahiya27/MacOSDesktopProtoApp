|---|---|

**Marking scheme:** **+4** for each correct answer · **−1** for each wrong answer · **0** if unattempted.

**Instructions:** Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. For percolation problems use the rule percolation rate = volume of water ÷ time taken (mL/min). No calculator is needed — all numbers are designed to work by hand. Most questions need two to four reasoning steps; watch for traps. Do not write on this paper — mark answers on your answer sheet.

1. The layered top-to-bottom slice of soil, showing all its horizons, is called the:
   (A) soil texture
   (B) percolation rate
   (C) soil profile
   (D) humus layer

2. Which horizon of a soil profile is the dark, humus-rich topsoil where most plant roots grow?
   (A) the C horizon
   (B) the R horizon
   (C) the B horizon
   (D) the A horizon

3. Arrange the soil horizons in order from the surface downward.
   (A) bedrock, C, B, A, O
   (B) A, O, B, C, bedrock
   (C) O, A, B, C, bedrock
   (D) O, B, A, C, bedrock

4. Soil is formed mainly by the:
   (A) sudden cooling of lava in one night
   (B) freezing of rainwater in winter
   (C) slow weathering of rocks over a very long time
   (D) rapid evaporation of river water

5. The dark, crumbly material formed from decayed plant and animal remains in the top layer of soil is called:
   (A) clay
   (B) silt
   (C) bedrock
   (D) humus

6. A gardener digs a pit and finds, just under the topsoil, a lighter, harder layer where minerals washed down from above have collected. This layer is the:
   (A) A horizon
   (B) C horizon
   (C) O horizon
   (D) B horizon

7. The solid, unweathered rock at the very bottom of a soil profile is the:
   (A) subsoil (B horizon)
   (B) bedrock (R horizon)
   (C) topsoil (A horizon)
   (D) organic layer (O horizon)

8. Which statement about how soil forms is correct?
   (A) Soil forms in a few days after heavy rain.
   (B) Soil is made entirely of living organisms.
   (C) Soil never contains any rock material.
   (D) Building a centimetre of new topsoil can take hundreds of years.

9. A desert soil often shows almost no O horizon. The most likely reason is:
   (A) too much rainfall washes the O horizon away
   (B) wind packs the particles too tightly
   (C) deserts have no bedrock underneath

10. Which of these correctly pairs a soil type with its particle size?
    (A) clayey — largest particles
    (B) sandy — smallest particles
    (C) sandy — largest particles
    (D) loamy — only one particle size

11. Water rushes straight through sandy soil mainly because:
    (A) the large gaps between big particles let water drain fast
    (B) sand particles dissolve in water
    (C) sand repels water
    (D) sand has no air spaces at all

12. Which soil holds the most water and drains the slowest?
    (A) sandy soil
    (B) clayey soil
    (C) loamy soil
    (D) silt alone

13. Loamy soil is the best for growing most crops because it:
    (A) is made only of large sand grains
    (B) holds enough water for roots yet lets the extra drain away
    (C) never contains any humus
    (D) is the same as pure clay

14. Two pots are filled to the same level — one with sandy soil, one with clayey soil — and equal water is poured on each. After ten minutes the:
    (A) sandy pot has drained more water out of the bottom
    (B) clayey pot has drained more water out of the bottom
    (C) both drain exactly the same amount
    (D) neither lets any water through

15. Black cotton soil of the Deccan plateau, which becomes very sticky when wet and cracks when dry, is mainly:
    (A) sandy soil
    (B) pure silt
    (C) loose gravel
    (D) clayey soil

16. Which property best explains why sandy soil dries out quickly after rain?
    (A) high percolation rate and low water-holding capacity
    (B) high water-holding capacity
    (C) very small air gaps
    (D) very high humus content

17. Soil is described as "teeming with life." Which best supports this statement?
    (A) Soil is only broken rock with nothing living in it.
    (B) A single gram of soil can contain millions of micro-organisms.
    (C) Only earthworms live in soil and nothing else.
    (D) Living things avoid soil completely.

18. Roughly, the four main ingredients of soil are:
    (A) only sand and water
    (B) mineral particles, air, water, and humus
    (C) plastic, glass, metal, and stone
    (D) only humus and air

19. When a soil is completely water-logged after heavy rain, plant roots can struggle because the soil now has very little:
    (A) air
    (B) water
    (C) clay
    (D) weight

20. Which agent is **not** normally responsible for weathering rock into soil over time?
    (A) a single bolt of lightning
    (B) flowing and freezing water
    (C) growing plant roots
    (D) wind

21. A student heats a sample of moist soil in a dry test tube and sees droplets form on the cooler upper walls. This shows that soil contains:
    (A) plastic
    (B) air
    (C) moisture (water)
    (D) salt only

22. To show that soil contains **air**, the best simple demonstration is to:
    (A) drop a lump of dry soil into water and watch bubbles rise
    (B) heat the soil until droplets appear on the glass
    (C) rub the soil between your fingers
    (D) weigh the soil on a balance

23. Percolation rate is best defined as the:
    (A) weight of soil per unit volume
    (B) amount of humus in the soil
    (C) number of organisms in the soil
    (D) speed at which water seeps down through soil

24. In an experiment, 200 mL of water takes 20 minutes to percolate through a soil sample. The percolation rate is:
    (A) 0.1 mL/min
    (B) 10 mL/min
    (C) 4000 mL/min
    (D) 220 mL/min

25. 150 mL of water percolates through a soil column in 5 minutes. The percolation rate is:
    (A) 30 mL/min
    (B) 750 mL/min
    (C) 3 mL/min
    (D) 145 mL/min

26. Soil X lets 300 mL through in 10 minutes; soil Y lets 300 mL through in 30 minutes. Which soil is more likely sandy?
    (A) soil Y, because it is slower
    (B) both are equally sandy
    (C) neither — sand never lets water through
    (D) soil X, because it has the higher percolation rate

27. A loamy sample drains 240 mL of water in 8 minutes. Its percolation rate is:
    (A) 248 mL/min
    (B) 30 mL/min
    (C) 0.03 mL/min
    (D) 1920 mL/min

28. A sandy soil has a percolation rate of 25 mL/min. How long will 200 mL of water take to percolate through it?
    (A) 8 minutes
    (B) 5000 minutes
    (C) 0.125 minutes
    (D) 175 minutes

29. Soil P percolates 200 mL in 8 minutes; soil Q percolates 360 mL in 12 minutes. Which has the **higher** percolation rate?
    (A) soil Q
    (B) soil P
    (C) both equal
    (D) cannot be compared

30. Two soils drain equal volumes of water. Sandy soil takes 4 minutes; clayey soil takes 40 minutes. The ratio of their percolation rates (sandy : clayey) is:
    (A) 1 : 10
    (B) 4 : 40
    (C) 10 : 1
    (D) 1 : 1

31. Water-holding capacity is **highest** in:
    (A) clayey soil
    (B) sandy soil
    (C) gravel
    (D) coarse sand

    (B) sandy, loamy, clayey
