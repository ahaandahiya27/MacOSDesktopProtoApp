Q1

If 3(x − 4) + 5 = 2(x + 1) − 4, then x equals:

  • A−1
  • B1
  • C5✓
  • D11

Working

✓

Expand: 3x − 12 + 5 = 2x + 2 − 4. Combine: 3x − 7 = 2x − 2. Subtract 2x: x − 7 = −2. Add 7: x = 5. Quick check: LHS = 3·5 − 7 = 8; RHS = 2·5 − 2 = 8. ✓

Q2

Solve: (x + 2)/3 − (x − 1)/4 = 2.

  • A7
  • B13✓
  • C8
  • D19

Working

✓

Multiply both sides by 12 to clear denominators: 4(x + 2) − 3(x − 1) = 24. Expand: 4x + 8 − 3x + 3 = 24. Simplify: x + 11 = 24. So x = 13. Always clear the denominator FIRST when you have unlike fractions.

Q4

A two-digit number is such that the **product** of its digits is 18, and the **digit sum** is 9. The number could be:

  • A27 or 72
  • B19 or 91
  • C18 or 81
  • D36 or 63✓

Working

✓

Two digits a, b with a + b = 9 and a × b = 18. They are roots of t² − 9t + 18 = 0, which factors as (t − 6)(t − 3) = 0. So the digits are 3 and 6 — giving the two-digit number 36 or 63.

Q7

The perimeter of an isosceles triangle is 36 cm. If the base is 6 cm shorter than each equal side, the equal sides each measure:

Q9

Q15

  • A3✓

  • D5

Working

✓

Work the brackets inside-out: 4 − [3 − (2x − 1)] = 4 − [3 − 2x + 1] = 4 − [4 − 2x] = 4 − 4 + 2x = 2x. So 2x = 6 → x = 3. Sign care matters here.

Q16

Five years ago, Rita was 4 times as old as Sam. Five years from now, she will be twice as old as Sam. Sam's current age is:

  • A15
  • B10✓
  • C20
  • D25

Working

✓

Set up two equations from "5 years ago" and "5 years from now". Let Sam's current age = S, Rita's = R. Then R − 5 = 4(S − 5) and R + 5 = 2(S + 5). From the first: R = 4S − 15. Sub into the second: 4S − 15 + 5 = 2S + 10 → 4S − 10 = 2S + 10 → 2S = 20 → S = 10.

Q43

Solve: (2x − 1)(x + 1) = (2x + 1)(x − 1).

  • A0✓
  • B1
  • C−1
  • D1/2

Working

✓

Expand both: LHS = 2x² + 2x − x − 1 = 2x² + x − 1. RHS = 2x² − 2x + x − 1 = 2x² − x − 1. Equate: x − 1 = −x − 1 → 2x = 0 → x = 0. The 2x² terms and the constants cancelled — a common trap is to forget that this LEAVES a linear equation.
