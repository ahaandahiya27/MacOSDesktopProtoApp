Classic Paper-4 traps: confusing the ROLE of a case-ending with its mere shape (-म् can be nominative neuter OR accusative; -औ is dual not plural; -भिः is means not possession), mis-splitting तत्पुरुष vs बहुव्रीहि compounds, reading वन्दे as a verb of affection rather than reverence, and treating ह्रस्व/दीर्घ vowel length or sandhi joins as cosmetic when they are meaning-bearing. 1. A reader argues that वन्दे मातरम् could equally be glossed 'May the Mother be saluted' (a wish/passive). Within the meaning the chapter teaches, why is the chapter's own gloss 'I salute the Mother' more accurate?
(A) वन्दे is genuinely passive, so 'may the Mother be saluted' is the literal sense and the chapter's gloss is a loose paraphrase.(B) Both readings are identical in Sanskrit because वन्दे has no person built into it.(C) वन्दे is an active, first-person-singular finite verb carrying the agent 'I' inside it, so the speaker performs the salutation now — it is neither a passive ('be saluted') nor an optative wish ('may ... be').(D) वन्दे is an imperative addressed to the Mother, so 'be saluted, O Mother' is the precise sense.
2. Two students debate the emotional register of वन्दे मातरम्. One says it expresses reverence; the other says affection. Using the verb the chapter actually uses, which judgement is right and why?
(A) Reverence — वन्दे is a verb of bowing/saluting, a different lexical root from any verb of love or fondness, so the line conveys honour rather than tender affection.(B) Affection — वन्दे is the standard Sanskrit verb for loving someone dearly.(C) Neither, because वन्दे only means 'to look at', so the line is merely descriptive.(D) Both equally, because in Sanskrit a single verb covers reverence and affection without distinction.
3. The chapter notes that the indeclinable नमः can be construed with a dative ('homage TO X'), while वन्दे takes an accusative object. From this, what general principle about how a word governs its case can a student infer?
(A) Any word meaning 'salute' must take the accusative, so नमः with a dative is simply an error in the chapter.(B) Case-government depends only on the case of the previous word in the sentence, not on the saluting word.(C) Both वन्दे and नमः are indeclinables and therefore neither can govern any case at all.(D) A word's case-government is a property of that particular word, not of the meaning 'salute' in general — a transitive verb like वन्दे demands a direct object (accusative), whereas the indeclinable नमः licenses a dative of the one honoured.
4. Consider the invented line वन्दे गङ्गाम् हिमालयम् च ('I salute the Ganga and the Himalaya'). What does this show about how many objects a single वन्दे can govern, and in what case?
(A) वन्दे can govern only one object, so the second word हिमालयम् must be nominative.(B) When two objects are joined by च, the second one switches to the instrumental as the 'means'.(C) वन्दे can govern more than one object joined by च, and every such object stands in the accusative — here both गङ्गाम् and हिमालयम् are accusative singular.(D) Both गङ्गाम् and हिमालयम् become genitive because च forces possession.
5. A learner writes नमः भारतमातरम् intending 'homage to the Motherland', reasoning that since वन्दे takes the accusative, नमः must too. Evaluate the construction the chapter would endorse.
(A) The sentence is fully correct: नमः always governs the accusative exactly like वन्दे.(B) नमः should take a genitive, giving नमः भारतमातुः.(C) The natural construction is नमः with a dative (e.g. भारतमात्रे) meaning 'homage TO the Motherland'; copying वन्दे's accusative onto नमः misapplies one word's government to a different word.(D) नमः should take the instrumental, giving नमः भारतमात्रा.
6. Why can the single word वन्दे count as a complete Sanskrit sentence, while the single word मातरम् cannot — even though both are 'about' saluting?
(A) मातरम् is the real verb and वन्दे the noun, so मातरम् alone is the complete sentence.(B) Both are complete sentences, since any single Sanskrit word is a sentence.(C) Neither is complete, because a Sanskrit sentence needs at least three words.(D) वन्दे is a finite verb that already contains a subject ('I') and an action ('salute'), so it states a complete event; मातरम् is only an accusative noun naming a would-be object, with no verb or subject of its own.
7. In वन्दे सुजलाम् सुफलाम् मातरम् the three accusatives agree. If a student instead wrote वन्दे सुजला सुफला माता, what single underlying mistake explains ALL three altered words at once?
(A) Each word has a different, unrelated error, so there is no single explanation.(B) All three words have been put back into the nominative (the dictionary/subject form) instead of the accusative the object of वन्दे requires, so the same case-error repeats across the adjective–adjective–noun group.(C) The words were changed to the instrumental to show the means of saluting.(D) Only माता is wrong; the two adjectives सुजला and सुफला were correct as the object form.
8. The chapter uses the accusative of वन्दे's object. A student claims 'the accusative is just whatever ending a noun happens to have after वन्दे'. Choose the most precise correction of this idea.
(A) Correct as stated: the accusative is defined simply as 'any ending appearing after वन्दे'.(B) The accusative is a grammatical role (the direct object / thing acted upon) that the noun is put INTO because वन्दे governs it; the ending is the marker of that role, not the cause of it.(C) The accusative is defined by position only — the word immediately after the verb is accusative regardless of its ending.(D) The accusative is the same as the nominative, so वन्दे makes no real demand on the noun.
9. मातृ is an ऋकारान्त feminine. Its accusative singular is मातरम्. The agent-noun दातृ ('giver', masculine ऋ-stem) forms its accusative singular as दातारम् with a LONG आ in the suffix, unlike मातरम्. What best explains why kinship मातृ keeps a short अ while agent दातृ lengthens it?
(A) There is no difference; मातरम् should really be मातारम् and the chapter form is a misprint.(B) ऋ-stems split into two sub-patterns: kinship terms like मातृ/पितृ take the short (गुण) form मातरम्/पितरम्, while agent nouns like दातृ/कर्तृ take the lengthened (वृद्धि) form दातारम्/कर्तारम् in the strong cases.(C) The length depends only on gender: all feminine ऋ-stems are short and all masculine ones are long.(D) दातृ is not an ऋ-stem at all, so the comparison is invalid.
10. By the declension of मातृ (acc. sg. मातरम्, gen. sg. मातुः), the kinship stem दुहितृ ('daughter') should form its accusative singular and genitive singular respectively as:
(A) दुहित्रम् (accusative) and दुहित्रस्य (genitive)(B) दुहिता (accusative) and दुहितृम् (genitive)(C) दुहितरम् (accusative singular) and दुहितुः (genitive singular)(D) दुहितारम् (accusative) and दुहितारः (genitive)
11. A learner produces वन्दे मातृम् for 'I salute the Mother', simply tacking -म् onto the dictionary stem मातृ. Why is this wrong even though -म् is an accusative marker?
(A) मातृम् is actually correct, and मातरम् is an alternative poetic spelling.(B) The error is the case, not the form: मातृम् is a fine accusative but वन्दे wants the nominative.(C) ऋ-stems do not form the accusative by adding -म् to the bare stem; the stem first changes grade (मातृ → मातर) and then takes -म्, giving मातरम् — so मातृम् skips the required stem-change.(D) मातृ never takes -म् in any case, so any -म् form is impossible.
12. Given मातृ → माता (nom. sg.), मातरम् (acc. sg.), मातरौ (acc. dual), मातरः (nom. pl.), which single statement about the SHAPE of these endings is correct?
(A) माता and मातरम् share a stem, but मातरौ and मातरः belong to a completely different noun.(B) The same surface piece -रम्/-रौ/-रः rides on a strengthened stem मातर-, so number and case are told apart by the ending attached to that one strong stem, not by changing the root vowel each time.(C) All four are the singular of मातृ, differing only in spelling.(D) मातरः is the accusative singular and मातरम् is the nominative plural.
13. मातरः is nominative PLURAL ('mothers, as subjects'). In the song's vocative-like address to the one Motherland, why would मातरः be doubly wrong?
(A) It is wrong in both number and role: मातरः is plural ('mothers') whereas the song addresses one Mother, and it is nominative (subject) whereas the object of वन्दे must be accusative — so the form misses on count and on case.(B) It is wrong only in number: मातरः is the correct accusative but happens to be plural.(C) It is wrong only in case: मातरः is correctly singular but is nominative.(D) It is entirely correct, since मातरः is the accusative singular of मातृ.
14. The chapter pictures the Himalaya as a crown (मुकुट) at Bharat's head, the ocean washing her feet, fields green with crops, and holy places dotting the land. Read together, what unified portrait do these four images build?
(A) A catalogue of unrelated geographical facts with no figurative unity.(B) A warrior-queen arming for battle, with the mountains and seas as her weapons.(C) A single living Mother whose very body is the land — crowned at the head, washed at the feet, green and fertile in body, and sacred throughout — honoured from head to foot and in spirit.(D) A foreign land described coldly so that students learn place-names only.
15. पर्वतराजः is read as पर्वतानाम् राजा ('king OF mountains'), where the first member hides a genitive — a तत्पुरुष. By contrast, चन्द्रमुखी ('moon-faced [woman]') means 'one WHOSE face is like the moon', pointing outside itself to a person. What is the structural difference?
(A) Both are बहुव्रीहि, since each points to an outside thing.(B) पर्वतराजः is a तत्पुरुष naming one of its own members (a king, who IS a mountain-king), while चन्द्रमुखी is a बहुव्रीहि naming an outside referent (a woman) characterised by the compound — the whole denotes something not literally named inside it.(C) Both are तत्पुरुष, since each simply names its head member.(D) पर्वतराजः is बहुव्रीहि and चन्द्रमुखी is तत्पुरुष — the labels are the other way round.
16. रत्नाकरः stands for the ocean, analysed as रत्नानाम् आकरः ('mine of jewels'). The compound names the ocean WITHOUT using the word समुद्र. What does this reveal about how such epithets work?
(A) रत्नाकरः literally contains the letters of समुद्र inside it, which is how it points to the sea.(B) रत्नाकरः is just an alternative spelling of समुद्र with the same meaning and no descriptive content.(C) It is a descriptive epithet whose members ('jewels' + 'mine/store') describe a property of the ocean, letting the reader decode the intended referent from sense and context rather than from a hidden copy of the word समुद्र.(D) Sanskrit bans the plain word समुद्र in poetry, so a substitute compound is legally required.
17. सस्यश्यामला is split सस्य + श्यामला and glossed 'dark-green BY/WITH crops'. Using the same instrumental-type तत्पुरुष logic, the coined word जलशीतला would most naturally mean:
(A) cool BY/WITH water (made cool by water)(B) the king of waters(C) one who has no water (water-less)(D) water and coolness together (a pair)
18. देशभक्तः ('country-devoted') is a तत्पुरुष whose head is भक्तः. Now consider चन्द्रशेखरः ('moon-crested', a name of Shiva) meaning 'one ON WHOSE crest the moon sits'. Which compound is बहुव्रीहि and why?
(A) देशभक्तः is बहुव्रीहि because it points to a country outside the compound.(B) चन्द्रशेखरः is बहुव्रीहि: it does not denote a 'crest' or a 'moon' but an outside person (Shiva) characterised by having the moon on his crest; देशभक्तः, by contrast, denotes its own head (a devotee) and is तत्पुरुष.(C) Both are बहुव्रीहि because both contain two nouns.(D) Neither is बहुव्रीहि; both simply name their first member.
19. कृषकबान्धवाः is glossed 'farmer-brethren'. A student insists the head (the word that fixes what the whole compound IS) is कृषक. Why is that backwards, and which member is the head?
(A) In such a तत्पुरुष the LAST member is the head, so बान्धव ('brethren/kinsmen') is the head and कृषक merely qualifies it — the compound denotes brethren (who are farmers), and the plural ending sits on बान्धवाः.(B) कृषक is the head, so the compound denotes farmers qualified as 'brotherly'.(C) Neither member is the head; both are equal as in a द्वन्द्व 'farmers and brethren'.(D) The head is whichever member is shorter, here कृषक.
20. आनन्द ('bliss') begins with a long आ; carelessly shortening it risks producing the spurious अनन्द. A learner says 'इ and ई are written differently but sound the same, so swapping them is harmless'. Apply the chapter's vowel-length principle.
(A) Correct — only आ and अ differ in meaning; इ and ई are truly interchangeable.(B) Wrong — इ (short, one मात्रा) and ई (long, two मात्राः) are distinct sounds of different duration, and swapping them can change a word's identity, exactly as आ↔अ can; length is meaning-bearing for every vowel pair.(C) Correct — vowel length never affects meaning, so any swap is harmless.(D) Wrong, but only because इ has no long counterpart, so the swap is simply impossible.
21. The chapter ties आनन्दमठः ('Abbey of Bliss') to the long आ of आनन्द. Why does the long आ matter for the TITLE specifically, beyond mere spelling?
(A) The long आ marks the title as plural, naming several abbeys.(B) The long आ is a case-ending showing the title is the object of वन्दे.(C) The long आ identifies the author's name rather than the book's meaning.(D) The long आ keeps the first member meaning 'bliss' (आनन्द); shortening it to अनन्द would change the sense, so the very name 'Abbey of Bliss' depends on preserving the vowel length.
22. A मात्रा is defined in the chapter's vowel-length idea as a unit of duration: one मात्रा for a ह्रस्व vowel, two for a दीर्घ. By this measure, which set is ALL दीर्घ (two-मात्रा) vowels?
(A) आ, ई, ऊ(B) अ, इ, उ(C) अ, आ, इ(D) इ, उ, ऋ
23. Why does mis-stretching a vowel risk more damage in Sanskrit than a casual mispronunciation in everyday English speech, according to the chapter's logic?
(A) Because in Sanskrit vowel length is phonemic — a short and a long vowel are different sounds that can mark different words (आनन्द vs अनन्द), so a length slip can change or destroy the word's very meaning, not just its 'accent'.(B) Because Sanskrit has no long vowels, so any lengthening is automatically an error.(C) Because length changes only the gender of a word, never its meaning.(D) Because Sanskrit words are all so short that any change at all makes them unpronounceable.
24. The chapter assigns detailed study of ह्रस्व/दीर्घ length to the वर्णमात्रा chapter (Ch. 13), not Ch. 1. What is the best reading of WHY Ch. 1 raises vowel length at all, if it is taught fully elsewhere?
(A) Because vowel length is actually unique to Ch. 1 and is never revisited.(B) Because a concrete word in this very lesson (आनन्द in आनन्दमठः) depends on the long आ, so Ch. 1 flags the principle in context and points forward to where it is treated systematically.(C) Because Ch. 1 is about conjugations, so vowel length is the only grammar it can mention.(D) Because the cross-reference is a printing error; vowel length has nothing to do with this lesson.
25. The phrase सस्यश्यामला arises from joining सस्य + श्यामला with no extra sound inserted at the seam. By contrast, joining स + ईश would, under vowel-sandhi, fuse to सेश-type forms (अ + ई → ए). What does this teach about reading compounds back into their parts?
(A) All compounds are formed by fusing the seam vowels, so सस्यश्यामला must also hide a fused vowel.(B) No compound ever changes sounds at the seam, so अ + ई never becomes ए.(C) Splitting is impossible once words are joined, because sandhi is irreversible.(D) Some joins simply place members side by side (सस्य + श्यामला) while others fuse vowels at the seam (अ + ई → ए); to split a compound you must know whether a sandhi change occurred, then undo it to recover the original members.
26. In recovering members from a compound, a student undoes a seam where ए appears and proposes the originals were अ + इ or अ + ई. Why is this a reasonable inference about the join?
(A) Because the vowel-sandhi rule अ/आ + इ/ई → ए runs in one direction when forming words, so an ए at a seam can be traced back to a preceding अ/आ followed by इ/ई as the likely originals.(B) Because ए can only ever come from joining two ए vowels.(C) Because ए at a seam always means the members were अ + उ.(D) Because ए never results from sandhi, so the student must be inventing the rule.
27. वन्दे मातरम् as printed shows no vowel-fusion between the two words. If a poet instead wrote वन्द इह (वन्दे + इह), why might the seam look different from वन्दे मातरम्?
(A) Because मातरम् and इह are the same word, so the seam is identical in both.(B) Because sandhi happens only before consonants, so मातरम् would change but इह would not.(C) Because वन्दे loses its meaning whenever any word follows it.(D) Because मातरम् begins with a consonant, so वन्दे sits beside it unchanged; but इह begins with a vowel इ, which can trigger vowel-sandhi at the seam (ए + इ interacting), altering the written join.
28. A learner splits सस्यश्यामला as सस्य + अश्यामला and reads 'crops WITHOUT darkness'. Why is this split both phonetically and semantically wrong?
(A) There is no अ-prefix here: the second member is श्यामला ('dark/green'), not अश्यामला; inventing a negative अ- both misreads the sounds at the seam and flips the meaning from 'dark-green with crops' to its opposite.(B) The split is correct; सस्यश्यामला really does mean 'crops without darkness'.(C) The split is fine phonetically but only the meaning is slightly off.(D) श्यामला does carry a hidden negative, so 'without darkness' is the literal sense.
29. Which pair correctly identifies a compound member-boundary AND preserves the intended sense?
(A) पर्वतराजः → पर्व + तराजः = 'a balance of festivals'(B) देशभक्तः → देशभ + क्तः = 'a shining country'(C) सस्यश्यामला → सस्यश्यामल + आ = 'a green man, feminine ending'(D) रत्नाकरः → रत्न(ानाम्) + आकरः = 'mine of jewels' (the ocean)
30. In समुद्रः भारतमातुः चरणौ प्रक्षालयति, three different nouns carry three different roles. Which mapping is correct for समुद्रः, चरणौ and भारतमातुः?
(A) समुद्रः = accusative object; चरणौ = nominative subject; भारतमातुः = instrumental means.(B) समुद्रः = nominative subject (the doer); चरणौ = accusative dual (the two feet, the object washed); भारतमातुः = genitive (whose feet — possession).(C) समुद्रः = genitive possessor; चरणौ = instrumental means; भारतमातुः = nominative subject.(D) समुद्रः = instrumental means; चरणौ = genitive of the feet; भारतमातुः = accusative object.
31. The form चरणौ ('two feet') ends in -औ. A student says this -औ must be nominative plural ('feet, as subjects'). Correct the number AND the case at once.
(A) -औ is nominative plural, so 'the feet' are the subject doing the washing.(B) -औ is accusative singular, naming one foot as the object.(C) -औ is genitive dual, meaning 'of the two feet'.(D) -औ on चरणौ is the DUAL (exactly two), and here it is the accusative dual (the object washed), not a plural and not a nominative subject.
32. स्वेदबिन्दुभिः सिञ्चन्ति ('they water with sweat-drops') uses -भिः. A learner glosses it 'OF the sweat-drops'. Identify the case actually present and the case the learner confused it with.
(A) -भिः is genitive plural, so 'of the sweat-drops' is exactly right.(B) -भिः is accusative plural, so it names the drops as the object watered.(C) -भिः is the instrumental plural ('with/by means of'); the learner confused it with the genitive ('of'), which would instead be स्वेदबिन्दूनाम्.(D) -भिः is nominative plural, so the drops are the subject doing the watering.
33. Compare the roles of कृषकाः and स्वेदबिन्दुभिः in 'कृषकाः स्वेदबिन्दुभिः भूमिम् सिञ्चन्ति' (the farmers water the land with sweat-drops). Which assignment is right?
(A) कृषकाः = nominative plural agent (the doers); स्वेदबिन्दुभिः = instrumental plural means ('with'); भूमिम् = accusative object (the thing watered).(B) कृषकाः = instrumental means; स्वेदबिन्दुभिः = nominative agent; भूमिम् = genitive possessor.(C) कृषकाः = accusative object; स्वेदबिन्दुभिः = genitive possessor; भूमिम् = nominative subject.(D) All three are instrumental, expressing three different means.
34. Why does the poem use स्वेदबिन्दुभिः in the PLURAL instrumental rather than a singular, and what does this choice contribute to the imagery?
(A) Because the instrumental case has no singular form, plural is the only option.(B) Because the farmers are plural, the means must automatically copy that plural for agreement.(C) Because the watering is done by MANY drops of sweat, the natural means-form is the instrumental plural — and the plurality reinforces the image of sustained, abundant toil by the farmers.(D) Because -भिः can only be singular, and the plural meaning comes from सिञ्चन्ति alone.
35. If the poem said the farmers water the land 'with their tears' instead of sweat, which form would अश्रु ('tear', neuter) take, and what role would it play?
(A) अश्रूणि — accusative plural, the thing being watered.(B) अश्रुभिः — instrumental plural ('with/by means of tears'), the means of the watering, exactly parallel to स्वेदबिन्दुभिः.(C) अश्रूणाम् — genitive plural, 'of the tears'.(D) अश्रवः — nominative plural, the subject doing the watering.
36. वन्दे ('I salute') is first-person singular with a -ए (आत्मनेपद) ending. Built on the same root वन्द्, the THIRD-person singular ('he/she salutes') is वन्दते. Comparing वन्दे and वन्दते, what does the contrast demonstrate?
(A) वन्दे and वन्दते are the same form with an optional extra syllable and identical meaning.(B) वन्दते is the plural of वन्दे, meaning 'we salute'.(C) Person and number are signalled by the personal ENDING on the same root (वन्द्): -ए gives 'I salute', -ते gives 'he/she salutes' — so changing the ending changes who performs the action.(D) The root itself changes from वन्द् to वन्दत् to mark the third person.
37. सिञ्चन्ति ('they water', ending -अन्ति) and वन्दते ('he/she salutes', ending -ते) take DIFFERENT personal endings for comparable persons. What does this contrast reveal about Sanskrit verbs?
(A) Every Sanskrit root uses exactly one universal set of endings, so -अन्ति and -ते must mark different tenses, not different sets.(B) Sanskrit verbs mark only tense and never person or number.(C) Sanskrit verbs belong to different conjugation classes/pada and therefore draw on different SETS of personal endings (e.g. parasmaipada -अन्ति vs ātmanepada -ते), so the ending depends on the root's type, not just on the person.(D) Person and number are marked on the noun, so the verb endings are meaningless.
38. From वन्दे ('I salute', 1st sg.), the first-person PLURAL ('we salute') of the same ātmanepada root is वन्दामहे. A student instead writes वन्दन्ते for 'we salute'. What is the error?

Worked Solutions

1. (C) वन्दे ends in the first-person-singular middle ending and means 'I salute/revere'; the doer 'I' is encoded in the verb, making it an active statement performed by the speaker. The wish-reading 'may ... be saluted' would need an optative form, and the passive 'be saluted' would need a passive stem with the Mother as grammatical subject — neither is present. The claim that वन्दे carries no person is false, since the ending itself marks first-person singular. It is also not an imperative addressed to the Mother, because the Mother is the accusative object मातरम्, not the addressee.

2. (A) The chapter glosses वन्दे as 'I salute/bow/revere', built on the root for salutation, which is lexically distinct from any verb of affection; hence the register is reverence. Treating वन्दे as 'love' confuses two different roots. It does not mean 'look at'. And Sanskrit does keep reverence and affection in separate verbs, so they are not interchangeable.

3. (D) वन्दे, a finite transitive verb, takes its object in the accusative, while नमः, an indeclinable, can be paired with a dative meaning 'homage to'. So the lesson is that government attaches to the individual word, not to the gloss 'salute'. That refutes the idea that every 'salute' word must take the accusative. Case-government is not decided by the previous word's case. And वन्दे is a verb, not an indeclinable, so the claim that neither governs a case is false.

4. (C) A transitive verb can take coordinated objects joined by च, and each remains in the accusative as the thing acted upon; गङ्गाम् and हिमालयम् are both accusative singular. There is no rule capping वन्दे at one object, so the second is not nominative. च merely coordinates and does not convert anything to instrumental or genitive.

5. (C) The chapter distinguishes नमः (an indeclinable that pairs with the dative of the one honoured) from the verb वन्दे (which takes an accusative object). So 'homage to the Motherland' wants a dative, and forcing an accusative because वन्दे does so is the error. नमः is not a verb that governs the accusative. Genitive would mean 'of', and instrumental would mean 'by means of' — neither expresses 'homage to'.

6. (D) A finite verb like वन्दे encodes person, number and action, so 'I salute' is a whole clause. मातरम् is a case-form of a noun and supplies no verb, hence cannot stand alone. The roles are not reversed (मातरम् is not a verb). Not every single word is a complete sentence, and there is no three-word minimum in Sanskrit.

7. (B) मातरम्, सुजलाम् and सुफलाम् are all feminine accusative singular agreeing as the object of वन्दे; माता, सुजला and सुफला are the nominative forms. So one and the same shift — accusative back to nominative — accounts for every word. They were not given three different errors, nor turned instrumental, and the adjectives are not exempt: agreement forces them into the accusative too.

8. (B) Case is a role: वन्दे, being transitive, requires its object to take the accusative (the patient/direct-object role), and the ending merely signals that role. Defining the accusative as 'whatever follows वन्दे' confuses marker with cause. Position alone does not assign case in Sanskrit, which has relatively free word order. And the accusative is genuinely distinct from the nominative, so वन्दे does impose a real requirement.

9. (B) Among ऋ-stems, kinship nouns (मातृ, पितृ) use the गुण grade giving मातरम्, पितरम्, while agent/doer nouns (दातृ, कर्तृ) use the वृद्धि grade giving दातारम्, कर्तारम् in strong cases. So the contrast is a kinship-vs-agent distinction, not a misprint. It is not purely gender-based, since some masculine kinship stems (पितृ) are also short. And दातृ is indeed an ऋ-stem, so the comparison stands.

10. (C) दुहितृ is a kinship ऋ-stem patterning exactly like मातृ: accusative singular दुहितरम् (cf. मातरम्) and genitive singular दुहितुः (cf. मातुः). The forms with -त्रम्/-त्रस्य wrongly drop into a different stem class. दुहिता is the nominative, not the accusative, and दुहितृम् is not a valid genitive. The lengthened दुहितारम् belongs to the agent-noun pattern, not to kinship stems.

11. (C) For ऋ-stems the strong stem (मातर-) appears before the accusative-singular -म्, yielding मातरम्; मातृम् wrongly attaches -म् to the unchanged bare stem. So both forms are not equivalent — मातृम् is simply not a valid form. The case is right (वन्दे does want the accusative), so the fault is the formation, not the case. And मातृ does take -म् once it is in its strong grade, as मातरम् shows.

12. (B) माता, मातरम्, मातरौ, मातरः are all forms of one stem मातृ: the strong stem मातर- carries the endings (-Ø/-म्/-औ/-:) that distinguish nominative, accusative, dual and plural. They are not from different nouns, nor are they all singular (मातरौ is dual, मातरः is plural). And the labels are not swapped: मातरम् is accusative singular, मातरः is nominative plural.

13. (A) मातरः encodes nominative plural; the song reveres a single Motherland as the object of वन्दे, which needs accusative singular मातरम्. So मातरः errs on number (plural vs singular) AND case (nominative vs accusative). It is not merely one of those errors, and it is certainly not the correct accusative singular.

14. (C) Each image maps onto a body part or quality of a personified Mother (crown=head, sea=feet, crops=fertile body, holy sites=sacredness), so together they revere Bharat head-to-foot and spiritually. They are figurative, not a bare fact-list. There is no battle imagery, so 'warrior-queen with weapons' is foreign to the passage. And the tone is loving reverence for one's own land, not a cold catalogue of a foreign country.

15. (B) पर्वतराजः denotes a king (its own head member), so it is तत्पुरुष; चन्द्रमुखी denotes a woman who is not named inside the compound but is characterised by having a moon-like face, the hallmark of बहुव्रीहि. So they differ in type. They are not both बहुव्रीहि (पर्वतराजः names its own head) nor both तत्पुरुष (चन्द्रमुखी points outside). And the labels are not swapped — पर्वतराजः is the तत्पुरुष and चन्द्रमुखी the बहुव्रीहि.

16. (C) रत्नाकरः describes the sea by a property — it stores jewels (pearls, corals) — and the reader infers 'ocean' from that description plus context; it does not embed समुद्र. It is not a spelling variant, since its members carry real descriptive content. And there is no rule forbidding समुद्र; the epithet is a poetic choice, not a legal substitute.

17. (A) सस्यश्यामला has the relation 'dark/green by means of crops' (an instrumental-type तत्पुरुष), so जलशीतला parallels it as 'cool by means of water'. 'King of waters' would be a genitive तत्पुरुष like पर्वतराजः, a different relation. 'Water-less' would be a negative बहुव्रीहि, not this pattern. And 'water and coolness together' would be a द्वन्द्व, which this compound is not.

18. (B) चन्द्रशेखरः names a third party (Shiva) possessing the feature 'moon on the crest', the signature of बहुव्रीहि; देशभक्तः names its own head (the devotee) and is तत्पुरुष. देशभक्तः does not point to a country as its referent — its referent is the devotee. Merely containing two nouns does not make a compound बहुव्रीहि. And तत्पुरुष names its HEAD (second member), not its first member.

19. (A) In a determinative (तत्पुरुष) compound the final member is the head, here बान्धव; कृषक modifies it, and the case/number ending appears on the head बान्धवाः (nominative plural). So कृषक is not the head. It is not a द्वन्द्व of two equal nouns ('farmers and brethren'). And headedness is fixed by structure, not by which word is shorter.

20. (B) The chapter treats vowel length (मात्रा) as meaning-bearing: इ and ई differ in duration and can distinguish words, just as आ and अ do. So the learner is wrong, and the principle is not limited to आ/अ. Vowel length does affect meaning generally. And इ does have a long counterpart ई, so the swap is possible — which is precisely why it matters.

21. (D) आनन्द means 'bliss' precisely because of its long आ; with a short अ the sense collapses, so the title 'Abbey of Bliss' (आनन्दमठः) hinges on that length. The आ is not a plural marker, not a case-ending making the title an object, and it labels the meaning of the word, not the author's name.

22. (A) The दीर्घ (long, two-मात्रा) vowels among these are आ, ई, ऊ; their short (ह्रस्व, one-मात्रा) partners are अ, इ, उ. So अ, इ, उ is the all-short set, not all-long. अ, आ, इ mixes short and long. And इ, उ, ऋ are all short, not long.

23. (A) Length in Sanskrit is meaning-distinguishing (phonemic): आनन्द and अनन्द would be different items, so a slip alters the word itself. Sanskrit does have long vowels (that is the whole point). Length is not merely a gender switch; it can change the lexical meaning. And the issue is meaning, not pronounceability.

24. (B) Ch. 1 needs the long आ to keep आनन्द/आनन्दमठः meaningful, so it introduces the idea in context and defers the full treatment to the वर्णमात्रा chapter. Length is not unique to Ch. 1 — that is why the forward reference exists. Ch. 1 is not 'about conjugations only'. And the link to आनन्द makes the mention relevant, not an error.

25. (D) सस्यश्यामला shows a join with no seam change, whereas अ + ई → ए shows vowel fusion; so a reader must check for and reverse any sandhi to recover members. Not all joins fuse (this one does not), and seam fusion does occur (अ+ई→ए), so the two absolute claims are both false. Sandhi is reversible by rule, which is exactly how words are split.

26. (A) The standard guṇa-sandhi अ/आ + इ/ई → ए lets a reader reverse an ए at a seam into a preceding अ/आ plus इ/ई. ए does not require two ए vowels to arise. अ + उ would give ओ, not ए. And ए certainly can result from sandhi, so the rule is real, not invented.

27. (D) Sandhi at a word-seam is driven by the following sound: मातरम् starts with a consonant (no vowel fusion), while इह starts with a vowel इ, which can interact with the final ए of वन्दे. The two following words are not the same. Vowel-sandhi is triggered by a following vowel, not by consonants, so the rule in one distractor is inverted. And वन्दे does not lose meaning merely because a word follows it.

28. (A) The members are सस्य + श्यामला, giving 'dark/green by means of crops'; there is no negative अ-, so reading अश्यामला both mis-segments the sounds and reverses the meaning to 'not dark'. It is therefore not correct, not merely 'slightly off' in meaning, and श्यामला has no hidden negative.

29. (D) रत्नाकरः splits as रत्न + आकर ('mine/store of jewels'), the ocean — correct boundary and sense. पर्वतराजः must split पर्वत + राज ('king of mountains'), not पर्व + तराज. देशभक्तः splits देश + भक्त ('country-devoted'), not देशभ + क्त. And सस्यश्यामला splits सस्य + श्यामला, not by peeling a final आ off a coined masculine.

30. (B) The ocean (समुद्रः) is the nominative subject doing the washing; the two feet (चरणौ) are the accusative-dual object; भारतमातुः is genitive marking whose feet. The other mappings scramble these roles — e.g. making the ocean the object or the means, or the Motherland the subject/object — none of which fits 'the ocean washes the Motherland's two feet'.

31. (D) Sanskrit marks the dual distinctly, and -औ here gives accusative dual चरणौ ('the two feet' as object of प्रक्षालयति). It is not a plural (Sanskrit would use a different plural ending), not nominative (the feet are washed, not washing), and not a singular (there are two feet). It is also not genitive, which would express 'of the feet'.

32. (C) -भिः marks the instrumental plural, the means by which the watering is done; 'of' would be the genitive स्वेदबिन्दूनाम्. So the gloss 'of' mistakes instrumental for genitive. -भिः is not accusative (the drops are the means, not the thing watered) nor nominative (they are not the subject — the farmers are).

33. (A) The farmers (कृषकाः) are the nominative subject/agent; the sweat-drops (स्वेदबिन्दुभिः) are the instrumental means; the land (भूमिम्) is the accusative object. The other assignments swap agent and means, or misread the object as subject, or wrongly flatten all three into the instrumental — none matches 'the farmers water the land with sweat'.

34. (C) Sweat falls in many drops, so the means is naturally plural, and the plurality underlines the farmers' abundant, ongoing labour. The instrumental does have singular forms, so plural is a choice, not a necessity. Sanskrit does not force a means to copy the subject's number (means and agent are independent). And -भिः is itself a plural ending, so the claim that it is singular is false.

35. (B) 'With tears' is the means, so neuter अश्रु takes the instrumental plural अश्रुभिः, mirroring स्वेदबिन्दुभिः. The accusative अश्रूणि would make the tears the thing watered, the genitive अश्रूणाम् would mean 'of the tears', and a nominative would make the tears the doer — none fits the means-role 'with tears'.

36. (C) वन्दे (1st sg.) and वन्दते (3rd sg.) share the root वन्द् and differ only in the personal ending, which fixes person/number. They are not identical in meaning. वन्दते is third-person singular, not the plural 'we salute' (that would be वन्दामहे). And the root stays वन्द्; it is the ending, not the root, that changes.

37. (C) -अन्ति (parasmaipada) and -ते (ātmanepada) are from different ending-sets, chosen by the root's class/pada, illustrating that ending choice depends on the verb's type. There is no single universal ending-set, so the difference is not one of tense. Sanskrit verbs do mark person and number (the endings prove it), and person is encoded in the verb, not the noun.

38. (D) वन्दन्ते is third-person plural ('they salute'), while first-person plural 'we salute' is वन्दामहे; the forms are not swapped. वन्दन्ते is plural, not the singular वन्दे. And it is third person, not the second-person plural (which would be वन्दध्वे).

39. (A) Placing कृषकबान्धवाः (toiling with sweat) beside सस्यश्यामला (crop-green) makes the abundance the fruit of human labour, so crediting it to the ocean alone, or to snow alone, or to nothing, all miss the intended causal link the poet draws between the farmers' sweat and the green fields.

40. (C) तीर्थक्षेत्राणि introduces sacredness, rounding out the praise of natural features with spiritual worth, so it fits the hymn's reverent portrait. It says nothing about a lack of rivers, is not a detached tourist list, and does not imply the land is small — it adds a dimension of holiness, not a limitation.

41. (D) जिज्ञासा (the children's desire to know) triggers the mother's explanation, so removing it would remove the explanatory dialogue that structures the lesson. जिज्ञासा does not place the Himalaya, does not cause वन्दे's accusative government (that is grammatical), and does not identify the author — those are unrelated to the children's curiosity.

42. (C) Appearing within the novel आनन्दमठः sets the song inside a story, lending it narrative and patriotic context. Being in a novel does not make it anonymous — the chapter names Bankim Chandra. It is a song, not a recipe. And being set in an 1882 novel does not make it older than all Sanskrit literature.

43. (B) When salutation is expressed by the noun वन्दनम्, its target appears in the genitive (भारतमातुः, 'salutation OF the Motherland'); the finite verb वन्दे instead governs an accusative object (मातरम्). So भारतमातुः is genitive, not accusative. It is not the means (instrumental) nor the subject (the gods, देवाः, are the nominative subject).

44. (B) Adjective–noun agreement forces सस्यश्यामलाम् and सुजलाम् into the same feminine accusative singular as मातरम्, giving three accusative words. Adjectives do not stay nominative while the noun is accusative (that breaks agreement). सस्यश्यामलाम् is accusative, not genitive. And वन्दे does take an object, so 'none' is wrong.

45. (A) The nominative singular of मातृ is माता (ending in long आ, no -म्), and मातरम् carries the accusative-singular -म्; so -म् marks the accusative, not the nominative, and merely being word-final tells us nothing. माता is not the accusative, मातरम् is not a genitive of possession, and it is not a vocative of address — it is the accusative object.

46. (C) वन्दे needs its object in the accusative, so माता must be मातरम्; and the instrumental पर्वतराजेन ('by the king of mountains') supplies a 'means' that the sentence does not call for, so it is out of place — two faults. The sentence is therefore not correct as written. माता is genuinely wrong (not 'fine'), so it is not a single-fault sentence, and the fix is not merely swapping वन्दे for नमः.

47. (D) देशभक्तः splits as देश + भक्तः ('devoted to country'), and shortening आ to अ (आनन्द→अनन्द) can wreck meaning since length is phonemic — both rules applied correctly. आ and अ are not interchangeable, so 'length never matters' is false. देशभक्तः can be split. And its sense is 'country-devoted', not 'devotion's country', and length affects meaning, not merely gender.

48. (A) मातरम् is the accusative singular, the object form (e.g. the one saluted), so that pairing is right. मातरम् is not the nominative (that is माता). माता is not the genitive (that is मातुः). And मातुः is the genitive, not the accusative.

49. (A) The doers are nominative (कृषकाः), the fields watered are the accusative object (क्षेत्राणि), and the means 'with water' is instrumental (जलैः): कृषकाः क्षेत्राणि जलैः सिञ्चन्ति. Making कृषक instrumental (कृषकैः) wrongly demotes the agent. Putting the fields in instrumental (क्षेत्रैः) loses the object. And कृषकान् makes the farmers the object rather than the subject.

50. (B) A first-person-singular verb encodes its subject 'I', so वन्दे already carries the subject — it is simply unwritten as a separate pronoun. Sanskrit clauses do have subjects (encoded in the verb). मातरम् is the accusative object, not a hidden 'I'. And वन्दे is a finite verb, not a noun, so the sentence is verb + object, not two subjects.

51. (D) वन्दे (1st sg., root वन्द्) takes the accusative object मातरम् (stem मातृ), and the song opens Bankim Chandra's आनन्दमठः (1882) — all four facts correct. The verb is not third-person plural and मातरम् is not nominative. वन्दे does not mean 'I love', मातरम् is not genitive, and the date is 1882, not the 12th century. And वन्दे is a verb, not a noun, with a known author.

52. (D) The correct statement is right on all three: meaning (reverence for the Mother), imagery (Himalaya-crown, ocean-feet), and grammar (वन्दे + accusative मातरम्, genitive भारतमातुः चरणौ). The warrior-king option errs in grammar (nominative माता) and inverts the imagery. The foreign-queen option errs in meaning (वन्दे ≠ 'love') and imagery (deserts). And the trade-routes option errs in meaning, grammar (genitive object) and author (it is Bankim Chandra, not Tagore).

53. (D) Every adjective must agree with मातरम् in feminine accusative singular, giving सुजलाम् सुफलाम् सस्यश्यामलाम् मातरम्. The all-nominative version (सुजला ... माता) drops the object case. The -ऐः forms are instrumental plural ('by means of'), not agreeing accusatives. And the -आयाः/मातुः forms are genitive ('of'), again not the required accusative.

54. (A) वन्दे conveys reverence, not affection, and the 'Mother' is the personified Motherland (भारतमाता), not a household mother — two separate errors. वन्दे does not mean 'I look at', and 'love' is not the right verb. The 'Mother' is the land personified, not literally the Himalaya. So the rendering is wrong on both counts, not acceptable.

55. (D) श्यामला denotes dark/green colour, and सस्य ('crops') anchors that to fertile fields, giving 'dark-green with crops' — not white. So the snow-white rendering inverts the colour. श्यामला does not mean white in any reading here, and it is not 'sea-blue' either; the image is green farmland.

56. (A) नमः, an indeclinable, pairs with a dative ('homage TO'), while the verb वन्दे takes an accusative object (मातरम्). So the two saluting words govern different cases — they are not both accusative, the assignment is not reversed (नमः is not the accusative one), and neither takes a genitive 'of' for this meaning.

57. (C) The -औ ending on मातरौ marks the accusative dual ('two mothers'), so it cannot mean one Mother; the singular needs मातरम्. -औ is not the accusative singular (that is -म्). It is accusative dual here, the object of वन्दे, not a nominative subject. And it is the dual (exactly two), not a general plural for 'more than zero'.

58. (B) वन्दे is active first-person, takes accusative मातरम्; रत्नाकरः is the ocean; पर्वतराजः (Himalaya) is a genitive तत्पुरुष ('king of mountains') — all four facts correct. वन्दे is not passive with nominative माता, and रत्नाकरः is not the Himalaya, nor is पर्वतराजः a द्वन्द्व. वन्दे does not mean 'love' with a genitive, रत्नाकरः is not the farmers, and पर्वतराजः denotes its own head (a king), so it is तत्पुरुष, not बहुव्रीहि. And वन्दे is not third-person with a dative object, nor is रत्नाकरः the crops.

59. (B) Because मठ begins with the consonant म, no vowel-sandhi occurs at the seam; आनन्द ('bliss') and मठ ('abbey') stand side by side, yielding 'Abbey of Bliss'. There is no आ+अ vowel fusion to undo, since the second member starts with a consonant. The word denotes the abbey itself (its head member मठ), so it is not a बहुव्रीहि pointing to an outside person. And मठ is a noun, not a verb, so the whole is a compound noun, not a verb-phrase.

60. (D) Citation forms (सुजला, माता) are nominative; वन्दे demands its object in the accusative, and the adjective must agree, so both become accusative: वन्दे सुजलाम् मातरम्. The dictionary instinct fails because वन्दे does not govern the nominative. Changing only the adjective (leaving माता) or only the noun (leaving सुजला) breaks adjective-noun agreement -- both words must shift together to the accusative.
