Set 1 · Q1–Q10

Q1

'Financial infrastructure' is:

  • Athe roads and bridges of a country
  • Bthe schools and hospitals of a country
  • Cthe network of banks, payment systems, stock markets and institutions that move and manage money✓
  • Dthe army and the police

Working

✓

Financial infrastructure is banks, payment systems, stock markets and institutions that move and manage money. Roads are physical infrastructure, schools social, and the army state machinery.

Q2

Financial infrastructure is said to ____ physical infrastructure.

  • Areplace
  • Bcomplement✓
  • Cdestroy
  • Dhave nothing to do with

Working

✓

Financial infrastructure complements physical infrastructure, moving money rather than goods. It does not replace, destroy, or stand apart from it.

Q3

Which is part of financial infrastructure?

  • Ahighways and ports
  • Bschools and hospitals
  • Cbanks and the stock market✓
  • Dpower grids and pipelines

Working

✓

Banks and the stock market are financial infrastructure. Highways and ports are transport, schools and hospitals social, and grids and pipelines physical-utility infrastructure.

Q4

A 'bank' is a financial institution that:

  • Aonly prints the country's currency
  • Bonly builds roads
  • Ccollects money as deposits and lends money as loans✓
  • Donly sells shares to people

Working

✓

A bank collects deposits and lends loans. Printing currency is the RBI's job, road-building is not a bank's, and selling shares is the stock market.

Q5

The claim 'a bank just keeps your money safe in a locker' is wrong because a bank:

  • Akeeps every rupee untouched in a vault
  • Blends most of the deposits out as loans, keeping only some as reserves✓
  • Cnever lends to anyone
  • Dgives all the money away free

Working

✓

A bank lends most deposits out as loans, keeping some as reserves - connecting savers and borrowers. It does not keep every rupee idle, refuse to lend, or give money away.

Q6

A 'savings account' is mainly for:

  • Aa company's daily business with very frequent transactions
  • Blocking money away for a fixed period only
  • Ckeeping money safe while earning some interest, with easy withdrawals✓
  • Dbuying shares on the stock market

Working

✓

A savings account keeps money safe, earns interest and allows easy withdrawals. Heavy daily business use is a current account, locking money for a period is a fixed deposit, and buying shares is the stock market.

Q7

A 'fixed deposit' account:

  • Aallows unlimited daily withdrawals
  • Blocks money away for a set period and usually pays higher interest✓
  • Cpays no interest at all
  • Dis a share in a company

Working

✓

A fixed deposit locks money for a set period at higher interest. It is not for unlimited withdrawals, interest-free, or a company share.

Q8

A 'current account' is most suited to:

  • Aa child saving pocket money slowly
  • Blocking savings away for five years
  • Ca business making many transactions each day✓
  • Dbuying a single share

Working

✓

A current account suits businesses with frequent daily transactions. Slow saving suits a savings account, locking money a fixed deposit, and share-buying the stock market.

Q9

A 'deposit' is:

  • Amoney placed in a bank account that can be withdrawn and often earns interest✓
  • Bmoney borrowed from a bank to repay later
  • Ca share of a company's profit
  • Dthe original sum before interest

Working

✓

A deposit is money placed in a bank, withdrawable and often interest-earning. Borrowed money is a loan, a profit share is a dividend, and the original sum is the principal.

Q10

'Interest' is:

  • Athe original sum of money deposited
  • Bthe extra money a bank pays a depositor, or charges a borrower, over time for the use of money✓
  • Ca share of a company's profit
  • Da paper cheque

Working

✓

Interest is the extra money paid or charged over time for the use of money. The original sum is the principal, a profit share a dividend, and a cheque a payment instrument.

Q11

  • Bspending money as fast as possible

Compounding is earning interest on interest, speeding growth. It is not fast spending, idle money, or losing to inflation.

Q13

  • Ba share of company profit
  • Cthe original sum of money lent or saved, before any interest is added✓
  • Dthe benchmark rate set by the RBI

Working

✓

The principal (Latin principalis, 'chief') is the original sum before interest. Interest, a dividend and the benchmark rate are different.

Q14

  • Ashrink to nothing

Working

✓

Compounding (interest on interest) makes money grow faster over time than a single simple-interest payment. It does not shrink, stay fixed, or become a share.

Q15

A loan is borrowed money repaid later with interest. A deposit is money kept safe, a share is company ownership, and the benchmark rate is set by the RBI.

Q16

'Credit', from the Latin for 'to trust', is:

  • Bmoney lent in the trust that it will be repaid✓
  • Ca share traded on the market
  • Da paper cheque

Working

✓

Credit (Latin credere, 'to trust') is money lent in trust of repayment. It is not printed currency, a share, or a cheque.

Q17

When a bank gives a loan, it expects the borrower to:

  • Anever repay anything
  • Brepay less than was borrowed
  • Cbecome the bank's owner
  • Drepay the amount later, with interest✓

Working

✓

A borrower repays the loan with interest. They do not repay nothing, repay less, or become the bank's owner.

Q18

A family borrowing from a bank to build a house is taking a:

  • Adeposit, earning them interest
  • Bdividend from a company
  • Cloan, to be repaid with interest✓
  • Dshare in the bank

Working

✓

Borrowing to build a house is a loan, repaid with interest. A deposit earns interest for the saver, a dividend is a profit share, and a share is company ownership.

Q19

Banks mainly earn money from:

  • Aprinting their own currency
  • Bthe gap between the higher interest they charge borrowers and the lower interest they pay depositors✓
  • Ccharging nothing to anyone
  • Dgiving away free loans

Working

✓

Banks earn from charging borrowers more interest than they pay depositors. They do not print currency, charge nothing, or give free loans.

Q20

The claim 'money in a savings account just sits idle in the bank' is wrong because the bank:

  • Alocks every rupee away untouched

Working

✓

The bank lends most of your deposit out, keeping your savings working in the economy. It does not lock it away, lose it, or print money.

Q35

Inflation (Latin inflare, 'to blow up') is a general rise in prices, reducing money's buying power; the RBI tries to control it. It is not falling prices, cheques, or a dividend.

✓

Keeping inflation in check protects the value of money. Runaway rising prices erode savings, money's value does matter, and unlimited inflation is harmful.
