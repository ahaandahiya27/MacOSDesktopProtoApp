**Instructions:** Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. National-olympiad depth. Game logic: the NEXT verse opens with the LAST heard अक्षर (full consonant+vowel syllable, e.g. ते not त्), never the last bare consonant or the previous opener; a player who ends on a long vowel (...या) hands over या not य. Sandhi at seams: श्लोक+अन्त्य is savarṇa-dīrgha (अ+अ→आ), निः+गन्ध and साधोः+विपरीतम् are visarga→र् before voiced sounds — distinct mechanisms, do not conflate. The तादर्थ्ये चतुर्थी -आय endings (विवादाय/दानाय/रक्षणाय) are DATIVE OF PURPOSE, not genitive/locative, and they line up विद्या-धन-शक्ति → विवाद-मद-परिपीडन (wicked) AGAINST ज्ञान-दान-रक्षण (good) — same gift, विपरीत use; never swap the triple's order. परेषाम् = 'of OTHERS' (gen. pl. of पर). खल = a fault of WILL (moral), मूर्ख = a fault of intellect; विद्या = studied learning, ज्ञान = realised insight; worth rests on शीलम् (=वृत्तम्/आचरणम्), so the शीलम्-less are भारभूताः (=भारकराः). किंशुक = the bright but SCENTLESS Flame of the Forest; the simile dies if the scentlessness is dropped. क्रीडाम वयम् is a cohortative 'let us play' (1st-person plural, आत्मनेपद-sense exhortation), not a statement or command to others.    (A) त् — only the final bare consonant is carried, because vowels are dropped at a word's end
   (B) शो — the FIRST syllable of the closing word is what the next player must echo
   (C) ते — the rule passes on the LAST full syllable actually pronounced, and शोभन्ते ends in the syllable ते (त् + ए), so the next verse must begin with ते
   (D) न — the penultimate syllable न is taken, since ते is treated as a mere case-ending
