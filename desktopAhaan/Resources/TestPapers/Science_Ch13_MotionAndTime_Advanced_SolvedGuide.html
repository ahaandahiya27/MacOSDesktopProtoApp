Set 1 · Q1–Q10

Q1

A ceiling fan spinning at constant speed has each blade-tip moving in a circle once every 0.4 s. Which two motion descriptors fit the tip best?

  • Acircular and oscillatory
  • Brectilinear and periodic
  • Crandom and periodic
  • Dcircular and periodic✓

Working

✓

Each blade-tip traces a circle, so the motion is circular. It also returns to the same orientation every 0.4 s, so it's periodic. It is NOT oscillatory (oscillation needs back-and-forth across an equilibrium point), and clearly not rectilinear or random.

Q2

A child swings from rest, rises to the same height on the other side, and stops there forever (no further push). The motion shown is:

  • Aperiodic, because it crossed the centre once
  • Buniform circular motion
  • Coscillatory only — not periodic, because it didn't repeat✓
  • Drandom motion

Working

✓

Oscillation requires back-and-forth across an equilibrium; the swing did that once. Periodicity requires the cycle to REPEAT — a single excursion doesn't qualify. So it's oscillatory but not periodic. Option A confuses crossing with repeating; B and D are unrelated.

Q3

A car covers 50 km in the 1st hour, 70 km in the 2nd hour, and 90 km in the 3rd hour. Its motion is best described as:

  • Auniform
  • Bat rest
  • Cnon-uniform, speeding up✓
  • Dnon-uniform, slowing down

Working

✓

Equal time-intervals (1 hour each) but unequal distances (50 → 70 → 90 km) → non-uniform. The distance per hour is GROWING, so the car is speeding up. Slowing down would mean shrinking distances per equal time.

Q4

A train 200 m long crosses a 300 m platform completely in 25 s. The train's speed is:

  • A12 m/s
  • B4 m/s
  • C8 m/s
  • D20 m/s✓

Working

✓

The train's nose enters the platform and the train's tail leaves the other end — total distance covered = train length + platform length = 200 + 300 = 500 m. Speed = 500 m ÷ 25 s = 20 m/s.

Q5

A man walks **half** of his journey at 4 km/h and the **other half** at 6 km/h. His average speed for the whole journey is:

  • A5 km/h
  • B5.2 km/h
  • C4.8 km/h✓
  • D2.4 km/h

Working

✓

Equal distances → use harmonic mean: avg = 2·v₁·v₂/(v₁+v₂) = 2·4·6/(4+6) = 48/10 = 4.8 km/h. The trap is option A — averaging speeds arithmetically only works for equal TIMES, not equal distances.

Q6

A boat moves at 12 km/h in still water. In a river flowing at 4 km/h, what are the boat's speeds **downstream** and **upstream**?

  • A12 km/h both ways
  • B8 km/h and 16 km/h
  • C16 km/h and 8 km/h✓
  • D4 km/h and 12 km/h

Working

✓

Downstream the current helps: 12 + 4 = 16 km/h. Upstream the current opposes: 12 − 4 = 8 km/h. Many kids reverse this — remember "down" means with the flow, which adds.

Q7

A pendulum's length is **doubled**. By what factor does its time period change? (Use *T* ∝ √*L*.)

  • A×2
  • B×4
  • C÷2
  • D×√2✓

Working

✓

T ∝ √L, so doubling L scales T by √2 (about 1.414×). Option A is the common mistake — period does NOT scale linearly with length.

Q8

A pendulum's length is **quartered** (cut to one-fourth). Its time period:

  • Adoubles
  • Bhalves✓
  • Cis one-fourth as long
  • Dis unchanged

Working

✓

T ∝ √L, and √(1/4) = 1/2. So the period halves. Option C is the natural trap — but length-quartered does NOT quarter the period; the square root softens it.

Q9

Two cyclists start at the same point. P moves east at 5 m/s, Q moves west at 3 m/s. Their **rate of separation** is:

  • A8 m/s✓
  • B2 m/s
  • C15 m/s
  • D0 m/s

Working

✓

Moving in OPPOSITE directions adds their speeds for the rate of separation: 5 + 3 = 8 m/s. They draw apart at 8 m every second.

Q10

A car travelling at 20 m/s overtakes a truck moving at 15 m/s in the same direction. The relative speed of the car with respect to the truck is:

  • A35 m/s
  • B20 m/s
  • C0 m/s
  • D5 m/s✓

Working

✓

In the SAME direction, the relative speed is the difference: 20 − 15 = 5 m/s. From the truck driver's frame, the car only seems to approach at 5 m/s.

Set 2 · Q11–Q20

Q11

A satellite circles the Earth at 7.5 km/s. Expressed in km/h, its speed is:

  • A2700 km/h
  • B270 km/h
  • C27 000 km/h✓
  • D270 000 km/h

Working

✓

7.5 km/s × 3600 s/h = 27 000 km/h. Many ISS-style satellites really do orbit at roughly this speed — about 27 000 km/h or Mach 25.

Q12

A car maintains an average speed of 60 km/h for a 240 km trip. If the first 120 km were covered at 40 km/h, what speed must the car average over the second 120 km?

  • A80 km/h
  • B100 km/h
  • C120 km/h✓
  • D90 km/h

Working

✓

Target avg of 60 km/h × 4 h trip would give 240 km total — so total time must be 4 hours. First 120 km at 40 km/h takes 3 hours. The remaining 120 km must therefore be covered in 1 hour → 120 km/h. Note this is faster than the target average because the slow first half ate up most of the time budget.

Q13

The Moon's gravity is about *g*/6 of Earth's. A pendulum that has a time period of 1 s on Earth will, on the Moon, have a time period of about:

  • A1/√6 s
  • B6 s
  • C1 s — gravity doesn't matter
  • D√6 s ≈ 2.45 s✓

Working

✓

T = 2π√(L/g). If g shrinks by a factor of 6, then T grows by √6 ≈ 2.45×. So a 1-second Earth pendulum becomes about 2.45 s on the Moon.

Q14

A cricket ball is bowled at 144 km/h toward a batsman 18 m away. How long does the ball take to reach the batsman? (Assume constant speed.)

  • A0.45 s✓
  • B0.18 s
  • C1.0 s
  • D0.05 s

Working

✓

144 km/h × 5/18 = 40 m/s. Time = distance ÷ speed = 18 m ÷ 40 m/s = 0.45 s. That's why batters have only about half a second to react — they're really good.

Q15

A pendulum-clock that loses 6 minutes per day has a period that is approximately:

  • A0.4% too long✓
  • B0.4% too short
  • C6% too long
  • D6% too short

Working

✓

A day is 1440 minutes; losing 6 min is 6/1440 = 1/240 ≈ 0.4%. "Losing time" means the clock's seconds are LONGER than true seconds — its period is too LONG by ~0.4%.

Q16

A car covers a journey in three equal **time** segments at 30, 50, and 70 km/h. Its average speed for the whole journey is:

  • A50 km/h✓
  • B45 km/h
  • C47 km/h
  • D60 km/h

Working

✓

Equal TIME segments → arithmetic mean: (30+50+70)/3 = 150/3 = 50 km/h. This case is straightforward because the same hours weight each speed equally.

Q17

A car covers a journey in three equal **distance** segments at 20, 30, and 60 km/h. Its average speed for the whole journey is:

  • A30 km/h✓
  • B36.7 km/h
  • C40 km/h
  • D50 km/h

Working

✓

Equal DISTANCE segments → harmonic mean. avg = 3/(1/20+1/30+1/60) = 3/((3+2+1)/60) = 3/(6/60) = 30 km/h. The slow legs eat up so much time that they dominate.

Q18

On a distance–time graph the line is straight from (0 s, 0 m) to (10 s, 60 m), then horizontal from (10 s, 60 m) to (15 s, 60 m), then straight from (15 s, 60 m) back to (25 s, 0 m). At t = 20 s the object is:

  • Aat rest
  • Bat the starting point
  • C30 m from the start, returning✓
  • D90 m from the start

Working

✓

From t=15 s to t=25 s the line falls from 60 m to 0 m, slope = −6 m/s. At t = 20 s, the object has descended for 5 s at 6 m/s → distance from start = 60 − 30 = 30 m, moving back toward the start.

Q19

In the previous question, the **total distance travelled** in 25 s is:

  • A60 m
  • B0 m
  • C120 m✓
  • D30 m

Working

✓

Up: 60 m (during 0→10 s). At rest: 0 m (during 10→15 s, no distance covered). Back: 60 m (during 15→25 s). Total path length = 60 + 0 + 60 = 120 m.

Q20

A bike's wheel has a circumference of 2 m. The bike moves at 18 km/h. The wheel turns about how many times per second?

  • A2.5✓
  • B9
  • C1.0
  • D5

Working

✓

18 km/h = 5 m/s. Each revolution covers one circumference = 2 m. Revolutions per second = 5 ÷ 2 = 2.5.

Set 3 · Q21–Q30

Q21

The slope of a distance–time graph gives the:

  • Atotal distance
  • Btotal time
  • Cspeed at that moment✓
  • Dacceleration

Working

✓

Slope of distance ÷ time = speed at that instant. The bigger the slope, the faster the motion at that moment. Slope is NOT mass, distance, or acceleration.

Q22

A car's distance–time graph is a parabola opening upward starting at the origin. The car is:

  • Aat rest
  • Bmoving at constant speed
  • Cspeeding up✓
  • Dslowing down

Working

✓

A parabola that bends UPWARD means distance grows faster and faster as time passes — the object is speeding up. A straight line would be constant speed; a curve flattening out would be slowing.

Q23

Two pendulums A and B have lengths 1 m and 4 m. The ratio of their time periods *T*ₐ : *T_b* is:

  • A1 : 2✓
  • B2 : 1
  • C1 : 4
  • D4 : 1

Working

✓

T ∝ √L. T_a : T_b = √1 : √4 = 1 : 2. So pendulum B (4 m) has TWICE the period of pendulum A (1 m), even though its length is 4× longer.

Q24

A simple pendulum's period is 2 s on Earth. To make it 1 s, its length must be changed to:

  • Aone-fourth✓
  • Bhalf
  • Cdouble
  • Dfour times

Working

✓

T ∝ √L, so to halve T you need to QUARTER L. Many kids halve the length and get a √2 factor wrong; the correct answer is one-fourth.

Q25

A lightning flash is seen, then thunder is heard 6 s later. Sound travels at 330 m/s; light is essentially instant. The storm is about:

  • A1.98 km away✓
  • B6 km away
  • C19.8 km away
  • D60 km away

Working

✓

Sound travels 330 m/s × 6 s = 1980 m = 1.98 km. The flash arrived essentially the instant the lightning struck (light is much faster than sound), so the 6-second gap is the sound's travel time.

Q26

Two trains move toward each other on parallel tracks at 60 km/h and 40 km/h. The gap closes at:

  • A100 km/h✓
  • B20 km/h
  • C50 km/h
  • D200 km/h

Working

✓

Trains approach each other; their CLOSING speed is the sum: 60 + 40 = 100 km/h. The gap shrinks at 100 km every hour.

Q27

A person walks at 3 km/h on a moving walkway that travels at 5 km/h in the same direction. Their ground speed is:

  • A2 km/h
  • B4 km/h
  • C5 km/h
  • D8 km/h✓

Working

✓

Walking IN THE SAME DIRECTION as the walkway, ground speed adds: 3 + 5 = 8 km/h. Compare to standing still on the walkway (5 km/h) — walking adds your foot-speed.

Q28

On a distance–time graph, line A passes through (0, 0) and (10, 50); line B passes through (0, 20) and (10, 30). When and where do the lines first meet?

  • Anever — A is steeper but starts lower
  • Bat t = 4 s, distance = 20 m
  • Cat t = 5 s, distance = 25 m✓
  • Dat t = 10 s, distance = 50 m

Working

✓

Line A: y = 5x. Line B: y = 20 + x. They meet when 5x = 20 + x → 4x = 20 → x = 5 s. At that moment y = 25 m. So they first cross at (5 s, 25 m).

Q29

Cars A and B follow the lines from Q28. At **t = 2 s**, the **gap** between them is:

  • A0 m
  • B20 m
  • C30 m
  • D12 m✓

Working

✓

At t = 2 s: A is at 5×2 = 10 m, B is at 20 + 2 = 22 m. Gap = 22 − 10 = 12 m. B is still ahead (it had a 20 m head start; A has only gained 2 m so far).

Q30

A bus moves north at 30 km/h while a man inside walks toward the front at 5 km/h relative to the bus. The man's speed **relative to the ground** is:

  • A25 km/h north
  • B5 km/h north
  • C35 km/h north✓
  • D30 km/h north

Working

✓

Same direction (north). The man's ground speed = bus speed + his speed relative to bus = 30 + 5 = 35 km/h north.

Q31

A pendulum makes 24 oscillations in exactly 30 s. The frequency of its oscillation is:

  • A1.25 Hz
  • B4.0 Hz
  • C0.25 Hz
  • D0.8 Hz✓

Working

✓

Frequency = oscillations ÷ time = 24 ÷ 30 s = 0.8 Hz. So the pendulum completes 0.8 oscillations every second on average.

Q32

Frequency and time period are linked by *f* = 1/*T*. A pendulum with *T* = 0.5 s has frequency:

  • A2 Hz✓
  • B0.5 Hz
  • C5 Hz
  • D0.05 Hz

Working

✓

f = 1/T = 1/0.5 = 2 Hz. A half-second period means twice-per-second oscillation.

Q33

A car accelerates from rest, covering 2 m in the 1st second, 6 m in the 2nd second, and 10 m in the 3rd second. Average speed over the first three seconds is:

  • A6 m/s✓
  • B18 m/s
  • C2 m/s
  • D10 m/s

Working

✓

Total distance in 3 seconds: 2 + 6 + 10 = 18 m. Total time = 3 s. Average speed = 18 ÷ 3 = 6 m/s. The motion itself is non-uniform (it's accelerating), but the AVERAGE is still well-defined.

Q34

A train of length 100 m, moving at 36 km/h, takes how long to pass a stationary observer completely?

  • A10 s✓
  • B2.8 s
  • C36 s
  • D100 s

Working

✓

36 km/h = 10 m/s. To pass an observer completely, the train's whole length must cross — so distance = train length = 100 m. Time = 100 ÷ 10 = 10 s.

Q35

Two pendulums A (period 2 s) and B (period 3 s) start in sync. They will next be **simultaneously back in phase** at a multiple of:

  • A5 s
  • B6 s✓
  • C1 s
  • D12 s

Working

✓

They will be back in phase when both have completed an integer number of periods. The smallest common multiple of 2 s and 3 s is 6 s — after 6 s, A has done 3 full swings and B has done 2 full swings, both back at the same position.

Q36

A quartz oscillator vibrates at 32 768 Hz inside most wristwatches. Its period is about:

  • A32 768 s
  • B30 ms
  • C30 μs✓
  • D3 s

Working

✓

T = 1/f = 1/32768 ≈ 3.05 × 10⁻⁵ s ≈ 30 μs (microseconds). Quartz vibrates incredibly fast, which is why digital watches keep time so accurately.

Q37

A car driver's reaction time is 0.6 s. When she sees a hazard while driving at 20 m/s, the car covers **before** she even touches the brake:

  • A6 m
  • B12 m✓
  • C33 m
  • D1.2 m

Working

✓

During the 0.6 s reaction time the car travels at 20 m/s × 0.6 s = 12 m — completely before the brake even starts working. That's why following distance matters so much at speed.

Q38

Light from the Sun takes about 8 minutes to reach Earth. If the speed of light is 3 × 10⁵ km/s, the Sun is approximately:

  • A14 million km away
  • B144 million km away
  • C1.44 × 10⁸ km away
  • DBoth B and C describe the same distance✓

Working

✓

300 000 km/s × 480 s (8 minutes) = 1.44 × 10⁸ km. That same number written in plain words is 144 million km. So options B and C are two formats of the SAME distance — D acknowledges both are correct.

Q39

A car maintains a steady 72 km/h for 30 minutes, then 90 km/h for 20 minutes. Its average speed for the 50-minute drive is closest to:

  • A79 km/h✓
  • B81 km/h
  • C76 km/h
  • D84 km/h

Working

✓

Distance: 72 km/h × 0.5 h = 36 km; 90 km/h × (1/3) h = 30 km. Total 66 km in 50 min (5/6 h). Avg = 66 ÷ (5/6) = 66 × 6/5 = 79.2 km/h ≈ 79 km/h.

Q40

The **steepness** of a distance–time graph line corresponds to:

  • Athe object's mass
  • Bthe object's speed✓
  • Cthe elapsed time
  • Dthe choice of axes only

Working

✓

A steeper d-t line = larger distance covered per unit time = higher speed. Steepness has nothing to do with mass, elapsed time, or axis choice (axes scale, not slope-meaning).

Set 5 · Q41–Q50

Q41

On a distance–time graph, two cars start at the same place. Car A's line is a straight slope; car B's line is a curve that **bends upward**. After 10 s:

  • Acar A is always ahead
  • Bcar B is always ahead
  • Ccar B started slower but ends up ahead if the curve bends enough✓
  • Dboth cars are always at the same point

Working

✓

Initially the steady straight-line car (A) is ahead because B starts slower. But B's speed is GROWING (the curve bends upward). If the curve bends enough, B's accumulated distance can overtake A's by t = 10 s. So C is correct: B may catch up and even surpass.

Q42

A bus driver covers 90 km between two cities, planning to average 60 km/h. The first 45 km took 1 hour. To still hit the 60 km/h average, the remaining 45 km must be covered in:

  • A30 minutes✓
  • B45 minutes
  • C1 hour
  • D0 minutes — impossible without an instantaneous teleport

Working

✓

Total trip target: 90 km ÷ 60 km/h = 1.5 hours. First 45 km already took 1 hour. So 30 minutes remain for the second 45 km → 45 km / 0.5 h = 90 km/h needed. So drive for 30 minutes.

Q43

A grandfather clock pendulum's length is doubled by accident. After exactly 1 (correct) hour, the clock's minute hand will be approximately:

  • A30 minutes behind correct time
  • B17.5 minutes behind correct time✓
  • Con time
  • D17.5 minutes ahead

Working

✓

L doubles → T scales by √2 ≈ 1.414. The clock's seconds are now 1.414× too long, so it counts only 1/1.414 ≈ 0.707 of the true elapsed time. In 1 true hour (60 min) it shows 60 × 0.707 ≈ 42.4 min — about 17.6 min behind. Closest: 17.5 min behind.

Q44

A traveller logs three consecutive 1-hour segments at speeds 20 km/h, 40 km/h and 60 km/h. The average speed for the 3 hours is:

  • A40 km/h✓
  • B30 km/h
  • C36 km/h
  • D45 km/h

Working

✓

Equal TIMES (1 hour each) → arithmetic mean of speeds: (20+40+60)/3 = 120/3 = 40 km/h. Same problem shape as Q16.

Q45

Same traveller, three consecutive segments **each 60 km long** at 20 km/h, 40 km/h and 60 km/h. Average speed for the whole 180 km is closest to:

  • A40 km/h
  • B32.7 km/h✓
  • C30 km/h
  • D50 km/h

Working

✓

Equal DISTANCES (60 km each) → harmonic mean. avg = 3 ÷ (1/20 + 1/40 + 1/60) = 3 ÷ (11/120) = 360/11 ≈ 32.7 km/h. Notice it's slower than the time-weighted version because the slow legs hog more time.

Q46

A sand clock measures a fixed time interval because:

  • Asand falls at the speed of light
  • Bthe gravitational pull on the sand is roughly constant✓
  • Csand has no mass
  • Dthe glass shape changes over time

Working

✓

Sand falls under gravity. As long as gravity is roughly constant (it is, anywhere on Earth's surface), the sand drops at a roughly constant rate, so it takes the same time to flow through. A sand clock would NOT work in zero gravity (e.g. on the ISS).

Q47

On a distance–time graph the line is a **horizontal** segment at distance 20 m, from t = 5 s to t = 12 s. During this segment the object's speed is:

  • A20 m/s
  • B0 m/s✓
  • C4 m/s
  • Dcannot be determined

Working

✓

Horizontal line on a distance–time graph = no change in distance over time = zero speed. The object is at rest at the 20 m mark from t = 5 s to t = 12 s.

Q48

A 200 m train moving at 20 m/s completely overtakes a 100 m train moving at 10 m/s in the same direction. How long does the overtake take, measured from when the noses align to when the tails clear?

  • A10 s
  • B30 s✓
  • C20 s
  • D15 s

Working

✓

Relative speed of the overtaking train with respect to the slower train = 20 − 10 = 10 m/s. Total distance to clear = 200 + 100 = 300 m (one train's nose must reach the tail of the other, then its own tail must clear). Time = 300 ÷ 10 = 30 s.

Q49

Two cars cover the same distance. Car A averages 60 km/h and car B averages 40 km/h. The ratio *time*_A : *time*_B is:

  • A3 : 2
  • B2 : 3✓
  • C1 : 1
  • D4 : 3

Working

✓

Same distance, so t = d/v → t ∝ 1/v. t_A : t_B = (1/60) : (1/40) = 40 : 60 = 2 : 3. Car A is faster, so its time is shorter — the smaller number in the ratio.

Q50

A wheel rolls without slipping at 5 m/s. Its diameter is 50 cm. The number of revolutions per second is closest to:

  • A1.6
  • B3.2✓
  • C10
  • D0.5

Working

✓

Diameter 0.5 m → circumference = π × 0.5 ≈ 1.57 m per revolution. At 5 m/s, revolutions per second = 5 ÷ 1.57 ≈ 3.18 ≈ 3.2.

Set 6 · Q51–Q60

Q51

Which of these statements is **false**?

  • Aperiod × frequency = 1 for any oscillator
  • Ba longer pendulum has a longer period
  • Cthe speed of light is much greater than the speed of sound
  • Dtwo pendulums with the same length but different bob masses have different periods✓

Working

✓

Statement D is FALSE — Galileo's discovery was that pendulum period depends ONLY on length (and g), NOT on bob mass. Two pendulums of the same length swing in identical times regardless of how heavy the bobs are. The other three statements are all true.

Q52

The slope of a **horizontal** segment on a distance–time graph corresponds to a speed of:

  • Amaximum
  • Bzero✓
  • Cnegative
  • Dundefined

Working

✓

A horizontal segment means distance is not changing as time passes → the object is stationary → speed = 0. The slope of a horizontal line is exactly zero.

Q53

A drone flies 600 m east in 40 s, then 600 m west in 20 s, ending where it started. The drone's average **speed** (total path ÷ total time) is:

  • A0 m/s
  • B20 m/s✓
  • C30 m/s
  • D10 m/s

Working

✓

Speed (path-length basis) = total path ÷ total time = (600+600) m ÷ (40+20) s = 1200 ÷ 60 = 20 m/s. Note that DISPLACEMENT-based average velocity would be 0 (drone returns to start), but the question asks for path-based speed.

Q54

A person crosses a 2 km bridge walking at 4 km/h. A second person cycles the same bridge at 12 km/h, but starts 15 minutes later. The cyclist finishes ahead of the walker by:

  • A0 min — they tie
  • B5 min✓
  • C10 min
  • D20 min

Working

✓

Walker: 2 km / 4 km/h = 0.5 h = 30 min, finishes at t = 30 min. Cyclist: 2 km / 12 km/h = 10 min, but starts at t = 15 min, so finishes at t = 25 min. Cyclist beats walker by 30 − 25 = 5 min.

Q55

A pendulum-clock is taken from sea level (g = 9.8 m/s²) to a mountaintop (g = 9.7 m/s²). The clock will:

  • Arun faster than it did at sea level
  • Brun slower than it did at sea level✓
  • Ckeep the same time exactly
  • Dstop completely

Working

✓

T ∝ 1/√g. Smaller g (mountaintop) → larger T → seconds are too long → clock runs SLOWER than at sea level. This is why precision pendulum clocks must be re-calibrated when moved.

Q56

A car travels 30 km, then idles in traffic for 30 minutes, then travels another 30 km. The first 30 km took 1 hour; the second 30 km took 30 minutes. The average speed for the **entire trip (including the idle)** is:

  • A60 km/h
  • B40 km/h
  • C30 km/h✓
  • D45 km/h

Working

✓

Total distance = 30 + 30 = 60 km. Total time = 1 h (first leg) + 0.5 h (idle) + 0.5 h (second leg) = 2 h. Avg = 60 ÷ 2 = 30 km/h. The idle time counts even though no distance was covered.

Q57

On a graph of **total distance travelled** (cumulative) vs time, the slope can never be:

  • Azero
  • Bnegative✓
  • Cvery large
  • Dthe same at two different points

Working

✓

Cumulative distance only increases (or stays the same) — you can't "untravel" distance you've already covered. So the slope cannot be negative. All other options (zero slope = at rest, very large slope = high speed, same slope at two points = same speed twice) are possible.

Q58

A wave on a string oscillates 20 times every 5 s. Its frequency is:

  • A4 Hz✓
  • B0.25 Hz
  • C100 Hz
  • D0.4 Hz

Working

✓

Frequency = oscillations ÷ time = 20 ÷ 5 = 4 Hz. Higher frequency = more vibrations per second.

Q59

Spot the **incorrect** unit conversion among these four:

  • A36 km/h = 10 m/s
  • B54 km/h = 15 m/s
  • C72 km/h = 20 m/s
  • D90 km/h = 30 m/s✓

Working

✓

Check each conversion using 1 km/h = 5/18 m/s. A: 36 × 5/18 = 10 ✓. B: 54 × 5/18 = 15 ✓. C: 72 × 5/18 = 20 ✓. D: 90 × 5/18 = 25, but the option says 30 — that's wrong by 5 m/s. So D is the incorrect conversion.

Q60

A 60 m long train crosses a 240 m long bridge completely in 15 s. Its speed is:

  • A4 m/s
  • B20 m/s✓
  • C16 m/s
  • D12 m/s

Working

✓

To cross a bridge, the train must travel one bridge-length PLUS one train-length: 240 + 60 = 300 m. Speed = 300 m ÷ 15 s = 20 m/s. Like the platform problem in Q4. --- *End of solutions. Maximum marks: 240.*
