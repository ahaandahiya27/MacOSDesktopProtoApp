Set 1 · Q1–Q10

Q1

Which statement about air is correct?

  • AAir has no weight and exerts no pressure.
  • BAir exerts pressure only downward.
  • CAir exerts pressure only at sea level.
  • DAir has weight and exerts pressure in all directions.✓

Working

✓

Air is matter: it has mass (weight) and, like any fluid, it presses equally in every direction — up, down and sideways. It does not press only downward or only at sea level.

Q2

Wind is best described as:

  • Aair that is being heated by the Sun
  • Bwater vapour rising into the sky
  • Cair moving from a region of higher pressure to a region of lower pressure✓
  • Dair moving from a region of lower pressure to a region of higher pressure

Working

✓

Wind is simply moving air, and air always flows from where the pressure is higher to where it is lower. Heating by itself is not wind; the *movement* it sets up is.

Q3

A little water is boiled in a tin can, the cap is screwed on tightly, and the can is then cooled. The can gets crushed because:

  • Athe cold water outside pushes the walls in
  • Bthe metal shrinks and tears
  • Cthe air and steam inside cool, their pressure drops, and the higher outside air pressure crushes it✓
  • Dcooling creates extra air inside that pushes the walls

Working

✓

Boiling drives out most air and fills the can with steam; capping it traps low-density hot gas. On cooling, the steam condenses and the trapped gas contracts, so the inside pressure drops well below the outside air pressure, which then crushes the can. (The walls are pushed in by air, not by cold water or shrinking metal.)

Q4

At a beach on a hot afternoon you feel a cool wind blowing from the sea toward the land. This sea breeze happens because:

  • Athe sea heats up faster than the land
  • Bthe land heats up faster than the sea, so warm air over the land rises and cooler sea air moves in✓
  • Cthe land is always cooler than the sea
  • Dsea water evaporates and pushes the air toward land

Working

✓

Land heats faster than water under the same Sun. The hot air over the land expands, becomes lighter and rises, lowering the pressure there; cooler, higher-pressure air from over the sea flows in to replace it — a sea breeze.

Q5

You hold two inflated balloons hanging side by side a few centimetres apart and blow a stream of air through the gap between them. The balloons will:

  • Amove apart
  • Bstay exactly where they are
  • Cboth rise upward
  • Dmove toward each other✓

Working

✓

The fast air stream you blow between the balloons has lower pressure than the still air on their outer sides. The higher outside pressure pushes the balloons together, so they move toward each other. (The common guess "move apart" is the trap.)

Q6

Inside the towering clouds of a thunderstorm, the chief reason for the violent activity is:

  • Acold dry air sinking quickly to the ground
  • Blightning being created first and then pulling up the air
  • Crapidly rising warm, moist air together with falling water drops✓
  • Dthe ground heating the clouds from below by touch

Working

✓

A thunderstorm's energy comes from warm, moisture-laden air shooting upward while water drops fall back down through it. Lightning is a *result* of this churning, not its cause; cold sinking air would suppress a storm.

Q7

Air over the equator and air over the poles are heated very differently by the Sun. The direct result of this uneven heating is that:

  • Alarge-scale wind currents are set up that move heat around the planet✓
  • Bthere is no wind anywhere on Earth
  • Cthe poles become hotter than the equator
  • Dair stops having any weight near the equator

Working

✓

The equator receives far more direct sunlight than the poles. This temperature difference creates pressure differences, which drive large-scale wind currents that carry heat from hot regions toward cold ones.

Q8

As a cyclone forms over a warm ocean, water vapour in the rising air condenses into droplets. This condensation helps the cyclone grow because it:

  • Acools the air and stops the wind
  • Bmakes the air heavier so it sinks back down
  • Creleases heat, which warms the air further, lowers the pressure more, and pulls in even faster wind✓
  • Dadds weight that raises the pressure at the centre

Working

✓

When water vapour condenses it releases its stored (latent) heat. That extra warmth makes the rising air even lighter, drops the central pressure further, and pulls in faster wind carrying still more moist air — a self-strengthening loop.

Q9

Wind blows fast over the curved top of a roof while the air inside the house is still. During a storm a poorly tied roof tends to be lifted off because:

  • Athe rain makes the roof float
  • Bthe fast air above the roof has lower pressure than the still air below, so the roof is pushed up✓
  • Cthe still air below has lower pressure and sucks the roof down
  • Dwind always pushes roofs straight down

Working

✓

Fast wind streaming over the curved roof has lower pressure than the still air trapped inside the house. The greater pressure below then pushes the roof upward, so a loosely tied roof can be lifted off.

Q10

The calm, clear, low-pressure region at the very centre of a cyclone is called the:

  • Aeye✓
  • Beye wall
  • Cstorm surge
  • Dfront

Working

✓

The calm, clear, low-pressure centre of a cyclone is the eye. The violent ring around it is the eye wall.

Q11

Two identical sealed tins are taken to the top of a tall mountain and to the seashore. Compared with the seashore tin, the mountain-top tin experiences:

  • Agreater outside air pressure
  • Blower outside air pressure, because there is less air above it✓
  • Cexactly the same outside air pressure
  • Dno air pressure at all

Working

✓

Air pressure comes from the weight of the air column above you. On a mountain there is less air overhead, so the outside pressure is lower than at the seashore.

Q12

On a hot day the air just above a sun-baked open field tends to:

  • Asink, because hot air is heavier
  • Bstay perfectly still
  • Crise, because it expands, becomes lighter, and is pushed up✓
  • Dturn into water

Working

✓

Sun-heated air over a hot field expands, grows less dense (lighter) and is pushed up by the cooler, denser air around it. Hot air rises; it does not sink or sit still.

Q13

The single greatest cause of death and destruction along the coast when a cyclone makes landfall is usually the:

  • Astorm surge — a huge rise of sea water flooding the low coast✓
  • Blightning strikes
  • Cloud thunder
  • Dhailstones

Working

✓

The deadliest part of a landfalling cyclone is usually the storm surge — a great heap of sea water driven onto the low coast, drowning huge areas. Lightning, thunder and hail cause far less of the destruction.

Q14

A balloon is stretched over the mouth of an empty bottle, and the bottle is then placed in a bowl of hot water. The balloon inflates because:

  • Ahot water leaks into the bottle
  • Bthe glass squeezes the air upward
  • Cthe air inside warms, expands, and pushes the balloon outward✓
  • Dthe balloon makes its own air

Working

✓

Warm water heats the air inside the bottle; the air expands and, having nowhere else to go, pushes out into the balloon and inflates it. No water enters and the balloon makes no air of its own.

Q15

At night the wind at a coast usually blows from the land toward the sea (a land breeze) because:

  • Athe land stays warmer than the sea all night
  • Bthe sea cools faster than the land
  • Cthe land cools faster than the sea, so now the warmer air is over the sea and rises, drawing air out from the land✓
  • Dwinds always reverse at sunset for no reason

Working

✓

At night the land loses heat faster than the sea, so now the sea is the warmer surface. Air over the sea rises, lowering pressure there, and cooler air flows out from the land toward the sea — a land breeze.

Q16

When a cyclone alert is issued for a coastal area, the safest immediate action is to:

  • Ago to the beach to watch the waves
  • Bmove to a safer place such as a cyclone shelter and follow official instructions✓
  • Cignore it unless the wind is already strong
  • Ddrive toward the sea to escape inland traffic

Working

✓

The right response to a cyclone alert is to move to a safe place such as a cyclone shelter and follow the authorities' instructions. Going to the beach or toward the sea, or ignoring the warning, is dangerous.

Q17

The flash of lightning and the rumble of thunder are produced at the same instant, yet we usually see the lightning before we hear the thunder. This is because:

  • Athunder happens a few seconds after lightning
  • Blight travels much faster than sound✓
  • Csound travels faster than light
  • Dthe eye reacts before the ear by several seconds

Working

✓

Light and sound are made together in a lightning stroke, but light travels enormously faster than sound, so the flash reaches our eyes well before the thunder reaches our ears.

Q18

India's summer monsoon brings heavy rain mainly because, in summer, the moisture-laden winds blow:

  • Afrom the sea toward the land✓
  • Bfrom the land toward the sea
  • Cstraight downward from the sky
  • Dfrom the poles toward the equator

Working

✓

In summer the land heats more than the sea, drawing moist air inland from the sea toward the land; that sea-to-land flow carries the rain of the summer monsoon.

Q19

A strip of paper is held just below your lower lip and you blow hard straight across its top surface. The free end of the strip will:

  • Abe pushed straight down
  • Bnot move at all
  • Clift up✓
  • Dcurl toward your mouth

Working

✓

Blowing across the top of the strip makes the air there move fast, so its pressure drops below the still air underneath. The higher pressure below then lifts the free end up.

Q20

A cyclone gathers strength over warm tropical seas but quickly weakens after it moves onto land. The main reason it weakens over land is that:

  • Athe land is colder than space
  • Bbuildings physically block the wind
  • Cthe eye closes up on contact with soil
  • Dit is cut off from the warm, moist sea air that fed it✓

Working

✓

A cyclone is fed by warm, moist air from a warm sea. Once it moves over land it loses that supply of warm moist air, so it weakens. (Buildings and "cold land" are not the main reason.)

Q21

The narrow ring of the most violent, fastest winds and heaviest rain in a cyclone is the:

  • Aeye wall✓
  • Beye
  • Ccalm centre
  • Douter edge, where the storm is mildest

Working

✓

The eye wall is the narrow ring just around the eye, where the winds are fastest and the rain heaviest — the most violent part of the cyclone.

Q22

Air pressure pushes on us from every side, yet we are not crushed. The best explanation is that:

  • Athe pressure of the fluids and air inside our body pushes outward and balances it✓
  • Bair pressure is too weak to be felt at all
  • Cour skin is completely airtight and stiff
  • Dgravity cancels the air pressure exactly

Working

✓

The fluids and air inside our body push outward with about the same pressure as the atmosphere pushes inward, so the forces balance and we are not crushed. Air pressure is real and strong; gravity does not cancel it.

Q23

The instrument used to measure the speed of the wind is the:

  • Athermometer
  • Bbarometer
  • Canemometer✓
  • Drain gauge

Working

✓

Wind speed is measured with an anemometer. (A thermometer measures temperature, a barometer pressure, a rain gauge rainfall.)

Q24

Why does air rush in to take the place of warm air that has risen above a hot region?

  • Abecause the cooler air is attracted by sunlight
  • Bbecause cooler air is lighter than warm air
  • Cbecause the Sun pulls the air sideways
  • Dbecause the rising warm air leaves a region of lower pressure behind it✓

Working

✓

When warm air rises away from a hot region it leaves lower pressure behind. Surrounding higher-pressure air then rushes in to fill that low-pressure region. Cooler air is denser, not lighter, so options B and C are wrong.

Q25

During a severe cyclone, people are advised **not** to drink water from open, flooded sources mainly because:

  • Athe flood water is likely contaminated and can spread disease✓
  • Bflood water is too cold
  • Cthe water has too much salt to taste good
  • Dopen water reflects lightning

Working

✓

Flood water during a cyclone is usually contaminated with sewage and debris and can spread disease, so people are told not to drink from open flooded sources.

Q26

By day the air pressure over the warm land is lower than over the cooler sea. Knowing wind blows from high to low pressure, the daytime breeze must blow:

  • Afrom the land to the sea
  • Bfrom the sea to the land✓
  • Cstraight up only
  • Din a full circle with no net direction

Working

✓

Wind flows from high pressure to low pressure. Here the sea has the higher pressure and the land the lower, so the daytime breeze blows from the sea to the land.

Q27

A sealed plastic bottle is filled with hot air and capped, then put in a refrigerator for an hour. When taken out it is found dented inward. The dent appears because:

  • Athe fridge squeezed it mechanically
  • Bcold makes plastic expand and fold
  • Cthe cooled air inside lost pressure, so the outside air pressed the sides in✓
  • Dwater from the fridge soaked into the plastic

Working

✓

Cooling the trapped air shrinks it and lowers its pressure below the outside air pressure, which then presses the bottle's sides inward, denting it. (The fridge does not squeeze it mechanically.)

Q28

A coastal town keeps getting accurate cyclone warnings a day or two in advance. The technology most responsible for this early warning is:

  • Athe sundial
  • Bthe household thermometer
  • Cweather satellites and radar✓
  • Dthe kitchen pressure cooker

Working

✓

Modern early warnings come from weather satellites and radar, which spot and track cyclones a day or two ahead. A sundial, thermometer or pressure cooker cannot do this.

Q29

Lightning is essentially:

  • Asunlight reflected off rain
  • Bburning gas in the upper air
  • Csound turning into light
  • Da huge electric spark caused by charges built up in storm clouds✓

Working

✓

Lightning is a giant electric spark: charges build up inside towering storm clouds and then jump as a huge discharge. It is not reflected sunlight or burning gas.

Q30

Coastal regions of India have a more moderate, less extreme daily temperature than far-inland regions largely because:

  • Awater heats and cools more slowly than land, so sea breezes keep the coast milder✓
  • Bthe sea has no effect on coastal climate
  • Cinland air has no weight
  • Dthe coast receives no sunlight

Working

✓

Water heats and cools much more slowly than land, so the nearby sea moderates coastal temperatures and the daily sea/land breezes keep the coast milder than the far interior.

Q31

A train standing on a platform is about to be passed by a fast express. A person standing very close to the platform edge can be dangerously pulled toward the moving train because:

  • Athe fast-moving train pushes air against the person
  • Bthe fast air between the person and the train has lower pressure, so the higher pressure behind the person pushes them in✓
  • Cthe train magnetically attracts people
  • Dthe rails vibrate and pull people forward

Working

✓

The fast train drags the air between it and the person, making that air move quickly and so lowering its pressure. The higher pressure on the person's far side then pushes them toward the train — which is why one must stand well back.

Q32

Which statement correctly compares a tornado with a cyclone?

  • Aa tornado is always far larger than a cyclone
  • Ba tornado has no spinning motion
  • Ctornadoes and cyclones are exactly the same size
  • Da tornado is generally a much smaller, narrower, very violent whirling column of air✓

Working

✓

A tornado is generally a much smaller, narrower but extremely violent whirling column of air, while a cyclone is a far larger system. Both spin, so "no spinning" and "same size" are wrong.

Q33

In the self-feeding cycle of a growing cyclone, which sequence is correct?

  • Apressure rises → wind slows → cyclone weakens
  • Bcold air sinks → it warms the sea → wind stops
  • Cmoist air rises → vapour condenses → heat released → pressure falls → faster wind rushes in → more moist air rises✓
  • Dlightning forms → it heats the sea → the eye widens

Working

✓

The growing-cyclone loop runs: moist air rises → vapour condenses → heat is released → central pressure falls → faster wind rushes in → it brings still more moist air, repeating the cycle. Rising pressure or sinking cold air would stop a cyclone, not build one.

Q34

Recorded wind speeds in the eye wall of a strong cyclone can reach roughly:

  • A5 to 10 km/h
  • B30 to 40 km/h
  • Cthe same as a gentle breeze
  • D150 to 250 km/h✓

Working

✓

In a strong cyclone the eye-wall winds can reach roughly 150 to 250 km/h — far above a gentle breeze.

Q35

Two neighbouring regions of the atmosphere have a large pressure difference between them. Compared with two regions whose pressures are nearly equal, the wind between the first pair will be:

  • Aweaker
  • Bstronger✓
  • Cexactly the same
  • Dabsent

Working

✓

The bigger the pressure difference between two regions, the harder the air is pushed from high to low — so a large pressure difference gives a stronger wind.

Q36

An anemometer often has small cups mounted on arms that spin in the wind. The **faster** the cups spin, the:

  • Alower the wind speed
  • Bhigher the air temperature
  • Chigher the wind speed✓
  • Dheavier the rainfall

Working

✓

An anemometer's cups are spun by the wind: the faster they spin, the higher the wind speed. (It does not measure temperature or rainfall.)

Q37

The sea breeze and the land breeze both occur because:

  • Athe Moon pulls the air toward the coast
  • Bland and water heat up and cool down at different rates✓
  • Csea water is saltier than land
  • Dthe coast is always windier than inland

Working

✓

Both the sea breeze and the land breeze arise because land and water heat up and cool down at different rates, creating the pressure differences that drive the breezes.

Q38

A weather report says the air pressure over a region has suddenly **fallen** sharply. This most likely signals:

  • Aan approaching storm or cyclone, because storms are low-pressure systems✓
  • Bclear, calm, settled weather ahead
  • Cthat the wind will completely stop
  • Dthat the temperature has dropped to freezing

Working

✓

Storms and cyclones are low-pressure systems, so a sharp, sudden fall in air pressure usually warns that a storm is approaching — not that calm, settled weather is on the way.

Q39

Why does a cyclone's storm surge cause such severe flooding of low-lying coasts?

  • Athe surge is only rainwater collecting slowly
  • Bthe sea level drops, exposing dry land
  • Cthe surge is made entirely of fresh river water
  • Dthe very low pressure and fierce winds heap up a great mound of sea water that washes far inland✓

Working

✓

A cyclone's very low central pressure and fierce winds pile up a great mound of sea water (the storm surge) that washes far inland, flooding low coasts. It is not slow rainwater or river water, and the sea does not retreat.

Q40

A glass tumbler is filled to the brim with water, a stiff card is held over the mouth, and the tumbler is quickly turned upside down. The card and water stay in place because:

  • Athe air pressure pushing up on the card is greater than the weight of the water pushing down✓
  • Bthe water is glued to the card
  • Cwater cannot fall when upside down
  • Dthe glass holds the water by magnetism

Working

✓

The atmosphere pushes up on the card with a pressure equal to the weight of many metres of air — far more than the weight of the small column of water in the glass — so the card and water are held in place.

Q41

On a calm, very hot and humid summer afternoon, conditions are ripe for a thunderstorm because such weather provides:

  • Acold, dry, sinking air
  • Bstrong steady wind from the poles
  • Clow humidity and clear skies for days
  • Dplenty of warm, moisture-rich air that can rise quickly✓

Working

✓

Thunderstorms need plenty of warm, moisture-rich air that can rise quickly. A hot, humid afternoon supplies exactly that. Cold dry or low-humidity air would not build a storm.

Q42

During India's **winter** months the prevailing winds over much of the country tend to blow:

  • Afrom the sea toward the land, bringing heavy rain
  • Bstraight upward
  • Cfrom the equator toward the poles, bringing snow
  • Dfrom the land toward the sea, so they are mostly dry✓

Working

✓

In winter the land cools more than the sea, so winds tend to blow from the land out toward the sea. Because they come off the dry land, the winter winds are mostly dry — the opposite of the summer monsoon.

Q43

Which of these is a sound safety measure to take when a cyclone warning is in force?

  • Bstore drinking water and keep emergency supplies and a radio ready✓

✓

A sound cyclone precaution is to store drinking water and keep emergency supplies and a radio ready. Leaving windows wide open, going out to photograph the storm, or sheltering under a lone tall tree are all unsafe.

Q44

Wind blows hard across the open mouth of a chimney at the top of a house. As a result, smoke inside is drawn **up** the chimney more strongly. This is because the fast wind across the top:

  • Araises the pressure at the chimney mouth

  • Chas no effect on the smoke
  • Dlowers the pressure at the chimney mouth, so the higher pressure below pushes the smoke up✓

✓

Q45

Put the early life of a cyclone in the right order: (i) wind spirals inward, gaining speed (ii) the Sun heats the ocean and warm moist air rises (iii) water vapour condenses, releasing heat and dropping the pressure further. The correct order is:

  • A(i) → (ii) → (iii)
  • B(iii) → (ii) → (i)
  • C(ii) → (iii) → (i)✓
  • D(ii) → (i) → (iii)

Working

✓

A cyclone begins with the Sun heating the ocean so warm moist air rises (ii); that vapour then condenses, releasing heat and dropping the pressure further (iii); the falling pressure finally makes the wind spiral inward, gaining speed (i). Order: (ii) → (iii) → (i).

Q46

The very low pressure at the centre of a cyclone is important because it:

  • Ais what draws the surrounding air rushing inward as fierce wind✓
  • Bkeeps the wind from blowing at all
  • Cmakes the sea level rise far out at sea only
  • Dwarms the upper atmosphere and dries it out

Working

✓

The very low pressure at the centre is exactly what pulls the surrounding higher-pressure air rushing inward, and that inrush is the cyclone's fierce wind. Low pressure drives the wind, it does not stop it.

Q47

Which set of features correctly describes a cyclone from centre outward?

  • Bhigh-pressure eye → dry eye wall → no clouds
  • Ccalm low-pressure eye → violent eye wall → outer spiralling clouds✓
  • Dcalm eye → calm eye wall → calm edge

Working

✓

From the centre outward a cyclone has a calm, low-pressure eye, then the violent eye wall, then bands of outer spiralling clouds. The eye and edges are not the violent parts.

Q48

A region of the atmosphere is being warmed strongly from below. Over time this region tends to develop:

  • Ahigher pressure, because warm air is heavier
  • Blower pressure, because the warm air expands and rises away✓
  • Cno change in pressure
  • Dzero air pressure

Working

✓

Air warmed from below expands, becomes lighter and rises away, leaving lower pressure behind. Warm air is less dense, so it does not raise the pressure.

Q49

A weather station reports that its anemometer cups are spinning very slowly today. This tells us that:

  • Athe wind is light today✓
  • Bthe air is very hot
  • Cheavy rain is falling
  • Dthe air pressure is extremely high

Working

✓

The anemometer cups are turned by the wind, so very slow cups mean the wind is light that day. Spin speed does not report temperature, rain or pressure directly.

Q50

Why does the sea breeze typically arrive only **during the day** and not at night?

  • Athe sea is only warm at night
  • Bwind cannot blow after sunset
  • Conly during the day does the land heat faster than the sea, creating the needed pressure difference✓

✓

The sea breeze needs the land to be warmer than the sea, which only happens by day when the land heats faster under the Sun. At night that condition is gone (it even reverses), so the daytime sea breeze does not blow at night.

Q52

Q53

The modern reason coastal communities suffer fewer cyclone deaths than they did a century ago is mostly that:

  • Acyclones have become much weaker over time
  • Bthe sea no longer floods the coast
  • Cpeople have stopped living near the coast entirely
  • Daccurate satellite warnings now give people time to evacuate to shelters✓

Working

✓

Far fewer people die in cyclones today mainly because accurate satellite warnings give time to evacuate to shelters. Cyclones are not weaker, the sea still floods, and the coast is still inhabited.

Q54

Which correctly orders the events of a thunderstorm developing into rain?

  • Arain falls first, then warm air rises, then clouds form
  • Bwarm moist air rises and cools → water vapour forms tall clouds → drops grow heavy and fall as rain✓
  • Clightning strikes the ground → clouds appear → air warms
  • Dcold air sinks → the sea heats → rain rises into the sky

Working

✓

A thunderstorm's rain forms in order: warm moist air rises and cools → its water vapour builds tall clouds → the drops grow heavy and fall as rain. Rain or lightning cannot come first, and cold sinking air would not start it.

Q55

The whole pattern of winds and the monsoon over India is ultimately driven by:

  • Athe Moon's gravity alone
  • Bthe uneven heating of the Earth's land, sea and atmosphere by the Sun✓
  • Cocean currents with no role for the Sun
  • Dthe spinning of household fans

Working

✓

The Sun's uneven heating of land, sea and atmosphere creates the pressure differences that drive all winds, including the monsoon. The Moon and household fans are not responsible, and ocean currents themselves are ultimately Sun-driven.

Q56

In the bottle-and-balloon activity, after the hot-water bath the same bottle is moved into a bowl of ice water. The balloon will now:

  • Aget sucked inward / collapse into the bottle as the air inside cools and its pressure drops✓
  • Binflate even more
  • Cstay exactly as it was in the hot water
  • Dpop off and float away

Working

✓

Cooling the bottle in ice water shrinks the trapped air and drops its pressure below the outside air, so the balloon is sucked inward / collapses into the bottle — the reverse of the hot-water case.

Q57

As a cyclone intensifies over the sea, the pressure at its centre and the speed of its winds change in which way?

  • Athe centre pressure rises and the winds get faster
  • Bthe centre pressure falls lower and the winds get faster✓
  • Cthe centre pressure falls and the winds get slower
  • Dboth the pressure and the wind speed stay constant

Working

✓

As a cyclone intensifies its central pressure falls even lower, and because air rushes harder toward that deeper low, the winds get faster. (Falling pressure with faster — not slower — wind is the key pairing.)

  • Bthe wind to blow straight upward
  • Cthe usual sea breeze to weaken or reverse, since the needed pressure difference is gone or flipped✓

Fast wind over a roof lowers the pressure above it, creating an upward lift that can tear a loose roof off. Strong, well-anchored roofs resist that lift — which is why coastal homes use them.
