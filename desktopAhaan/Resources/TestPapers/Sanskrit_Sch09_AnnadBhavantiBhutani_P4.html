National-olympiad traps: the ablative (पञ्चमी, -आत्) marks अपादान — the source a thing 'arises FROM', never an agent that 'makes' it, so अन्नात् ≠ a maker and ≠ the dative अन्नाय ('for food'); भवन्ति governs भूतानि (नपुंसकलिङ्ग nom. pl., identical to acc. pl.) which agrees in being 3rd-person plural, NOT because भूतानि is the source; in अन्नाद्भवन्ति the त् of अन्नात् voices to द् before voiced भ् (case unchanged); the wheel भूतानि←अन्न←पर्जन्य←यज्ञ←कर्म is read by ablatives, so 'X from Y' means Y is the ablative; भवन्ति/सम्भवः/समुद्भवः all share √भू ('become'), making the chain one of coming-into-being not manufacture; जानीयात् is विधिलिङ् (optative, 'should know') of √ज्ञा (the -ईय- marker), distinct from present जानाति, future ज्ञास्यति, imperative जानीहि; ज्ञेयः is the -य gerundive ('to be known'), parallel to कार्य, distinct from past participle ज्ञात and agent ज्ञाता; ब्रह्म (नपुंसक, सर्वव्याप्तः परमेश्वरः, impersonal) ≠ ब्रह्मा (masc. creator) ≠ ब्रह्मन् (priest); ओषधीनाम् is genitive plural of the ई/इ-feminine ओषधि; रसायन is an Ayurvedic word re-coined for modern chemistry; उत्पत्तिक्रमम् ('order of arising') is the कर्म (object) of जानीयात्. Worked Solutions

1. (B) A wheel image stresses mutual interdependence: every link receives from one neighbour and gives to another, so none stands alone and the cycle is maintained only by taking part in it. The other options either privilege one link as self-standing, claim self-production, or deny that order matters.

2. (A) Yajna directly produces rain in the chain, so removing yajna fails rain first; the failure then cascades through food to beings. Beings and food fail later, not first, and action is the upstream cause that is not itself produced by yajna.

3. (C) The ablative says 'arises from' (source, not maker), and an all-pervading ground underlies all links — so the picture is emergence-within-one-ground, not manufacture-by-a-builder, not chance, and not a hierarchy of independent links.

4. (A) Ablative = source of emergence, never agency of fabrication; humans participate in and depend on the cycle rather than authoring beings. The other options grant fabricating agency, drop human involvement, or invert the chain.

5. (D) The chapter links the ancient verse to modern science (e.g. रसायनशास्त्रम्) precisely to show two angles on one world. They are neither contradictory, nor literally the same text, nor wholly unrelated.

6. (D) Herbs/plants are the link turning rain into food; remove them and food fails despite rain, then beings suffer. Herbs are not the source of rain, and beings come from food (not directly from rain).

7. (B) Both वृक्षात् and अन्नात् are ablatives marking अपादान, the fixed point something moves or arises away from. Locative marks place, dative the recipient, accusative the object — none fits 'from'.

8. (D) Purpose/benefit is the dative (अन्नाय); the -आत् form is ablative of source. So the verse means emergence from food, refuting the 'for the sake of' reading; it is neither genitive nor accusative here.

9. (C) The source 'cloud' must be ablative मेघात् ('from the cloud'), exactly parallel to अन्नात्. Accusative मेघं, dative मेघाय, and genitive मेघस्य would mean 'the cloud (object)', 'for the cloud', and 'of the cloud' respectively.

10. (A) अपादान is precisely the point of origin/separation; food is that origin, so it takes the ablative, while the arising beings are the nominative subject. Word length, importance, or sentiment have no bearing on case assignment.

11. (A) In the chain rain → food → beings, rain reaches beings only through food; it is not a direct source of beings, is part of the same cycle, and beings do not produce rain.

12. (D) A finite verb agrees with its nominative subject; भूतानि (neuter nom. pl.) drives the plural भवन्ति. The ablative अन्नाद् is not the subject, and agreement is grammatical, not positional or euphonic.

13. (B) Neuter a-stems have one form (here भूतानि) for both nom. and acc. plural. भूतान्/भूताः are masculine endings, and भूतेषु is locative, none of which fits a neuter subject/object.

14. (B) भवन्ति is a finite verb; भूतानि is a participle-derived noun. They are not both verbs, not both nouns, and their categories are not reversed.

15. (B) Context decides: a life-cycle verse selects the 'living being' sense of भूत. The plural ending alone does not fix the meaning, the ghost sense exists elsewhere in Sanskrit, and भूत is neuter, not feminine.

16. (D) भूत embraces all that has come into being, so the claim covers humans, animals and plants. Restricting it to humans, to animals, or to the dead all narrow भूत improperly.

17. (C) The source word is ablative, the verb is present plural, and the subject is neuter nominative plural. The other rows misassign case, mood, tense or gender.

18. (B) The voicing seen before भ् happens because भ् is voiced; before the unvoiced च् the त् does not voice (it assimilates to च्). च् is unvoiced, the rule is not sentence-final-only, and voicing occurs before any voiced consonant, not only भ्.

19. (A) Before the voiced ध्, the त् voices to द्, giving तद्धर्मः — exactly the अन्नाद् pattern. It does not aspirate to थ्, nasalise to न्, or stay unchanged here.

20. (D) Final unvoiced stops voice in voiced surroundings (vowels are voiced), giving जगदीशः, वागीशः — the same voicing principle as त्→द् in अन्नाद्. They do not stay unchanged, aspirate, or nasalise here.

21. (D) Sandhi alters only the surface sound, never the case: अन्नाद् stays ablative. The three true statements correctly describe the voicing, its trigger, and the unchanged case.

22. (A) The underlying ablative ends in त् (अन्नात्); the द् is only the sandhi-surface form. The other splits keep the surface द्, break the words wrongly, or invent a short vowel.

23. (C) The repeated √भू ('to be/become') marks every step as a becoming-from-a-source, not destruction, not synonymy, and not enumeration.

24. (B) The optative marker here is the -ईय- before the ending. The जा- and the -त् appear in other forms too (जानाति also has जा- and ends in -त्), and prefix-absence is not an optative signal.

25. (A) वदेत्/पालयेत्/जानीयात् are all optatives giving advice ('one should ...'). They are not past narration, not present statement of fact, and softer than the direct imperative command.

26. (D) The optative advises; the imperative commands. The two are not swapped, not identical, and neither is a past or future tense.

27. (D) जानीयात् is विधिलिङ् (optative), जानाति is present, ज्ञास्यति is future. The other rows shuffle the labels or wrongly call all three optative.

28. (A) Glossing an optative with another optative keeps the 'ought to' force intact. The gloss does not alter tense, number, or voice.

29. (C) Only जानीयात् carries the 'should' force. जानाति states a present fact, अजानात् reports the past, and ज्ञास्यति predicts the future — none means 'should know'.

30. (B) The -य gerundive expresses 'to be X-ed', so ज्ञेय = 'to be known', parallel to कार्य = 'to be done'. It is not a plural, not the agent (ज्ञाता), and not the abstract noun (ज्ञानम्).

31. (C) A gerundive takes the agent in the instrumental (सर्वैः) and means 'is to be known', so the correct sentence is the passive-prescriptive one. The others treat ज्ञेयः as a past verb, a proper name, or an active agent.

32. (A) ज्ञेयः is a passive necessity-form centred on the thing to be known; जानन् is an active present participle centred on the knower. ज्ञेयः is neither a past form nor a future-certainty form.

33. (B) बोद्धव्यः is the -तव्य gerundive ('to be understood'), the structural twin of ज्ञेयः. The others are a present verb, a past verb, and an abstract noun — none expresses 'must be understood' as a gerundive.

34. (C) The three are a desiderative noun, an optative, and a gerundive — three distinct derivations of one root. They are not all present verbs, not mislabelled as command/noun/past, and not from different roots.

35. (B) Curiosity sparks inquiry, duty directs it, and the subject stands as worth knowing — a natural arc. The distractors invert or sever the progression.

36. (C) ब्रह्म (neuter) is the impersonal Absolute, ब्रह्मा (masc.) the creator-god, ब्रह्मन् a priest. The other rows misassign gender and meaning or collapse the three into one.

37. (C) सर्वव्याप्त means 'pervading all' — omnipresence, not specifically omnipotence, omniscience, or mercy.

38. (B) Neuter gender here marks an impersonal, ungendered ground — fitting for an all-pervading principle, not a personal creator. It does not mean weakness, inertness, or plurality.

39. (C) A human officiant who chants is the priest, ब्रह्मन्. The impersonal Absolute and the creator-god do not chant as ritual officiants, and रसायन means chemistry.

40. (D) The ablative-of-source avoids a maker, matching the impersonal ground ब्रह्म. It is not the personal creator ब्रह्मा, not the priest ब्रह्मन्, and not groundless chance.

41. (B) Here यज्ञ widens to mean selfless action generally, fire-ritual being one instance. It is not narrowed to temple पूजा, not chemistry, and not a meal.

42. (C) यज्ञ specifies that selfless action sustains the cycle; removing it would let any action qualify, losing the moral point. The chain would not keep the same meaning, would not re-source rain from food, and would not free beings from food.

43. (C) The contrast is one of ritual tradition: fire-offering vs (often image-based) devotion. It is not about sound, not mere spelling, and both are nouns.

44. (D) Both are worship yet differ in ritual form (fire-offering vs image-devotion). They are not identical, not chemistry/herb, and not tenses.

45. (D) The -ईनाम् ending marks the genitive plural of an ī/i-stem feminine like ओषधि. The other parses misname the stem class and the case-number.

46. (A) Genitive plural gives 'of the herbs'. 'From', 'to', and 'by' would be ablative, dative, and instrumental respectively — none of which is the -ईनाम् form.

47. (B) Plants convert rain into food on which beings live, so herbs sit between rain and food. They are not the source of yajna, not ब्रह्म, and not chemistry.

48. (D) The accusative marks the कर्म (direct object) — what is known. The knower is the कर्ता (nominative), the means the करण (instrumental), and the source the अपादान (ablative).

49. (B) The compound joins 'arising' with 'order' to mean the sequence in which things come to be. It is not destruction, not a single act, and not an origin-less sequence.

50. (D) शास्त्रम् means a systematic discipline or treatise, so रसायनशास्त्रम् = chemistry. It does not mean a herb, a ritual, or the Absolute.

51. (A) Re-using रसायन for chemistry shows a living language adapting old vocabulary, paralleling English's classical borrowings. It does not show poverty of vocabulary, mere coincidence, or that modern chemistry already existed.

52. (C) अन्नाद् is ablative (not nominative), भूतानि means 'beings' (not ghosts), and जानीयात् is optative (not past) — three errors. So 'all three' is correct.

53. (A) Only the first row gets case, mood/tense, and meaning all right. The others misassign the case of अन्नाद्, the form of भवन्ति, and the meaning of भूतानि.

54. (A) Each ablative fixes the arrow's direction (from source to product). Reversing the arrows reverses the claimed sources, e.g. making beings produce food, which the verse denies. The verse does have a fixed productive order.

55. (C) भू ('become') fits emergence-from-a-source, the very idea the ablative carries; कृ ('make') would smuggle in an agent. The verbs are not synonyms here, करोति is not required, and भवन्ति is the apt choice.

56. (A) The ablative gives source-not-maker and the present भवन्ति gives an ongoing arising — beings emerge from food. The distractors reverse the source, posit a personal maker, or mistranslate भूतानि and the tense.

57. (C) Ablatives express 'arising from' (dependence), and the verse contains no commanding force, so 'interdependence' fits better than 'command'. There are no imperatives, the order is not random, and the ablative is a case of source, not authority.

58. (D) जानीयात् is the optative, ज्ञेयः the gerundive (to be known), and ज्ञाता the agent (knower). The other rows scramble these or wrongly call all three past participles.
