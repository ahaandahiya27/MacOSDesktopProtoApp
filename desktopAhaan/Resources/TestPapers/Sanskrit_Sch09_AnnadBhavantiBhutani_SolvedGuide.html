Set 1 · Q1–Q10

Q1

अन्नाद् भवन्ति भूतानि means:

  • Abeings create food
  • Bfrom food, beings come to be✓
  • Cfood destroys beings
  • Dbeings eat ghosts

Working

✓

It means 'from food, beings come to be'. It does not say beings create food, food destroys beings, or beings eat ghosts.

Q2

This verse is taken from the:

  • ABhagavad Gita (3.14)✓
  • BRamayana
  • CArthashastra
  • DIsha Upanishad

Working

✓

The verse is from the Bhagavad Gita (3.14). It is not the Ramayana, the Arthashastra, or the Isha Upanishad.

Q3

भूतानि in the verse means:

  • Aghosts
  • Bfoods
  • Cbeings / living creatures✓
  • Drains

Working

✓

भूतानि means 'beings / living creatures'. It does not mean ghosts, foods, or rains.

Q4

भवन्ति means:

  • Athey come to be / they arise✓
  • Bthey destroy
  • Cthey eat
  • Dthey should know

Working

✓

भवन्ति means 'they come to be / arise' (from भू). It does not mean destroy, eat, or 'should know'.

Q5

अन्नात् means:

  • Ato food
  • Bof food
  • Cwithout food
  • Dfrom food (the source)✓

Working

✓

अन्नात् is ablative, 'from food'. 'To food' is dative, 'of food' genitive, and 'without food' would need a different construction.

Q6

According to Gita 3.14, beings come from food, food from ____, rain from ____, and yajna from ____.

  • Aaction ... rain ... yajna
  • Byajna ... action ... rain
  • Cfood ... beings ... rain
  • Drain ... yajna ... action (karma)✓

Working

✓

Food comes from rain, rain from yajna, and yajna from action (karma). The other orderings scramble the chain.

Q7

In the cycle, अन्नम् (food) comes from:

  • Aयज्ञ (sacrifice)
  • Bपर्जन्य (rain)✓
  • Cकर्म (action)
  • Dभूत (beings)

Working

✓

Food comes from rain (पर्जन्य). Rain comes from yajna, yajna from action, and beings from food.

Q8

पर्जन्य (rain) comes from:

  • Aअन्न (food)
  • Bभूत (beings)
  • Cओषधि (herbs)
  • Dयज्ञ (sacrifice)✓

Working

✓

Rain comes from yajna (sacrifice). Food comes from rain, not the reverse; beings and herbs are elsewhere in the cycle.

Q9

यज्ञ (sacrifice) comes from:

  • Aरसायन (chemistry)
  • Bभूत (beings)
  • Cकर्म (action)✓
  • Dअन्न (food)

Working

✓

Yajna comes from action (कर्म). Chemistry, beings and food are not its source in the verse.

Q10

Reversed, the chain reads action -> yajna -> rain -> food -> ____.

  • Abeings (भूतानि)✓
  • Bghosts
  • Cherbs
  • Dchemistry

Working

✓

The chain ends in beings (भूतानि). Not ghosts, herbs, or chemistry.

Q16

In अन्नाद्भवन्ति, the -द् at the end of अन्नाद् results from:

  • Aa plural ending
  • Ba verb root
  • Cthe past tense
  • Dsandhi (अन्नात् + भवन्ति, त् becoming voiced द् before भ्)✓

Working

✓

The -द् results from sandhi: अन्नात् + भवन्ति, where त् becomes voiced द् before the voiced भ्. It is not a plural ending, a root, or a tense.

Q17

The sandhi अन्नात् + भवन्ति -> अन्नाद्भवन्ति changes त् to द् because:

  • Athe word is plural
  • Bthe verb is past tense
  • Cthe following sound भ् is voiced✓
  • Dthe noun is feminine

Working

✓

त् becomes the voiced द् because the following भ् is voiced. Plurality, tense and gender are irrelevant here.

Q18

Recognising the sandhi lets you see that अन्नाद् is still the ____ case.

  • Anominative
  • Bablative ('from food')✓
  • Caccusative
  • Dgenitive

Working

✓

Despite the sandhi, अन्नाद् is still the ablative ('from food'). It is not nominative, accusative, or genitive.
