**Marking scheme:** **+4** for each correct answer · **−1** for each wrong answer · **0** if unattempted.

**Instructions:** Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. This paper goes deeper than Paper 1: sort each example into *physical* or *social* infrastructure, reason from a facility to the development it unlocks (a port to the warehouses around it, a railway to the markets it reaches), and keep the history straight — the British built the railways in 1853 for their *own* trade, and concern for public works is as old as Kautilya's *Arthashastra*. Watch the recurring traps: infrastructure is far more than "roads and buildings"; caring for it is a *shared* duty of government **and** citizens; and J.C. Bose's wireless work (1895) came *before* the technology became famous abroad. This paper does not repeat any question from Paper 1.
