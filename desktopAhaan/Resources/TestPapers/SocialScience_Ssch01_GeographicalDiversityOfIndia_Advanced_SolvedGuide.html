Set 1 · Q1–Q10

Q1

The Himalayas were thrown up when two plates collided. Which two?

  • Athe Indian plate and the African plate
  • Bthe Pacific plate and the Eurasian plate
  • Cthe Indian plate and the Eurasian plate✓
  • Dthe Arabian plate and the Indian plate

Working

✓

The Himalayas rose where the northward-drifting **Indian plate** rammed into the **Eurasian plate**. The collision crumpled the rocks between them into the youngest, highest mountains on Earth — and the push still continues.

Q2

The Himalayas are classed as "fold mountains" because they formed by:

  • Athe squeezing and buckling of rock layers under compression✓
  • Blava cooling layer upon layer
  • Cwind piling up sand over ages
  • Da single block of crust dropping between two faults

Working

✓

Fold mountains form when horizontal rock layers are **squeezed and buckled** by compression — exactly what happened as India pressed into Asia. Lava layers make volcanic mountains; dropped blocks make block (fault) mountains.

Q3

Barren Island, India's only active volcano, lies in the Andaman Sea, yet the Himalayas have no active volcanoes. The reason is that:

  • Athe Himalayas are simply too cold for volcanoes
  • Bthe Andamans are older than the Himalayas
  • Cthe Himalayas are made of limestone, which cannot melt
  • Dthe Andamans sit near a subduction zone while the Himalayas formed by collision without one plate diving under✓

Working

✓

The Andaman arc sits above a **subduction zone**, where one oceanic plate dives beneath another and melts to feed volcanoes like Barren Island. The Himalayas formed by a continent–continent **collision** with no plate diving deep enough to melt, so no volcanoes — not a matter of cold or rock type.

Q4

The fertile Northern Plains were built mainly from the alluvium of which three river systems?

  • ANarmada, Tapi and Mahanadi
  • BIndus, Ganga and Brahmaputra✓
  • CGodavari, Krishna and Kaveri
  • DSabarmati, Luni and Chambal

Working

✓

The Northern Plains are alluvium dropped by the **Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra** systems. The Narmada/Tapi (west-flowing) and the Godavari/Krishna/Kaveri (Peninsular) built other landscapes, not the great northern plain.

Q5

Walking from the edge of the plains into the Himalayan foothills you cross a pebbly belt, then a marshy one. Their correct names are:

  • ABhabar then Terai✓
  • BKhadar then Bhangar
  • CTerai then Bhabar
  • DDoab then Dun

Working

✓

Just below the foothills comes the pebbly, porous **Bhabar** belt where streams vanish underground; below it the water re-emerges to make the marshy **Terai**. So the order from the hills outward is Bhabar then Terai.

Q6

The Deccan Plateau's black soil traces back to:

  • Aancient sea beds rich in shells
  • Bglaciers grinding granite into powder
  • Cvast lava flows that cooled into basalt (the Deccan Traps)✓
  • Dwind-blown dust from the Thar

Working

✓

The Deccan's black soil weathered from the **basalt of the Deccan Traps** — vast sheets of lava that erupted and cooled long ago. That iron-rich rock gives the dark, moisture-holding "regur" soil.

Q7

Which statement correctly contrasts the Western and Eastern Ghats?

  • Athe Western Ghats are broken and lower; the Eastern Ghats are continuous and higher
  • Bboth are continuous chains of equal height
  • Cthe Eastern Ghats receive far more rain than the Western Ghats
  • Dthe Western Ghats are continuous and higher; the Eastern Ghats are broken and lower✓

Working

✓

The **Western Ghats are a continuous, higher** wall (broken only by a few passes); the **Eastern Ghats are lower and broken** into detached hills by the great east-flowing rivers. The Western, not Eastern, Ghats catch the heavy rain.

Q8

The Narmada and Tapi reach the Arabian Sea as estuaries rather than deltas mainly because:

  • Athey carry no silt at all
  • Bthey flow swiftly through hard-rock rift valleys, so silt is swept out to sea✓
  • Cthe Arabian Sea is too shallow for deltas
  • Dthey dry up before reaching the coast

Working

✓

The Narmada and Tapi race down **hard-rock rift valleys**, so they reach the sea fast and their silt is flushed straight out — forming estuaries, not the slow, silt-dropping deltas of the east coast.

Q9

Which river does NOT build a delta on the eastern coast?

  • ANarmada✓
  • BGodavari
  • CKrishna
  • DKaveri

Working

✓

The **Narmada** flows *west* into the Arabian Sea (as an estuary) and builds no east-coast delta. The Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri all flow east and do build deltas.

Q10

Ladakh is called a "cold desert." This shows that a desert is defined by:

  • Ahigh temperature
  • Bthe presence of sand dunes
  • Cvery low rainfall, whatever the temperature✓
  • Dthe absence of any people

Working

✓

A desert is defined by **very low rainfall**, not by heat. Ladakh is freezing yet bone-dry — a *cold* desert — which is why "all deserts are hot" is a trap.

Q11

Lakshadweep and the Andaman & Nicobar Islands differ in origin: respectively they are:

  • Avolcanic and coral
  • Bboth coral
  • Cboth volcanic
  • Dcoral and tectonic/volcanic✓

Working

✓

Lakshadweep is built of **coral**; the Andaman & Nicobar Islands are **tectonic/volcanic** (part of a rising arc, with Barren Island active). So respectively: coral and tectonic/volcanic.

Q12

The highest mountain peak in India is:

  • AKangchenjunga✓
  • BMount Everest
  • CK2 (Godwin-Austen)
  • DNanda Devi

Working

✓

**Kangchenjunga** (8,586 m, Sikkim) is the highest mountain peak in India. Everest lies in Nepal/Tibet and K2 in the Pakistan-administered north; Nanda Devi is high but lower than Kangchenjunga.

Q13

The Aravalli Range tells us that mountains can be very old because the Aravallis are:

  • Astill rising rapidly
  • Bamong the world's oldest fold mountains, now worn low✓
  • Crecently formed volcanic cones
  • Dmade of fresh river silt

Working

✓

The **Aravallis are among the world's oldest fold mountains**, worn down by aeons of erosion to low ridges — proof that mountains age and shrink. The still-rising Himalayas are the young contrast.

Q14

The local name for the Western Ghats is:

  • Athe Vindhyas
  • Bthe Satpura
  • Cthe Sahyadri✓
  • Dthe Nilgiris

Working

✓

The Western Ghats are locally the **Sahyadri**. The Vindhya and Satpura border the plateau to the north; the Nilgiris are where the two Ghats meet.

Q15

Himalayan rivers flow all year while many Peninsular rivers shrink in summer because Himalayan rivers are fed by:

  • Aboth monsoon rain and melting snow/glaciers✓
  • Brain only
  • Cunderground springs only
  • Dsea water pushed inland

Working

✓

Himalayan rivers are fed by **both monsoon rain and melting snow/glaciers**, so they flow even in the dry season (perennial). Many Peninsular rivers, fed by rain alone, shrink once the monsoon ends.

Q16

The world's largest delta, shared by India and Bangladesh and famous for its mangroves, is formed by the:

  • AIndus
  • BGanga–Brahmaputra✓
  • CKaveri
  • DMahanadi

Working

✓

The **Ganga–Brahmaputra** delta (the Sundarbans), shared with Bangladesh, is the world's largest delta and is famous for its mangrove forest.

Q17

The flat, fertile land lying between two rivers is called a:

  • Adun
  • Bghat
  • Cbhabar
  • Ddoab✓

Working

✓

Land lying between two rivers is a **doab** (literally "two waters") — the Ganga–Yamuna doab is the classic example. A dun is a valley; a ghat is a mountain pass/step; bhabar is a foothill belt.

Q18

The Northern Plains are among the most densely populated regions of India chiefly because:

  • Athey are rich in metal ores
  • Bthe climate is the coolest in India
  • Cflat land, fertile alluvial soil and plentiful river water favour farming and settlement✓
  • Dthey are protected from every invader

Working

✓

The Northern Plains are densely peopled because **flat land, deep fertile alluvial soil and ample river water** make farming and settlement easy — not minerals, climate, or defence.

Q19

On the windward (western) side of the Western Ghats it rains heavily, while the Deccan interior stays dry. This dry zone is called a:

  • Arain-shadow area✓
  • Bflood plain
  • Cdelta
  • Dwatershed

Working

✓

Air that has dumped its rain on the windward Ghats descends dry over the Deccan, leaving a **rain-shadow area**. This is why the interior plateau is much drier than the coast.

Q20

India's Standard Time is fixed by the 82°30′E meridian passing near Mirzapur. The country's east–west spread therefore creates a clock difference of about:

  • Ahalf an hour
  • Btwo hours✓
  • Cone hour
  • Dfour hours

Working

✓

India spans about 30° of longitude. At 4 minutes of time per degree that is roughly **two hours** of real sun-time difference between the east and west — the reason a single Standard Time (82°30′E) is used.

Black (regur) soil is especially prized for growing:

  • Bcotton✓
  • Ccoffee
  • Drubber

The moisture-retentive black (regur) soil of the Deccan is famous for growing **cotton** ("black cotton soil"). Tea, coffee and rubber prefer the well-drained hill slopes.

Q25

By blocking the bitterly cold winds of Central Asia and forcing the monsoon to shed its rain, the Himalayas act mainly as:

  • Aa source of metals
  • Ba desert maker for the whole country
  • Ca flat farming belt
  • Da climatic barrier✓

Working

✓

By stopping the icy Central Asian winds and wringing rain from the monsoon, the Himalayas act as India's great **climatic barrier** — they shape the whole subcontinent's weather.

Q26

The Ganga begins from the Gangotri glacier; the Yamuna rises from the:

  • BSiachen glacier
  • CPindari glacier

The Ganga springs from the Gangotri glacier; the Yamuna rises from the **Yamunotri** glacier, close by in the Himalayas.

Q27

Geologically, Peninsular rivers are generally:

  • Amuch younger than Himalayan rivers
  • Babout the same age as Himalayan rivers
  • Colder than Himalayan rivers, flowing in shallow, settled valleys✓
  • Dentirely man-made canals

Working

✓

Peninsular rivers are **older than Himalayan rivers**; over long ages they have settled into shallow, broad, stable valleys, whereas the young Himalayan rivers still cut deep gorges.

Q28

The flat-floored longitudinal valleys of the Himalayan foothills, such as Dehra Dun, are called:

  • Adoabs
  • Bduns✓
  • Cdeltas
  • Dkarewas

Working

✓

The flat-floored longitudinal valleys of the lesser Himalayas, such as Dehra Dun, are called **duns**. Karewas are Kashmir lake deposits; doabs are river interfluves.

Q29

The chain of hills along India's north-eastern border — the Patkai, Naga and Mizo hills — is together called the:

  • AShivaliks
  • BVindhyas
  • CKarakoram
  • DPurvanchal✓

Working

✓

The north-eastern frontier hills — Patkai, Naga, Mizo — are together the **Purvanchal** (the "eastern hills"), a southern continuation of the Himalayan arc.

Q30

A student says, "All of India gets its rivers from Himalayan snow." The best correction is:

  • APeninsular rivers are rain-fed, so they depend on the monsoon, not snow✓
  • Bthe statement is fully correct
  • Conly Peninsular rivers come from snow
  • Dno Indian river depends on rain

Working

✓

The claim is wrong: **Peninsular rivers are rain-fed and depend on the monsoon**, not snow. Only the Himalayan rivers draw on snow-melt, so the blanket statement fails.

  • Byoung and rising region
  • Csandy desert basin
  • Dchain of active volcanoes

The Peninsular Plateau is a **stable, ancient block of hard rock** — one of Earth's oldest landmasses — in contrast to the young, restless, rising Himalayas.

Q33

  • Athe price of timber

✓

Mangroves thrive only in salty, tidal mud, so the Sundarbans show that vegetation is shaped by **the local landform and water conditions** — not by economics or tourism.

  • DNarmada and Tapi✓

✓

The **Narmada and Tapi** are the two large rivers that flow *west* into the Arabian Sea through rift (fault) valleys, which is why they make estuaries.

Q35

Tea gardens cling to the gentle, well-drained slopes of the Assam and Darjeeling hills rather than the flat plains because tea needs:

  • Bdesert heat

  • Dsalty coastal soil

✓

Tea cannot stand water around its roots, so it is grown on **sloping land where heavy rain drains away** — hence the hill gardens of Assam and Darjeeling rather than the flat, waterlogged plains.

Q38

The Peninsular Plateau is bordered to the north by two roughly east–west ranges, the:

Q39

Over the Thar the monsoon winds blow **roughly parallel to the Aravallis**, so almost no air is forced up to cool and rain — leaving the region arid. (Had the range stood across the wind, it would have wrung out rain.)

**Ladakh**, a high, cold, rain-shadow desert behind the Himalayas, is the "Roof of the World" region of India.

Q41

A delta forms where a river meets the sea and:

  • Aspeeds up and cuts a deep gorge
  • Bslows down and drops its silt into a fan of distributaries✓
  • Cevaporates completely
  • Dturns to flow back uphill

Working

✓

At its mouth a river **slows down and drops its silt into a spreading fan of distributaries** — that built-up sediment is a delta. Speeding up would cut a gorge, the opposite process.

✓

From the plains upward the three ranges are **Shivalik (outermost/lowest), Himachal (middle), Himadri (innermost, highest)**. Climbing toward the snow line you meet them in that order.

  • Bbhabar

  • Dkhadar

The terraced, saucer-shaped old lake deposits of the Kashmir Valley, prized for saffron and orchards, are the **karewas**.
