Worked Solutions

1. (B) The Guptas rose around the 3rd century CE after the Kushana Empire weakened. The Mauryas had fallen centuries earlier, the Pallavas were a contemporary southern kingdom, and the Hunas came later and helped end the Guptas.

2. (B) The Guptas ruled from Pataliputra (modern Patna). Kanchipuram was the Pallava capital, Pratishthana a Satavahana centre, and Socotra a distant trading island.

3. (A) The Guptas ruled c. 3rd-6th century CE. The 2nd-c-BCE-to-3rd-c-CE span is the Age of Reorganisation, the 6th-9th centuries are later, and the 1st-2nd centuries CE were Kushana times.

4. (B) The Guptas governed the north and centre directly, while the south (Pallavas) and northeast (Kamarupa) had their own kingdoms and the Guptas leaned on land grants and allies. They neither ruled all India village-by-village, nor were they a single city or a ruler-less alliance.

5. (C) The Iron Pillar is renowned for resisting rust across centuries. It is iron (not gold), linked to the Gupta 'Chandra' inscription (not the Maurya founder), and famed for rust-resistance rather than height.

6. (C) A special phosphorus-rich iron lets a thin protective film form, shielding the metal from oxygen and moisture. Iron normally rusts easily; there is no gold paint, and the dry-climate idea is not the chapter's explanation.

7. (A) The pillar's 'Chandra' is linked to the Gupta Chandragupta II ('Vikramaditya'), not the much earlier Maurya founder, nor Samudragupta, nor the Kushana Kanishka.

8. (D) Chandragupta Maurya founded the Maurya Empire centuries earlier; Chandragupta II was a later Gupta ruler. They share a name but are distinct people from different dynasties.

9. (D) Chandragupta I lifted the Guptas to an imperial power and took the grand title Maharajadhiraja. Samudragupta and Chandragupta II were his successors, and Kumaragupta a later ruler.

10. (B) The Licchavi marriage was a political alliance that raised Gupta prestige - diplomacy through marriage, not a mere religious rite, a tax device, or a calendar launch.

11. (B) Maharajadhiraja means 'great king of kings', asserting paramount power over lesser kings. It is not a humble servant title, a priestly office, or a naval rank.

12. (D) Gupta gold coinage advertised royal power, victories and legitimacy (and aided trade). Coins did not replace armies, carry Tamil poetry, or signal tribute to Rome.

13. (B) Samudragupta was the conquering 'warrior king' who enlarged the empire. He was not a pacifist, a foreign invader, or the pilgrim Faxian.

14. (D) The Prayaga Prashasti (the Allahabad pillar inscription) praises Samudragupta. The Aryabhatiya is Aryabhata's science treatise, the Meghadutam is Kalidasa's poem, and the Brihat Samhita is Varahamihira's encyclopedia.

15. (D) Harisena, Samudragupta's court poet, composed the Prayaga Prashasti. Kalidasa served at Chandragupta II's court, Aryabhata was a mathematician, and Varahamihira an astronomer-scholar.

16. (B) A prashasti is a praise-inscription of a ruler's deeds. The tax-free village is an agrahara, the ritual is the ashvamedha, and the monastery is a vihara.

17. (B) A court poet's praise of his patron is likely flattering, so its claims should be weighed against other evidence - neither swallowed whole nor dismissed entirely, and not assumed hostile.

18. (C) Chandragupta II 'Vikramaditya' presided over the golden flowering of art and learning. Samudragupta was the warrior king, Chandragupta I the founder, and Kumaragupta a later ruler.

19. (C) From vikrama ('valour') + aditya ('sun'), Vikramaditya means 'Sun of Valour'. 'King of kings' is Maharajadhiraja; the others are invented titles.

20. (D) By supporting artists and scholars, Chandragupta II was their patron. A regent governs for another (which he was not), nor was he a foreign conqueror or a pilgrim.

21. (A) Chandragupta II's reign is linked to Kalidasa and the Iron Pillar's Chandra inscription. The conquest reign and Prayaga Prashasti belong to Samudragupta, the founding to Chandragupta I, and the Huna invasions to the later decline.

22. (D) Prabhavati Gupta, a Gupta princess married into another dynasty, ruled as regent for her sons and maintained alliances - diplomacy in action. She was not an army commander, the author of the Aryabhatiya, or a metallurgist.

23. (B) A regent rules in place of a monarch who cannot (often a minor). The traveller is a pilgrim, the praise-poet writes a prashasti, and the star-student is an astronomer.

24. (A) A queen ruling as regent and steering alliances shows royal women could hold genuine power. They were not barred from governance, confined to farming, or forbidden cross-dynasty marriage, which was itself a key alliance tool.

25. (C) Gupta land grants (agraharas) were inscribed on durable copper plates. Palm-leaf 'newspapers', printed paper and clay coins are anachronistic or invented.

26. (C) An agrahara was a (usually tax-free) land grant to brahmins or temples. The grand title is Maharajadhiraja, the ritual the ashvamedha, and coinage a separate matter.

27. (C) Chakravartin denotes a universal ruler claiming dominion over all lesser kings. It is not a priest, a village head, or an ambassador.

28. (D) Each tax-free grant meant less revenue for the crown, which could weaken central finances over time - a subtle administrative cost. It did not compel wars, disable coins, or halt trade.

29. (A) Lofty titles and a layered administration advertised supreme authority and helped run the realm. They did not mask a missing army, mark the Guptas as foreign, or remove the need for officials.

30. (D) The Guptas governed the core directly but leaned on land grants and allied or subordinate kings elsewhere, unlike the more centralised Mauryan model. There was no elected council, no withdrawal from governance, and no Roman administration.

31. (D) Gupta trade crossed the Indian Ocean, reaching Socotra and beyond. It did not reach the Americas, the Arctic, or Britain across the Atlantic.

32. (D) Such graffiti shows Indian merchants ranged far across the Indian Ocean in trade - not conquest of Socotra, an invasion of India, or the island being a capital.

33. (B) Coins, copper-plate grants and traders' graffiti are concrete sources for Gupta rulers, administration and far-flung trade. They do not show an absence of records or of trade, and they ARE inscriptions to be read, not a substitute for them.

34. (A) Faxian was a Chinese Buddhist pilgrim who toured India and recorded what he saw. He was not a Gupta court poet, a Roman trader, or a Huna general.

35. (C) A pilgrim travels to sacred sites. The child-king's stand-in is a regent, the praise-poet writes a prashasti, and coins are made by mint-workers.

36. (A) Faxian noted both prosperity AND the harsh exclusion of the chandalas, who lived outside towns - so society was not equal. He did not report banned trade or an absence of cities.

37. (C) Faxian saw mainly prosperous cities from his own viewpoint, so his account is partial and must be cross-checked - neither perfectly complete and neutral, nor worthless, nor a late forgery.

38. (B) 'Classical Age' (from Latin classicus, 'of the highest class') refers to its peak achievements in art, science and literature. It does not claim social equality, an absence of war, or a break with earlier learning.

39. (C) Aryabhata was a mathematician and astronomer, author of the Aryabhatiya. He was not a king, a painter, or a pilgrim.

40. (A) The Aryabhatiya (c. 500 CE) is Aryabhata's compact treatise on mathematics and astronomy. The praise-inscription is the Prayaga Prashasti, Kalidasa wrote literature, and a monastery is a vihara.

41. (A) The place-value system with zero (shunya) hugely simplified calculation and spread worldwide. It was not a one-off, was not limited to architecture, and works alongside the other digits rather than replacing them.

42. (A) Astronomy (Greek astron 'star' + nomos 'law') studies the stars, planets and sky. Traditional medicine is Ayurveda, carving is sculpture, and praise-poetry is the prashasti tradition.

43. (B) Varahamihira wrote the wide-ranging Brihat Samhita. The Prayaga Prashasti praises Samudragupta, the Meghadutam is Kalidasa's poem, and the Aryabhatiya is Aryabhata's.

44. (C) Kalidasa was the celebrated poet-playwright of the Meghadutam and other works. The planet-measurer is Aryabhata or Varahamihira, the ironsmith is anonymous, and the pilgrim is Faxian.

45. (D) Kalidasa's enduring popularity shows the lasting legacy of Gupta literature. It is not evidence that nothing survived, that the plays are modern, or that Gupta writing was only scientific.

46. (C) Ayurveda (ayus 'life' + veda 'knowledge') is the 'science of life', India's traditional medicine. 'Sun of valour' is Vikramaditya, 'great king of kings' is Maharajadhiraja, and the star-science is astronomy.

47. (B) To codify is to set knowledge down systematically. It is the opposite of destroying or hiding it, and is not the same as translating it.

48. (D) Ajanta's paintings and the sculptures of Sarnath and Udayagiri are celebrated Gupta art. They are not instruments, export goods, or land-grant deeds.

49. (C) Using Garuda (Vishnu's vahana, or mount) as an emblem shows the Guptas drawing on Hindu imagery for royal authority. They did not worship only Greek gods, reject religion, or rule as a Buddhist-only dynasty.

50. (D) Repeated Huna invasions in the 6th century gravely weakened the Guptas. Rome did not conquer them, the Pallavas were a southern kingdom, and Chandragupta Maurya belonged to a far earlier era.

51. (A) The Hunas were Central Asian invaders entering through the northwest, like earlier waves. They did not come from the Tamil south, the ocean islands, or the northeastern Brahmaputra valley.

52. (A) From the Indo-Greeks to the Hunas, the northwest was repeatedly the entry route - a recurring gateway, not a sealed wall, the chief farmland, or a seaport.

53. (C) The Pallavas, based at Kanchipuram, flourished in the south during the Gupta age. The Shungas, Kushanas and Chedis belonged to earlier periods and other regions.

54. (C) The Pallavas ruled from Kanchipuram. Pataliputra was the Gupta capital, while Ujjain and Pratishthana were other cities.

55. (A) Kamarupa was the northeastern kingdom of the Brahmaputra valley. The Kaveri delta was Chola country, the Indus the northwest, and the Deccan the Satavahana heartland.

56. (B) Strong contemporaries in the south and northeast show the Guptas shared the subcontinent rather than ruling all of it. India was not a single kingdom, China-ruled, or Roman.

57. (B) The 'golden' label fits its cultural and economic peak; society remained unequal, as the exclusion of the chandalas shows. It does not mean universal equality, an absence of conflict, or isolation.

58. (A) The order is Chandragupta I (founder), then his son Samudragupta (warrior king), then Chandragupta II ('Vikramaditya'). The other orderings scramble this.

59. (A) Samudragupta is the one praised in the Prayaga Prashasti. The Iron Pillar links to Chandragupta II (not Chandragupta I), the first imperial title to Chandragupta I (not II), and Kanishka was a Kushana, not a Gupta.

60. (D) Brilliant Gupta science and literature flourished even as the chandalas were harshly excluded - high culture alongside an unequal society. The other options deny either the inequality or the achievements.
