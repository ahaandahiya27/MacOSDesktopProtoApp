Set 1 · Q1–Q10

Q1

A friend says, "It is raining right now" (weather), "the rains come every June" (season), and "this region is dry over the decades" (climate). The word that describes the **decades-long pattern** is:

  • Aweather
  • Bseason
  • Cforecast
  • Dclimate✓

Working

✓

The **decades-long pattern** of a place is its **climate**. "Raining right now" is weather (hourly), "rains every June" is a season (yearly), and the long average over decades is climate.

Q2

Climate differs from weather mainly because climate is measured over:

  • Amany years, at least several decades✓
  • Ba few hours
  • Cone week
  • Da single afternoon

Working

✓

Climate is measured over **many years — at least several decades**. A single afternoon, a few hours or one week describe weather, not climate.

Q3

Besides the usual four seasons, India is famous for an extra one — the:

  • Awindy season
  • Brainy season (monsoon)✓
  • Cfoggy season
  • Ddust season

Working

✓

Alongside the usual four seasons, India is famous for its **rainy season — the monsoon**, so vital that whole festivals and the farming year revolve around it.

Q4

The traditional Indian calendar divides the year not into four but into how many ṛitus (seasons)?

  • Afive
  • Bfour
  • Csix✓
  • Dtwelve

Working

✓

The traditional Indian calendar divides the year into **six ṛitus** (such as Vasanta, Grīṣma, Varṣhā…), not four.

Q5

The Thar Desert is described as having an **arid** climate, which means it:

  • Ais always cold
  • Bis high in the mountains
  • Cis covered in forest
  • Dgets very little rainfall✓

Working

✓

An **arid** climate means **very little rainfall** — the defining trait of the Thar Desert. Deserts are about dryness, not necessarily heat.

Q6

Shimla is much cooler than the plains around it chiefly because of its:

  • Ahigh altitude✓
  • Blow latitude
  • Cnearness to the sea
  • Dthick forests

Working

✓

Shimla is cool because of its **high altitude**: temperature falls as you climb, so a hill town stays cooler than the plains at the same latitude.

Q7

The five factors that decide a place's climate are latitude, altitude, distance from the sea, winds and:

  • Apopulation
  • Btopography✓
  • Clanguage
  • Dsoil colour

Working

✓

The fifth climate factor, with latitude, altitude, distance from the sea and winds, is **topography** — the lie of the land (mountains, slopes, coasts).

Q8

Kanniyakumari, near the Equator, is warm all year. The factor most responsible is its:

  • Ahigh altitude
  • Bdistance from the sea
  • Clow latitude✓
  • Dleeward position

Working

✓

Kanniyakumari is warm year-round because of its **low latitude** near the Equator, where the Sun's rays fall almost straight down all year.

Q9

As you climb a mountain the temperature drops. This shows the effect of:

  • Alatitude
  • Blongitude
  • Cocean currents
  • Daltitude✓

Working

✓

Temperature dropping as you go up shows the effect of **altitude** — the higher you climb in the troposphere, the colder it gets.

Q10

Mumbai is never very hot or very cold through the year. This even, mild pattern is called a:

  • Amaritime climate✓
  • Bcontinental climate
  • Cdesert climate
  • Dalpine climate

Working

✓

Mumbai's even, mild, never-extreme pattern is a **maritime climate**: the nearby sea, slow to warm and cool, smooths out the temperature.

Set 2 · Q11–Q20

Q11

Nagpur, far inland, bakes in summer and chills in winter — a **continental** climate. The reason inland places swing more is that:

  • Athey are nearer the Equator
  • Bland heats and cools faster than the sea, and there is no nearby sea to moderate it✓
  • Cthey are always at high altitude
  • Dwinds never reach them

Working

✓

Inland Nagpur swings to extremes because **land heats and cools faster than the sea, and there is no nearby sea to moderate it** — the hallmark of a continental climate.

Q12

The monsoon winds themselves are an example of which climate factor at work?

  • Aaltitude
  • Blatitude
  • Cwinds✓
  • Dsoil

Working

✓

The monsoon winds are the climate factor of **winds** in action — moving air that carries heat and moisture across regions.

Q13

The Western Ghats force the moist monsoon air to **rise**, cool and rain. This is the climate factor of:

  • Apopulation
  • Blatitude
  • Cdistance from the sea
  • Dtopography✓

Working

✓

The Western Ghats forcing air to rise and rain is the factor of **topography** — the shape of the land steering the weather.

Q14

A small area whose climate differs from the region around it — say a shaded forest patch or a city centre — is called a:

  • Amicroclimate✓
  • Bmacroclimate
  • Crain shadow
  • Dmonsoon

Working

✓

A small pocket whose climate differs from the surrounding region is a **microclimate** — like a shaded forest patch or a warm city centre.

Q15

A city centre is warmer than the green countryside nearby, forming an "urban heat island." This warmth comes mainly from:

  • Amore trees in the city
  • Bconcrete and tar that store heat, with little vegetation to cool the air✓
  • Cthe city being at higher altitude
  • Dthe sea breeze

Working

✓

An urban heat island is warm because **concrete and tar store heat while there is little vegetation to cool the air** — not because of trees, altitude or the sea.

Q16

The word "monsoon" comes from the Arabic *mausim*, meaning:

  • Arain
  • Bwind
  • Cseason✓
  • Driver

Working

✓

"Monsoon" comes from the Arabic *mausim*, meaning **season** — fitting, since it names the seasonal reversing winds.

Q17

Most of India's rain is brought by the **southwest monsoon**, which blows in during:

  • Awinter
  • Bthe dry spring
  • Conly at night
  • Dsummer (roughly June–September)✓

Working

✓

Most of India's rain arrives with the **southwest monsoon in summer (roughly June–September)**, when moist sea winds sweep inland.

Q18

The **northeast (winter) monsoon** is important because it brings rain especially to:

  • Athe Tamil Nadu (south-east) coast✓
  • Bthe Thar Desert
  • Cthe Himalayas only
  • Dthe whole of north India

Working

✓

The **northeast (winter) monsoon** brings rain especially to **the Tamil Nadu (south-east) coast**, watering it while much of India is dry.

Q19

The engine that drives the monsoon is the **unequal heating** of:

  • Atwo different deserts
  • Bland and the ocean✓
  • Cday and night
  • Drivers and lakes

Working

✓

The monsoon's engine is the **unequal heating of land and the ocean** — they warm at different rates, setting up the pressure differences that drive the winds.

Q20

In summer the land heats up faster than the sea, so air over the hot land rises and the pressure there drops. The result is that moist winds blow:

  • Afrom the land out to the sea
  • Bstraight up and stop
  • Cfrom the sea in towards the land, bringing rain✓
  • Donly along rivers

Working

✓

In summer the hot land's air rises and pressure drops, so **moist winds blow from the sea in towards the land, bringing rain**. (In winter it reverses.)

Set 3 · Q21–Q30

Q21

The slope of a mountain that faces the rain-bearing wind, and so gets **heavy rain**, is the:

  • Aleeward side
  • Bnorthern side only
  • Crain-shadow side
  • Dwindward side✓

Working

✓

The slope facing the rain-bearing wind, which gets heavy rain, is the **windward side**.

Q22

The sheltered slope, away from the wind, where the air has already dropped its moisture, is the:

  • Aleeward side✓
  • Bwindward side
  • Ccoastal side
  • Dequatorial side

Working

✓

The sheltered slope, where the air has already lost its moisture, is the **leeward side** — the dry rain-shadow side.

Q23

The dry interior Deccan lies in the **rain shadow** of the Western Ghats because:

  • Ait is too hot for rain
  • Bthe Ghats squeeze the rain out on their windward west, leaving little for the leeward east✓
  • Cit has no rivers
  • Dthe monsoon never reaches that far

Working

✓

The Deccan is dry because **the Ghats squeeze the rain out on their windward west, leaving little for the leeward east** — the classic rain-shadow effect.

Q24

Mawsynram and Cherrapunji are among the wettest places on Earth because they sit on the **windward** hills that:

  • Ablock the Sun
  • Bare nearest the Equator
  • Ccatch monsoon air forced up the Khasi hills, wringing out enormous rain✓
  • Dlie in a rain shadow

Working

✓

Mawsynram and Cherrapunji are drenched because they **catch monsoon air forced up the Khasi hills, wringing out enormous rain** — windward extremes, not rain-shadow.

Q25

On the west coast, the **windward western** slopes of the Western Ghats are:

  • Adeserts
  • Bbone dry
  • Cfrozen all year
  • Ddrenched by the monsoon✓

Working

✓

The **windward western** slopes of the Western Ghats are **drenched by the monsoon**; it is the leeward east that turns dry.

Q26

Places near the **Equator** are hot all year because there the Sun's rays strike:

  • Aalmost straight down, most directly✓
  • Bat a low slanting angle
  • Conly in summer
  • Dnever

Working

✓

Near the Equator the Sun's rays strike **almost straight down, most directly**, concentrating heat — which is why low-latitude lands are hot all year.

Q27

The tropical wet climate, with heavy rain and warm temperatures, is found along India's:

  • AThar desert
  • Bwest coast✓
  • Chigh Himalayas
  • Dcold Ladakh

Working

✓

India's **west coast** has the tropical wet climate — warm with heavy monsoon rain caught by the Ghats.

Q28

The cold, snowy **alpine** climate in India belongs to the:

  • ANorthern Plains
  • Beast coast
  • CHimalayas✓
  • DDeccan plateau

Working

✓

The cold, snowy **alpine** climate belongs to the **Himalayas**, where great altitude brings year-round cold.

Q29

The interior Deccan, with moderate rain and dry spells, is broadly:

  • Aalpine
  • Bpolar
  • Ctropical wet
  • Dsemi-arid✓

Working

✓

The interior Deccan, with moderate rain and dry spells, is broadly **semi-arid** — drier than the coast but not a true desert.

Q30

The hot, dry, dusty summer wind that blows over north India is called the:

  • Aloo✓
  • Bmonsoon
  • Csea breeze
  • Dtrade wind

Working

✓

The hot, dry, dusty north-Indian summer wind is the **loo**, notorious for its scorching afternoons.

Set 4 · Q31–Q40

Q31

A cyclone is an intense **low-pressure** storm formed over the sea; its calm, cloudless centre is the:

  • Atail
  • Beye✓
  • Cband
  • Dfront

Working

✓

A cyclone's calm, cloudless centre is the **eye**, ringed by the fiercest winds.

Q32

In India, severe cyclones most often strike the:

  • Awestern Thar
  • BHimalayan north
  • Ceastern (Bay of Bengal) coast✓
  • Dcentral plateau

Working

✓

Severe cyclones most often strike India's **eastern (Bay of Bengal) coast**, which faces the storm-breeding warm waters.

Q33

The **natural** greenhouse effect is actually essential because it:

  • Acools the Earth dangerously
  • Bblocks all sunlight
  • Ccreates the monsoon
  • Dtraps just enough of the Sun's heat to keep Earth warm enough for life✓

Working

✓

The **natural** greenhouse effect is essential because it **traps just enough of the Sun's heat to keep Earth warm enough for life** — without it the planet would freeze.

Q34

Burning coal, oil and gas is a problem because it:

  • Aadds greenhouse gases that strengthen the effect and warm the planet further✓
  • Bcools the planet
  • Cmakes more oxygen
  • Dstops cyclones

Working

✓

Burning coal, oil and gas **adds greenhouse gases that strengthen the effect and warm the planet further** — the human-driven part of climate change.

Q35

"Mitigation" of climate change means:

  • Arecovering after a flood
  • Btaking steps that slow warming and cut its causes, like planting trees and using clean energy✓
  • Cpredicting the weather
  • Dbuilding taller houses

Working

✓

Mitigation means **taking steps that slow warming and cut its causes — planting trees, using clean energy** — acting on the *causes*, not just coping afterward.

Q36

"Resilience" to disasters means a community's ability to:

  • Astop disasters from ever happening
  • Bignore warnings
  • Cwithstand and recover quickly after a difficulty like a flood✓
  • Dmove to another country

Working

✓

Resilience is the capacity to **withstand and recover quickly after a difficulty like a flood** — bouncing back, not preventing every disaster.

Q37

The national body that records weather data and issues forecasts and warnings in India is the:

  • ANDRF
  • BRBI
  • CISRO
  • DIndia Meteorological Department (IMD)✓

Working

✓

India's forecasting and warning body is the **India Meteorological Department (IMD)**.

Q38

When a cyclone or flood actually strikes, the trained force that carries out rescue and relief on the ground is the:

  • ANational Disaster Response Force (NDRF)✓
  • BIMD
  • CSurvey of India
  • DISRO

Working

✓

When a disaster actually strikes, ground rescue and relief are carried out by the **National Disaster Response Force (NDRF)** — IMD warns, NDRF acts.

Q39

A worrying sign of climate change for Indian farmers is that the monsoon has become more:

  • Areliable and even
  • Berratic — sometimes late, sometimes in damaging bursts✓
  • Ccold
  • Dsalty

Working

✓

A worrying climate-change sign for farmers is a monsoon turned **erratic — sometimes late, sometimes in damaging bursts** — rather than steady.

Q40

Many Indian festivals — Onam, Pongal, Baisakhi — are tied to the seasons because:

  • Athey were chosen at random
  • Bthe government fixed them
  • Cfarming life follows sowing and harvest, which follow the climate and monsoon✓

✓

Festivals like Onam, Pongal and Baisakhi track the seasons because **farming life follows sowing and harvest, which follow the climate and monsoon**.

✓

Q44

  • Bsea breeze

✓

Hill stations stay cool despite the latitude because of **altitude** — height, not nearness to sea or Equator, is doing the cooling.

  • DQ's higher altitude makes it cooler✓

✓

With P at sea level and Q 1,500 m up at the same latitude, the best single explanation for Q's cooler 24 °C is **Q's higher altitude makes it cooler**.

Q46

✓

The leeward land is dry because the arriving air **has already dropped most of its moisture climbing the windward side** — a rain shadow.
