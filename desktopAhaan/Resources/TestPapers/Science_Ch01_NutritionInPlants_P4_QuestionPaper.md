**Instructions:** Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. Classic traps: treating respiration as switched off in daylight; reading a net gas measurement as if only one process were running; confusing which factor (chlorophyll vs light vs CO2 vs water) a single controlled set-up actually isolates; assuming soil nitrogen is a raw material of the day's photosynthesis or that air nitrogen is directly usable; calling a parasite a saprotroph/symbiont; assuming a positive starch test proves the tissue is green; and mixing up xylem (water, up) with phloem (food, out from leaves). Do not write on this paper — mark answers on your answer sheet.

1. A sealed glass chamber holds one bean leaf in bright, steady light. Over a fixed hour the leaf takes in 18 units of carbon dioxide for one process while a second process inside the same leaf releases 6 units of carbon dioxide. A meter on the chamber wall measures only the NET change of the gas in the surrounding air. What does the meter read for carbon dioxide over that hour?
   (A) A net fall of 18 units, because respiration switches off in the light and only photosynthesis runs.
   (B) A net fall of 12 units, because photosynthesis used up more carbon dioxide than respiration released.
   (C) A net rise of 6 units, because only the carbon dioxide given out by the leaf reaches the air.
   (D) No change at all, because the two processes always cancel exactly inside a leaf.

2. In the same chamber as above, the experimenter now wants the carbon dioxide meter to read ZERO net change without removing the leaf or stopping either process. The single condition that achieves this is when:

   (B) the light is made as bright as possible, since maximum photosynthesis balances everything.
   (C) all the chlorophyll is removed so the leaf neither makes nor uses any gas.
   (D) the light is dimmed until photosynthesis takes in carbon dioxide at exactly the same rate respiration releases it.
