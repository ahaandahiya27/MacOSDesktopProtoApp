Set 1 · Q1–Q10

Q1

Soil is best described as a mixture of:

  • Aonly broken rock
  • Bbroken rock, decayed organic matter, water, air and microbes✓
  • Cpure sand only
  • Donly water and air

Working

✓

Soil is a living mixture of broken-down rock, decayed organic matter (humus), water, air and micro-organisms.

Q2

The vertical section through the soil from the surface down to the bedrock is called the:

  • Apercolation rate
  • Bsoil profile✓
  • Chumus layer
  • Dwater table

Working

✓

The vertical section from surface to bedrock is the soil profile.

Q3

The dark, fertile organic matter formed by the decay of plant and animal remains is:

  • Aclay
  • Bhumus✓
  • Cgravel
  • Dbedrock

Working

✓

The dark, fertile organic matter from decayed remains is humus.

Q4

In a soil profile, the topmost surface layer rich in dead leaves and organic matter is the:

  • AC horizon
  • BB horizon
  • CO horizon✓
  • Dbedrock

Working

✓

The surface organic layer of dead leaves and humus is the O horizon.

Q5

  • AA horizon (topsoil)✓
  • BB horizon (subsoil)
  • CC horizon
  • Dparent rock

Working

✓

The dark topsoil where most roots grow is the A horizon.

Q6

The B horizon (subsoil) is generally:

  • Athe richest in humus
  • Blighter in colour, containing minerals washed down from above✓
  • Cmade of pure bedrock
  • Dwhere most roots grow

Working

✓

The B horizon (subsoil) is lighter, holding minerals leached down from above.

Q7

The lowest layer of weathered parent rock in the soil profile is the:

  • AO horizon
  • BA horizon

  • Dhumus

✓

The weathered parent-rock layer is the C horizon.

Q8

  • A100% mineral particles
  • Babout 45% minerals, 25% air, 25% water, 5% humus✓
  • C90% water
  • Dhalf air and half rock only

Working

✓

A fertile soil is roughly 45% minerals, 25% air, 25% water and 5% humus.

Q9

The mineral particles in soil are formed when larger rocks are broken down by:

  • Aphotosynthesis
  • Bweathering by water, wind, ice and living things✓
  • Cburning
  • Dmagnetism

Working

✓

Rock is broken into soil particles by weathering — water, wind, ice and biological action.

Q10

Soils are classified into their three main types mainly by the:

  • Acolour of the soil
  • Bsize of their mineral particles✓
  • Csmell of the soil
  • Ddepth of the bedrock

Working

✓
