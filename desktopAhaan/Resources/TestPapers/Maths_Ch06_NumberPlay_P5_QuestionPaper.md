**Instructions:** Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. An odd count of odd numbers forces an odd sum and parity survives every reversal; the Virahanka-Fibonacci rule adds the two previous terms (its parity repeats O,O,E with period 3 and its units digits with period 60); in the taller-in-front game a call at position k is at most k-1 and the sum of all calls counts taller-ahead pairs; a supercell strictly exceeds every edge-neighbour; Kaprekar fixes 6174 (4-digit) and 495 (3-digit); Collatz always falls to 1; digit-sum governs divisibility by 9 and 3 while the alternating digit-sum governs 11. Do not write on this paper — mark answers on your answer sheet.

1. A whole number N has digit-sum 23. What is the remainder when N is divided by 9, and what is the remainder when N is divided by 3?
   (A) remainder 2 on division by 9, remainder 2 on division by 3
   (B) remainder 5 on division by 9, remainder 0 on division by 3
   (C) remainder 5 on division by 9, remainder 2 on division by 3
   (D) remainder 0 on division by 9, remainder 2 on division by 3

2. The five-digit number 8A35B is divisible by 9, and its digit B is exactly 2 more than its digit A. What is the value of A?
   (A) 3
   (B) 1
   (C) 5
   (D) 0

3. A number N leaves remainder 7 when divided by 11. The number M is formed by reversing the digits of the four-digit number N. Which statement is always true about M's remainder when divided by 11?
   (A) M leaves remainder 4 (because reversing a 4-digit number negates its alternating digit-sum mod 11)
   (B) M also leaves remainder 7, since reversal never changes the value mod 11
   (C) M leaves remainder 0, since any number and its reverse differ by a multiple of 11
   (D) it cannot be determined without the actual digits

4. Ahaan multiplies three whole numbers and gets an EVEN product, then adds the same three numbers and gets an ODD sum. What is the only possible parity pattern of the three numbers?

   (D) all three are odd
