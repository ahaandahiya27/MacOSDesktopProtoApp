**Marking scheme:** **+4** for each correct answer · **−1** for each wrong answer · **0** if unattempted.

**Instructions:** Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. An odd count of odd numbers forces an odd sum; the Virahanka-Fibonacci rule adds the two previous terms (not a constant ratio); in the taller-in-front game a call at position k can be at most k-1; Kaprekar's routine fixes 6174 (4-digit) and 495 (3-digit), Collatz always falls to 1; a supercell must strictly exceed every edge-neighbour. 1. Kishor adds up 7 numbers. He tells you their total is odd and that an EVEN number of them are even. What is the smallest possible count of ODD numbers he could have used?
   (A) 0
   (B) 1
   (C) 2
   (D) 7

2. A whole number is called 'balanced' if it equals the sum of an even count of odd numbers plus any number of even numbers. Which of these totals can NEVER be balanced using exactly four odd numbers and three even numbers?

   (B) 40

   (D) 50

3. The numbers 1 to 50 are written down. How many of them have a digit-sum that is itself even?

4. A number N leaves remainder 2 when divided by 9. What is the remainder when the number formed by reversing N's digits is divided by 9?
