Set 1 · Q1–Q10

Q1

The layered top-to-bottom slice of soil, showing all its horizons, is called the:

  • Asoil texture
  • Bpercolation rate
  • Csoil profile✓
  • Dhumus layer

Working

✓

The complete vertical top-to-bottom section through soil, showing all its horizons, is the soil profile. ("Texture" is about particle size; "percolation rate" is a drainage measure.)

Q2

Which horizon of a soil profile is the dark, humus-rich topsoil where most plant roots grow?

  • Athe C horizon
  • Bthe R horizon
  • Cthe B horizon
  • Dthe A horizon✓

Working

✓

The A horizon is the topsoil — dark, rich in humus and minerals, and the layer where most roots grow. The O horizon is the surface litter, B is the lighter subsoil, C is broken parent rock.

Q3

Arrange the soil horizons in order from the surface downward.

  • Abedrock, C, B, A, O
  • BA, O, B, C, bedrock
  • CO, A, B, C, bedrock✓
  • DO, B, A, C, bedrock

Working

✓

From the surface downward the horizons run O (organic litter), A (topsoil), B (subsoil), C (weathered rock), then bedrock — so O, A, B, C, bedrock.

Q4

Soil is formed mainly by the:

  • Asudden cooling of lava in one night
  • Bfreezing of rainwater in winter
  • Cslow weathering of rocks over a very long time✓
  • Drapid evaporation of river water

Working

✓

Soil forms by the slow weathering of rocks — water, wind, ice, and living things break rock into fine particles over a very long time. It is a gradual process, not a sudden one.

Q5

The dark, crumbly material formed from decayed plant and animal remains in the top layer of soil is called:

  • Aclay
  • Bsilt
  • Cbedrock
  • Dhumus✓

Working

✓

Humus is the dark, crumbly material formed from decayed plant and animal remains; it makes the topsoil fertile. Clay and silt are mineral particles; bedrock is solid rock.

Q6

A gardener digs a pit and finds, just under the topsoil, a lighter, harder layer where minerals washed down from above have collected. This layer is the:

  • AA horizon
  • BC horizon
  • CO horizon
  • DB horizon✓

Working

✓

Just below the topsoil (A horizon) lies the B horizon (subsoil) — lighter and harder, where clay and minerals washed down from above accumulate.

Q7

The solid, unweathered rock at the very bottom of a soil profile is the:

  • Asubsoil (B horizon)
  • Bbedrock (R horizon)✓
  • Ctopsoil (A horizon)
  • Dorganic layer (O horizon)

Working

✓

The bottom of a soil profile is the unweathered solid rock, the bedrock or R horizon. Topsoil (A horizon), subsoil (B horizon) and the organic layer (O horizon) all lie above it.

Q8

Which statement about how soil forms is correct?

  • ASoil forms in a few days after heavy rain.
  • BSoil is made entirely of living organisms.
  • CSoil never contains any rock material.
  • DBuilding a centimetre of new topsoil can take hundreds of years.✓

Working

✓

Soil forms extremely slowly — building even one centimetre of new topsoil can take hundreds of years. Soil is not made entirely of living things, it does contain rock-derived mineral particles, and it certainly does not form in a few days.

Q9

A desert soil often shows almost no O horizon. The most likely reason is:

  • Atoo much rainfall washes the O horizon away
  • Bwind packs the particles too tightly
  • Cdeserts have no bedrock underneath
  • Dthere is very little plant litter to form humus✓

Working

✓

The O horizon is built from plant litter turning into humus. Deserts have very few plants, so there is little litter — hence almost no O horizon. (It is not washed away by rain — deserts get little rain.)

Q10

Which of these correctly pairs a soil type with its particle size?

  • Aclayey — largest particles
  • Bsandy — smallest particles
  • Csandy — largest particles✓
  • Dloamy — only one particle size

Working

✓

Sandy soil has the largest particles, clayey soil the smallest, and loamy soil is a mixture of several particle sizes. So the correct pairing is "sandy — largest particles."

Set 2 · Q11–Q20

Q11

Water rushes straight through sandy soil mainly because:

  • Athe large gaps between big particles let water drain fast✓
  • Bsand particles dissolve in water
  • Csand repels water
  • Dsand has no air spaces at all

Working

✓

Big sand particles leave large gaps (pores) between them, so water drains straight through quickly. Sand does not dissolve, does not repel water, and it does have air spaces.

Q12

Which soil holds the most water and drains the slowest?

  • Asandy soil
  • Bclayey soil✓
  • Cloamy soil
  • Dsilt alone

Working

✓

Clayey soil has the tiniest, most tightly packed particles, so it drains slowest and holds the most water. Sandy drains fastest; loamy is in between.

Q13

Loamy soil is the best for growing most crops because it:

  • Ais made only of large sand grains
  • Bholds enough water for roots yet lets the extra drain away✓
  • Cnever contains any humus
  • Dis the same as pure clay

Working

✓

Loam is a balanced mix of sand, silt, clay and humus, so it holds enough water for roots yet still lets surplus water drain — ideal for most crops. It is not pure clay and does contain humus.

Q14

Two pots are filled to the same level — one with sandy soil, one with clayey soil — and equal water is poured on each. After ten minutes the:

  • Asandy pot has drained more water out of the bottom✓
  • Bclayey pot has drained more water out of the bottom
  • Cboth drain exactly the same amount
  • Dneither lets any water through

Working

✓

Sandy soil has a high percolation rate, so far more water drains out of the bottom of the sandy pot than the clayey one in the same ten minutes. (The clayey pot holds the water and lets little through.)

Q15

Black cotton soil of the Deccan plateau, which becomes very sticky when wet and cracks when dry, is mainly:

  • Asandy soil
  • Bpure silt
  • Cloose gravel
  • Dclayey soil✓

Working

✓

Black cotton soil (Deccan plateau) is sticky when wet, cracks when dry, and holds water — these are the signs of clayey soil.

Q16

Which property best explains why sandy soil dries out quickly after rain?

  • Ahigh percolation rate and low water-holding capacity✓
  • Bhigh water-holding capacity
  • Cvery small air gaps
  • Dvery high humus content

Working

✓

Sandy soil drains fast (high percolation rate) and holds little water (low water-holding capacity), so it loses its moisture quickly and dries out soon after rain.

Q17

Soil is described as "teeming with life." Which best supports this statement?

  • ASoil is only broken rock with nothing living in it.
  • BA single gram of soil can contain millions of micro-organisms.✓
  • COnly earthworms live in soil and nothing else.
  • DLiving things avoid soil completely.

Working

✓

Soil is "alive" because a single gram can hold millions of micro-organisms (plus worms, insects, fungi). The other options deny that soil contains life.

Q18

Roughly, the four main ingredients of soil are:

  • Aonly sand and water
  • Bmineral particles, air, water, and humus✓
  • Cplastic, glass, metal, and stone
  • Donly humus and air

Working

✓

The four main ingredients of soil are mineral particles, air, water, and humus (organic matter). Plastic/glass/metal are pollutants, not natural soil components.

Q19

When a soil is completely water-logged after heavy rain, plant roots can struggle because the soil now has very little:

  • Aair✓
  • Bwater
  • Cclay
  • Dweight

Working

✓

When soil is water-logged, water fills the pore spaces and pushes the air out, so the roots get very little air (oxygen) — which is why they struggle.

Q20

Which agent is **not** normally responsible for weathering rock into soil over time?

  • Aa single bolt of lightning✓
  • Bflowing and freezing water
  • Cgrowing plant roots
  • Dwind

Working

✓

Weathering is the slow break-up of rock by water (flowing and freezing), wind, and growing roots over long times. A single bolt of lightning is a one-off event, not a normal soil-forming weathering agent.

Set 3 · Q21–Q30

Q21

A student heats a sample of moist soil in a dry test tube and sees droplets form on the cooler upper walls. This shows that soil contains:

  • Aplastic
  • Bair
  • Cmoisture (water)✓
  • Dsalt only

Working

✓

Heating moist soil drives off water as vapour, which condenses into droplets on the cooler glass — proof that the soil contained moisture (water).

Q22

To show that soil contains **air**, the best simple demonstration is to:

  • Adrop a lump of dry soil into water and watch bubbles rise✓
  • Bheat the soil until droplets appear on the glass
  • Crub the soil between your fingers
  • Dweigh the soil on a balance

Working

✓

Dropping a lump of dry soil into water makes bubbles rise as water fills the pore spaces and pushes the trapped air out — a simple proof that soil contains air. (Heating shows water; weighing and rubbing show neither directly.)

Q23

Percolation rate is best defined as the:

  • Aweight of soil per unit volume
  • Bamount of humus in the soil
  • Cnumber of organisms in the soil
  • Dspeed at which water seeps down through soil✓

Working

✓

Percolation rate is the speed at which water seeps down through soil (volume of water ÷ time). It is not a weight, a humus measure, or an organism count.

Q24

In an experiment, 200 mL of water takes 20 minutes to percolate through a soil sample. The percolation rate is:

  • A0.1 mL/min
  • B10 mL/min✓
  • C4000 mL/min
  • D220 mL/min

Working

✓

Percolation rate = volume ÷ time = 200 mL ÷ 20 min = 10 mL/min. (Multiplying instead of dividing gives the trap 4000.)

Q25

150 mL of water percolates through a soil column in 5 minutes. The percolation rate is:

  • A30 mL/min✓
  • B750 mL/min
  • C3 mL/min
  • D145 mL/min

Working

✓

Rate = 150 mL ÷ 5 min = 30 mL/min. (750 = 150 × 5, the multiply trap; 145 = 150 − 5, the subtract trap.)

Q26

Soil X lets 300 mL through in 10 minutes; soil Y lets 300 mL through in 30 minutes. Which soil is more likely sandy?

  • Asoil Y, because it is slower
  • Bboth are equally sandy
  • Cneither — sand never lets water through
  • Dsoil X, because it has the higher percolation rate✓

Working

✓

Same volume (300 mL): soil X takes 10 min, soil Y takes 30 min. X is faster, so X has the higher percolation rate — the mark of sandy soil. (Slower = clayey.)

Q27

A loamy sample drains 240 mL of water in 8 minutes. Its percolation rate is:

  • A248 mL/min
  • B30 mL/min✓
  • C0.03 mL/min
  • D1920 mL/min

Working

✓

Rate = 240 mL ÷ 8 min = 30 mL/min. (248 = 240 + 8 is the add trap.)

Q28

A sandy soil has a percolation rate of 25 mL/min. How long will 200 mL of water take to percolate through it?

  • A8 minutes✓
  • B5000 minutes
  • C0.125 minutes
  • D175 minutes

Working

✓

Time = volume ÷ rate = 200 mL ÷ 25 mL/min = 8 minutes. (Multiplying 200 × 25 gives the trap 5000.)

Q29

Soil P percolates 200 mL in 8 minutes; soil Q percolates 360 mL in 12 minutes. Which has the **higher** percolation rate?

  • Asoil Q✓
  • Bsoil P
  • Cboth equal
  • Dcannot be compared

Working

✓

Rate of P = 200 ÷ 8 = 25 mL/min; rate of Q = 360 ÷ 12 = 30 mL/min. Q (30) > P (25), so soil Q has the higher rate.

Q30

Two soils drain equal volumes of water. Sandy soil takes 4 minutes; clayey soil takes 40 minutes. The ratio of their percolation rates (sandy : clayey) is:

  • A1 : 10
  • B4 : 40
  • C10 : 1✓
  • D1 : 1

Working

✓

For equal volumes, rate is inversely proportional to time. Sandy : clayey rate = (1/4) : (1/40) = 10 : 1. (Writing 4 : 40 = 1 : 10 reverses it — the classic trap.)

Set 4 · Q31–Q40

Q31

Water-holding capacity is **highest** in:

  • Aclayey soil✓
  • Bsandy soil
  • Cgravel
  • Dcoarse sand

Working

✓

Clayey soil, with its tiny tightly-packed particles, holds the most water — the highest water-holding capacity. Sand, gravel and coarse sand all drain freely.

Q32

Which soil type usually has the **best aeration** (most air for roots)?

  • Awater-logged soil
  • Bclayey soil
  • Csandy soil✓
  • Dcompacted clay

Working

✓

Sandy soil has large air-filled gaps between its big particles, so it is the best aerated. Clayey, compacted and water-logged soils all squeeze out the air.

Q33

Paddy (rice) is grown in fields that are kept flooded with water. The most suitable soil for paddy is therefore:

  • Asandy soil, because it drains fast
  • Bgravel, because it has big gaps
  • Cclayey soil, because it holds water✓
  • Ddry desert soil

Working

✓

Paddy fields are kept flooded, so the soil must retain water — clayey soil, which holds water well, suits paddy best. Sandy soil and gravel would drain the water away.

Q34

Cotton grows best in:

  • Awater-logged clay that never drains
  • Bsolid bedrock
  • Cpure beach sand with no nutrients
  • Dloamy or sandy-loam soil rich in humus✓

Working

✓

Cotton grows best in well-drained but humus-rich loamy or sandy-loam soil — enough water plus good aeration. Water-logged clay, bedrock and nutrient-free sand are all unsuitable.

Q35

A farmer's field stays water-logged for days after every rain, and crops keep rotting. To improve drainage he should mix in some:

  • Amore fine clay
  • Bplastic sheeting
  • Csand✓
  • Dextra water

Working

✓

Mixing in sand opens up larger pore spaces, raising the percolation rate so the water-logged clay drains better. Adding more clay or water makes it worse; plastic sheeting blocks drainage.

Q36

Which crop is best matched to a soil that holds water well and is poorly aerated?

  • Agroundnut in dry sand
  • Bpaddy (rice)✓
  • Ccactus
  • Dwatermelon on a sand dune

Working

✓

A soil that holds water well but is poorly aerated is clayey soil, and the crop matched to flooded, water-holding soil is paddy (rice). Cactus, groundnut and watermelon all prefer dry, sandy conditions.

Q37

A cactus naturally thrives in:

  • Awater-logged clay
  • Bflooded paddy fields
  • Cthick humus bog
  • Dwell-drained sandy soil✓

Working

✓

A cactus is adapted to dry conditions and thrives in well-drained sandy soil. Water-logged clay, flooded paddy fields and humus bogs would rot its roots.

Q38

Which sequence of soils is in **increasing** order of percolation rate (slowest to fastest)?

  • Aclayey, loamy, sandy✓
  • Bsandy, loamy, clayey
  • Cloamy, sandy, clayey
  • Dsandy, clayey, loamy

Working

✓

Percolation rate increases (slowest to fastest) as particle size grows: clayey (slowest) < loamy (medium) < sandy (fastest). So clayey, loamy, sandy.

Q39

The wearing-away and removal of fertile topsoil by wind or water is called:

  • Apercolation
  • Bweathering
  • Ccrop rotation
  • Dsoil erosion✓

Working

✓

The wearing-away and removal of fertile topsoil by wind or water is soil erosion. Percolation is water seeping down; weathering forms soil; crop rotation maintains fertility.

Q40

Which human activity speeds up soil erosion the most?

  • Aplanting trees along field edges
  • Bcutting down forests on hill slopes✓
  • Cbuilding check-dams in gullies
  • Dploughing along the contour

Working

✓

Cutting down forests on hill slopes removes the roots that bind the soil, so rain washes the topsoil away — the biggest erosion-accelerator listed. Planting trees, check-dams and contour ploughing all reduce erosion.

Set 5 · Q41–Q50

Q41

Cutting flat, step-like platforms into a hillside to slow water and stop soil washing down is called:

  • Acontour ploughing
  • Bcrop rotation
  • Covergrazing
  • Dterracing✓

Working

✓

Cutting flat, step-like platforms into a hillside is terracing; it slows water and stops soil washing down. Contour ploughing follows the slope's lines; crop rotation and overgrazing are unrelated.

Q42

Ploughing **along** the elevation lines of a slope, so each furrow traps water instead of channelling it downhill, is called:

  • Acontour ploughing✓
  • Bterracing
  • Cdeforestation
  • Dmulching

Working

✓

Ploughing along the slope's elevation (contour) lines so each furrow traps water is contour ploughing. Terracing cuts steps; deforestation and mulching are different practices.

Q43

Why does keeping a field covered with plants or mulch reduce soil erosion?

  • AThe cover makes the soil heavier so it cannot move.
  • BMulch turns soil into solid rock.
  • CThe cover stops all rain from ever reaching the ground.
  • DPlant roots and mulch break the force of rain and hold the soil in place.✓

Working

✓

Plant roots bind the soil and the leafy cover/mulch breaks the force of falling raindrops, so the soil is held in place and washes away less. The cover does not turn soil to rock or block all rain.

Q44

Rows of trees planted to slow down wind and protect a field from wind erosion are called:

  • Acheck-dams
  • Bterraces
  • Cshelter belts✓
  • Dfurrows

Working

✓

Rows of trees planted to slow the wind and shield a field from wind erosion are shelter belts. Check-dams trap runoff in gullies; terraces and furrows are ground-shaping methods.

Q45

Crop rotation helps keep soil fertile mainly because:

  • Ait removes all the soil's water
  • Bit makes the soil purely sandy
  • Cgrowing legumes restores nitrogen taken out by cereal crops✓
  • Dit stops the soil from ever being ploughed

Working

✓

Crop rotation keeps soil fertile because growing legumes restores the nitrogen that cereal crops remove, so fertility is renewed without heavy fertiliser use.

Q46

In a cereal–legume–cereal rotation, the legume (such as gram or peas) helps because bacteria in its roots:

  • Afix nitrogen from the air into the soil✓
  • Beat up the soil's nitrogen
  • Cturn the soil into clay
  • Dincrease the percolation rate to zero

Working

✓

Legumes host Rhizobium bacteria in their root nodules; these bacteria fix nitrogen from the air into the soil as usable nitrates — the opposite of eating it up.

Q47

Which of these is a form of **soil pollution**?

  • Aearthworms moving through the soil
  • Bplastic waste and harmful chemicals dumped on the soil✓
  • Crain percolating through the soil
  • Dhumus forming from fallen leaves

Working

✓

Dumping plastic waste and harmful chemicals on the soil is soil pollution. Earthworms, percolating rain and humus from fallen leaves are all natural and beneficial.

Q48

Deforesting a steep hillside is especially harmful because, with the tree roots gone:

  • Athe soil becomes more fertile
  • Bpercolation completely stops
  • Cheavy rain washes the loose topsoil away easily✓
  • Dthe soil holds more water than before

Working

✓

Once the tree roots that held the soil are gone, heavy rain on the bare hillside easily washes the loose topsoil away. Deforestation lowers fertility and water retention, not raises them.

Q49

A field of bare, dry soil on a windy plain is most at risk from:

  • Awater-logging
  • Bwind erosion✓
  • Ctoo much humus
  • Dcrop rotation

Working

✓

Bare, dry soil on a windy plain has no cover and loose fine particles, so it is most at risk from wind erosion. (Dry soil is not at risk of water-logging.)

Q50

Why is losing topsoil to erosion such a serious problem for farming?

  • ATopsoil regrows fully within a week.
  • BTopsoil has no nutrients anyway.
  • CTopsoil is the humus-rich layer where crops feed, and it takes centuries to rebuild.✓
  • DErosion only removes harmless bedrock.

Working

✓

The topsoil is the humus-rich layer where crops get their nutrients, and because soil forms so slowly it takes centuries to rebuild — so losing it is a serious, near-permanent loss.

Set 6 · Q51–Q60

Q51

A farmer wants a soil that holds enough water for vegetables but still drains after rain and is easy to till. He should choose:

  • Apure sand
  • Bloamy soil✓
  • Cpure clay
  • Dsolid bedrock

Working

✓

A soil that holds enough water for vegetables yet still drains and is easy to till is loamy soil — the balanced mix. Pure sand drains too fast, pure clay water-logs, and bedrock is not soil.

Q52

Equal volumes of water are poured on three samples. Sandy drains in 2 min, loamy in 6 min, clayey in 18 min. Which has the **lowest** percolation rate?

  • Aloamy
  • Bclayey✓
  • Csandy
  • Dall equal

Working

✓

For equal volumes, the soil that takes the longest time has the lowest rate. Clayey takes 18 min (longest), so clayey has the lowest percolation rate. Sandy (2 min) is fastest.

Q53

A soil column drains 300 mL in 6 minutes; a second column of the same soil drains 600 mL. Assuming the same rate, the second takes:

  • A12 minutes✓
  • B3 minutes
  • C1 minute
  • D6 minutes

Working

✓

Rate = 300 mL ÷ 6 min = 50 mL/min. Time for 600 mL = 600 ÷ 50 = 12 minutes. (Doubling the volume doubles the time, from 6 to 12 min.)

Q54

Which statement about loamy soil is **false**?

  • AIt is a mixture of sand, silt, clay and humus.
  • BIt has the slowest percolation rate of all soils.✓
  • CIt is well suited to most crops.
  • DIt holds water moderately well.

Working

✓

Loamy soil does NOT have the slowest percolation rate — clayey soil does; loam drains moderately. The other three statements (a mix of sand/silt/clay/humus, suited to most crops, holds water moderately) are all true, so the false one is option (B).

Q55

A gram of garden soil supports more living organisms than a gram of beach sand mainly because garden soil:

  • Ais heavier
  • Bhas larger particles
  • Ccontains no air
  • Dis rich in humus and holds moisture for organisms✓

Working

✓

Garden soil is rich in humus and holds moisture, providing food and water for organisms, so it supports far more life than dry, nutrient-poor beach sand. Weight and particle size are not the reason.

Q56

Soil A drains 100 mL in 4 min; soil B drains 100 mL in 4 min as well, but soil B's sample is twice as deep. The slower-percolating arrangement, per unit depth, is:

  • Asoil B✓
  • Bsoil A
  • Cboth identical per unit depth
  • Dneither lets water through

Working

✓

Both drain 100 mL in 4 min, so both have the same overall rate — but soil B's column is twice as deep, so per unit depth the water moves more slowly through it. Hence soil B is the slower-percolating arrangement per unit depth.

Q57

Why does over-watering a clayey-soil pot often harm the plant?

  • AClay drains so fast the roots dry out.
  • BClay has too much air already.
  • CClay holds the extra water and starves the roots of air.✓
  • DClay turns the water into humus.

Working

✓

Clayey soil holds the extra water in its tiny pores, pushing out the air, so the roots are starved of oxygen and the plant suffers. (Clay drains slowly, not fast, and does not turn water into humus.)

Q58

A hill farmer plants crops in straight up-and-down rows on a steep slope and clears all the trees. After the monsoon his field has deep channels and lost soil. The single best change he could make is to:

  • Aplough up-and-down even more
  • Bcut down the remaining vegetation
  • Cpour extra water on the slope
  • Dterrace the slope and plough along the contour✓

Working

✓

The damage comes from up-and-down ploughing on a bare, deforested slope. The best fix is to terrace the slope and plough along the contour, so water is trapped instead of channelling downhill. Ploughing up-and-down more or clearing more vegetation makes it worse.

Q59

Which best describes the role of percolation for groundwater?

  • APercolation removes all water from the ground.
  • BWater that percolates down helps refill underground water stores.✓
  • CPercolation only matters in deserts.
  • DPercolation has no effect on groundwater.

Working

✓

Water that percolates downward through the soil seeps into the ground and helps refill underground water stores (aquifers). It does not remove all groundwater, and it matters everywhere, not just in deserts.

Q60

A soil sample lets 480 mL of water through in 12 minutes. At the same rate, how much water would percolate in 5 minutes?

  • A40 mL
  • B2400 mL
  • C200 mL✓
  • D96 mL

Working

✓

Rate = 480 mL ÷ 12 min = 40 mL/min. In 5 min the volume = 40 × 5 = 200 mL. (2400 = 480 × 5 is the multiply trap; 96 = 480 ÷ 5 ignores the rate.) --- *Solutions complete: 60 / 60.*
