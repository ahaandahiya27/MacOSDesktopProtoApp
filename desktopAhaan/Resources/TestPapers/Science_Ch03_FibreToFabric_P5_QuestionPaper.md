1. A lab gives four results for one mystery thread: it keeps most of its strength when soaked, chars rather than melts, gives a burnt-hair smell, and mats together after hot agitated washing. A student says all four clues are needed to prove it is wool and not silk. Which single clue actually separates wool from silk here?
   (A) The burnt-hair smell, because only wool smells of burnt hair while silk smells papery
   (B) The matting after hot agitated washing, because both wool and silk are protein fibres that char and smell of burnt hair and resist wetting, but only wool's scaly fibres felt and mat
   (C) The wet strength, because silk loses all strength when wet while wool keeps it
   (D) The charring rather than melting, because silk melts like a synthetic while wool chars
