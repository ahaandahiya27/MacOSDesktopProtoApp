Set 1 · Q1–Q10

Q1

A 'government' is best described as:

  • Athe set of institutions through which a country is run✓
  • Ba single elected leader acting entirely alone
  • Cthe army that defends a country's borders
  • Dthe group of all citizens who vote

Working

✓

A government is the set of institutions that make rules, carry them out and ensure they are followed. It is not one leader alone, the army, or the whole electorate.

Q2

Which of these is a core job of any government?

  • Arunning every shop and farm directly
  • Bmaking rules, carrying them out, and ensuring they are followed✓
  • Cchoosing each family's religion
  • Dwriting the country's songs and poems

Working

✓

Government's core is the legislative, executive and judicial functions - making, enforcing and upholding rules. It does not run all businesses, dictate religion, or write art.

Q3

'Every country has a government, but governments are not all the same' means that:

  • Aonly democracies count as real governments
  • Ball countries are run somehow, but the kind of government varies✓
  • Cevery government works in exactly the same way
  • Da country can exist with no government at all

Working

✓

Every country has some government, yet they differ in how power is held and used. Not only democracies count, they are not identical, and a country needs some government to run.

Q4

Making the laws of a country is the ____ function of government.

  • Aexecutive
  • Blegislative✓
  • Cjudicial
  • Dceremonial

Working

✓

Making rules is the legislative function. Carrying them out is executive, ensuring they are followed is judicial, and 'ceremonial' is not one of the three functions.

Q5

Carrying out and implementing the laws is the ____ function.

  • Alegislative
  • Bjudicial
  • Cexecutive✓
  • Delectoral

Working

✓

Putting laws into action is the executive function. Making laws is legislative, judging disputes is judicial, and 'electoral' is not one of the functions here.

Q6

Settling disputes and ensuring that laws are followed is the ____ function.

  • Ajudicial✓
  • Blegislative
  • Cexecutive
  • Dadvisory

Working

✓

Ensuring laws are obeyed and settling disputes is the judicial function, the work of the courts. The others make or carry out laws.

Q7

A court strikes down an order because it breaks the law. Which function of government is at work?

  • Alegislative
  • Bexecutive
  • Cjudicial✓
  • Dceremonial

Working

✓

A court judging whether something follows the law is the judicial function. Lawmaking is legislative and enforcing is executive.

Q8

The chapter says one government differs from another based on questions such as:

  • Aonly which country is the largest
  • Bonly which country has the biggest army
  • Conly which country is the oldest
  • Dwho decides 'this is the government', how it is formed, its parts, and what it works for✓

Working

✓

Governments differ by who holds power, how they form, their parts and their aims - not merely size, army, or age.

Q9

The deepest question separating a democracy from a dictatorship is:

  • Ahow many people live in the country
  • Bwho holds power and how the government is chosen✓
  • Cwhich language the people speak
  • Dhow much land the country has

Working

✓

The key difference is who holds power and how rulers are chosen. Population, language and land do not by themselves decide the type of government.

Q10

'Democracy' literally means:

  • Arule of one king
  • Brule of the priests
  • Crule of the people✓
  • Drule of the army

Working

✓

Democracy means 'rule of the people' (from Greek demos + kratos). One-king rule is monarchy, priestly rule is theocracy, and army rule is not democracy.

Set 2 · Q11–Q20

Q11

In a democracy, the ultimate source of power is:

  • Aa hereditary king
  • Bthe people, who choose their representatives✓
  • Ca single religious leader
  • Dthe wealthiest families

Working

✓

In a democracy the people are the source of power and choose their representatives. A king, a religious head, or rich families holding power describe other forms.

Q12

The phrase 'rule of the people' implies that, in a democracy, leaders are:

  • Aappointed for life by the previous ruler
  • Bchosen by and answerable to the people✓
  • Cselected only by the army
  • Dborn into the role

Working

✓

'Rule of the people' means leaders are chosen by and accountable to the people, not life-appointed, army-selected, or born to rule.

Q13

The school student-committee story is used in the chapter mainly to explain:

  • Ahow to win a sports match
  • Bhow taxes are collected
  • Chow representation works - choosing a few to act for many✓
  • Dhow a king inherits a throne

Working

✓

The committee story illustrates representation: students choose a few to act on behalf of all. It is not about sports, taxes, or royal inheritance.

Q14

A 'representative' is:

  • Aa person who rules because of his birth
  • Ba person chosen to act or decide on behalf of others✓
  • Cthe richest person in a group
  • Da judge who settles disputes

Working

✓

A representative is chosen to act for others. Birth-rule is monarchy, wealth-rule is oligarchy, and a dispute-settler is a judge.

Q15

Choosing class representatives instead of every student deciding everything is most like:

  • Arepresentative democracy✓
  • Babsolute monarchy
  • Ctheocracy
  • Ddictatorship

Working

✓

Electing a few to act for all mirrors representative democracy. The other forms concentrate power in a king, religious leaders, or a single ruler.

Q16

Which is a fundamental principle of democracy?

  • Apower passed down within one family
  • Bequality of all citizens✓
  • Crule by religious law alone
  • Ddecisions made by a few wealthy people

Working

✓

Equality is a core democratic principle. Family-inherited power, religious rule and decisions by a wealthy few describe monarchy, theocracy and oligarchy.

Q17

'Universal adult franchise' means:

  • Aonly landowners may vote
  • Bonly men may vote
  • Conly members of the government may vote
  • Devery citizen above a certain age has the right to vote✓

Working

✓

Universal adult franchise grants every citizen above a set age the right to vote. Limiting the vote to landowners, men, or the government denies this principle.

Q18

'Suffrage' is:

  • Athe duty to pay taxes
  • Bthe power to make laws
  • Cthe right to vote in elections✓
  • Dthe office of a judge

Working

✓

Suffrage (Latin suffragium) is the right to vote. Tax-paying, lawmaking and judging are different things.

Q19

An 'independent judiciary' matters in a democracy because it:

  • Aalways rules in the government's favour
  • Bis controlled by the ruling party
  • Conly gives advice to the king
  • Dcan decide cases fairly, even against the government, ensuring laws are followed✓

Working

✓

An independent judiciary judges fairly, even against the government, upholding the law. A judiciary that always favours the government, is party-controlled, or merely advises a king is not independent.

Q20

Why are fundamental rights important in a democracy?

  • Athey let the majority do absolutely anything it wants
  • Bthey belong only to the rulers
  • Cthey protect citizens' freedoms even from the government and the majority✓
  • Dthey apply only during elections

Working

✓

Fundamental rights guard citizens' freedoms, which even the government and the majority cannot simply take away. They are not a blank cheque for the majority, rulers-only, or election-only.

Set 3 · Q21–Q30

Q21

'Accountability' in a democracy means the government is:

  • Afree from any questioning by citizens
  • Banswerable only to a foreign power
  • Canswerable to the people who elected it✓
  • Dable to ignore the courts

Working

✓

Accountability means the government must answer to the people who elected it. It is not freedom from questions, foreign answerability, or court-ignoring.

Q22

In a 'direct democracy', the people:

  • Adecide on issues themselves, often by voting directly✓
  • Bnever vote at all
  • Cobey a hereditary king
  • Dlet a small group decide everything

Working

✓

In a direct democracy citizens decide issues themselves. Not voting, obeying a king, or letting a few decide describe non-democratic or representative forms.

Q23

A 'referendum' is:

  • Aan election of representatives to a parliament
  • Ba decision made by judges
  • Ca law made by a king
  • Da direct vote in which all citizens decide a single question themselves✓

Working

✓

A referendum (Latin 'a thing to be referred') is a direct citizen vote on one question. Electing representatives, judges deciding, or a king's law are different.

Q24

In a 'representative democracy', citizens:

  • Avote directly on every single law themselves
  • Bhave no vote at all
  • Celect representatives to make decisions on their behalf✓
  • Dinherit their seats in parliament

Working

✓

In a representative democracy citizens elect representatives to decide for them. Voting on every law is direct democracy, no vote is undemocratic, and inherited seats are not democratic.

Q25

Most large modern countries use representative rather than direct democracy mainly because:

  • Ait is impractical for millions of citizens to vote directly on every decision✓
  • Bcitizens are not allowed to have any opinions
  • Cdirect democracy is illegal worldwide
  • Drepresentatives are always kings

Working

✓

With millions of people, deciding every issue by direct vote is impractical, so representatives are elected. Citizens do have opinions, direct democracy is not illegal, and representatives are elected, not kings.

Q26

India is a:

  • Apresidential democracy
  • Babsolute monarchy
  • Ctheocracy
  • Dparliamentary democracy✓

Working

✓

India is a parliamentary democracy, where real executive power lies with the PM and council of ministers. It is not presidential, an absolute monarchy, or a theocracy.

Q27

The claim 'India is a presidential democracy because it has a President' is wrong because:

  • AIndia actually has no President at all
  • Breal executive power lies with the prime minister and council of ministers, who must keep the Lok Sabha's confidence✓
  • CIndia is ruled by a hereditary king
  • Dthe President of India is elected directly by all voters

Working

✓

Despite having a President, India is parliamentary: the PM and ministers, drawn from Parliament, hold real executive power and need the Lok Sabha's confidence. India does have a President (a largely nominal head), is not a monarchy, and the President is not directly elected by all voters.

Q28

The USA is the chapter's example of a:

  • Aparliamentary democracy
  • Bconstitutional monarchy
  • Cpresidential democracy✓
  • Doligarchy

Working

✓

The USA is a presidential democracy, where the president is elected and works independently of the legislature. India is the parliamentary example; the UK a constitutional monarchy.

Q29

A key difference between parliamentary and presidential democracy is that, in a parliamentary system:

  • Athe head of government is elected separately and is independent of the legislature
  • Bthere are never any elections
  • Cthe head of government is drawn from the legislature and must keep its confidence✓
  • Da king makes all the laws

Working

✓

In a parliamentary system the head of government comes from and answers to the legislature, unlike a presidential system where the president is separately elected and independent. Both hold elections, and neither is ruled by a law-making king.

Q30

In India, real executive power is held by the:

  • APresident acting alone
  • Bprime minister and the council of ministers✓
  • CChief Justice
  • Dhereditary royal family

Working

✓

Real executive power in India rests with the PM and the council of ministers. The President is a largely nominal head, the Chief Justice heads the judiciary, and there is no ruling royal family.

Set 4 · Q31–Q40

Q31

In the USA's presidential system, the president is:

  • Achosen by and answerable to the legislature
  • Belected and works independently of the legislature✓
  • Ca hereditary monarch
  • Dappointed by the judges

Working

✓

The US president is elected and operates independently of the legislature. Being chosen by the legislature describes a parliamentary head; a monarch inherits; and judges do not appoint the president.

Q32

The three organs of government are the:

  • Alegislature, executive and judiciary✓
  • Barmy, police and courts
  • Cking, council and priests
  • Dvoters, parties and media

Working

✓

The three organs are the legislature (makes laws), executive (carries them out) and judiciary (upholds them). The other lists are not the three organs.

Q33

'Separation of powers' means the three organs:

  • Aare all controlled by one single person
  • Bnever communicate with one another at all
  • Care abolished in a democracy
  • Dwork independently and do not interfere in each other's functions✓

Working

✓

Separation of powers keeps the legislature, executive and judiciary independent so none dominates the others. It is not one-person control, total silence, or abolition.

Q34

Why does separation of powers help protect democracy?

  • Ait lets the executive control the courts
  • Bit stops any one organ from gaining total control and abusing power✓
  • Cit gives all power to the legislature
  • Dit removes the need for any laws

Working

✓

By keeping the organs independent, separation of powers prevents any one from seizing total control - a check against abuse. It does not let one organ control another or remove laws.

Q35

If a country's ruler controls the lawmakers, the enforcers and the judges all at once, it most clearly lacks:

  • Aa national flag
  • Bseparation of powers✓
  • Ca large population
  • Dany rivers or mountains

Working

✓

One ruler controlling all three organs means no separation of powers, a hallmark of dictatorship. A flag, population size and geography are unrelated.

Q36

A 'monarchy' is a government ruled by:

  • Aelected representatives
  • Breligious leaders
  • Ca small group of wealthy families
  • Da monarch (king or queen), usually inheriting the position within the family✓

Working

✓

A monarchy is ruled by a king or queen, usually by inheritance. Elected representatives, religious leaders and wealthy-family rule describe democracy, theocracy and oligarchy.

Q37

In an 'absolute monarchy' (like Saudi Arabia), the king:

  • Atruly holds and exercises ruling power✓
  • Bhas only nominal, ceremonial power
  • Cis elected every five years
  • Dis chosen by the courts

Working

✓

In an absolute monarchy the king really rules. Nominal, ceremonial power describes a constitutional monarch; absolute monarchs are not elected or court-chosen.

Q38

In a 'constitutional monarchy' (like the UK), the king or queen:

  • Aholds all real power and personally makes every law
  • Bis elected by the people every term
  • Chas mostly nominal power while an elected parliament makes the laws✓
  • Ddoes not exist at all

Working

✓

In a constitutional monarchy the monarch's power is largely nominal while an elected parliament governs. The monarch does not hold all power, is not elected, and does exist.

Q39

The claim 'a country with a king or queen cannot be a democracy' is:

  • Aalways true, because any monarch blocks democracy
  • Btrue only for absolute monarchies that are democracies
  • Cfalse, because the UK has no monarch at all
  • Dfalse - a constitutional monarchy like the UK is a parliamentary democracy with a nominal king✓

Working

✓

A constitutional monarchy (UK) is a parliamentary democracy where the monarch's power is nominal, so a king and democracy can coexist. An absolute monarchy is not a democracy, and the UK does have a monarch.

Q40

The UK shows that the same country can be:

  • Aonly ever a pure absolute monarchy
  • Ba democracy that has banned all elections
  • Cboth a democracy and a monarchy at once (a constitutional monarchy)✓
  • Da country with no government

Working

✓

The UK is both a democracy and a monarchy - a constitutional monarchy. It is not an absolute monarchy, an election-free state, or government-less.

Q41

A 'theocracy' is a government in which a country is ruled by:

  • Aan elected parliament
  • Bthe rules of religion and by religious leaders✓
  • Ca hereditary king with only nominal power
  • Da small group of wealthy families

Working

✓

A theocracy is rule by religious law and leaders. An elected parliament is democracy, a nominal king a constitutional monarchy, and wealthy-family rule oligarchy.

Q42

A 'dictatorship' is a government in which:

  • Aone person or a small group holds absolute power with no limits from a constitution or law✓
  • Bpower is shared among elected representatives
  • Cthe people vote directly on every issue
  • Da monarch's power is purely ceremonial

Working

✓

In a dictatorship one person or a small group wields absolute, unchecked power. Shared elected power, direct voting and a ceremonial monarch describe other forms.

Q43

The word 'theocracy' uses the Greek root 'kratos', the same root found in:

  • A'democracy'✓
  • B'monarchy'
  • C'oligarchy'
  • D'anarchy'

Working

✓

Kratos ('power, rule') gives both theo-cracy and demo-cracy. Mon-archy, olig-archy and an-archy come from the other root, arkhe ('rule').

Q44

A dictatorship differs most sharply from a democracy in that it:

  • Aholds the largest elections in the world
  • Balways protects minority rights
  • Chas no real limits on the ruler's power and no genuine accountability to the people✓
  • Dhas the most independent courts

Working

✓

A dictatorship concentrates unchecked power with no real accountability - the opposite of democracy's elections, rights and independent courts.

Q45

An 'oligarchy' is a government in which:

  • Aa small, powerful group - often wealthy families - makes the important decisions✓
  • Bevery adult citizen votes on the laws
  • Ca single king inherits power
  • Dreligious leaders rule by sacred law

Working

✓

An oligarchy is rule by a small powerful group, often the wealthy. Universal voting is democracy, inherited single rule is monarchy, and sacred-law rule is theocracy.

Q46

The word 'oligarchy' comes from Greek roots meaning:

  • A'few' and 'to rule'✓
  • B'people' and 'power'
  • C'one' and 'rule'
  • D'god' and 'power'

Working

✓

Oligarchy is from oligos ('few') + arkho ('to rule'). 'People + power' gives democracy, 'one + rule' monarchy, and 'god + power' theocracy.

Q47

The chapter warns that even a real democracy can drift toward oligarchy if:

  • Atoo many ordinary people vote
  • Bthe courts become independent
  • Cfundamental rights are well protected
  • Da small powerful group gains too much control✓

Working

✓

Democracies can slide toward oligarchy when a small powerful group seizes too much control. Wide voting, independent courts and protected rights strengthen democracy, not weaken it.

Q48

The 'Peek into History' shows that the idea of the ruled choosing their rulers is:

  • Abrand-new, invented in the 21st century
  • Bfound only outside India
  • Cancient, with examples from early India, Greece and Rome✓
  • Dimpossible before modern times

Working

✓

Choosing rulers is an ancient idea, seen in early India, Greece and Rome. It is not new, India-absent, or impossible in the past.

Q53

  • Aalways a king who inherits the throne
  • Belected, not a hereditary monarch✓
  • Cchosen by religious leaders
  • Dappointed by a foreign country

✓

In a republic the head of state is elected rather than a hereditary monarch. Inheritance, religious selection and foreign appointment describe other arrangements.

Q54

Why are 'democracy' and 'republic' NOT just two words for the same thing?

  • Athey mean exactly the same and always describe the same country
  • Ba republic forbids all voting

Democracy concerns who rules (the people); a republic concerns the head of state being elected rather than royal. They overlap but differ, so they are not identical, vote-banning, or king-requiring.

Q55

India is best described as a:

  • Ademocracy that keeps a hereditary king
  • Btheocracy ruled by religious law
  • Cabsolute monarchy
  • Ddemocratic republic - rule by the people, with an elected (not hereditary) head of state✓

✓

India is a democratic republic: the people rule and the head of state is elected, not a king. It is not a monarchy, a theocracy, or an absolute monarchy.

Q56

The United Kingdom shows that a country can be a democracy while still:

  • Abanning all elections forever
  • Bbeing ruled entirely by religious law
  • Chaving no parliament at all
  • Dkeeping a monarch as a nominal head of state✓

Working

✓

The UK is a democracy that keeps a monarch (nominal head) - a constitutional monarchy. It has not banned elections, is not religious rule, and does have a parliament.

Q57

The claim 'holding an election automatically makes a country a real democracy' is wrong because a genuine democracy also needs:

  • Aequality, freedom of speech, fundamental rights and an independent judiciary✓
  • Bonly a very large army
  • Conly a hereditary king
  • Dnothing more than a single vote

Working

✓

Elections alone are not enough; real democracy also needs equality, free speech, rights and an independent judiciary. An army, a king, or a lone vote do not make a democracy.

Q58

The idea that 'in a democracy the majority can do absolutely anything it wants' is mistaken because democracy:

  • Alets the majority remove anyone's fundamental rights by a vote
  • Bgives the majority control of the courts

Working

✓

Democracy is more than majority rule: it protects minorities and individuals, follows a constitution, and limits power through separation of powers. The majority cannot simply vote away fundamental rights or seize the courts.

Q59

  • Ba system that no country actually uses
  • Cidentical to a dictatorship
  • Dthe most popular form of government in the world today✓

Working

✓

The chapter notes democracy is the world's most popular form of government today, despite its flaws. It is not rare, unused, or the same as dictatorship.

Q60

  • Aan oligarchy wearing the appearance of a democracy✓
  • Ba healthy, full democracy
  • Ca constitutional monarchy

✓

Real power held by one small wealthy group, despite votes, is an oligarchy dressed as a democracy - exactly the drift the chapter warns about. It is not a healthy democracy, a constitutional monarchy, or a direct democracy.
