# Olympiad Test Paper — Science (Class 7)
## Chapter 3: Fibre to Fabric

| | |
|---|---|

| **Total Questions** | 60 (Multiple Choice, single correct) |
| **Maximum Marks** | 240 |

**Marking scheme:** **+4** for each correct answer · **−1** for each wrong answer · **0** if unattempted.

**Instructions:** Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. This paper covers wool and silk — their animal sources, processing steps, the life cycle of the silk moth, and the people who do the work. Many questions ask you to put steps in the right order or to spot a wrong reason; read every option before choosing. Do not write on this paper — mark answers on your answer sheet.

1. Which of the following pairs are **both** animal fibres?
   (A) cotton and silk
   (B) jute and wool
   (C) flax and cotton
   (D) wool and silk

2. Wool and silk feel different from cotton mainly because:
   (A) all three are made of the same material
   (B) cotton is made of protein and wool of cellulose
   (C) silk is a synthetic fibre and cotton is natural
   (D) wool and silk are made of protein, while cotton is made of cellulose

3. A burning sample gives a smell like burning hair. The fibre is most likely:
   (A) cotton
   (B) jute
   (C) wool
   (D) flax

4. Which of these is **not** a wool-yielding animal?
   (A) yak
   (B) camel
   (C) goat
   (D) silk moth

5. The thick coat of hair on a sheep, from which wool is obtained, is called the:
   (A) fleece
   (B) cocoon
   (C) sericin
   (D) burr

6. The main reason a sheep grows a thick fleece is to:
   (A) provide wool for humans
   (B) keep the animal cool in summer
   (C) protect it from insect bites
   (D) trap a layer of air that keeps the animal warm

7. Pashmina, one of the softest and warmest wools, comes from a kind of:
   (A) sheep
   (B) silkworm
   (C) goat
   (D) camel

8. Wool keeps you warmer than a smooth plant fibre of the same thickness mainly because wool fibres:
   (A) trap more air between and within the fibres
   (B) are heavier
   (C) are coloured
   (D) are made of cellulose

9. In India, sheep reared for wool are found in the highlands of states such as:
   (A) Kerala and Tamil Nadu
   (B) West Bengal and Odisha
   (C) Jammu & Kashmir and Himachal Pradesh
   (D) Goa and Andhra Pradesh

10. The correct order of the **first three** steps in processing wool is:
    (A) scouring → shearing → sorting
    (B) shearing → scouring → sorting
    (C) sorting → shearing → scouring
    (D) shearing → sorting → scouring

11. Shearing is best done in spring or early summer rather than winter because:
    (A) wool grows faster in summer
    (B) removing the fleece in cold winter would leave the sheep without protection from the cold
    (C) the dye sticks better in summer
    (D) shears work only in warm weather

12. Scouring is the step in which the sheared fleece is:
    (A) washed to remove grease, dust and dirt
    (B) cut from the sheep's body
    (C) dyed in different colours
    (D) twisted into yarn

13. If the scouring step were skipped, the most direct problem would be that the wool:
    (A) would be too short
    (B) could not be dyed at all
    (C) would catch fire easily
    (D) would stay full of grease and dirt, spoiling later steps

14. Sorting separates the cleaned wool into different grades on the basis of features such as:
    (A) texture, length and colour
    (B) the price of the sheep
    (C) the season it was sheared
    (D) the country of origin only

15. "Picking out the burrs" in wool processing means removing:
    (A) the grease from the fibre
    (B) the dye from the wool
    (C) small bits of plant material caught in the fleece
    (D) the skin from the fleece

16. In wool processing, dyeing comes **after** sorting and picking burrs but **before** rolling into yarn. Natural fleece before dyeing is usually:
    (A) creamy white
    (B) bright red
    (C) jet black
    (D) deep blue

17. The very last of the six wool steps turns the cleaned, coloured fibres into:
    (A) a fleece
    (B) yarn ready for weaving
    (C) raw cocoons
    (D) sericin

18. A factory removes burrs **before** scouring the fleece. The most likely result is that:
    (A) the wool will be cleaner than usual
    (B) the grease and dirt make it hard to spot and remove the burrs, so burrs are missed
    (C) nothing changes, since order does not matter
    (D) the wool will be dyed automatically

19. The serious occupational disease that wool **sorters** are at risk of is:
    (A) the common cold
    (B) sorter's disease (anthrax)
    (C) malaria
    (D) tooth decay

20. Sorter's disease is caused by:
    (A) bacterial spores (Bacillus anthracis) present in raw fleece
    (B) a virus in the dye
    (C) eating wool
    (D) cold weather alone

21. A wool sorter is most likely to catch sorter's disease by:
    (A) drinking warm water used in scouring
    (B) inhaling dust and spores from the raw fleece
    (C) touching dyed yarn
    (D) wearing a finished sweater

22. Which step in wool processing carries the **highest** risk of sorter's disease?
    (A) rolling the finished yarn
    (B) wearing a woollen scarf
    (C) handling raw, unwashed fleece during sorting
    (D) buying wool from a shop

23. The buyer of a finished, scoured and dyed woollen sweater is at very low risk of sorter's disease because:
    (A) sweaters are made of cotton
    (B) the disease only affects sheep
    (C) the fleece has been cleaned and processed, removing the spores before the wool reaches the shop
    (D) the dye kills all bacteria in the body

24. A sensible safety measure in a modern wool factory is to:
    (A) make sorters work without masks to save cost
    (B) skip the scouring step
    (C) shear the sheep in winter
    (D) provide masks, gloves and good ventilation, and disinfect fleece before sorting

25. Two sheep breeds are compared. Merino has very few coarse "guard" hairs; Lincoln has many. Which breed gives the **finer, softer** wool?
    (A) Merino
    (B) Lincoln
    (C) both give equally soft wool
    (D) neither gives soft wool

26. A sheep's fleece contains two kinds of hair. The coarse outer hairs are the **guard hairs**, and the fine inner hairs form the:
    (A) cocoon
    (B) burrs
    (C) undercoat
    (D) sericin

27. The reason sorting is needed before spinning fine yarn is that:
    (A) all the fibres in a fleece are exactly the same
    (B) sorting adds colour to the wool
    (C) sorting removes the grease
    (D) a fleece mixes coarse and fine hairs of different lengths, which must be separated for the right quality

28. Why should a woollen sweater be washed in **cool** water with a mild detergent rather than hot water?
    (A) hot water has no effect on wool
    (B) hot water and rough washing make the fibres lock together and the sweater shrinks
    (C) cool water dyes the wool
    (D) wool dissolves in cold water

29. A child washes a pure-wool sweater in very hot water and scrubs it hard. The most likely result is that the sweater:
    (A) grows larger
    (B) changes into cotton
    (C) stays exactly the same size
    (D) shrinks and becomes matted

30. Sericulture is the term for the:
    (A) rearing of silkworms for silk
    (B) rearing of sheep for wool

    (D) dyeing of wool

    (A) honeybee

    (C) silk moth
    (D) housefly

    (A) egg into a larva

    (B) unwound from the softened cocoon

    (B) the wrong colour
