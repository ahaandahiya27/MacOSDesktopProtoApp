# Science — Chapter 12: Reproduction in Plants · Paper 2 (Advanced) Solutions

**1. (C)** The male reproductive part of a flower, taken as a whole, is the **stamen**. The pistil is the female part; sepals are the green protective parts; the ovule is a structure inside the ovary.

**2. (A)** A stamen has two parts: the slender stalk called the **filament** and the knob at its top called the **anther**, which makes pollen.

**3. (D)** The pistil runs **stigma → style → ovary** from top to bottom. The stigma catches pollen, the style is the tube, and the ovary at the base holds the ovules.

**4. (B)** Pollen grains are made inside the **anther**, the swollen tip of the stamen.

**5. (B)** After fertilisation the ovule becomes the **seed** and the surrounding ovary ripens into the **fruit**. This "ovule → seed, ovary → fruit" rule is the classic trap — keep them paired correctly.

**6. (D)** A flower carrying both stamens and a pistil is **bisexual** (e.g. mustard, rose). A flower with only one of the two is unisexual.

**7. (A)** **Papaya and watermelon** typically bear unisexual flowers (separate male and female flowers). Mustard, rose, China rose and pea are bisexual.

**8. (C)** Pollen moving from anther to stigma of the same flower (or another flower on the same plant) is **self-pollination**.

**9. (A)** Cross-pollination means pollen reaches the stigma of a flower on **a different plant of the same kind** — a different individual, but the same species.

**10. (C)** If male and female flowers sit on separate plants, the only sexual route is **cross-pollination**, because no single plant has both gamete types in one flower.

**11. (D)** Wind-pollinated flowers are usually **small, dull, with light pollen and feathery stigmas** — they spend nothing on attracting animals and everything on catching air-borne pollen.

**12. (B)** Bright petals, scent and nectar are all lures for **insects and other animals**, the pollinators that carry pollen for the plant.

**13. (D)** Wind carries pollen blindly, so most grains never reach a stigma; the flower compensates by making **huge amounts** so that at least some succeed.

**14. (B)** On a compatible stigma the pollen grain grows a **pollen tube** that pushes down through the style.

**15. (C)** The pollen tube's job is to **carry the male gamete down to the ovule** so fertilisation can happen.

**16. (A)** Fertilisation is the **fusion of the male gamete with the egg (female gamete) inside the ovule**, producing a zygote.

**17. (C)** The single cell formed right after the gametes fuse is the **zygote**, the first cell of the new plant.

**18. (A)** A pea pod is the fruit (ripened ovary); the peas inside are the **seeds** (ripened ovules).

**19. (B)** The hard mango stone is **the seed of the mango** (the ovule, hardened). The juicy part is the fleshy fruit around it.

**20. (D)** Each fertilised ovule becomes one seed, so an ovary with many ovules yields a fruit with **many seeds** (think of a papaya or watermelon).

**21. (A)** Reproduction from a single parent giving identical offspring is **asexual reproduction**.

**22. (D)** Asexual offspring are **clones — genetically identical to the single parent**, because no mixing of two parents' material takes place.

**23. (B)** Growing a new plant from a root, stem or leaf without seeds is **vegetative propagation**.

**24. (C)** A potato's "eyes" are buds on a swollen underground **stem**, so growing a plant from a potato piece is propagation by stem.

**25. (B)** *Bryophyllum* grows tiny plantlets in the notches of its **leaf** margin — propagation by leaf.

**26. (C)** Sweet potato and dahlia store food in swollen **roots** that can sprout new plants.

**27. (A)** To get a plant identical to a prized parent, take a **cutting** (vegetative propagation); seeds would mix in another parent's traits through pollination.

**28. (D)** Vegetative propagation makes clones, so it does **not** increase variety — that statement is the odd one out. Its real advantages are identical offspring, earlier flowering, and multiplying plants that set few seeds.

**29. (D)** In yeast a bulge swells on the parent cell and pinches off — this is **budding**.

**30. (A)** Budding in both yeast and *Hydra* shares the feature that **a bud grows out of the parent body and detaches as a new individual**; only one parent is involved.

**31. (C)** *Spirogyra* breaks into pieces that each regrow — **fragmentation**.

**32. (B)** Bread mould and ferns reproduce by **spores**, made in large numbers and able to survive hard times.

**33. (C)** Spores survive unfavourable conditions because they are **light and have a tough protective covering**, letting them wait and travel until conditions improve.

**34. (B)** Scattering seeds away from the parent is seed **dispersal**.

**35. (D)** Winged, lightweight seeds of drumstick and maple ride on **wind**.

**36. (A)** The coconut floats because of its air-trapping husk, so it is dispersed by **water**.

**37. (A)** Hooked, spiny seeds of *Xanthium* and *Urena* disperse by **clinging to the fur of animals**.

**38. (B)** When castor and balsam fruits dry and burst to fling seeds out, the agent is **the plant's own bursting — explosion**.

**39. (D)** A juicy, tasty fruit is bait: **animals eat the fruit and drop or pass the seeds** elsewhere.

**40. (C)** Dispersal helps the species by **reducing competition for water, minerals and sunlight, and by letting it colonise new places** instead of crowding under the parent.

**41. (D)** A seed "waking up" to grow into a seedling is **germination**.

**42. (C)** Lilies reproduce vegetatively through underground **bulbs**.

**43. (A)** The green, leaf-like parts enclosing a bud before it opens are the **sepals**.

**44. (B)** A money-plant cutting roots from its **stem node**, which produces both new roots and shoots.

**45. (B)** Sexual reproduction **involves the fusion of male and female gametes and produces variation**, whereas asexual reproduction needs one parent and makes clones.

**46. (D)** The correct order is **pollination → fertilisation → seed and fruit formation**: pollen must arrive, gametes must fuse, then the seed and fruit form.

**47. (C)** Seedless banana cannot be grown from seed, so use **vegetative propagation with suckers/offshoots** to make identical plants.

**48. (A)** If pollen never reaches a stigma, **pollination fails, so fertilisation and seed formation cannot happen** — the reproductive chain is broken at the first step.

**49. (A)** Each corn silk catches pollen and leads down to one developing grain, so a silk acts as **a stigma-and-style leading to an ovule**.

**50. (D)** Deliberately cross-pollinating two varieties to combine good traits is **hybridisation**.

**51. (C)** "All flowers self-pollinate" is wrong because **unisexual flowers lack one of the parts, so a single such flower cannot self-pollinate** — it has no internal partner.

**52. (B)** Because clones are genetically identical, **if one is susceptible to a new disease they all are**, so the whole crop can fall together.

**53. (C)** The coconut stays buoyant chiefly through its **air-trapping fibrous husk**, which works like a built-in float.

**54. (D)** A large feathery stigma **increases the surface area to trap airborne pollen**, a perfect match for wind pollination.

**55. (B)** With its anthers destroyed, a bisexual flower can still set seed only if **pollen from another flower reaches its stigma (cross-pollination)**.

**56. (A)** The correct contrast: **seeds form by sexual reproduction after fertilisation, while spores are an asexual route in ferns and fungi**.

**57. (B)** Strawberry's horizontal ground-running stems that root at intervals are called **runners**.

**58. (A)** A desert plant may favour asexual reproduction because it **can multiply quickly from one parent without depending on pollinators or seed set**.

**59. (C)** A grafted bud keeps its own identity, so the fruit tastes like **the sweet orange the bud came from**, not the root-stock.

**60. (D)** Tracing a seed back, the events run **pollination → fertilisation → the fertilised ovule → seed**: pollen arrives, gametes fuse, and only then does the ovule mature into a seed.
