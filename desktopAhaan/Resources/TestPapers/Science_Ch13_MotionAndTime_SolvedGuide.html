Types of Motion

Know the four words: Rectilinear = straight line · Circular = round a circle · Periodic = repeats after a fixed time · Oscillatory = to-and-fro (a special periodic motion). Every oscillatory motion is periodic, but not every periodic motion is to-and-fro.
Q1

Which is an example of periodic motion that is not oscillatory?

  • A A swinging pendulum
  • B A vibrating guitar string
  • C The Earth revolving around the Sun ✓
  • D A child on a swing
1

Pendulum, guitar string and swing all go to-and-fro → those are oscillatory.

2

The Earth repeats its path every year (periodic) but moves in a circle, not to-and-fro → periodic but not oscillatory → C.

Q20

A stone tied to a string and whirled in a circle shows:

  • A rectilinear
  • B circular motion ✓
  • C oscillatory
  • D random
1

A body moving along a circular path = circular motion → B.

Q39

Which statement about motion types is correct?

  • A every periodic motion is also oscillatory
  • B "periodic" and "oscillatory" mean the same
  • C no oscillatory motion is periodic
  • D every oscillatory motion is periodic, but not every periodic motion is oscillatory ✓
1

To-and-fro motion always repeats, so oscillatory ⊂ periodic. But circular revolution repeats without being to-and-fro → D.

💡

Picture it: "oscillatory" is a small box that sits inside the bigger box called "periodic."

Q45

A train moving on a long straight track exhibits:

  • A rectilinear motion ✓
  • B circular
  • C oscillatory
  • D random
1

Motion along a straight line = rectilinear → A.

Q51

A switched-on ceiling-fan blade and a swinging pendulum show, respectively:

  • A oscillatory and circular
  • B circular and oscillatory ✓
  • C both circular
  • D both oscillatory
1

Fan blade goes round and round → circular. Pendulum swings to-and-fro → oscillatory. In that order → B.

Measuring Time & Clocks

The SI unit of time is the second. A sundial needs sunlight; a water clock and sand clock work in the dark. An odometer measures total distance; a speedometer measures instant speed.
Q10

The SI unit of time is the:

  • A minute
  • B hour
  • C second ✓
  • D day
1

The internationally agreed (SI) unit of time is the second → C.

Q14

Which ancient device tells time using the Sun's shadow?

  • A sundial ✓
  • B water clock
  • C sand clock
  • D pendulum clock
1

A sundial reads time from the position of a shadow cast by the Sun → A.

Q19

Which timekeeper would fail on a cloudy night?

  • A water clock
  • B sand clock
  • C pendulum clock
  • D sundial ✓
1

No Sun = no shadow, so a sundial cannot work. The other three don't need light → D.

Q36

An odometer fitted in a vehicle measures:

  • A speed
  • B total distance travelled ✓
  • C time
  • D fuel level
1

Odo = road/distance. The odometer records the total distance a vehicle has travelled → B. (The speedometer shows speed.)

Q46

To find the average speed over a whole trip, the readings you need come from:

  • A the speedometer only
  • B the odometer and a clock ✓
  • C a clock only
  • D a sundial
1

Average speed = distance ÷ time. Distance comes from the odometer, time from a clock → B. (The speedometer only gives speed at one instant.)

3

The Pendulum

Time period = time for ONE complete swing = total time ÷ number of oscillations. A pendulum's period depends only on its length (and gravity) — never on the bob's mass. Longer → slower → bigger period → fewer swings.
Length is the only thing that changes a pendulum's swing rate. Mass makes no difference at all.
Q9

A pendulum completes 20 oscillations in 40 s. Its time period is:

  • A 2 s ✓
  • B 0.5 s
  • C 20 s
  • D 800 s
1

Period = time ÷ swings = 40 ÷ 20 = 2 s → A.

Q13

Increasing the length of a simple pendulum will:

  • A decrease its period
  • B leave it unchanged
  • C stop it swinging
  • D increase its period ✓
1

Longer pendulum swings more slowly, so each swing takes longer → period increases → D.

Q23

Two pendulums have the same length but different bob masses. Their periods are:

  • A equal ✓
  • B larger for heavier bob
  • C larger for lighter bob
  • D zero for heavier bob
1

Period depends on length, not mass. Same length → equal periods → A.

💡

Common myth: "heavier swings slower." Not true for a pendulum — mass cancels out.

Q26

A pendulum makes 30 oscillations in 60 s. Its string is lengthened so its period becomes 3 s. How many oscillations in 60 s now?

  • A 30
  • B 20 ✓
  • C 45
  • D 90
1

New period = 3 s per swing. Swings in 60 s = 60 ÷ 3 = 20 → B. (The old 30-in-60 info is a distractor.)

Q30

Pendulum X is longer than pendulum Y. In one minute, which makes more oscillations?

  • A X
  • B both equal
  • C depends on bob mass
  • D Y ✓
1

Shorter = faster swing = more swings per minute. Y is shorter → Y makes more → D.

Q43

A pendulum makes 30 complete oscillations in 45 s. How many in 3 minutes?

  • A 90
  • B 120 ✓
  • C 60
  • D 200
1

Period = 45 ÷ 30 = 1.5 s.

2

3 min = 180 s. Swings = 180 ÷ 1.5 = 120 → B.

💡

Always convert minutes to seconds first when the period is in seconds.

Q56

To make a pendulum clock run slow (lose time), you should:

  • A shorten the pendulum
  • B use a heavier bob
  • C lengthen the pendulum ✓
  • D use a lighter bob
1

Longer pendulum → slower swings → clock ticks fewer times each second → runs slow → C. (Mass changes nothing.)

Calculating Speed, Distance & Time

The one triangle that runs this whole chapter: Speed = Distance ÷ Time, so Distance = Speed × Time and Time = Distance ÷ Speed. Before plugging in, make the units agree (metres+seconds, OR km+hours).
Cover the one you want: cover D → S×T; cover S → D/T; cover T → D/S.
Q3

A snail crawls 6 m in 2 minutes. Its speed is:

  • A 0.05 m/s ✓
  • B 0.5 m/s
  • C 3 m/s
  • D 0.1 m/s
1

The answer is in m/s, so turn 2 min into seconds: 2 × 60 = 120 s.

2

Speed = 6 ÷ 120 = 0.05 m/s → A.

⚠️

Trap C (3 m/s) comes from dividing by 2 instead of 120. Convert minutes to seconds first!

Q11

A train moves at 90 km/h. How far in 10 seconds?

  • A 900 m
  • B 150 m
  • C 25 m
  • D 250 m ✓
1

Time is in seconds, so convert speed: 90 × 5/18 = 25 m/s.

2

Distance = 25 × 10 = 250 m → D.

Q17

A car travels at 54 km/h for 20 minutes. The distance is:

  • A 27 km
  • B 1080 km
  • C 18 km ✓
  • D 9 km
1

Keep km, so turn 20 min into hours: 20/60 = 1/3 h.

2

Distance = 54 × 1/3 = 18 km → C.

Q18

How long does a 240 km journey take at a steady 80 km/h?

  • A 3 h ✓
  • B 320 h
  • C 2 h
  • D 0.33 h
1

Time = distance ÷ speed = 240 ÷ 80 = 3 h → A.

A sprinter runs 100 m in 12.5 s. The average speed is:

  • A 12.5 m/s
  • B 8 m/s ✓
  • C 80 m/s
  • D 0.125 m/s

Speed = 100 ÷ 12.5 = 8 m/s → B.

Q32

A car moves at a steady 54 km/h. How many metres in 2 minutes?

  • A 108 m

  • C 1800 m ✓
  • D 900 m
1

Answer in metres → convert: 54 × 5/18 = 15 m/s; 2 min = 120 s.

2

Distance = 15 × 120 = 1800 m → C.

Q42

A cheetah runs at a steady 30 m/s. How long to cover 540 m, and what is this speed in km/h?

  • A 18 s and 30 km/h
  • B 9 s and 108 km/h
  • C 18 s and 108 km/h ✓
  • D 18 s and 8.3 km/h

Time = 540 ÷ 30 = 18 s.

Q53 · focus

A swimmer covers 1.5 km in 25 minutes. Her average speed in m/s is:

  • A 60 m/s
  • B 1.0 m/s ✓ Correct
  • C 0.06 m/s

Unit Conversion & Comparing Speeds

The two magic numbers: km/h → m/s : × 5/18 and m/s → km/h : × 18/5 (= × 3.6). To compare or order speeds, first put them ALL in the same unit — bigger-looking numbers can be slower in disguise.
Q4

Convert 72 km/h into m/s.

  • A 25 m/s
  • B 200 m/s
  • C 18 m/s
  • D 20 m/s ✓
1

72 × 5/18 = 4 × 5 = 20 m/s → D.

Q5

Convert 15 m/s into km/h.

  • A 45 km/h
  • B 54 km/h ✓
  • C 540 km/h
  • D 4.17 km/h
1

15 × 3.6 = 54 km/h → B.

⚠️

Trap D comes from dividing by 3.6 (wrong direction). Going m/s → km/h the number gets bigger, so you multiply.

Q16

Object X moves at 36 km/h; object Y at 12 m/s. Which is faster?

  • A X
  • B both equal
  • C Y ✓
  • D cannot compare
1

Convert X to m/s: 36 × 5/18 = 10 m/s.

2

Y = 12 m/s > 10 m/s, so Y is faster → C.

Q27

Three runners: P at 5 m/s, Q at 21.6 km/h, R covers 300 m in 1 minute. Who is fastest?

  • A P
  • B R
  • C Q ✓
  • D all equal
1

Put all in m/s. P = 5 m/s.

2

Q = 21.6 ÷ 3.6 = 6 m/s.

3

R = 300 ÷ 60 = 5 m/s.

4

Biggest is Q (6 m/s) → C.

Q29 · focus

Arrange in increasing order: 10 m/s, 30 km/h, 720 m/min.

  • A 30 km/h < 10 m/s < 720 m/min ✓ Correct
  • B 10 m/s < 30 km/h < 720 m/min
  • C 720 m/min < 10 m/s < 30 km/h ✗ Your answer
  • D all equal
Once all in km/h: 30 < 36 < 43.2. The "biggest number" (720) is actually the fastest.
1

10 m/s = 10 × 3.6 = 36 km/h.

2

30 km/h stays 30 km/h.

3

720 m/min = 720 × 60 = 43200 m/h = 43.2 km/h.

4

Order: 30 < 36 < 43.2 → A.

⚠️

What tripped you: comparing raw numbers across different units. Convert to one unit first — every single time.

Q49

A car at 90 km/h and a bike at 22 m/s travel toward each other. Which is faster, and by how much?

  • A the bike, by 3 m/s
  • B equally fast
  • C the car, by 3 m/s ✓
  • D the car, by 68 m/s
1

Car: 90 × 5/18 = 25 m/s.

2

25 − 22 = 3, so the car is faster by 3 m/s → C.

Q59

Which is the slowest speed?

  • A 8 m/s ✓
  • B 36 km/h
  • C 600 m/min
  • D 0.5 km/min
1

Convert all to m/s: A = 8.

2

B: 36 ÷ 3.6 = 10 m/s. C: 600 ÷ 60 = 10 m/s. D: 500 m ÷ 60 s ≈ 8.33 m/s.

3

Smallest is 8 m/s → A.

6

Uniform vs Non-uniform Motion

Uniform motion = equal distances in equal times (speed stays the same). Non-uniform = speed changes. With a table, check the jump in distance each second: equal jumps = uniform; growing jumps = speeding up; shrinking jumps = slowing down; no jump = at rest.
Q2

A bus covers the first 100 m in 5 s and the next 100 m in 4 s. Its motion is:

  • A uniform, because distances are equal
  • B non-uniform, because equal distances take unequal times ✓
  • C uniform, because straight line
  • D at rest
1

Speeds: 100/5 = 20 m/s then 100/4 = 25 m/s. Speed changed → non-uniform → B.

⚠️

"Equal distances" or "straight line" do not mean uniform. Only equal distances in equal times does.

Q24

Time (s): 0,1,2,3,4 → Distance (m): 0,5,10,15,20. The motion is:

  • A 20 m/s, uniform
  • B 5 m/s, non-uniform
  • C speeding up
  • D 5 m/s, uniform ✓
1

Distance rises by 5 m every second (5,10,15,20) → equal jumps → uniform, speed 5 m/s → D.

Q25

Time (s): 0,1,2,3,4 → Distance (m): 0,10,20,20,20. Between t = 2 s and t = 4 s the object is:

  • A moving at 10 m/s
  • B moving backward
  • C speeding up
  • D at rest ✓
1

From t=2 to t=4 the distance stays at 20 m (no change) → the object is at rest → D.

Q33

A ball rolling up a slope covers 5 m in the 1st second, 3 m in the 2nd, 1 m in the 3rd. Its motion is:

  • A uniform
  • B non-uniform, speeding up
  • C non-uniform, slowing down ✓
  • D at rest
1

Distance each second shrinks: 5, 3, 1 → covering less each second → slowing down → C. (Just what you'd expect rolling uphill.)

Q47

Time (s): 0,2,4,6 → Distance (m): 0,8,16,24. The motion is:

  • A non-uniform, 4 m/s
  • B uniform, 8 m/s
  • C uniform, 4 m/s ✓
  • D uniform, 2 m/s
1

Distance rises 8 m every 2 s → speed = 8 ÷ 2 = 4 m/s, steady → uniform → C.

⚠️

Watch the time step! Here time jumps by 2 s, not 1 s, so divide the 8 m by 2.

Q50

Time (s): 0,1,2,3 → Distance (m): 0,2,8,18. The motion is:

  • A non-uniform, speeding up ✓
  • B uniform, 6 m/s
  • C uniform, 2 m/s
  • D at rest
1

Distance jumps each second are 2, 6, 10 m — getting bigger → speeding up → A.

7

Average Speed

The most-trapped idea in the whole chapter. Average speed = total distance ÷ total time — never the average of the two speeds. The shortcut (add and halve) works only when the two TIMES are equal. The test loves giving equal DISTANCES to make it fail. Remember to include any rest/halt time in the total time.
Q6 · focus

A car goes A→B at 60 km/h and returns B→A at 40 km/h. Average speed for the whole trip?

  • A 50 km/h ✗ Your answer
  • B 100 km/h
  • C 24 km/h
  • D 48 km/h ✓ Correct
Equal distance, but the slow half eats more time — so the average drops below 50.
1

Let each way be 240 km. Going = 240/60 = 4 h; back = 240/40 = 6 h.

2

Total distance = 480 km; total time = 10 h.

3

Average = 480 ÷ 10 = 48 km/h → D. (Algebra shortcut: 2d ÷ (d/60 + d/40), the d cancels, = 48.)

⚠️

Your trap: averaging 60 and 40 to 50. The slow part lasts longer, so the true average is pulled below the middle.

Q7 · focus

Anya drives 60 km at a steady 40 km/h. Ravi drives the first 30 km at 30 km/h and the last 30 km at 60 km/h. Who arrives first?

  • A Anya
  • B Ravi ✗ Your answer
  • C same time ✓ Correct
  • D cannot tell
1

Anya: 60/40 = 1.5 h.

2

Ravi: 30/30 + 30/60 = 1 + 0.5 = 1.5 h. Equal → C.

⚠️

Your trap: "Ravi hits 60, so he wins." But his slow first half cancels that gain. Check the actual time, not the top speed.

Q12

A cyclist rides 30 km in 2 h, rests 1 h, then rides 20 km in 1 h. Average speed for the whole journey?

  • A 16.7 km/h
  • B 12.5 km/h ✓
  • C 25 km/h
  • D 10 km/h
1

Total distance = 30 + 20 = 50 km.

2

Total time INCLUDES the rest: 2 + 1 + 1 = 4 h.

3

Average = 50 ÷ 4 = 12.5 km/h → B.

⚠️

Trap 16.7 forgets the rest hour. Resting still counts as time on the trip!

Q22

A car travels at 40 km/h for 2 h, then 60 km/h for 2 h. Average speed?

  • A 48 km/h
  • B 100 km/h
  • C 50 km/h ✓
  • D 52 km/h
1

Here the TIMES are equal (2 h each), so the shortcut is allowed.

2

Distance = 40×2 + 60×2 = 200 km; time = 4 h; average = 50 km/h → C.

💡

Compare with Q6: there the distances were equal (shortcut fails). Here the times are equal (shortcut works). Always check which one is equal.

Q28 · focus

A car covers 360 km: first 180 km at 90 km/h, next 180 km at 60 km/h. Average speed?

  • A 72 km/h ✓ Correct
  • B 75 km/h ✗ Your answer
  • C 70 km/h
  • D 80 km/h
1

Times: 180/90 = 2 h and 180/60 = 3 h.

2

Average = 360 ÷ 5 = 72 km/h → A.

⚠️

Your trap: (90+60)/2 = 75. Equal distances → must use total ÷ total. Answer sits below the middle (72).

Q38

A train runs 120 km in 2 h, halts 20 min, then runs 90 km in 1 h 40 min. Average speed?

  • A 52.5 km/h ✓
  • B 60 km/h
  • C 70 km/h
  • D 63 km/h
1

Total distance = 120 + 90 = 210 km.

2

Total time = 2 h + 20 min + 1 h 40 min = 4 h (20 min + 1 h 40 min = 2 h).

3

Average = 210 ÷ 4 = 52.5 km/h → A.

⚠️

Trap 70 ignores the halt. Add every minute, including the stop.

Q44

A car travels 60 km in 1 h, stops 30 min, then another 60 km in 1 h. Average speed?

  • A 48 km/h ✓
  • B 60 km/h
  • C 40 km/h
  • D 50 km/h
1

Distance = 120 km; time = 1 + 0.5 + 1 = 2.5 h.

2

Average = 120 ÷ 2.5 = 48 km/h → A.

Q55 · focus

Three legs: 20 km at 40 km/h, 30 km at 60 km/h, 10 km at 20 km/h. Average speed?

  • A 40 km/h ✓ Correct
  • B 42 km/h
  • C 45 km/h ✗ Your answer
  • D 60 km/h
1

Times: 20/40 = 0.5, 30/60 = 0.5, 10/20 = 0.5 h.

2

Total distance = 60 km; total time = 1.5 h.

3

Average = 60 ÷ 1.5 = 40 km/h → A.

⚠️

Your trap: a guessed shortcut gave 45. Always compute each time and add — never average the speeds.

8

Distance–Time Graphs

Two ideas unlock every graph: the steepness (slope) is the speed, and the height is the distance from start. Steeper = faster · flat = stopped · steepening curve = speeding up · flattening curve = slowing down · line coming back down = returning toward start.
Q8

A cyclist's graph: 0–10 s slanted line, 10–20 s horizontal line, 20–30 s steeper slanted line. The cyclist:

  • A moves, then stops, then moves faster ✓
  • B speeds up the whole time
  • C moves, slows, stops
  • D stays at rest
Slant = moving · flat = stopped · steeper slant = moving faster.
1

Slanted = moving, horizontal = at rest, steeper slant = moving again but faster → A.

Q15

P and Q are straight lines from the origin. P is steeper than Q. Which is faster?

  • A Q
  • B P ✓
  • C both equal
  • D cannot decide
1

Slope = speed. Steeper line = greater speed → P → B.

Q21

A distance–time curve that bends upward (slope keeps increasing) shows:

  • A uniform motion
  • B non-uniform motion with increasing speed ✓
  • C rest
  • D backward motion
The curve gets steeper → speed is increasing → speeding up.
1

Slope keeps growing → speed keeps growing → non-uniform, speeding up → B.

Q34 · focus

Two cars start from the same point. Their graph lines cross at t = 3 s. At that instant the two cars:

  • A have the same speed
  • B both stop
  • C reverse direction
  • D are at the same distance from the start ✓ Correct
Lines meet → same height → same distance from start. Slopes differ → speeds differ.
1

Height = distance from start. Where lines cross, both heights match → same distance from start → D.

2

Their slopes are clearly different, so the speeds are not equal.

⚠️

Trap: "crossing = same speed." No — crossing = same place. Two cars can be side by side with one zooming and one crawling.

Q37 · focus

A curve that becomes less steep (flattens out) means the object is:

  • A speeding up
  • B slowing down ✓ Correct
  • C at rest
  • D constant speed
Still rising (still moving), but climbing less each second → slowing down.
1

Line still goes up → still moving. But each second it rises less → speed dropping → B.

💡

"Flattening" ≠ stopped. A fully horizontal line = stopped; a curve becoming flatter = still moving, just slower.

Q40

A line passes through (0 s, 0 m) and (4 s, 20 m). The speed is:

  • A 4 m/s
  • B 20 m/s
  • C 5 m/s ✓
  • D 80 m/s
1

Slope = (20 − 0) ÷ (4 − 0) = 5 m/s → C.

Q41 · focus

A line passes through (2 s, 6 m) and (8 s, 30 m). The speed is:

  • A 5 m/s
  • B 6 m/s
  • C 3 m/s ✗ Your answer
  • D 4 m/s ✓ Correct
Subtract both: rise ÷ run = (30−6) ÷ (8−2).
1

Rise = 30 − 6 = 24 m; run = 8 − 2 = 6 s.

2

Speed = 24 ÷ 6 = 4 m/s → D.

⚠️

Your trap: doing 6/2 or 30/8. Because the line doesn't start at zero, you must subtract first values from second on both axes.

Q52 · focus

A line (distance from start) rises and then comes back down to the time axis. The object:

  • A sped up continuously
  • B stopped permanently ✗ Your answer
  • C moved in a circle
  • D returned to its starting point ✓ Correct
Distance from start returns to 0 → it's back where it began.
1

Back down to the axis means distance from start = 0 → it returned to the start → D.

⚠️

Your trap: "going down = stopped." A flat line = stopped. A line going down = getting closer to start (coming home).

Q57

A distance–time line becomes steeper as time goes on. The object is:

  • A slowing down
  • B at rest
  • C constant speed
  • D speeding up ✓
1

Steeper line = bigger slope = greater speed → speeding up → D.

9

Comparing Journeys

To compare two travellers, work out each one's speed or time separately, then compare. A head start or a high top speed can be misleading — only the actual numbers decide.
Q35

Two cars cover the same distance: A takes 2 h, B takes 3 h. Which is faster, and in what ratio (A : B)?

  • A A is faster, 3 : 2 ✓
  • B B faster, 2 : 3
  • C A faster, 2 : 3
  • D equally fast
1

Same distance D: speed_A = D/2, speed_B = D/3. Less time → faster, so A is faster.

2

Ratio A:B = (1/2) : (1/3) = 3 : 2 → A.

💡

For the same distance, speed and time are flipped: take less time → more speed.

Q48

Car P covers 60 km in 1.5 h; car Q covers 80 km in 2 h. Then:

  • A P is faster
  • B Q is faster
  • C cannot decide
  • D both have equal speed ✓
1

P = 60 ÷ 1.5 = 40 km/h; Q = 80 ÷ 2 = 40 km/h. Equal → D.

Q58

A town is 100 km away. A bus leaves at 9:00 at 50 km/h; a car leaves at 9:30 at 100 km/h, both non-stop. Who arrives first?

  • A the bus
  • B same time
  • C the car ✓
  • D cannot decide
1

Bus: 100 ÷ 50 = 2 h from 9:00 → arrives 11:00.

2

Car: 100 ÷ 100 = 1 h from 9:30 → arrives 10:30.

3

10:30 is earlier → the car arrives first → C.

💡

The bus's 30-min head start is smaller than the car's speed advantage. Compute both arrival clock times and compare.

Q60

Object A moves 100 m in 4 s; object B moves 150 m in 5 s. Which is correct?

  • A A faster by 5 m/s
  • B B is faster by 5 m/s ✓
  • C equal speed
  • D B faster by 50 m/s
1

A = 100 ÷ 4 = 25 m/s; B = 150 ÷ 5 = 30 m/s.

2

B − A = 30 − 25 = 5, so B is faster by 5 m/s → B.

The 6 Golden Rules ⭐

Master these and this whole chapter becomes easy.

①
Speed = Distance ÷ Time.Rearrange for the other two. This single triangle powers almost every question.
②
Match units before you calculate.×5/18 for km/h→m/s, ×3.6 for m/s→km/h. Decide units from what the answer asks for.
③
Average speed = total distance ÷ total time.Never average the two speeds. Include rest/halt time. The shortcut only works when the TIMES are equal.
④
On a graph: slope = speed, height = distance.Steeper = faster, flat = stopped, line coming down = returning to start.
⑤
Slope = rise ÷ run — always subtract.(second − first) on both axes. Don't just divide the final numbers.
⑥
A pendulum's period depends on length only.Longer = slower = fewer swings. Mass never matters. Period = total time ÷ swings.
