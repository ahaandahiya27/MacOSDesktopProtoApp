**Marking scheme:** **+4** for each correct answer · **−1** for each wrong answer · **0** if unattempted.

**Instructions:** Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. Constructions are described in words — picture the compass arcs and straightedge lines as you read. For tiling questions, the one rule that decides everything is that the angles meeting at a point must sum to **exactly 360°**; watch the traps that a polygon tiles whenever 360° is "close", that you can trisect like you bisect, and that a construction is "just a careful drawing". Do not write on this paper — mark answers on your answer sheet.

1. In a construction, a "straightedge" is used to:
   (A) measure exact lengths in centimetres
   (B) draw straight lines only (it carries no markings)
   (C) measure angles like a protractor
   (D) swing arcs of equal radius

2. To construct the perpendicular bisector of a segment XY, you draw arcs from X and from Y. For the construction to work, those arcs must be drawn with:
   (A) any two different radii
   (B) a radius exactly equal to half of XY
   (C) a radius equal to the full length XY only
   (D) the same radius from both X and Y, that radius being more than half of XY

3. Every point lying on the perpendicular bisector of segment AB is:

4. The perpendicular bisector construction crosses the segment XY at its midpoint AND meets it at an angle of:

5. Why does the equal-arc construction land exactly on the midpoint of XY, with no measuring?
