Worked Solutions

1. (C) Multiply step by step. 1.2 × 0.15: digits 12 × 15 = 180; one decimal place plus two decimal places makes three, so 0.180 = 0.18. Then 0.18 × 5 = 0.90 = 0.9. Reading the digits 9 with no decimal places, or counting only two places to get 0.09, both misplace the point; 0.18 is only the first product before multiplying by 5.

2. (A) Scale BOTH numbers by 100 so the divisor becomes a whole number: 7.2 × 100 = 720 and 0.08 × 100 = 8. Then 720 ÷ 8 = 90. If you scale only the divisor (8) but leave 7.2, you get 0.9; scaling only 7.2 to 720 against 0.08 mishandled gives 9 or 900. Scaling both by the same power of ten is the rule.

3. (A) 0.46 × 1000 shifts the point three places right: 460. Then ÷ 100 shifts it two places left: 4.60 = 4.6. Net effect is shifting one place right, which also gives 4.6. Stopping at 460 forgets the division; leaving 0.46 cancels the shifts wrongly; 46 shifts only one place for the multiply.

4. (D) Estimate: about 5 × 3 = 15, so the answer is near 15. Exactly: 49 × 31 = 1519; one decimal place in each factor makes two, so 15.19. The estimate rules out 1.519 (near 1.5) and 151.9 (near 150). 15.9 comes from a careless digit multiplication, not 49 × 31.

5. (D) 0.375 = 375/1000. Divide top and bottom by 125: 375 ÷ 125 = 3 and 1000 ÷ 125 = 8, giving 3/8. The value 375/100 misreads three decimal places as two; 3/80 over-divides the denominator; 37/100 drops a digit.

6. (B) Moving from 0.0042 to 42 shifts the decimal point four places to the right (0.0042 → 0.042 → 0.42 → 4.2 → 42). Four places right means × 10000. Three places (× 1000) gives 4.2; two places (× 100) gives 0.42; five places (× 100000) gives 420.

7. (D) Take 2.5 × 0.4 first: digits 25 × 4 = 100, one decimal place plus one decimal place is two places, so 1.00 = 1. Then 1 × 0.3 = 0.3. Reading 0.12 stops after a wrong pairing; 3 and 0.03 misplace the point around the value 1 × 0.3.

8. (D) Scale both numbers by 1000 to clear the divisor: 0.144 × 1000 = 144 and 0.012 × 1000 = 12. Then 144 ÷ 12 = 12. Scaling only one number, or stopping the point shift early, produces 1.2, 120, or 0.12.

9. (C) Dividing by 1000 shifts the decimal point three places to the left: 56.7 → 5.67 → 0.567 → 0.0567. Only two places gives 0.567; four places gives 0.00567; multiplying instead of dividing gives 56700.

10. (C) Estimate: 0.6 ÷ 0.3 = 2, so the answer is near 2. Scale both by 10: 6.12 ÷ 3 = 2.04. The estimate near 2 rejects 20.4 and 0.204. 2.4 comes from dividing 0.6 by 0.3 carelessly while ignoring the 12 thousandths.

11. (B) 7 ÷ 8 = 0.875 (8 into 70 is 8 remainder 6; 60 gives 7 remainder 4; 40 gives 5). The value 0.78 misreads the long division; 0.0875 shifts the point one place too far left; 1.142 is 8 ÷ 7, the reciprocal direction.

12. (B) After the point the places are tenths, hundredths, thousandths. Here 7 is tenths, 0 is hundredths, 6 is thousandths, so 6 means 0.006. Calling it 0.06 or 0.6 counts too few places past the point; 6 ones ignores the decimal point entirely.

13. (D) Digits: 125 × 16 = 2000. Factors have two and two decimal places, four in all, so place the point four places: 0.2000 = 0.2. Reading 2 ignores the point; counting too few or too many places gives 0.02 or 0.002. (Trailing zeros in 2000 are dropped after placing the point.)

14. (B) Scale both by 1000: 9 × 1000 = 9000 and 0.036 × 1000 = 36. Then 9000 ÷ 36 = 250. Scaling only the divisor, or shifting the point one place wrong, gives 25, 2500, or 2.5.

15. (A) Multiplying by 10000 shifts the point four places right: 0.0035 → 0.035 → 0.35 → 3.5 → 35. Three places gives 3.5; five would give 350; two gives 0.35.

16. (D) Estimate: 20 × 0.5 = 10, so the answer is near 10. Exactly: 198 × 51 = 10098; one plus two decimal places make three, so 10.098. The estimate near 10 rejects 100.98 and 1.0098. 10.98 comes from a slip in the digit product.

17. (A) 0.04 = 4/100 = 1/25 (divide top and bottom by 4). The value 1/4 = 0.25, far too big; 4/10 = 0.4, ten times too big; 1/250 = 0.004, ten times too small.

18. (A) 2.4 × 3.5: digits 24 × 35 = 840; one plus one decimal place makes two, so 8.40 = 8.4. 24 × 3.5 = 84 (ten times bigger), 0.24 × 3.5 = 0.84 (ten times smaller), 2.4 × 35 = 84. Only the first lands the point to give 8.4.

19. (B) (0.3)³ = 0.3 × 0.3 × 0.3. 0.3 × 0.3 = 0.09 (digits 9, two decimal places). Then 0.09 × 0.3 = 0.027 (digits 27, three decimal places). 0.27 counts only two places; 0.0027 counts four; 0.9 is 0.3 × 3, not the cube.

20. (C) Scale both by 1000: 4.8 × 1000 = 4800 and 0.015 × 1000 = 15. Then 4800 ÷ 15 = 320. Scaling only one number, or stopping the point shift early, gives 32, 3200, or 3.2.

21. (B) 0.05 × 1000 shifts the point three places right: 50. Then ÷ 10 shifts one place left: 5.0 = 5. Net shift is two places right, also 5. Stopping at 50 forgets the divide; 0.5 over-divides; 500 forgets the divide and over-multiplies.

22. (B) Estimate: 36 ÷ 0.6 = 60, so the answer is near 60. Scale both by 10: 350.4 ÷ 6 = 58.4. The estimate near 60 rejects 5.84, 584 and 5840, which all misplace the point. So 58.4 is correct.

23. (B) 9/200: multiply top and bottom by 5 to get 45/1000 = 0.045. The value 0.45 = 9/20, ten times too big; 0.0045 = 9/2000, ten times too small; 4.5 = 9/2.

24. (A) The digit 2 sits in the tenths place, so it stands for 0.2; replacing it with 7 makes that place 0.7. The change is 0.7 − 0.2 = 0.5. Treating tenths as ones gives 5; as hundredths gives 0.05; 0.7 ignores the value already there.

25. (D) Cost = 48.50 × 1.5 = 72.75 (digits 485 × 15 = 7275, two decimal places). Change = 100 − 72.75 = 27.25. Giving ₹72.75 reports the cost, not the change; ₹2.725 misplaces the point; ₹51.50 is 100 − 48.50, ignoring the 1.5 kg.

26. (D) 100 ÷ 12.50: scale both by 10 to get 1000 ÷ 125 = 8 exactly. So 8 pens cost 8 × 12.50 = 100, leaving ₹0. Buying 7 leaves ₹12.50 (one more pen affordable); 80 pens misplaces the point in the division.

27. (A) Total cut = 0.8 + 0.65 + 0.45 = 1.9 m. Remaining = 2.4 − 1.9 = 0.5 m. Reporting 1.9 gives the cut length, not what remains; 0.05 misplaces the point; 0.55 comes from a subtraction slip.

28. (C) 25.5 ÷ 0.85: scale both by 100 to get 2550 ÷ 85 = 30. Scaling only one number, or shifting the point wrong, gives 3 or 300. The value 21.675 is 25.5 × 0.85 (multiplying instead of dividing).

29. (B) Do the bracket first: 2.5 + 1.5 = 4.0. Then 4.0 × 0.6 = 2.4. Skipping the bracket or misplacing the point gives 24 or 0.24; 4.6 mistakenly multiplies only the 1.5 by 0.6 and adds.

30. (B) Division first: 0.6 ÷ 0.4, scale both by 10 to get 6 ÷ 4 = 1.5. Then 1.5 + 0.5 = 2. Adding before dividing gives 1.1; misplacing the quotient gives 0.65 or 11.

31. (A) 0.25 × 0.25 = 0.0625 (digits 625, four decimal places). Then 0.0625 × 16 = 1.0 = 1. Misplacing the point along the way gives 0.1, 10, or 0.01.

32. (D) Scale both by 1000: 1.25 × 1000 = 1250 and 0.005 × 1000 = 5. Then 1250 ÷ 5 = 250. Scaling only one number, or stopping the point shift early, gives 25, 2500, or 2.5.
