Set 1 · Q1–Q10

Q1

Although about three-quarters of Earth's surface is covered by water, the fraction that is fresh water available for human use is:

  • Amore than half
  • Babout one-quarter
  • Ca very tiny fraction✓
  • Dexactly zero

Working

✓

Earth looks blue, but usable fresh water is only **a very tiny fraction** of all its water — most is salty ocean or locked in ice.

Q2

Most of Earth's water is found in the:

  • Aoceans, as salt water✓
  • Brivers and lakes
  • Catmosphere as vapour
  • Dbodies of living things

Working

✓

The greatest share of Earth's water sits in the **oceans as salt water**, which we cannot drink or use for most crops without treatment.

Q3

The largest store of Earth's **fresh** water is locked up in:

  • Arivers
  • Bclouds
  • Cponds
  • Dice caps and glaciers✓

Working

✓

Of the fresh water, the largest part is frozen in **ice caps and glaciers**, far from where most people live.

Q4

The continuous movement of water between the ground, water bodies, the atmosphere and back is called the:

  • Anitrogen cycle
  • Bwater cycle✓
  • Ccarbon cycle
  • Drock cycle

Working

✓

The endless journey of water from ground to sky and back is the **water cycle**.

Q5

The change of liquid water into water vapour is called:

  • Acondensation
  • Bevaporation✓
  • Cprecipitation
  • Dinfiltration

Working

✓

Liquid water turning into water vapour is **evaporation**.

Q6

  • Aevaporation
  • Bmelting
  • Cfreezing
  • Dcondensation✓

Working

✓

Q7

  • Aevaporation of surface water✓
  • Bcondensation of clouds
  • Cthe freezing of glaciers
  • Dthe digging of wells

Working

✓

The Sun powers the water cycle chiefly by driving the **evaporation of surface water** into vapour.

Q8

  • Arespiration
  • Bgermination
  • Ctranspiration✓
  • Dpollination

✓

Plants release water vapour from their leaves by **transpiration**, adding moisture to the air.

  • Dtree line

✓

The top of the saturated underground zone is the **water table**.

Q10

  • Aatmosphere

  • Diceberg

Q13

✓

Pumping faster than rain can recharge means the water table will **fall lower and lower** over the years.

Q14

✓

The dry-season failure shows the **water table drops in the dry season and rises after the rains recharge it** — the pump only reaches water when the table is high enough.

Q15

✓

Letting rain soak in through **rainwater-harvesting structures** is the way to help a falling table recover.

Q16

Concrete and tar **block rainwater from soaking in to recharge the aquifer**, so the table falls.
