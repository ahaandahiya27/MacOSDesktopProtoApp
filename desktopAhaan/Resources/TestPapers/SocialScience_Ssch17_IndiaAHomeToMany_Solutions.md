## Worked Solutions

**1. (A)** Vasudhaiva kutumbakam means 'the whole world is one family', the chapter's core value. 'A guest is like God' is atithi devo bhava; the others are not the phrase's meaning.

**2. (A)** Atithi devo bhava ('a guest is like God') expresses hospitality. It is not about excluding foreigners, trade, or polytheism.

**3. (A)** India has long been home to many communities from around the world. It was not closed, single-community, or refugee-ruled.

**4. (D)** Communities kept their traditions while integrating into Indian society - identity-preserving integration. They were not forced to abandon traditions, kept apart, or made rulers.

**5. (B)** Vasudhaiva kutumbakam frames diversity as a strength and an ideal of acceptance. It is not a weakness, threat, or trivial matter.

**6. (B)** Newcomers came either for refuge (fleeing persecution) or opportunity (trade). It was not trade-only, conquest-only, or study-only.

**7. (D)** Many (Jews, Syriac Christians, Parsis, Baha'is) fled persecution, and the Siddis were brought enslaved - not all came freely to trade.

**8. (C)** A refugee flees danger with little choice, unlike those who come freely seeking opportunity. Both are newcomers, and refugees can keep traditions.

**9. (C)** The two paths are 'refuge' (fleeing persecution) and 'opportunity' (seeking trade). They are not conquest/trade, war/peace, or land/sea.

**10. (D)** Arab merchants came for opportunity (trade); Parsis came for refuge (fleeing persecution). The order is opportunity then refuge, not the reverse, and neither came to conquer.

**11. (D)** India's Jewish communities include the Bene Israel and Cochin Jews. The Mappila are Kerala Muslims, the Siddis African-origin, and the others are different communities.

**12. (C)** A synagogue (Greek synagoge, 'a gathering together') is a Jewish place of worship. A fire temple is Zoroastrian, a mosque Muslim, and a church Christian.

**13. (B)** Keeping synagogues and faith while joining Indian society shows identity-preserving integration. It was not loss of traditions, separation, or newcomer-rule.

**14. (A)** The Jews sought refuge from persecution and kept their traditions in India. They did not come to conquer, rule, or only study.

**15. (C)** The Syriac Christians are an ancient community of Kerala. Gujarat is linked to the Parsis, Tibet to Tibetan refugees, and Armenia to the Armenians.

**16. (B)** The Syriac Christians are among the oldest Christian communities and kept their traditions in India. They are not modern, the enslaved Siddis, or Zoroastrians.

**17. (A)** The Syriac Christians preserved their faith while joining Indian society. They did not lose their religion, refuse to settle, or conquer Kerala.

**18. (C)** The Parsis follow Zoroastrianism. Judaism is the Jews', the Baha'i faith the Baha'is', and Tibetan Buddhism the Tibetans'.

**19. (B)** The Parsis settled mainly in Gujarat. Kerala is linked to the Syriac Christians, Tibet to Tibetan refugees, and Armenia to the Armenians.

**20. (A)** Zoroastrianism was founded by the prophet Zarathushtra. Ahura Mazda is its one God (not its human founder), and a Mappila merchant is unrelated.

**21. (D)** Zoroastrianism worships one God, Ahura Mazda, and stresses choosing good over evil. It is not polytheist, godless, or Buddhist.

**22. (A)** Zoroastrianism is one of the world's oldest religions. It is not the newest, a branch of Judaism, or a modern Indian invention.

**23. (D)** The Parsis fled persecution and preserved their fire temples and faith in India. They did not come to conquer, only to trade, or to rule.

**24. (C)** Islam first came peacefully via Arab merchants on the west coast from the 7th century, long before the later invasions. It did reach India, not only recently, and not only by conquest.

**25. (D)** Arab merchants built India's oldest mosque on the west coast. A synagogue is Jewish, a fire temple Zoroastrian, and a monastery Tibetan Buddhist.

**26. (B)** Arab merchants came seeking opportunity through trade, not refuge, conquest, or as enslaved people.

**27. (C)** The Mappila are the Kerala Muslim community formed as Arab merchants settled and married locally. The other options describe Tibetans, Siddis and the Polish children.

**28. (D)** Islam's peaceful arrival via traders shows religion spreading by trade and settlement, not only conquest. Religions do spread peacefully, and trade and religion did mix here.

**29. (D)** Islam came peacefully via Arab merchants from the 7th century, well before the later invasions. It was not after the Ghurids, in the 20th century, or never.

**30. (C)** The Siddis are of African origin. Tibetan, Armenian and Polish communities are separate groups in the chapter.

**31. (B)** The Siddis were brought enslaved between the 7th and 19th centuries. They did not come as free traders, conquerors, or Polish children.

**32. (C)** The Siddis are now a scheduled tribe, officially recognised for special support. They are not an embassy, guild, or dynasty.

**33. (A)** A scheduled tribe is constitutionally recognised for special support and protection. It is not banned, foreigner-only, or a ruling group.

**34. (B)** The Armenians came largely for trade, settling and building churches. They did not conquer, rule, or enslave.

**35. (C)** These communities kept their faith and customs while contributing to Indian life. They did not erase Indian culture, get expelled, or refuse to settle.

**36. (B)** The Baha'is came seeking refuge and acceptance, adding to India's diversity. They were not conquerors, original inhabitants, or rulers over others.

**37. (C)** The Baha'is show India's acceptance continuing into modern times. It did not stop after antiquity, apply only to traders, or favour one religion.

**38. (A)** The 'Good Maharaja' sheltered Polish children fleeing war. He did not lead an invasion, found Zoroastrianism, or build the oldest mosque.

**39. (C)** The Polish children were refugees fleeing war's dangers. They were not traders, soldiers, or original inhabitants.

**40. (C)** Sheltering the Polish children shows India's tradition of giving refuge. It is the opposite of refusing help, conquest, or rejecting refugees.

**41. (A)** Tibetan refugees fled danger and were given shelter in India. They were not mere traders, conquerors, or original inhabitants.

**42. (A)** Sowa Rigpa is the Tibetan 'Art of Healing', now part of India's AYUSH programme. It is not a synagogue, a Zoroastrian prayer, or a trade route.

**43. (A)** Sowa Rigpa joining AYUSH shows newcomers enriching India's knowledge and culture. They did not subtract from it, hoard it, or abandon it.

**44. (B)** The Tibetan refugees show India offering refuge to those fleeing danger. It was not trader-opportunity, conquest, or rejection.

**45. (C)** Persecution is hostility and ill-treatment, often on religious or other grounds - what many refugees fled. It is not a welcome, a trade deal, or medicine.

**46. (D)** Refuge or asylum is shelter for those fleeing danger. It is not trade profit, a place of worship, or a tax.

**47. (A)** Cosmopolitan (Greek kosmos 'world' + polites 'citizen') means at home with many cultures. It is not closed, single-culture, or single-religion.

**48. (D)** Pluralism (Latin plus/pluris, 'more') is the peaceful coexistence of many communities and faiths. It is not one-community rule, removing minorities, or a trade route.

**49. (A)** Ethos (Greek ethos, 'character') is a people's guiding spirit or values - here, acceptance. It is not medicine, a temple, or trade goods.

**50. (B)** To emigrate is to leave a country; to immigrate is to enter one (both from Latin migrare). It is not double-entry, ruling, or trading.

**51. (D)** Tolerance (Latin tolerare, 'to bear') is accepting and respecting different beliefs and customs. It is not forced uniformity, rejection, or indifference.

**52. (D)** Communities kept synagogues, fire temples, churches and languages while integrating - identity-preserving integration. They did not abandon traditions, fail to settle, or rule.

**53. (D)** India allowed newcomers to integrate while keeping their identities. It was neither total separation, total assimilation, nor takeover.

**54. (B)** Newcomers added foods, words, crafts, music and faiths, enriching Indian culture. They did not subtract from it, hoard it, or replace it.

**55. (B)** The chapter treats diversity as a source of strength and richness, in the spirit of vasudhaiva kutumbakam. It is not a weakness, to be erased, or unimportant.

**56. (A)** Borrowed foods, words, crafts and music show diversity enriching culture. It does not destroy it, leave it unaffected, or make all cultures identical.

**57. (B)** Many communities coexisting peacefully is pluralism. It is the opposite of persecution, conquest, or enslavement.

**58. (D)** The Parsis came for refuge from persecution; Arab merchants for trade opportunity. The reversal, the 'Siddis as free traders' (they were enslaved) and 'Tibetans as conquerors' (they were refugees) are wrong.

**59. (C)** Vasudhaiva kutumbakam ('the world is one family') is presented as having universal relevance for today's world. It is not ancient-only, meaningless now, or trader-only.

**60. (B)** Many came fleeing persecution (not all to trade), communities kept their identities, and diversity enriched Indian culture - correcting all three errors. The other options keep one or more misconceptions.
