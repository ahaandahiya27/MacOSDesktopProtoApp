## Worked Solutions

**1. (C)** The word is श्लोक ('verse') + अन्त्य ('last') + अक्षर ('letter/syllable'), capped by a feminine -ई that names the game. The split श्लोक+अन्त+क्षरी invents a non-word क्षरी and ignores अक्षर; श्लोका+न्त्य+अक्षरी mis-segments and reverses the sense to 'first'; श्लोक+अन्त्य+क्षीरी drags in क्षीर ('milk'), unrelated to the syllable अक्षर.

**2. (D)** श्लोक ends in अ and अन्त्य begins with अ; अ + अ combine to the long आ (दीर्घ सन्धि), giving श्लोकान्त्य. अ + अ never gives ए — guṇa (ए) arises from अ + इ. No visarga is present, and nothing is doubled here.

**3. (B)** The -ई of श्लोकान्त्याक्षरी forms a feminine substantive (the game's name), exactly as the feminine -ई appears in नदी or यशस्विनी. The -ति of गच्छति is a verbal (present-tense) ending, -आय in रामाय is a dative ending, and the augment-plus-ending of अगच्छत् marks past tense — none of these is the feminine -ई.

**4. (D)** विद्याहीन unpacks as विद्यया हीनः, 'devoid of (by means of) knowledge' — a तत्पुरुष where हीन ('bereft') takes विद्या as its complement. A द्वन्द्व would list two coordinate items ('knowledge and the deprived'), which is nonsense here; 'more than knowledge' reverses the sense of हीन; and 'low-grade knowledge' makes विद्या the head, but the head is the deprived person, not the knowledge.

**5. (D)** विद्याहीनाः ends in -आः, the masculine nominative PLURAL, and it agrees with the plural verb शोभन्ते ('they shine'). A singular would be विद्याहीनः with शोभते; the genitive plural is विद्याहीनानाम्; the accusative plural is विद्याहीनान्. The -आः subject form plus a plural verb fixes it as nominative plural.

**6. (C)** शोभन्ते is the ātmanepada third-person PLURAL present of √शुभ् — 'they shine'. The singular would be शोभते; a past sense would need the augment अ- (e.g. अशोभन्त); and the second-person plural would be शोभध्वे. The -न्ते ending is the marker of third-person plural here.

**7. (C)** निर्गन्ध = निः ('without') + गन्ध ('scent'), so निर्गन्धम् पुष्पम् is a 'scent-less flower' — it says nothing against colour, exactly as the verse praises the किंशुक's colour while denying it fragrance. 'Colour-less' would need निर्वर्ण/अवर्ण, not निर्गन्ध; 'both' over-reads the single prefix निः; and 'fragrant' is the very opposite of निर्गन्ध.

**8. (C)** The visarga of निः changes to र् because the following sound गँ (the voiced ग) triggers ः → र् — standard visarga sandhi, giving निर्गन्ध. It is not vowel lengthening (no अ+अ here), not guṇa (no इ/उ following), and no anusvāra is involved.

**9. (B)** इव is the particle of comparison, 'like/as', so 'किंशुकाः इव' = 'like किंशुka flowers' — the unlearned are compared to bright-but-scentless blossoms. इव does not negate (that is न/अ-), does not assign object-hood (it is not a verb's complement), and does not form a question (that would be किम्/अपि).

**10. (C)** The simile's force lies in a flower that is gorgeously coloured yet has NO fragrance — mirroring beauty/wealth with no knowledge. Saying just 'beautiful flower' keeps the colour but silently drops the scent-less point, collapsing the analogy. It is not about rarity, the colour is retained, and the grammatical subject is unaffected — the loss is specifically the निर्गन्ध detail.

**11. (A)** किंशुक is the Flame of the Forest (Butea monosperma), chosen because its flowers are brilliantly coloured but scentless — the exact mismatch the verse needs. The lotus (पद्म), rose (and its fragrance), and mango all carry fragrance or different traits, so none supplies the 'colour without scent' contrast.

**12. (D)** The verse's logic is: absence of knowledge ⇒ no true 'shine', even with looks. 'Beauty makes विद्या unnecessary' inverts the moral; 'lacking beauty defeats विद्या' is never claimed; and 'विद्या guarantees beauty' confuses inner worth with physical looks. Only the first conditional preserves 'knowledge, not looks, gives worth.'

**13. (A)** विवादाय ends in -आय, the dative singular, and here it expresses purpose ('knowledge [serves] FOR quarrel') — the तादर्थ्ये चतुर्थी use. The genitive would be विवादस्य ('of'), the locative विवादे ('in'), and the ablative विवादात् ('from') — none of which gives the 'for the sake of' purpose the verse intends.

**14. (A)** विवादाय, मदाय, परिपीडनाय each end in -आय — the dative/चतुर्थी expressing the purpose to which विद्या, धन and शक्ति are turned in the wicked. They are not genitives (which would end -स्य), not nominative subjects (the subjects are विद्या/धनम्/शक्तिः), and not vocatives (no address is being made).

**15. (C)** The verse aligns each in order: knowledge → quarrel (विवाद), wealth → pride (मद), power → oppression (परिपीडन). Each distractor rotates or swaps the mapping — e.g. attaching मद (pride, a wealth-disease) to विद्या, or परिपीडन (oppression, a power-abuse) to धन — breaking the one-to-one alignment the shloka fixes.

**16. (A)** मद is the 'intoxication' of arrogance that wealth breeds in the wicked. Generous giving is दान (the GOOD use of wealth), quarrel is विवाद (the wicked use of knowledge), and oppression is परिपीडन (the wicked use of power) — each belongs to a different gift, so मद alone fits धन's misuse.

**17. (C)** परेषाम् is the genitive plural 'of others', and परिपीडनाय is 'for tormenting' — so the wicked turn power to the oppression of OTHERS. Protection is रक्षण (the good use); 'themselves only' ignores परेषाम् ('others'); and 'sharing' is the opposite of पीडन ('tormenting').

**18. (C)** Using power to crush and humiliate others is precisely परिपीडन, the wicked use of शक्ति. रक्षण (protection) is its GOOD use; दान (giving) belongs to wealth; मद (pride) belongs to wealth's misuse — only परिपीडन matches an abuse of power against others.

**19. (B)** Just as विवादaya is 'for quarrel', ज्ञानाय is 'for knowledge[-sharing]' — both -आय datives of purpose, mirroring each other across the wicked/good split. The genitive would be ज्ञानस्य, the nominative ज्ञानम्, and the instrumental ज्ञानेन — none expresses the purpose the contrast requires.

**20. (A)** In the good, knowledge is shared (ज्ञान), wealth is given (दान), power protects (रक्षण), each in the same order as the gifts. The distractors shuffle these — e.g. assigning दान (a wealth-virtue) to विद्या or रक्षण (a power-virtue) to धन — breaking the verse's parallel one-to-one mapping.

**21. (D)** Both दान and मद spring from धन (wealth): the good give it (दान), the wicked swell with pride over it (मद) — they are the same gift's opposite outcomes. विवाद and परिपीडन are misuses of knowledge and power, not wealth; रक्षण is a power-virtue. Only मद is wealth's wicked twin to दान.

**22. (A)** Funding a free hospital is generous giving — दान, wealth's good use. मद would be flaunting riches arrogantly; रक्षण is what power (not wealth) does well; विवाद is knowledge misused. The act is wealth deployed for others' benefit, which the verse names दान.

**23. (B)** खलस्य साधोर्विपरीतम् — wicked and good use the SAME gifts oppositely; the good spread knowledge, the wicked quarrel with it. The claim flattens that contrast. The good DO possess विद्या (they just use it well); the uses are opposite, not identical; and विवाद is the WICKED, not the good, use.

**24. (A)** शक्तिः ends in the visarga of the feminine nominative singular and is the subject of the clause ('power [is] for oppressing others'). The genitive would be शक्तेः, the dative शक्त्यै, the instrumental शक्त्या — but here power is the doer/subject, hence nominative.

**25. (C)** परेषाम् is षष्ठी बहुवचन ('of others'), so परेषाम् परिपीडनाय = 'for the oppression OF others'. 'By others' would need an instrumental (परैः); 'self-oppression' contradicts पर ('other'); and 'one friend' is wrong in both number (plural) and sense (पर means 'other', not 'friend').

**26. (B)** खलस्य (-स्य) and साधोः (-ओः) are genitives: 'of the wicked / of the good', i.e. these are their respective uses. Datives would be खलाय/साधवे, ablatives खलात्/साधोः-different sense, vocatives खल/साधो. The षष्ठी here links the gifts to their possessor-users.

**27. (D)** साधोः ends in visarga; before the voiced व the visarga regularly becomes र्, giving साधोर्विपरीतम् — correct sandhi, not a misprint. It is not a vowel sandhi (the ओ is untouched) and व is not doubled. Knowing this prevents 'correcting' a proper form back to साधोःविपरीतम्.

**28. (D)** वि- on परीत yields 'reversed, opposite' (वि- as विरोध/विपर्यय), capturing that the good's use is the reverse of the wicked's. वि- can mean 'apart/away', but the resulting word here means 'opposite', not 'very', not 'with' (that is सम्/सह), nor 'falling down' (that is नि-/अव-).

**29. (C)** विपरीतम् is the hinge: it announces that for the good, every use is the REVERSE of the wicked's. किंशुक belongs to the different विद्याहीन verse, शोभन्ते is its verb, and अक्षर belongs to the game-name — none of those pivots the wicked/good contrast as विपरीतम् does.

**30. (B)** समानम् means 'the same', the exact opposite of विपरीतम् ('opposite'); swapping it would destroy the verse's truth by claiming khal and sādhu behave identically. It does not intensify the contrast (it erases it), समान and विपरीत are antonyms (not synonyms), and the subject stays people, not flowers.

**31. (D)** A खल misuses gifts on purpose — a flaw of CHARACTER (moral); a मूर्ख simply lacks wisdom — a flaw of INTELLECT, without malice. The reversal misattributes malice to the fool; and collapsing both into one category ignores that one is willed wickedness and the other innocent folly.

**32. (D)** The lawyer turns विद्या to harm BY CHOICE — malicious intent, the mark of the खल. The unlettered-but-kind farmer, the mistaken child, and the forgetful elder all lack knowledge or sharpness without ill will — that is मूर्ख / mere limitation, not wickedness.

**33. (D)** Bad advice from honest ignorance is folly without malice — मूर्ख. The minister twisting law, the knowing cheat, and the scholar inciting riots all act with deliberate ill-intent — that is the moral fault of the खल, not the innocent unwisdom of the मूर्ख.

**34. (A)** Turning विद्या/धन/शक्ति to quarrel/pride/oppression is a CHOICE — the moral defect of the खल. A मूर्ख errs from ignorance, not malicious choice, so the contrast needs the खल. A मूर्ख may still hold gifts; the two words are not interchangeable; and the मूर्ख is certainly not the साधु.

**35. (A)** He has विद्या but bends it to विवाद and shaming — willed misuse, the खल. He is not मूर्ख (he is plainly learned), not साधु (the good would SHARE learning), and 'किंशुक' is a flower, irrelevant; besides, the किंशुक is scentless, not fragrant.

**36. (C)** भूत is the past participle of √भू ('to be/become'), so भारभूत = 'become-a-burden'. Though भूत can elsewhere mean 'ghost' or 'creature', here the compound needs 'become'; 'earth' is भूमि/पृथ्वी, and 'truth' is सत्य — neither yields 'those who have become a burden'.

**37. (C)** Here -कर (from √कृ, 'to do/make') means 'making', so भारकर = 'one who makes a burden', matching भारभूत ('become a burden'). कर can elsewhere mean 'hand', 'tax', or 'ray', but in this gloss it is the agentive 'maker', confirming the burden sense.

**38. (A)** The chapter pins worth on शीलम्; the character-less person is the true burden, even if rich/handsome/high-born. Lacking wealth, beauty, or high birth does NOT make one a burden — those are precisely the externals the verse refuses to count. Only the absence of CHARACTER does.

**39. (D)** वृत्त and आचरण both mean 'conduct/behaviour', so शीलम् is moral conduct — how one habitually acts. Wealth is धन/लक्ष्मी, beauty is रूप, lineage is कुल — the very externals the chapter ranks BELOW शीलम्, not equates with it.

**40. (B)** Three externals cannot offset the absence of शीलम्; without character he is the burden the chapter names. Wealth/looks/birth do NOT secure worth here; noble family alone does not make a साधु (whose mark is good conduct); and the किंशुक comparison would, if anything, condemn him as bright-but-empty — and the किंशुक is scentless, not fragrant.

**41. (A)** The shlokas exalt inner goods — character and knowledge — over outward looks, wealth and birth. Putting wealth/beauty on top inverts the moral; birth-above-all is exactly what the chapter denies; and the किंशुक's colour is praised only to EXPOSE that mere beauty (no scent / no worth) is empty, not to crown रूपम्.

**42. (B)** With wealth and looks equal, the ONLY differentiator the chapter values is character — शीलम् — which the honest student has. Equal wealth and equal beauty cannot be the deciding factor (they cancel out), and birth is neither stated to differ nor counted by the chapter as the source of worth.

**43. (C)** विद्या is learning gained by study; ज्ञान is the deeper, realised knowing a sage attains. The reversal swaps their senses; they are not identical (the chapter contrasts them); and neither means wealth (धन) or character (शीलम्) — those are separate goods.

**44. (D)** विद्या (ends in आ) + आलय ('abode') → विद्यालय, 'abode of learning' = school; आ + आ merge to आ (दीर्घ). The output is a place, not a person (a wise person is ज्ञानी/विद्वान्), it is not a book, and the vowels involved are आ + आ, not इ + आ.

**45. (B)** The -इन् in ज्ञानी makes an agent ('one who has ज्ञान' = wise person), while -आलय in विद्यालय makes a place ('abode of विद्या' = school). Swapping them, or making both persons or both places, ignores that one suffix is agentive and the other locational.

**46. (B)** Since विद्या and ज्ञान are different (acquired learning vs deep insight), a wise person may have realised much with little schooling, and a heavily-schooled person may lack insight — the statement is coherent. It is not contradictory (the words differ), and neither entails the other automatically.

**47. (B)** लक्ष्मी is a feminine noun (the personified goddess of fortune), nominative लक्ष्मीः. It is not masculine or neuter, and every Sanskrit substantive carries a gender — so 'no gender' is impossible.

**48. (C)** Both derive from यशस् ('fame') with the possessive -विन्/-वत् sense; -इनी marks feminine (famous woman), the bare -वी masculine (famous man). Neither is a negation (that needs अ-/नि-), they share ONE root (यशस्), and the difference is gender, not number.

**49. (A)** The -वी/-विन् possessive that turns यशस् → यशस्वी turns तेजस् → तेजस्वी ('possessing lustre'). अतेजस् and तेजःहीन both NEGATE lustre (the opposite formation), and तेजाय is merely a dative 'for lustre', not a possessive adjective.

**50. (B)** Both verses subordinate the external to the internal: the scentless-but-bright किंशुक and the character-less-but-rich man are each 'empty splendour'. Praising outward splendour, or dismissing knowledge/character, inverts both verses; and neither verse praises wealth or beauty as the goal — they EXPOSE them.

**51. (D)** Every shloka turns on use and inner quality: gifts harm in the खल and help in the साधु; beauty without knowledge is empty; worth rests on शीलम्. 'More gifts = more worth' ignores misuse; beauty/wealth are shown insufficient; and even विद्या becomes विवाद when misused — so knowledge alone, misused, does NOT guarantee worth.

**52. (D)** The किंशुक is SCENTLESS, and it is a warning, not a model — so claiming it has fragrance proving inner worth is doubly false. The other three are accurate: knowledge → quarrel in the wicked, शीलम् = वृत्त/आचरण, and विपरीतम् names the opposite uses.

**53. (D)** Knowledge misused for quarrel marks the खल, and lacking शीलम् makes worth low — a chain the chapter endorses. Having विद्या does not by itself make a साधु (it can become विवाद); wealth and looks do NOT confer शीलम्; and being 'scentless like किंशुक' signals empty show, the OPPOSITE of wisdom.

**54. (B)** The verse's whole point (खलस्य साधोर्विपरीतम्) is that ONE gift yields OPPOSITE results in the wicked and the good — so दान and मद, both from धन, are contraries, not the same. They are not 'the same' (one gives, one hoards in pride); both DO arise from धन (so the other two distractors are factually wrong).

**55. (A)** The chapter repeatedly subordinates beauty (रूप), wealth (लक्ष्मी), birth (कुल) and even fame to the inner goods — knowledge rightly used and character (शीलम्). Crowning wealth/beauty, birth, or unearned fame each contradicts the express teaching that worth is inner, not outer.

**56. (C)** The game's rule (अन्त्य-अक्षर = 'last letter') is that each new verse STARTS with the LAST syllable of the previous one — here, क. Starting from the previous verse's first letter, choosing freely, or matching the new verse's ENDING all break the last-letter chain that defines the game.

**57. (A)** श्लोकान्त्याक्षरी keys on the LAST syllable; शोभन्ते ends in ते, so the next verse must begin with ते. शो is the FIRST syllable (used in antyakshari only if you misread the rule), and भ/न are interior syllables — none is the final अक्षर the game demands.

**58. (A)** दानाय is the -आय dative of purpose ('for giving'), mirroring the verse's pattern विद्या विवादाय / धनं मदाय; so धनम् दानाय = 'wealth is for giving'. A genitive would be दानस्य, an object दानम्, a locative दाने — none expresses the 'for the sake of' purpose the -आय ending carries.

**59. (B)** The chapter ties worth to character and the right USE of gifts: a learned khal turns विद्या to विवाद, and one without शीलम् is a burden — so learning alone does not secure worth. It is false that the learned are never worthless (the khal is the counterexample); learning does not destroy character; and the chapter does NOT make wealth the source of worth.

**60. (B)** Same gift (धन), opposite use: the साधु directs it to others as दान, the खल turns it inward as pride (मद) — the verse's विपरीत pivot is about USE and intent, not quantity. The amount and source are never the verse's criterion, and 'colour of wealth' confuses this verse with the किंशुक simile, which is about flowers, not money.
