Instructions: Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. Read each diagram description carefully and label the angles yourself. Remember the three big traps: corresponding/alternate angles are equal ONLY when the lines are parallel; co-interior (same-side interior) angles are SUPPLEMENTARY (add to 180°), not equal; and only PERPENDICULAR crossings give four equal angles. Chase angles step by step using linear pairs and vertically-opposite equalities — you rarely need a protractor. Do not write on this paper.

Worked Solutions

1. (A) Two distinct straight lines, once they touch, immediately separate (a straight line can't bend back to meet again). So they share at most one point. (Zero if parallel, one if intersecting — but "at most" is 1.)

2. (C) Vertically opposite angles are always equal, regardless of how the lines cross. So the angle opposite 110° is 110°. Trap: 70° is the *adjacent* (linear-pair) angle, not the opposite.

3. (D) Adjacent angles on a straight line form a linear pair summing to 180°. So 180° − 110° = 70°. Trap (A) 110° confuses adjacent with vertically opposite.

4. (B) All four equal ⇒ each = 360 ÷ 4 = 90°. Lines crossing at right angles are perpendicular.

5. (C) Let the smaller be x; the larger is 4x. Linear pair: x + 4x = 180 ⇒ 5x = 180 ⇒ x = 36. Larger = 4·36 = 144°. Trap (A) 36° is the *smaller* angle.

6. (B) Opposite edges of a sheet run the same way and never meet — parallel. Adjacent edges meet at a 90° corner — perpendicular.

7. (C) Each crossing of the transversal makes 4 angles; two crossings ⇒ 4 × 2 = 8 angles.

8. (D) Corresponding angles are equal when the lines are parallel, so the matching angle is 73°. Traps: 107° is its co-interior/linear-pair partner; 17° is its complement (irrelevant here).

9. (B) Co-interior (same-side interior) angles are supplementary on parallel lines: 180° − 118° = 62°. Trap (A) 118° wrongly assumes they are equal.

10. (A) Vertically opposite to 35° is 35°; the linear-pair neighbour is 180° − 35° = 145°. So 35° and 145°.

11. (B) Whatever the lines, opposite angles are equal: a = c (and b = d). a = b only if perpendicular; a + c = 180° is false in general (a + b = 180° is the true relation). So a = c.

12. (A) A transversal on parallel lines gives angles of just two sizes that are supplementary: 72° and 180° − 72° = 108°. So 72° and 108°. Trap (B) 72°+90° and (C) 72°+18° misuse complement/right-angle ideas.

13. (C) a ⊥ b means 90° between them. b ∥ c means c has b's direction, so a meets c at the same 90°. Answer 90°.

14. (D) Adjacent angles on a line: x + (x + 40) = 180 ⇒ 2x = 140 ⇒ x = 70. Trap (C) 110 is the value of the second angle, not x.

15. (A) Alternate interior angles are equal: 2x + 10 = 3x − 20 ⇒ 30 = x ⇒ x = 30. (Then each angle = 70°.) Trap (B) 10 comes from wrongly setting the sum to 180.

16. (C) Corresponding = 65° (equal), alternate = 65° (equal), co-interior = 180° − 65° = 115° (supplementary). So 65°, 65°, 115°. Trap (D) wrongly makes the co-interior angle equal too.

17. (B) Equal alternate angles force the lines parallel. (A) co-interior must sum to *180°*, not 200°; (C) equal co-interior would mean each 90° only — not a general parallel test; (D) is about one line only. Answer alternate angles equal.

18. (D) Co-interior angles are supplementary. Let the smaller be x, larger 2x: x + 2x = 180 ⇒ x = 60. Smaller = 60°. Trap (A) 30 ignores that they must add to 180°.

19. (A) An angle and its vertically opposite partner are equal, so each = 86° ÷ 2 = 43°. Trap (B) 86° forgets to halve.

20. (B) Corresponding angles equal: 5x = 3x + 40 ⇒ 2x = 40 ⇒ x = 20, so each = 5·20 = 100°. Trap (A) 20° gives x, not the angle.

21. (C) Co-interior angles between parallel lines are supplementary, not equal — so "they are equal" is the FALSE statement. The other three are all true.

22. (D) Corresponding angles are equal *only* when the lines are parallel. Here they differ (80° vs 95°), so the lines are not parallel. Trap (A) reverses the rule.

23. (B) Going round, the 1st and 3rd (both 2y) are a vertically opposite pair (equal ✓). The remaining pair is the 2nd and 4th, which are also vertically opposite, so the 4th = the 2nd = 3y.

24. (C) Vertically opposite ⇒ 3x − 10 = 2x + 25 ⇒ x = 35. ∠AOC = 3·35 − 10 = 95°. Trap (A) 35 reports x instead of the angle.

25. (A) The transversal cuts the parallels at the same inclination, so the acute angle at n equals the acute angle at m = 40° (corresponding/alternate equality). Trap (B) 50° is the complement.

26. (B) Co-interior angles are supplementary: 4x + (5x − 18) = 180 ⇒ 9x = 198 ⇒ x = 22. Trap (B-vs-C): setting them *equal* gives x = 18 (option A), the classic co-interior slip.

27. (D) Ray OP on a line ⇒ the two angles form a linear pair: 3x + 2x = 180 ⇒ 5x = 180 ⇒ x = 36°. Trap (B) 60° comes from dividing 180 by 3.

28. (C) Eight angles take two supplementary sizes, four of each. So 4 of them equal 124° (its vertically opposite + the two corresponding-position equals). Trap (B) 2 forgets the corresponding pair at the other line.

29. (A) Perpendicular lines: 90° between first and second. A line parallel to the first keeps its direction, so it meets the second at the same 90°.

30. (B) Alternate interior angles are equal on parallel lines, so the alternate of 118° is 118°. Trap (A) 62° wrongly treats them as supplementary (that's co-interior).

31. (D) Around the point the four angles are p, q, p, q with p + q = 180° (adjacent linear pair). With p = 3q: 3q + q = 180 ⇒ 4q = 180 ⇒ q = 45°.

32. (C) Co-interior supplementary: (x + 30) + 2x = 180 ⇒ 3x = 150 ⇒ x = 50. The two angles are 80° and 100°; the larger is 100°. Trap (B) 80° is the smaller.

33. (A) "Both supplementary AND equal": x + x = 180 ⇒ x = 90°. Trap (D) 180° is the sum, not each angle.

34. (C) The two co-interior angles sum to 180°; their halves sum to 90°. In the triangle formed by the two bisectors and the transversal, the bisectors meet at 180° − 90° = 90°. (A neat classic result.)

35. (D) ∠PAB and ∠ABS are co-interior ⇒ supplementary: 5x + 4x = 180 ⇒ 9x = 180 ⇒ x = 20. ∠PAB = 5·20 = 100°. Trap (B) 80° is ∠ABS.

36. (B) Vertically opposite angles, right-angle linear pairs, and two 90° angles are always equal. But corresponding angles when the lines are NOT parallel need not be equal — that's the answer.

37. (A) Alternate angles equal: 60 − x = 2x ⇒ 60 = 3x ⇒ x = 20, so each = 2·20 = 40°. Trap (B) 20° reports x, not the angle.

38. (C) The SAME-side interior angle pairs as co-interior with 105°, so it is supplementary: 180° − 105° = 75°. Trap (B) 105° wrongly treats them as equal (that would be alternate, opposite side).

39. (B) Vertically opposite ⇒ equal: 4x + 5 = 6x − 25 ⇒ 30 = 2x ⇒ x = 15. Trap (A) 10 comes from a sign slip.

40. (D) Equal corresponding angles guarantee the lines are parallel (the converse rule). Trap (C) "meet at 88°" misreads the equality.

41. (C) The given interior angle at l and the co-interior angle at m are supplementary: 7x + 2x = 180 ⇒ 9x = 180 ⇒ x = 20. So 7x = 140°. Trap (D) 120° = 6x, an arithmetic slip.

42. (A) If one of the four angles is 90°, its linear-pair neighbour is 90°, its vertically opposite is 90°, and the fourth is 90° too. So 4 of them are right angles.

43. (B) The exterior angle (130°) and the interior angle at the same crossing are a linear pair: 180° − 130° = 50°. Trap (A) 130° forgets the straight-line sum.

44. (D) Alternate exterior angles are equal on parallel lines: 3x + 15 = 4x − 5 ⇒ 20 = x. ∠a = 3·20 + 15 = 75°. Trap (A) 20 reports x.

45. (C) The NE corner and the SW corner are vertically opposite, hence equal: 65°. Trap (B/D) 115°/130° treat them as supplementary/double.

46. (A) With a right angle present, that whole crossing becomes four 90° angles, and corresponding angles at the parallel line are 90° too — so all eight angles are right angles. (This is why a perpendicular transversal makes a fully right-angled grid.)

47. (B) Co-interior sum 180°, difference 40°. Larger = (180 + 40) ÷ 2 = 110° (smaller 70°). Trap (A) 70° is the smaller angle.

48. (D) A, O, B are collinear so ∠AOB = 180°. ∠AOC + ∠COE + ∠EOB = 180° ⇒ 50° + 90° + ∠EOB = 180° ⇒ ∠EOB = 40°. Trap (B) 50° just echoes ∠AOC.

49. (A) The alternate-interior angle and the co-interior angle sharing that vertex form a linear pair (sum 180°). With alternate = 3 × co-interior: 3k + k = 180 ⇒ k = 45 ⇒ alternate = 135°. Trap (D) 45° is the co-interior partner.

50. (C) Four angles around a crossing come in two equal vertically-opposite pairs, with adjacent ones supplementary. 50°, 130°, 50° works: fourth angle = 130°, pairs 50/50 and 130/130, and 50 + 130 = 180 ✓. (B) needs four equal 60°s impossible; (D) 100° three times can't sum correctly.

51. (B) Co-interior angles sum to 180° in ratio 3:2 ⇒ 5 parts = 180° ⇒ 1 part = 36°. Smaller = 2 parts = 72°. Trap (A) 36° is one part, not the smaller angle.

52. (D) At 90° every one of the eight angles is a right angle, so there is just 1 distinct measure (90°). Trap (B) 2 forgets the perpendicular case collapses both sizes to 90°.

53. (A) ∠1 = ∠2 = 116° (corresponding, parallel). ∠2 and ∠3 are a linear pair ⇒ ∠3 = 180° − 116° = 64°. Trap (B) 116° forgets the linear-pair step.

54. (C) The alternate-interior angle and the co-interior angle at that vertex form a linear pair (sum 180°) in ratio 2:1 ⇒ 2k + k = 180 ⇒ k = 60. Alternate-interior = 2k = 120°. Trap (A) 60° is the co-interior partner.

55. (B) Corresponding angles equal (m ∥ n): x + 25 = 2x − 15 ⇒ 40 = x. Angle = 40 + 25 = 65°. Trap (A) 40 reports x.

56. (D) Alternate angles are equal: ∠A = ∠B. Given ∠A = 2∠B − 30 ⇒ ∠B = 2∠B − 30 ⇒ ∠B = 30, so ∠A = 30°. Trap (C) 60° doubles incorrectly.

57. (A) Four angles around a crossing form two vertically-opposite pairs. Three given are x, x, 2x, so the pair x, x is one vertically-opposite pair and 2x belongs to the other (whose partner is also 2x). Sum = x + x + 2x + 2x = 6x = 360 ⇒ x = 60. Check: 60° + 60° + 120° + 120° = 360° ✓. Trap (D) 90 would force all four equal, contradicting x ≠ 2x.

58. (C) The corresponding angle is 80°, so its bisector makes 40° with the transversal. Because the angle's other arm lies along line b's direction... measuring the acute angle the bisector makes with b: 80° − 40° = 40°. Trap (D) 80° forgets the bisecting.

59. (B) For parallel lines co-interior angles must sum to 180°. Here 100° + 90° = 190° ≠ 180°, so the lines are not parallel. Trap (A) assumes parallel without checking the sum.

60. (D) ∠Q is the alternate interior angle of ∠P across the (first) transversal between the parallel lines, so ∠Q = ∠P = 67° (alternate interior angles equal). The second-transversal clause is consistent extra detail. Trap (C) 113° treats them as co-interior (supplementary).
