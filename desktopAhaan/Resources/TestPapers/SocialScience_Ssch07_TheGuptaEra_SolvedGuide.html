Set 1 · Q1–Q10

Q1

The Gupta dynasty rose to power in northern India after which earlier power had weakened?

  • Athe Mauryas, immediately before them
  • Bthe Kushanas✓
  • Cthe Pallavas of the south
  • Dthe Hunas of Central Asia

Working

✓

The Guptas rose around the 3rd century CE after the Kushana Empire weakened. The Mauryas had fallen centuries earlier, the Pallavas were a contemporary southern kingdom, and the Hunas came later and helped end the Guptas.

Q2

The capital of the Gupta Empire was:

  • AKanchipuram
  • BPataliputra (present-day Patna)✓
  • CPratishthana
  • DSocotra

Working

✓

The Guptas ruled from Pataliputra (modern Patna). Kanchipuram was the Pallava capital, Pratishthana a Satavahana centre, and Socotra a distant trading island.

Q3

The Gupta Empire is dated roughly to which span?

  • Aabout the 3rd to the 6th century CE✓
  • Bthe 2nd century BCE to the 3rd century CE
  • Cthe 6th to the 9th century CE
  • Dthe 1st to the 2nd century CE

Working

✓

The Guptas ruled c. 3rd-6th century CE. The 2nd-c-BCE-to-3rd-c-CE span is the Age of Reorganisation, the 6th-9th centuries are later, and the 1st-2nd centuries CE were Kushana times.

Q4

Even at its height, the Gupta Empire is best described as:

  • Adirectly ruling every village across the whole subcontinent, just as the Mauryas had
  • Bdirectly governing mainly the north and centre, relying on land grants and allied kings elsewhere✓
  • Ccontrolling only the single city of Pataliputra
  • Da loose alliance of kingdoms with no central ruler at all

Working

✓

The Guptas governed the north and centre directly, while the south (Pallavas) and northeast (Kamarupa) had their own kingdoms and the Guptas leaned on land grants and allies. They neither ruled all India village-by-village, nor were they a single city or a ruler-less alliance.

Q5

The Iron Pillar of Delhi is famous chiefly because it:

  • Ais made of solid gold
  • Bwas raised by Chandragupta Maurya
  • Chas resisted rusting for well over a thousand years✓
  • Dis the tallest tower in India

Working

✓

The Iron Pillar is renowned for resisting rust across centuries. It is iron (not gold), linked to the Gupta 'Chandra' inscription (not the Maurya founder), and famed for rust-resistance rather than height.

Q6

The Iron Pillar resists rust because:

  • Airon simply cannot rust under any conditions
  • Bit is coated with fresh gold paint every year
  • Cits phosphorus-rich iron forms a thin protective layer that seals out oxygen and water✓
  • Dit stands in a place where rain never falls

Working

✓

A special phosphorus-rich iron lets a thin protective film form, shielding the metal from oxygen and moisture. Iron normally rusts easily; there is no gold paint, and the dry-climate idea is not the chapter's explanation.

Q7

The 'Chandra' inscription on the Iron Pillar is generally linked to which ruler?

  • AChandragupta II✓
  • BChandragupta Maurya
  • CSamudragupta
  • DKanishka

Working

✓

The pillar's 'Chandra' is linked to the Gupta Chandragupta II ('Vikramaditya'), not the much earlier Maurya founder, nor Samudragupta, nor the Kushana Kanishka.

Q8

Why must we NOT say 'Chandragupta II is just another name for Chandragupta Maurya'?

  • Athey are the same person with two spellings
  • BChandragupta II actually ruled before the Mauryas
  • Cneither of them really existed
  • Dthey are different rulers from different dynasties, centuries apart✓

Working

✓

Chandragupta Maurya founded the Maurya Empire centuries earlier; Chandragupta II was a later Gupta ruler. They share a name but are distinct people from different dynasties.

Q9

The ruler who first raised the Guptas to imperial status was:

  • ASamudragupta
  • BChandragupta II
  • CKumaragupta
  • DChandragupta I✓

Working

✓

Chandragupta I lifted the Guptas to an imperial power and took the grand title Maharajadhiraja. Samudragupta and Chandragupta II were his successors, and Kumaragupta a later ruler.

Q10

Chandragupta I strengthened the Guptas by marrying a Licchavi princess. This is an example of:

  • Aa purely religious ceremony with no political aim
  • Ba matrimonial alliance that boosted the dynasty's power and prestige✓
  • Ca way of collecting yearly taxes
  • Dan attempt to launch a new calendar era

Working

✓

The Licchavi marriage was a political alliance that raised Gupta prestige - diplomacy through marriage, not a mere religious rite, a tax device, or a calendar launch.

Set 2 · Q11–Q20

Q11

The grand title 'Maharajadhiraja' adopted by Gupta rulers means:

  • A'servant of the common people'
  • B'great king of kings', claiming supreme authority✓
  • C'chief priest of the temple'
  • D'commander of the navy'

Working

✓

Maharajadhiraja means 'great king of kings', asserting paramount power over lesser kings. It is not a humble servant title, a priestly office, or a naval rank.

Q12

Gupta kings issued finely made gold coins partly to:

  • Aremove the need for any army
  • Brecord the Sangam poems of the Tamil south
  • Cpay yearly tribute to Rome
  • Ddisplay their power, achievements and legitimacy✓

Working

✓

Gupta gold coinage advertised royal power, victories and legitimacy (and aided trade). Coins did not replace armies, carry Tamil poetry, or signal tribute to Rome.

Q13

Samudragupta is remembered above all as a:

  • Apacifist who never once went to war
  • Bwarrior king who greatly expanded the empire by conquest✓
  • Cforeign invader from Central Asia
  • DChinese pilgrim who toured India

Working

✓

Samudragupta was the conquering 'warrior king' who enlarged the empire. He was not a pacifist, a foreign invader, or the pilgrim Faxian.

Q14

The achievements of Samudragupta are recorded in the:

  • AAryabhatiya
  • BMeghadutam
  • CBrihat Samhita
  • DPrayaga Prashasti✓

Working

✓

The Prayaga Prashasti (the Allahabad pillar inscription) praises Samudragupta. The Aryabhatiya is Aryabhata's science treatise, the Meghadutam is Kalidasa's poem, and the Brihat Samhita is Varahamihira's encyclopedia.

Q15

The Prayaga Prashasti was composed by Samudragupta's court poet:

  • AKalidasa
  • BAryabhata
  • CVarahamihira
  • DHarisena✓

Working

✓

Harisena, Samudragupta's court poet, composed the Prayaga Prashasti. Kalidasa served at Chandragupta II's court, Aryabhata was a mathematician, and Varahamihira an astronomer-scholar.

Q16

A 'prashasti' is:

  • Aa tax-free village granted to brahmins
  • Ba formal inscription composed in praise of a king, recording his achievements✓
  • Ca horse-sacrifice ritual
  • Da Buddhist monastery

Working

✓

A prashasti is a praise-inscription of a ruler's deeds. The tax-free village is an agrahara, the ritual is the ashvamedha, and the monastery is a vihara.

Q17

Because the Prayaga Prashasti was written by the king's own court poet, a careful historian should:

  • Aaccept every word as plain, unbiased fact
  • Btreat its praise cautiously and cross-check it with other sources✓
  • Creject it as completely worthless
  • Dassume it was secretly written by an enemy

Working

✓

A court poet's praise of his patron is likely flattering, so its claims should be weighed against other evidence - neither swallowed whole nor dismissed entirely, and not assumed hostile.

Q18

The Gupta ruler under whom art, literature and science most flourished, titled 'Vikramaditya', was:

  • ASamudragupta
  • BChandragupta I
  • CChandragupta II✓
  • DKumaragupta

Working

✓

Chandragupta II 'Vikramaditya' presided over the golden flowering of art and learning. Samudragupta was the warrior king, Chandragupta I the founder, and Kumaragupta a later ruler.

Q19

The title 'Vikramaditya' (vikrama + aditya) means:

  • A'King of Kings'
  • B'Servant of the Gods'
  • C'Sun of Valour'✓
  • D'Lord of the Sea'

Working

✓

From vikrama ('valour') + aditya ('sun'), Vikramaditya means 'Sun of Valour'. 'King of kings' is Maharajadhiraja; the others are invented titles.

Q20

The flourishing of poets, scientists and artists at Chandragupta II's court shows that he acted as a:

  • Aregent ruling on behalf of a child
  • Bforeign conqueror from the northwest
  • Cwandering pilgrim
  • Dpatron - a supporter of artists and scholars✓

Working

✓

By supporting artists and scholars, Chandragupta II was their patron. A regent governs for another (which he was not), nor was he a foreign conqueror or a pilgrim.

Set 3 · Q21–Q30

Q21

Which set of achievements is correctly tied to Chandragupta II's reign?

  • Athe golden flowering of Kalidasa's poetry and the 'Chandra' Iron Pillar✓
  • Bthe Prayaga Prashasti and a conquest-heavy warrior reign
  • Cthe very first founding of the Gupta empire
  • Dthe Huna invasions that ended the dynasty

Working

✓

Chandragupta II's reign is linked to Kalidasa and the Iron Pillar's Chandra inscription. The conquest reign and Prayaga Prashasti belong to Samudragupta, the founding to Chandragupta I, and the Huna invasions to the later decline.

Q22

Prabhavati Gupta is remembered as a 'diplomat queen' because she:

  • Apersonally led the Gupta armies in every battle
  • Bwrote the Aryabhatiya on mathematics
  • Cinvented the rust-resistant iron of the pillar
  • Dwas a Gupta princess who, after her husband's death, governed as regent and kept strong ties between kingdoms✓

Working

✓

Prabhavati Gupta, a Gupta princess married into another dynasty, ruled as regent for her sons and maintained alliances - diplomacy in action. She was not an army commander, the author of the Aryabhatiya, or a metallurgist.

Q23

A 'regent' is someone who:

  • Atravels to holy places
  • Bgoverns a kingdom temporarily on behalf of a monarch unable to rule, often a child✓
  • Ccomposes praise-poems for kings
  • Dstudies the stars and planets

Working

✓

A regent rules in place of a monarch who cannot (often a minor). The traveller is a pilgrim, the praise-poet writes a prashasti, and the star-student is an astronomer.

Q24

Prabhavati Gupta's career, like that of earlier queens, shows that royal women in ancient India:

  • Acould at times wield real political power✓
  • Bwere never allowed anywhere near governance
  • Conly ever worked as farmers
  • Dwere forbidden from marrying into other dynasties

Working

✓

A queen ruling as regent and steering alliances shows royal women could hold genuine power. They were not barred from governance, confined to farming, or forbidden cross-dynasty marriage, which was itself a key alliance tool.

Q25

Gupta land grants were typically recorded on:

  • Apalm-leaf newspapers
  • Bprinted paper deeds
  • Ccopper plates✓
  • Dclay coins

Working

✓

Gupta land grants (agraharas) were inscribed on durable copper plates. Palm-leaf 'newspapers', printed paper and clay coins are anachronistic or invented.

Q26

An 'agrahara' was:

  • Aa grand royal title
  • Ba horse-sacrifice ritual
  • Ca village or piece of land granted by a king, often tax-free, to brahmins or temples✓
  • Da system of gold coinage

Working

✓

An agrahara was a (usually tax-free) land grant to brahmins or temples. The grand title is Maharajadhiraja, the ritual the ashvamedha, and coinage a separate matter.

Q27

The title 'Chakravartin' claimed by a ruler meant he was a:

  • Atemple priest
  • Bvillage headman
  • Cuniversal or supreme ruler over all lesser kings✓
  • Dforeign ambassador

Working

✓

Chakravartin denotes a universal ruler claiming dominion over all lesser kings. It is not a priest, a village head, or an ambassador.

Q28

Granting tax-free land to brahmins and temples could, over time, weaken a king because it:

  • Aforced the king to fight far more wars
  • Bmade the king's coins stop working
  • Cended all trade with Rome
  • Dreduced the taxes flowing into the royal treasury✓

Working

✓

Each tax-free grant meant less revenue for the crown, which could weaken central finances over time - a subtle administrative cost. It did not compel wars, disable coins, or halt trade.

Q29

The grand titles and elaborate administration of the Guptas were meant mainly to:

  • Aproject the emperor's supreme authority and organise the realm✓
  • Bhide the fact that the Guptas had no army
  • Cprove the Guptas were foreigners
  • Dremove any need for officials

Working

✓

Lofty titles and a layered administration advertised supreme authority and helped run the realm. They did not mask a missing army, mark the Guptas as foreign, or remove the need for officials.

Q30

Compared with the Mauryan model of ruling every region directly, the Gupta system relied more on:

  • Aa single elected national council
  • Bcomplete withdrawal from all governance
  • Cforeign Roman administrators
  • Dland grants and allied or subordinate kings✓

Working

✓

The Guptas governed the core directly but leaned on land grants and allied or subordinate kings elsewhere, unlike the more centralised Mauryan model. There was no elected council, no withdrawal from governance, and no Roman administration.

Set 4 · Q31–Q40

Q31

Gupta-age trade is described as reaching across the:

  • APacific Ocean to the Americas
  • Bfrozen Arctic seas
  • CAtlantic, but only as far as Britain
  • DIndian Ocean, as far as Socotra and beyond✓

Working

✓

Gupta trade crossed the Indian Ocean, reaching Socotra and beyond. It did not reach the Americas, the Arctic, or Britain across the Atlantic.

Q32

Indian traders' graffiti found in a cave on the faraway island of Socotra is taken as evidence that:

  • Athe Guptas had conquered and ruled Socotra
  • BSocotrans had invaded India
  • Cthe island was the Gupta capital
  • DIndian merchants travelled and traded far across the Indian Ocean✓

Working

✓

Such graffiti shows Indian merchants ranged far across the Indian Ocean in trade - not conquest of Socotra, an invasion of India, or the island being a capital.

Q33

Why is physical evidence like coins, copper plates and overseas graffiti so important for studying the Gupta age?

  • Ait proves the Guptas kept no written records at all
  • Bit gives historians concrete proof of rulers, land grants and long-distance trade✓
  • Cit shows the Guptas never traded with anyone
  • Dit removes the need to read any inscriptions

Working

✓

Coins, copper-plate grants and traders' graffiti are concrete sources for Gupta rulers, administration and far-flung trade. They do not show an absence of records or of trade, and they ARE inscriptions to be read, not a substitute for them.

Q34

Faxian, whose account describes Gupta society, was a:

  • AChinese Buddhist pilgrim who travelled across India✓
  • BGupta court poet
  • CRoman trader
  • DHuna general

Working

✓

Faxian was a Chinese Buddhist pilgrim who toured India and recorded what he saw. He was not a Gupta court poet, a Roman trader, or a Huna general.

Q35

A 'pilgrim' is best defined as:

  • Aa person who governs for a child-king
  • Ba poet who praises kings
  • Ca person who travels to holy places✓
  • Da maker of coins

Working

✓

A pilgrim travels to sacred sites. The child-king's stand-in is a regent, the praise-poet writes a prashasti, and coins are made by mint-workers.

Q36

Faxian paints Gupta cities as prosperous and charitable, yet he also recorded:

  • Athe harsh treatment of the 'untouchable' chandalas, who had to live outside the towns✓
  • Bthat everyone in society was perfectly equal
  • Cthat the Guptas had banned all trade
  • Dthat there were no cities at all

Working

✓

Faxian noted both prosperity AND the harsh exclusion of the chandalas, who lived outside towns - so society was not equal. He did not report banned trade or an absence of cities.

Q37

Faxian's record warns historians that a single eyewitness account:

  • Ais always a complete and perfectly neutral record
  • Bmust be ignored entirely
  • Cshows only part of the picture and should be checked against other sources✓
  • Dwas actually written long after the Gupta age

Working

✓

Faxian saw mainly prosperous cities from his own viewpoint, so his account is partial and must be cross-checked - neither perfectly complete and neutral, nor worthless, nor a late forgery.

Q38

The Gupta age is called India's 'Classical Age' mainly because of its:

  • Acomplete social equality for every person
  • Bhigh achievements in art, science and literature, later looked back on as a model✓
  • Ctotal absence of any warfare
  • Drejection of all earlier learning

Working

✓

'Classical Age' (from Latin classicus, 'of the highest class') refers to its peak achievements in art, science and literature. It does not claim social equality, an absence of war, or a break with earlier learning.

Q39

Aryabhata is best described as a:

  • Awarrior king
  • Bcourt painter
  • Cmathematician and astronomer✓
  • DBuddhist pilgrim

Working

✓

Aryabhata was a mathematician and astronomer, author of the Aryabhatiya. He was not a king, a painter, or a pilgrim.

Q40

The 'Aryabhatiya' is:

  • Aa short Sanskrit treatise on mathematics and astronomy, written around 500 CE✓
  • Ba praise-inscription of Samudragupta
  • Ca Kalidasa play about love
  • Da Buddhist monastery near Pataliputra

Working

✓

The Aryabhatiya (c. 500 CE) is Aryabhata's compact treatise on mathematics and astronomy. The praise-inscription is the Prayaga Prashasti, Kalidasa wrote literature, and a monastery is a vihara.

Set 5 · Q41–Q50

Q41

India's gift of 'shunya' (zero) and the place-value system mattered to the whole world because it:

  • Amade writing and calculating with large numbers far easier, and later spread across the world✓
  • Bwas used only once and then forgotten
  • Capplied only to temple architecture
  • Dreplaced the need for every other number

Working

✓

The place-value system with zero (shunya) hugely simplified calculation and spread worldwide. It was not a one-off, was not limited to architecture, and works alongside the other digits rather than replacing them.

Q42

'Astronomy', the science Aryabhata advanced, is:

  • Athe scientific study of the stars, planets and sky✓
  • Bthe study of traditional medicine
  • Cthe art of carving sculptures
  • Dthe writing of praise-poems

Working

✓

Astronomy (Greek astron 'star' + nomos 'law') studies the stars, planets and sky. Traditional medicine is Ayurveda, carving is sculpture, and praise-poetry is the prashasti tradition.

Q43

Varahamihira is remembered for his encyclopedic work, the:

  • APrayaga Prashasti
  • BBrihat Samhita✓
  • CMeghadutam
  • DAryabhatiya

Working

✓

Varahamihira wrote the wide-ranging Brihat Samhita. The Prayaga Prashasti praises Samudragupta, the Meghadutam is Kalidasa's poem, and the Aryabhatiya is Aryabhata's.

Q44

Kalidasa, a gem of the Gupta court, was a:

  • Aan astronomer who measured the planets
  • Ban ironsmith who built the pillar
  • Cpoet and playwright, author of works such as the Meghadutam✓
  • Da Chinese pilgrim

Working

✓

Kalidasa was the celebrated poet-playwright of the Meghadutam and other works. The planet-measurer is Aryabhata or Varahamihira, the ironsmith is anonymous, and the pilgrim is Faxian.

Q45

That Kalidasa's plays are still performed about 1,500 years later best illustrates:

  • Athat nothing from the Gupta age survived
  • Bthat the plays were really written in modern times
  • Cthat Gupta literature was only ever about science
  • Dthe lasting legacy of Gupta-age literature✓

Working

✓

Kalidasa's enduring popularity shows the lasting legacy of Gupta literature. It is not evidence that nothing survived, that the plays are modern, or that Gupta writing was only scientific.

Q46

'Ayurveda', codified in this age, literally means the:

  • A'sun of valour'
  • B'great king of kings'
  • C'science of life' (ayus + veda)✓
  • D'science of the stars'

Working

✓

Ayurveda (ayus 'life' + veda 'knowledge') is the 'science of life', India's traditional medicine. 'Sun of valour' is Vikramaditya, 'great king of kings' is Maharajadhiraja, and the star-science is astronomy.

Q47

To 'codify' a body of knowledge such as Ayurveda means to:

  • Adestroy and forget it
  • Barrange and write it down in an organised, systematic way✓
  • Ckeep it a strict secret forever
  • Dtranslate it into Greek

Working

✓

To codify is to set knowledge down systematically. It is the opposite of destroying or hiding it, and is not the same as translating it.

Q48

The cave paintings of Ajanta and the carvings of Sarnath and Udayagiri are examples of Gupta-age:

  • Ascientific instruments
  • Btrade goods exported to Rome
  • Croyal land-grant documents
  • Dartistic achievement✓

Working

✓

Ajanta's paintings and the sculptures of Sarnath and Udayagiri are celebrated Gupta art. They are not instruments, export goods, or land-grant deeds.

Q49

Garuda, shown as a royal emblem and the vahana of Vishnu on Gupta symbols, reflects that the Guptas:

  • Aworshipped only foreign Greek gods
  • Brejected all religion
  • Cdrew on Hindu religious imagery to express their authority✓
  • Druled as a Buddhist-only dynasty

Working

✓

Using Garuda (Vishnu's vahana, or mount) as an emblem shows the Guptas drawing on Hindu imagery for royal authority. They did not worship only Greek gods, reject religion, or rule as a Buddhist-only dynasty.

Q50

The Gupta Empire declined in the 6th century CE largely due to:

  • Aa Roman conquest
  • Ba Pallava conquest of the north
  • Cthe assassination of Chandragupta Maurya
  • Dinvasions by the Hunas✓

Working

✓

Repeated Huna invasions in the 6th century gravely weakened the Guptas. Rome did not conquer them, the Pallavas were a southern kingdom, and Chandragupta Maurya belonged to a far earlier era.

Q51

The Hunas who pressed on the Guptas came from:

  • ACentral Asia, entering through the northwest✓
  • Bthe Tamil far south
  • Cthe islands of the Indian Ocean
  • Dthe Brahmaputra valley

Working

✓

The Hunas were Central Asian invaders entering through the northwest, like earlier waves. They did not come from the Tamil south, the ocean islands, or the northeastern Brahmaputra valley.

Q52

The pattern of foreign groups entering India 'through the northwest' across many centuries suggests that region was:

  • Bcompletely sealed off by mountains

  • DIndia's main seaport

Working

✓

From the Indo-Greeks to the Hunas, the northwest was repeatedly the entry route - a recurring gateway, not a sealed wall, the chief farmland, or a seaport.

Q53

While the Guptas ruled the north, the south was home to the:

  • AShungas of central India
  • BKushanas of the northwest
  • CPallavas of Kanchipuram✓
  • DChedis of Kalinga

Working

✓

The Pallavas, based at Kanchipuram, flourished in the south during the Gupta age. The Shungas, Kushanas and Chedis belonged to earlier periods and other regions.

Q54

The Pallava kingdom had its capital at:

  • APataliputra
  • BUjjain
  • CKanchipuram✓
  • DPratishthana

Working

✓

The Pallavas ruled from Kanchipuram. Pataliputra was the Gupta capital, while Ujjain and Pratishthana were other cities.

Q55

The Kamarupa kingdom of this era lay in the:

  • ABrahmaputra valley of the northeast✓
  • BKaveri delta of the south
  • CIndus valley of the northwest
  • Dcentral Deccan plateau

Working

✓

Kamarupa was the northeastern kingdom of the Brahmaputra valley. The Kaveri delta was Chola country, the Indus the northwest, and the Deccan the Satavahana heartland.

Q56

That the Pallavas and Kamarupa flourished alongside the Guptas shows that Gupta India was:

  • Athe only kingdom anywhere in India
  • Ba north-centred empire sharing the subcontinent with other regional kingdoms✓
  • Cruled entirely from China
  • Da province of the Roman Empire

Working

✓

Strong contemporaries in the south and northeast show the Guptas shared the subcontinent rather than ruling all of it. India was not a single kingdom, China-ruled, or Roman.

Q57

To call the Gupta age a 'Golden Age' is accurate mainly about its:

  • Aperfect equality and happiness for every person
  • Bachievements in art, science, literature and trade, not social equality✓
  • Ccomplete freedom from any conflict
  • Dtotal isolation from the outside world

Working

✓

The 'golden' label fits its cultural and economic peak; society remained unequal, as the exclusion of the chandalas shows. It does not mean universal equality, an absence of conflict, or isolation.

Q58

Which sequence of Gupta rulers is in correct order (earliest first)?

  • AChandragupta I, then Samudragupta, then Chandragupta II✓
  • BChandragupta II, then Samudragupta, then Chandragupta I
  • CSamudragupta, then Chandragupta I, then Chandragupta II
  • DChandragupta II, then Chandragupta I, then Samudragupta

Working

✓

The order is Chandragupta I (founder), then his son Samudragupta (warrior king), then Chandragupta II ('Vikramaditya'). The other orderings scramble this.

Q59

Which pairing of ruler and achievement is correct?

  • ASamudragupta - celebrated in the Prayaga Prashasti✓
  • BChandragupta I - the rust-resistant Iron Pillar
  • CChandragupta II - the first Gupta to take an imperial title
  • DKanishka - a great Gupta emperor

✓

Samudragupta is the one praised in the Prayaga Prashasti. The Iron Pillar links to Chandragupta II (not Chandragupta I), the first imperial title to Chandragupta I (not II), and Kanishka was a Kushana, not a Gupta.

Q60

A student claims, 'The Gupta period proves science and art can flourish even while an empire is not perfectly fair.' The best support is:

  • Ddazzling work by Aryabhata and Kalidasa coexisted with the harsh exclusion of the chandalas✓

✓

Brilliant Gupta science and literature flourished even as the chandalas were harshly excluded - high culture alongside an unequal society. The other options deny either the inequality or the achievements.
