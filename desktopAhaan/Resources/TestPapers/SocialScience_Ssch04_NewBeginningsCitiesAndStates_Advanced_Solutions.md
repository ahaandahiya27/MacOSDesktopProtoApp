# Social Science — Chapter 4: New Beginnings: Cities and States · Paper 2 (Advanced) Solutions

**1. (D)** India's "First Urbanisation" was the **Harappan (Indus / Sindhu-Sarasvati) civilisation** — the Subcontinent's earliest cities, long before the Mauryas, Guptas or Mughals.

**2. (A)** After the Harappan cities declined, organised urban life largely vanished for about **a thousand years** — the long gap before the Second Urbanisation.

**3. (B)** The Second Urbanisation grew chiefly in the fertile **Ganga** plains, where rich soil and iron tools supported dense farming and new towns.

**4. (C)** *Jana* ("people") + *pada* ("foot") gives the sense of **a place where the people have set foot / settled** — a clan's territory.

**5. (D)** Each early janapada was led by a **rajaa (ruler)**, who in turn took counsel from assemblies.

**6. (A)** A mahaajanapada ("great janapada") formed when **smaller janapadas merged into a bigger, more powerful state**.

**7. (B)** The traditional list names **sixteen** great mahaajanapadas.

**8. (C)** Magadha, Kosala and Avanti were **monarchies, led by a rajaa** — power passing within a ruling family.

**9. (D)** A gana or sangha was an **early republic, where the assembly took the big decisions** — not a one-king monarchy.

**10. (A)** Two famous gana-sanghas were **Vajji and Malla**. (Magadha and Kosala were monarchies.)

**11. (B)** In a gana-sangha **the assembly took major decisions and could even select the rajaa** — the opposite of strict hereditary rule.

**12. (C)** *Sabhaa* and *samiti* are first mentioned in the **Vedas** — very old roots of consultative councils.

**13. (D)** A moat was a **deep, wide water-filled ditch for defence**, ringing the city's walls.

**14. (A)** Walls, ramparts, gates and moats together make up a city's **fortification**.

**15. (B)** India's first coins, the punch-marked coins, were **silver, with symbols punched into the metal**.

**16. (C)** They help historians because **each mahaajanapada generally issued its own, so the symbols identify the state** that made them.

**17. (D)** The four varnas were Brahmins, Kshatriyas, Vaishyas and **Shudras**.

**18. (A)** A jaati is **an occupational community whose skills and customs pass down the generations** — like weavers or metalworkers.

**19. (B)** The great northern route was the **Uttaraapatha**.

**20. (C)** The great southern route, from Kaushaambi across the Vindhyas, was the **Dakshinaapatha**.

**21. (D)** Iron metallurgy gave people **stronger ploughs and axes to clear forests and till hard soil** — boosting farming.

**22. (A)** The chain runs **iron tools → more farmland and food surplus → people freed for crafts and trade → towns grow** — surplus is the hinge.

**23. (B)** Archaeology is **the study of the human past through what people left behind — tools, coins, pottery, ruins**.

**24. (C)** A monarchy is rule by **a single, usually hereditary, ruler**.

**25. (D)** A republic (*res publica*) is a state where **there is no hereditary king and leaders are chosen and decide together**.

**26. (A)** *Rajaa* shares its Indo-European root with Latin *rex* and English **"regal" and "royal."**

**27. (B)** A guild was **an organised group of merchants or craftspeople of the same trade**.

**28. (C)** **Magadha** grew most powerful and would anchor the first great empire.

**29. (D)** Strikingly, many mahaajanapada capitals **are still living cities today**.

**30. (A)** Shishupalgarh was a fortified city of **Kalinga**.

**31. (B)** The three southern lineages were the Cholas, the Cheras and the **Pandyas**.

**32. (C)** The age of mahaajanapadas gave way to empires by about **300 BCE**.

**33. (D)** Capitals built strong walls and moats **because their wealth and grain stores made them targets, so defence mattered**.

**34. (A)** A coin beats barter because it **is a recognised, standard medium of exchange that everyone will accept** — no need to match wants.

**35. (B)** Jaatis were organised mainly around **occupation and inherited skill** — a community of weavers, another of smiths.

**36. (C)** The Kshatriyas were the varna expected to **defend the land and rule**.

**37. (D)** A food surplus mattered because it **could feed people who were not farmers — crafters, traders, rulers — allowing towns to grow**.

**38. (A)** In a gana-sangha decisions were taken **collectively, in the assembly**, not by one king.

**39. (B)** *Sabhaa* and *samiti* matter today because they **show very early roots of assembly-based, consultative decision-making in India**.

**40. (C)** Correct order: **(ii) the Harappan First Urbanisation → (i) the Second Urbanisation → (iii) the rise of empires.**

**41. (D)** *Metallurgy* (*metallon* + *ergon*) literally means the **work of getting and shaping metals**.

**42. (A)** The great routes carried not only trade but also **pilgrimage and travel**.

**43. (B)** Guilds helped the economy because they **agreed on standards, prices and training for their trade**.

**44. (C)** Iron tools mattered in the Ganga plains because they helped people **clear the thick forests and work the heavy, fertile soil**.

**45. (D)** Early republics needed assemblies to **take major decisions and choose leaders collectively**, having no single hereditary king.

**46. (A)** "Cities that never went to sleep" means they **have been continuously lived in from then until today**.

**47. (B)** *Fortification* (*fortis* + *facere*) means literally to **make strong**.

**48. (C)** A janapada becoming a mahaajanapada is the story of **smaller territories combining into larger, stronger states**.

**49. (D)** "Punch-marked" tells us the coins were made by **stamping marks into soft metal pieces by hand**.

**50. (A)** Archaeology is the key here **because so much of what survives is physical remains — coins, pottery, ruins — rather than detailed written records**.

**51. (B)** A food surplus most directly enabled **full-time specialists such as potters, smiths, traders and officials** — people who no longer had to farm.

**52. (C)** Vajji and Magadha differed in who held power: **in Vajji the assembly; in Magadha a hereditary rajaa**.

**53. (D)** The deeper engine of urban wealth was **iron tools, farming surplus, coined trade and organised states** acting together.

**54. (A)** A state's punch-marked coin acts almost like a **2,500-year-old fingerprint of that state** — its symbols mark who issued it.

**55. (B)** A sangha choosing its rajaa by assembly is closest to today's idea of **electing leaders**.

**56. (C)** The Second-Urbanisation cities were **a fresh wave of urban life arising about a thousand years after the first faded** — not the old Harappan cities reoccupied.

**57. (D)** Monarchy and gana-sangha respectively: **Magadha and Vajji**.

**58. (A)** The clear distinction: **varna names four broad categories, while jaatis are the many occupational communities**.

**59. (B)** Each mahaajanapada issuing its own coins shows they had **their own separate authority and economies**.

**60. (C)** Best summary: the rise of **new cities and new kinds of states — monarchies and republics — after a long urban gap**.
