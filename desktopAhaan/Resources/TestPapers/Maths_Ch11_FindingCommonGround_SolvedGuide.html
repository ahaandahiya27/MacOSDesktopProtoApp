Set 1 · Q1–Q10

Q1

The HCF of two numbers is the:

  • Asmallest number that both numbers divide into
  • Blargest number that divides both numbers exactly✓
  • Cproduct of the two numbers
  • Dsum of all common factors of the two numbers

Working

✓

The HCF (Highest Common Factor) is the largest number that divides both numbers exactly. (A) describes the LCM — the smallest number both divide into. The HCF is about dividing INTO the numbers; the LCM is what the numbers divide into.

Q2

Find the HCF of 24 and 36.

  • A6
  • B4
  • C12✓
  • D72

Working

✓

24 = 2³ × 3, 36 = 2² × 3². Common primes to the lowest power: 2² × 3 = 12. (72 is the LCM — taking highest powers gives the trap.)

Q3

Find the LCM of 12 and 18.

  • A36✓
  • B6
  • C72
  • D216

Working

✓

12 = 2² × 3, 18 = 2 × 3². LCM takes every prime to its highest power: 2² × 3² = 36. (6 is the HCF; 216 = 12 × 18 is the product, which only equals the LCM for co-prime numbers.)

Q4

Sameeksha tiles a 12 ft × 16 ft floor with the largest equal square tiles of whole-foot side that fit exactly. The side of each tile is:

  • A16 ft
  • B2 ft
  • C4 ft✓
  • D48 ft

Working

✓

The tile side must divide both 12 and 16 exactly, so it must be a common factor; the largest is HCF(12, 16). 12 = 2²×3, 16 = 2⁴, so HCF = 2² = 4 ft. (16 ft is the "HCF = the bigger number" trap; the HCF can't exceed the smaller dimension.)

Q5

For the same 12 ft × 16 ft floor, the number of largest square tiles needed is:

  • A6
  • B4
  • C48
  • D12✓

Working

✓

With 4 ft tiles the floor is (12 ÷ 4) × (16 ÷ 4) = 3 × 4 = 12 tiles. (A bigger tile gives FEWER tiles, so the HCF gives the fewest tiles for an exact fit.)

Q6

Which of these pairs is co-prime (HCF = 1)?

  • A8 and 15✓
  • B9 and 15
  • C8 and 12
  • D10 and 25

Working

✓

8 = 2³ and 15 = 3 × 5 share no prime, so HCF(8, 15) = 1 — co-prime. The others share a factor: 8 & 12 share 4, 9 & 15 share 3, 10 & 25 share 5. (Co-prime numbers need not themselves be prime — 8 and 15 are both composite.)

Q7

The LCM of two co-prime numbers 7 and 9 is:

  • A1
  • B16
  • C63✓
  • D7

Working

✓

7 and 9 are co-prime (HCF = 1), so their LCM equals their product: 7 × 9 = 63.

Q8

Two bells ring at intervals of 6 minutes and 8 minutes. If they ring together now, after how many minutes do they next ring together?

  • A48
  • B14
  • C2
  • D24✓

Working

✓

They ring together at common multiples of 6 and 8; the first is LCM(6, 8). 6 = 2×3, 8 = 2³, LCM = 2³×3 = 24 minutes. (48 = 6 × 8 is the trap — the product overshoots because they share the factor 2.)

Q9

The product of two numbers is 216 and their HCF is 6. Their LCM is:

  • A36✓
  • B1296
  • C30
  • D210

Working

✓

HCF × LCM = product, so LCM = product ÷ HCF = 216 ÷ 6 = 36. (1296 = 216 × 6 multiplies instead of divides; 210 = 216 − 6 subtracts.)

Q10

For any two numbers, HCF × LCM equals:

  • Athe sum of the two numbers
  • Bthe product of the two numbers✓
  • Cthe larger of the two numbers
  • Dthe difference of the two numbers

Working

✓

For any two numbers, HCF × LCM = the product of the two numbers. This lets you find one if you know the other.

Q11

Using 12 = 2² × 3 and 18 = 2 × 3², the HCF of 12 and 18 is:

  • A2² × 3² = 36
  • B2² × 3 = 12
  • C2³ × 3 = 24
  • D2 × 3 = 6✓

Working

✓

HCF takes each COMMON prime to its LOWEST power: from 2² × 3 and 2 × 3², the lowest powers are 2¹ and 3¹, giving 2 × 3 = 6. (36 uses highest powers — that's the LCM; 12 keeps 2² instead of the lower 2¹.)

Q12

Using 12 = 2² × 3 and 18 = 2 × 3², the LCM of 12 and 18 is:

  • A2 × 3 = 6
  • B2² × 3 = 12
  • C2² × 3² = 36✓
  • D2³ × 3³ = 216

Working

✓

LCM takes every prime to its HIGHEST power: 2² (from 12) and 3² (from 18), giving 2² × 3² = 36. (6 uses lowest powers — that's the HCF; 216 multiplies the two numbers.)

Q13

Two numbers have HCF 6 and LCM 72. If one number is 24, the other is:

  • A36
  • B18✓
  • C12
  • D8

Working

✓

HCF × LCM = product of the two numbers: 6 × 72 = 432. Other number = 432 ÷ 24 = 18. Check: HCF(24, 18) = 6 and LCM(24, 18) = 72. ✓

Q14

The longest tape that can measure 7 m, 3.85 m and 12.95 m an exact whole number of times is:

  • A7 cm
  • B5 cm
  • C35 cm✓
  • D70 cm

Working

✓

Convert to cm: 700, 385, 1295. The longest exact measuring length is HCF(700, 385, 1295). 700 = 2²×5²×7, 385 = 5×7×11, 1295 = 5×7×37. Common: 5 × 7 = 35 cm. ✓

Q15

Which statement is always TRUE?

  • AThe HCF of two numbers is never larger than the smaller number.✓
  • BThe LCM of two numbers is never larger than the larger number.
  • CThe HCF of two numbers is always one of the two numbers.
  • DThe LCM of two numbers always equals their product.

Working

✓

The HCF divides the smaller number, so it can never exceed it — always true. (B) is false (the LCM is usually larger than both); (C) fails for e.g. 12 and 16 (HCF 4 is neither); (D) holds only for co-prime pairs.

Q16

Find the HCF of 18, 24 and 30.

  • A2
  • B3
  • C12
  • D6✓

Working

✓

18 = 2×3², 24 = 2³×3, 30 = 2×3×5. Common primes to lowest power: 2 × 3 = 6.

Q17

Find the LCM of 4, 6 and 8.

  • A24✓
  • B12
  • C48
  • D16

Working

✓

4 = 2², 6 = 2×3, 8 = 2³. LCM takes highest powers: 2³ × 3 = 24. Check: 24 ÷ 4, ÷6, ÷8 are all whole. ✓

Q18

Three traffic lights change every 24 s, 36 s and 54 s. Having just changed together, after how long do all three change together again?

  • A108 s
  • B216 s✓
  • C6 s
  • D324 s

Working

✓

All three change together at LCM(24, 36, 54). 24 = 2³×3, 36 = 2²×3², 54 = 2×3³. Highest powers: 2³ × 3³ = 8 × 27 = 216 s. (6 s is the HCF — the trap of dividing instead.)

Q19

The greatest number that divides both 70 and 125 leaving remainder 0 is:

  • A25
  • B7
  • C5✓
  • D10

Working

✓

"Divides both leaving remainder 0" means a common factor; the greatest is HCF(70, 125). 70 = 2×5×7, 125 = 5³. Common: 5.

Q20

Two numbers are in the ratio 3 : 4 and their HCF is 5. The two numbers are:

  • A3 and 4
  • B9 and 12
  • C30 and 40
  • D15 and 20✓

Working

✓

Numbers in ratio 3 : 4 can be written 3k and 4k. Since 3 and 4 are co-prime, their HCF is k. Given HCF = 5, take k = 5: the numbers are 15 and 20. Check: HCF(15, 20) = 5 and 15 : 20 = 3 : 4. ✓ (30 and 40 have HCF 10, not 5; 3 and 4 have HCF 1.)

Set 3 · Q21–Q30

Q21

The LCM of 15 and 20 is 60. Their HCF is:

  • A5✓
  • B3
  • C4
  • D300

Working

✓

HCF × LCM = product, so HCF = (15 × 20) ÷ LCM = 300 ÷ 60 = 5. (300 forgets to divide.)

Q22

Which of the following is NOT a common multiple of 4 and 6?

  • A12
  • B18✓
  • C36
  • D24

Working

✓

Common multiples of 4 and 6 are multiples of LCM(4, 6) = 12: 12, 24, 36, 48… 18 is a multiple of 6 but not of 4, so it is NOT a common multiple. (12, 24, 36 all are.)

Q23

The number of common factors of 24 and 36 is:

  • A3
  • B4
  • C6✓
  • D12

Working

✓

Common factors of two numbers are exactly the factors of their HCF. HCF(24, 36) = 12, and 12 has factors 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12 — that is 6 common factors.

Q24

Two gears have 8 and 12 teeth and mesh together. After how many teeth of the larger gear pass do they return to their starting position together?

  • A96 teeth
  • B2 teeth
  • C4 teeth
  • D24 teeth✓

Working

✓

The gears realign after a number of teeth that is a multiple of both 8 and 12 — the LCM. 8 = 2³, 12 = 2²×3, LCM = 2³×3 = 24 teeth. (96 = 8 × 12 overshoots.)

Q25

The HCF of two numbers is 1. Which must be true?

  • AThe numbers are co-prime and their LCM equals their product.✓
  • BBoth numbers are prime.
  • CThe numbers are equal.
  • DOne number divides the other.

Working

✓

HCF = 1 means the numbers are co-prime, and for co-prime numbers LCM = product. (B) is false — 8 and 15 are co-prime yet neither is prime; (C) and (D) are false (equal numbers share a factor > 1, and if one divides the other the HCF is the smaller, not 1).

Q26

The HCF of 1794 and 2346 by repeated subtraction/division (Euclid) is:

  • A23
  • B138✓
  • C46
  • D6

Working

✓

Euclid: 2346 = 1×1794 + 552; 1794 = 3×552 + 138; 552 = 4×138 + 0. So HCF = 138. (138 = 2 × 3 × 23.) Listing factors here would be hopeless — that's the point of Euclid's method.

Q27

The smallest number that is exactly divisible by each of 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6 is:

  • A30
  • B720
  • C120
  • D60✓

Working

✓

Smallest number divisible by 1–6 = LCM(1,2,3,4,5,6). Highest powers among them: 2² (from 4), 3 (from 3 or 6), 5 (from 5): 4 × 3 × 5 = 60. (120 and 720 are larger common multiples, not the LEAST.)

Q28

Two ribbons of length 36 cm and 48 cm are cut into equal pieces of the greatest possible length with none left over. The length of each piece is:

  • A6 cm
  • B12 cm✓
  • C4 cm
  • D24 cm

Working

✓

Greatest equal piece length = HCF(36, 48). 36 = 2²×3², 48 = 2⁴×3, HCF = 2²×3 = 12 cm. (24 divides 48 but not 36.)

Q29

For the ribbons in the previous question, the total number of equal pieces obtained is:

  • A12
  • B4
  • C7✓
  • D8

Working

✓

With 12 cm pieces: 36 ÷ 12 + 48 ÷ 12 = 3 + 4 = 7 pieces.

Q30

If two numbers are both multiples of their HCF h, then h is also a factor of:

  • Atheir sum and their difference✓
  • Bonly the larger number
  • Cneither their sum nor their difference
  • Donly their product, never their sum

Working

✓

If h divides both a and b, then it divides a + b and a − b (since each is a sum/difference of multiples of h). This is exactly why Euclid's algorithm works.

Q31

The prime factorisation of 360 is:

  • A2³ × 3 × 5
  • B2² × 3² × 5
  • C2³ × 3² × 5✓
  • D2³ × 3² × 5²

Working

✓

360 = 2 × 180 = 2² × 90 = 2³ × 45 = 2³ × 9 × 5 = 2³ × 3² × 5. Check: 8 × 9 × 5 = 360. ✓ (2³×3×5 = 120 and 2²×3²×5 = 180 are wrong prime powers.)

Q32

The HCF of 2² × 3 × 5 and 2 × 3² × 7 is:

  • A2² × 3² × 5 × 7 = 1260
  • B2² × 3² = 36
  • C2 × 3 × 5 × 7 = 210
  • D2 × 3 = 6✓

Working

✓

HCF takes common primes to the lowest power. Both have 2 (powers 2 and 1 → take 2¹) and 3 (powers 1 and 2 → take 3¹); 5 and 7 are not common. HCF = 2 × 3 = 6. (1260 is the LCM; 210 wrongly includes the non-common 5 and 7.)

Q33

The LCM of 2² × 3 × 5 and 2 × 3² × 7 is:

  • A2 × 3 = 6
  • B2² × 3² × 5 × 7 = 1260✓
  • C2³ × 3³ × 5 × 7
  • D2² × 3 × 5 = 60

Working

✓

LCM takes every prime to its highest power: 2² (from the first), 3² (from the second), and 5 and 7 once each: 2² × 3² × 5 × 7 = 4 × 9 × 35 = 1260. (6 is the HCF.)

Q34

The greatest number that divides 43, 91 and 183 leaving the same remainder 3 in each case is:

  • A4✓
  • B8
  • C20
  • D40

Working

✓

A number leaving the same remainder when dividing 43, 91, 183 must divide their differences exactly: 91 − 43 = 48, 183 − 91 = 92, 183 − 43 = 140. HCF(48, 92, 140): 48 = 2⁴×3, 92 = 2²×23, 140 = 2²×5×7 → common 2² = 4. Check: 43 = 4×10 + 3, 91 = 4×22 + 3, 183 = 4×45 + 3. ✓

Q35

A shopkeeper has 84 kg of one type of rice and 108 kg of another. He packs each type into bags of equal, largest possible size (one type per bag). The bag size is:

  • A6 kg
  • B24 kg
  • C12 kg✓
  • D4 kg

Working

✓

Largest equal bag size (one type per bag) = HCF(84, 108). 84 = 2²×3×7, 108 = 2²×3³, HCF = 2²×3 = 12 kg. (24 divides neither exactly.)

Q36

For the rice in the previous question, the total number of bags is:

  • A32
  • B7
  • C9
  • D16✓

Working

✓

With 12 kg bags: 84 ÷ 12 + 108 ÷ 12 = 7 + 9 = 16 bags.

Q37

Which pair has its LCM equal to the larger of the two numbers?

  • A6 and 8
  • B4 and 6
  • C6 and 12✓
  • D9 and 15

Working

✓

The LCM equals the larger number exactly when the smaller number divides the larger. Testing each: 6 & 8 → LCM 24; 4 & 6 → LCM 12; 9 & 15 → LCM 45; but 6 & 12 → 6 divides 12, so LCM = 12 = the larger number. Hence (C) 6 and 12.

Q38

If a and b are two numbers with a a factor of b, then HCF(a, b) and LCM(a, b) are:

  • AHCF = b, LCM = a
  • BHCF = a, LCM = b✓
  • CHCF = 1, LCM = ab
  • DHCF = a, LCM = ab

Working

✓

If a is a factor of b, then a divides b, so the largest common factor is a itself (HCF = a) and the smallest common multiple is b itself (LCM = b). E.g. a = 4, b = 12: HCF 4, LCM 12.

Q39

Two bells ring at intervals of 18 minutes and 24 minutes. If they ring together at 9:00 am, they next ring together at:

  • A9:42 am
  • B10:00 am
  • C11:00 am
  • D10:12 am✓

Working

✓

Next together after LCM(18, 24) minutes. 18 = 2×3², 24 = 2³×3, LCM = 2³×3² = 72 min = 1 h 12 min. 9:00 + 1:12 = 10:12 am. (9:42 adds only 42 min; 11:00 overshoots.)

Q40

The smallest number which when divided by 12, 15 and 20 leaves remainder 5 in each case is:

  • A60
  • B65✓
  • C305
  • D55

Working

✓

Remainder 5 each time means (number − 5) is divisible by 12, 15, 20 → it is a multiple of LCM(12,15,20). 12 = 2²×3, 15 = 3×5, 20 = 2²×5 → LCM = 2²×3×5 = 60. Smallest number = 60 + 5 = 65. (60 forgets to add the remainder.)

Q41

The HCF of two consecutive whole numbers (such as 14 and 15) is always:

  • A1✓
  • B2
  • Cthe smaller number
  • Dtheir product

Working

✓

Two consecutive whole numbers share no common factor greater than 1 (any common divisor would divide their difference 1), so their HCF is always 1 — they are always co-prime.

Q42

The LCM of two numbers is 12 times their HCF. The sum of the HCF and LCM is 403, and one number is 93. The other number is:

  • A93
  • B31
  • C372
  • D124✓

Working

✓

Let HCF = h; LCM = 12h. h + 12h = 403 → 13h = 403 → h = 31, LCM = 372. HCF × LCM = product = 31 × 372 = 11532. Other number = 11532 ÷ 93 = 124. Check: HCF(93, 124) = 31, LCM = 372. ✓

Q43

Find the smallest 4-digit number exactly divisible by 8, 12 and 18.

  • A1008✓
  • B1080
  • C1044
  • D1000

Working

✓

Must be divisible by LCM(8, 12, 18). 8 = 2³, 12 = 2²×3, 18 = 2×3² → LCM = 2³×3² = 72. Smallest 4-digit multiple of 72: 1000 ÷ 72 ≈ 13.9, so 14 × 72 = 1008. ✓ (1080 = 15×72 is larger; 1000 isn't a multiple of 72.)

Q44

  • C12✓
  • D4

Working

✓

Each bouquet gets an equal share of both, so the number of bouquets must divide 24 and 36; the largest is HCF(24, 36) = 12. ✓

Q47

Which of these CANNOT be the HCF and LCM of a pair of numbers?

  • AHCF 4, LCM 24
  • BHCF 6, LCM 90
  • CHCF 8, LCM 60✓
  • DHCF 5, LCM 60

Working

✓

The HCF must divide the LCM for a valid pair. (A) 4 | 24 ✓; (B) 6 | 90 ✓; (D) 5 | 60 ✓; (C) 8 ∤ 60 (60 ÷ 8 isn't whole) — impossible. So (C) cannot occur.

Q48

Three pieces of wire of lengths 144 cm, 180 cm and 192 cm are cut into equal pieces of the greatest possible length. The number of such pieces is:

  • A12
  • B43✓
  • C36
  • D16

Working

✓

Greatest equal piece length = HCF(144, 180, 192). 144 = 2⁴×3², 180 = 2²×3²×5, 192 = 2⁶×3 → common 2²×3 = 12 cm. Pieces = 144/12 + 180/12 + 192/12 = 12 + 15 + 16 = 43.

Q49

Two numbers are co-prime. Which of these can be their LCM if the numbers are 12 and 35?

  • A7
  • B105
  • C60
  • D420✓

Working

✓

12 and 35 are co-prime (12 = 2²×3, 35 = 5×7 share nothing), so LCM = product = 12 × 35 = 420. (7 is just a factor; 105 = 3×5×7 misses the 2²; 60 ignores 35.)

Q50

A number when divided by 6, 9 and 15 leaves no remainder, and lies between 100 and 200. The number is:

  • A90
  • B135
  • C180✓
  • D120

Working

✓

Divisible by 6, 9 and 15 means a multiple of LCM(6, 9, 15). 6 = 2×3, 9 = 3², 15 = 3×5 → LCM = 2×3²×5 = 90. Multiples of 90 between 100 and 200: 180. (90 is below 100; 135 is divisible by 9 and 15 but not 6.)

Q51

Mira jogs around a track in 4 minutes, Sam in 6 minutes, and Lina in 9 minutes. Starting together, they are all back at the start together after:

  • A18 min
  • B36 min✓

  • D54 min

All back together after LCM(4, 6, 9). 4 = 2², 6 = 2×3, 9 = 3² → LCM = 2²×3² = 36 min. (18 is a multiple of 6 and 9 but not 4.)

Q52

If HCF(x, 18) = 6 and x is a two-digit number less than 30, then x could be:

  • A24✓
  • B15
  • C18
  • D27

Working

✓

Test HCF with 18 = 2 × 3² for each option. 24 = 2³ × 3 → common primes to lowest power 2¹ × 3¹ = 6 ✓. The others fail: HCF(15, 18) = 3, HCF(18, 18) = 18, HCF(27, 18) = 9. Only 24 gives HCF 6.

Q53

The product of the HCF and LCM of 16 and 20 is:

  • A4
  • B80
  • C320✓
  • D36

Working

✓

Product of HCF and LCM equals the product of the numbers: 16 × 20 = 320. (No need to find HCF and LCM separately — the identity gives it directly. HCF(16,20) = 4, LCM = 80, and 4 × 80 = 320. ✓)

✓

Q57

Buses to two towns leave a stop every 15 minutes and every 25 minutes. They leave together at 8:00 am. The next time both leave together is:

Q60

The HCF of two numbers is 13 and their LCM is 1989. How many such pairs of numbers (a, b) with a < b exist?

  • A1
  • B4
  • C3
  • D2✓

Working

✓

HCF = 13 means both numbers are 13a and 13b with a, b co-prime; LCM = 13ab = 1989 → ab = 153 = 9 × 17 = 3² × 17. Co-prime factor pairs (a, b) of 153 with a < b: (1, 153) and (9, 17). That gives 2 pairs: (13, 1989) and (117, 221). So 2 such pairs. ✓ --- *Solutions complete: 60 / 60.*
