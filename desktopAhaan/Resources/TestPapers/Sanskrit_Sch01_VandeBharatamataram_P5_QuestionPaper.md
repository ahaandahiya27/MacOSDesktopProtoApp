1. Three readers each defend a different parsing of वन्दे: (i) a passive 'is saluted', (ii) an optative 'may I salute', (iii) an indicative present 'I salute'. Which single grammatical fact, drawn from the form alone, simultaneously eliminates the passive AND the optative reading the chapter teaches?
   (A) वन्दे is genuinely a passive whose subject is 'the Mother', so only the optative reading is wrong.
   (B) वन्दे is a present-indicative ātmanepada finite verb carrying the agent 'I' and asserting the action as a fact now — it has neither the passive marker that would make the Mother the agent nor the mood-marker of a wish, so both rival readings are ruled out by the same form.
   (C) वन्दे has no person built into it, so it could equally be any of the three and grammar cannot decide.
   (D) वन्दे is an imperative addressed to the Mother, so 'be saluted' is right and only the indicative is excluded.

2. A learner reasons: 'मातरम् is the object, and the dictionary lists माता; so to make the object I just append -म् to माता, giving मातम्.' Two separate things are wrong here. Which option names BOTH?
   (A) मातम् is actually correct; the only error is that वन्दे should govern the nominative माता instead.
   (B) Both steps are fine; the sole error is that the object should be the dual मातरौ.
   (C) The base for case-endings is the stem मातृ (not the nominative माता), and the accusative singular of this ऋ-stem is मातरम् — formed by strengthening मातृ to मातर- then adding -म्; so the learner used the wrong base (the nominative form) and skipped the required stem-strengthening.
   (D) The base माता is correct, but the ending should be -आम्, giving मातआम्.

3. मातरम् (acc. sg.) and मातरौ (acc. du.) share the strengthened piece मातर-. A student concludes 'therefore -म् marks singular and -औ marks dual as endings tacked on मातर- in the same way.' What subtle correction does the chapter's declension actually require?
   (A) The student is wrong: मातरौ is built on a different stem मातार-, not मातर-, so the two do not share a base.
   (B) The student is wrong: -औ is a plural ending, so मातरौ means more than two mothers.
   (C) Both forms do ride on the strengthened stem मातर-, and there the student is right; the genuine subtlety is only that -म् is the accusative-SINGULAR marker while -औ is the accusative-DUAL marker, so number is read off the ending while the strengthened stem is shared — the student's description is essentially correct.
   (D) The student is wrong: -म् is the nominative ending, so मातरम् is the subject form.

4. The chapter says वन्दे governs an accusative object while नमः can be construed with a dative. A student infers: 'so every verb meaning salute takes accusative, and every indeclinable meaning salute takes dative.' Why is this generalisation unsafe even within the chapter's logic?
   (A) Because नमः is actually a verb and वन्दे an indeclinable, so the student's labels are swapped but the rule holds.
   (B) Because both वन्दे and नमः really take the genitive, so neither part of the generalisation is correct.
   (C) Because the dative and accusative are the same case in Sanskrit, so the distinction the student draws does not exist.
   (D) Case-government is a lexical property of each particular word, not of the meaning 'salute' or of the word's part of speech as a class — the chapter shows वन्दे (a verb) demanding accusative and नमः (an indeclinable) licensing dative as two specific facts, from which no blanket rule keyed to meaning or to verb-versus-indeclinable can be safely projected.

5. From वन्दे ('I salute', 1 sg. ātmanepada) the third singular is वन्दते and the first plural is वन्दामहे. A student must produce 'they (all) salute'. Choosing among वन्दे, वन्दते, वन्दामहे and वन्दन्ते, which is correct, and why is each rejected form rejected by a DIFFERENT mismatch?
   (A) वन्दामहे is correct for 'they salute'; the others are all simply singular forms.
   (B) वन्दते is correct for 'they salute'; -ते is the plural ending here.
   (C) वन्दन्ते is correct for 'they salute' (3rd plural); वन्दे is rejected as 1st singular ('I'), वन्दते as 3rd singular ('he/she'), and वन्दामहे as 1st plural ('we') — each wrong option fails on a distinct person-or-number value.
   (D) वन्दे is correct for 'they salute'; in ātmanepada the 1st-singular form doubles as the 3rd plural.

6. सिञ्चन्ति ('they water') ends in -अन्ति (parasmaipada) while वन्दते ('he/she salutes') ends in -ते (ātmanepada), yet both are third person. A learner says: 'since both are third person, the endings should match; the difference must mark tense, not person.' Correct the reasoning.
   (A) Correct: the endings differ because सिञ्चन्ति is present and वन्दते is past tense.
   (B) Correct: Sanskrit has only one ending-set, so -अन्ति and -ते must be two persons, third and second.
   (C) The learner is right that endings should match; वन्दते is therefore a misprint for वन्दन्ति.
   (D) Both endings do mark third person, but they belong to two different sets of personal endings (parasmaipada -अन्ति vs ātmanepada -ते) selected by the verb's class/pada — so the difference reflects which ending-set the root takes, not a difference of tense.

7. A student writes वन्दे सुजला सुफला सस्यश्यामला माता, then writes वन्दे सुजलाम् सुफलाम् सस्यश्यामलाम् मातरम्. The chapter calls the first wrong on a SINGLE underlying ground covering all four words. What is that ground, stated as a principle rather than a list?
   (A) The single ground is that all four words should be instrumental, since they describe the means by which the Mother is honoured.
   (B) As the object of वन्दे, the noun must stand in the accusative, and every adjective agreeing with it must copy that case, number and gender — so the lone principle 'agreement of the qualifiers with the accusative head' simultaneously requires मातरम् and all three adjectives to be accusative, which the first version violates by leaving them in their nominative citation forms.
   (C) There is no single ground; the noun is wrong for one reason (object-case) and the adjectives for an unrelated reason (gender).
   (D) The single ground is that वन्दे takes no object, so all four should remain nominative as in the first version.

8. पर्वतराजः is read पर्वतानाम् राजा ('king of mountains'), a तत्पुरुष naming its own head (a king). नीलकण्ठः ('blue-throated', a name of Shiva) names not a throat or a colour but an outside person who HAS a blue throat. Using these as models, classify देशभक्तः and identify why it is NOT of the नीलकण्ठः type.
   (A) देशभक्तः is a बहुव्रीहि like नीलकण्ठः, because it points to a country outside the compound that the devotee belongs to.
   (B) देशभक्तः is a तत्पुरुष like पर्वतराजः: its head भक्तः names exactly what the whole denotes (a devotee, qualified as country-directed), so it points INSIDE itself — unlike नीलकण्ठः, a बहुव्रीहि that points to an outside bearer, देशभक्तः does not denote some third thing 'possessing' a country-devotee.
   (C) देशभक्तः is a द्वन्द्व meaning 'country and devotee', two coordinated nouns, so it names neither member as head.
   (D) देशभक्तः and नीलकण्ठः are both तत्पुरुष, since each simply names its first member.

9. रत्नाकरः = रत्नानाम् आकरः ('mine of jewels') and names the ocean, yet the word समुद्र never appears. A student concludes 'so रत्नाकरः must secretly contain the letters of समुद्र.' Replace this with the correct account of how the epithet refers.
   (A) It refers by DESCRIPTION: its members ('jewels' + 'store/mine') name a characteristic property of the ocean, and the reader decodes the intended referent from that sense plus context — there is no hidden copy of समुद्र inside the word at all.
   (B) It refers because रत्नाकरः is merely an alternative spelling of समुद्र with identical letters rearranged.
   (C) It refers because Sanskrit poetry forbids the word समुद्र, so a legally required substitute is inserted.
   (D) It refers because आकरः by itself already means 'ocean', and रत्न- is only decorative.

10. कृषकबान्धवाः is glossed 'farmer-brethren'. A student claims the plural ending sits on the head AND that the head is the member naming the broad category 'brethren', so the compound denotes brethren who happen to be farmers. Evaluate both halves of the claim.
   (A) The head is correctly बान्धव, but the plural ending actually sits on कृषक, giving कृषकाः-बान्धव.
   (B) Both halves are right: in this तत्पुरुष the head is the last member बान्धव ('brethren'), which carries the nominative-plural ending -आः, and कृषक merely qualifies it — so the compound denotes brethren (the kinsmen) characterised as farmers.
   (C) The head is कृषक (the first member), so the compound denotes farmers who are merely brotherly, and the plural marks those farmers.
   (D) Neither member is head; this is a द्वन्द्व 'farmers and brethren', so both members would independently take plural endings.

11. सस्यश्यामला is split सस्य + श्यामला, an instrumental-type तत्पुरुष 'dark-green BY/WITH crops'. Applying the SAME relation, the coined word तपःकृशा (तपस् + कृशा) about an ascetic woman would most naturally mean:
   (A) made thin BY/THROUGH austerity (lean because of penance)
   (B) the queen of austerities (austerity's ruler)
   (C) one who has no austerity (austerity-less)
   (D) austerity and thinness paired together

12. समुद्रः भारतमातुः चरणौ प्रक्षालयति packs three roles. A student labels समुद्रः accusative (because it is first), चरणौ nominative (because -औ 'looks like a subject ending'), and भारतमातुः instrumental (because it precedes the object). Correct ALL THREE at once.
   (A) समुद्रः nominative subject, चरणौ accusative dual, भारतमातुः instrumental means — only the third label was wrong.
   (B) समुद्रः accusative object, चरणौ nominative subject, भारतमातुः genitive — only the first two were wrong.
   (C) समुद्रः is the nominative subject (the doer), चरणौ is the accusative dual (the two feet, the thing washed), and भारतमातुः is the genitive of possession ('whose feet') — none of the student's three labels is right.
   (D) समुद्रः instrumental means, चरणौ genitive dual, भारतमातुः nominative subject — all three differ from the student but in this way.

13. The chapter expresses 'the Motherland's feet' as भारतमातुः चरणौ with a genitive possessor. Now consider the invented देवाः भारतमातुः वन्दनम् कुर्वन्ति ('the gods perform salutation of the Motherland'). The SAME genitive भारतमातुः appears, yet वन्दे मातरम् uses the accusative. What single distinction explains why the SAME idea (salute the Motherland) takes different cases here?
   (A) What governs the case is the word the noun depends on: the finite verb वन्दे directly takes its object in the accusative (मातरम्), whereas the action-NOUN वन्दनम् takes a genitive complement ('salutation OF the Motherland'), so भारतमातुः is genitive — the difference is verb-government vs noun-complement, not a change in meaning.
   (B) The difference is that भारतमातुः is plural here and मातरम् singular, so number forces different cases.
   (C) The difference is that कुर्वन्ति is passive while वन्दे is active, so the object switches to genitive under the passive.
   (D) There is no real difference; भारतमातुः in the gods-sentence is actually accusative, just spelled like a genitive.

14. चरणौ ('two feet') and a hypothetical चरणाः ('many feet') differ only in the ending. A student says: 'since a person has exactly two feet, चरणौ and चरणाः would mean the same in practice, so the ending is redundant here.' Why does the chapter's dual/plural distinction make the ending NOT redundant?
   (A) Because Sanskrit has no plural for body-parts, चरणाः is impossible, so only the dual exists and the ending is trivially forced.
   (B) Because the dual and plural endings are identical in Sanskrit, the student is right that the choice is redundant.
   (C) Because -औ here is actually a singular, naming one foot, so it does differ from a plural but not as a dual.
   (D) Sanskrit grammatically marks 'exactly two' (dual -औ) distinctly from 'three or more' (plural), so चरणौ asserts precisely the pair of feet while चरणाः would assert three-or-more — the ending carries number information the meaning does not supply, and choosing dual is what correctly fits a two-footed referent.

15. स्वेदबिन्दुभिः सिञ्चन्ति uses instrumental -भिः. A learner argues: 'the drops do the watering, so the drops are the real agent; -भिः must therefore be marking the subject.' Untangle the roles the chapter assigns.
   (A) Correct: the drops are the agent, so -भिः is the agent-marker and the farmers are only the location of the action.
   (B) The grammatical subject (agent) is the farmers, carried by the verb सिञ्चन्ति and the nominative कृषक(बान्धव)-, while स्वेदबिन्दुभिः is the instrumental MEANS ('by means of sweat-drops') — the drops are the instrument the agents use, not the agent, so -भिः marks means and not the subject.
   (C) The drops are the object being watered, so -भिः is accusative, and the farmers water the drops.
   (D) The drops are the possessor, so -भिः is genitive ('of the drops'), and the watering has no stated means.

16. आनन्द ('bliss') has a long आ; carelessly shortening it yields the spurious अनन्द. A learner generalises: 'so only the आ/अ pair is meaning-bearing; the इ/ई and उ/ऊ pairs are just spelling variants.' Apply the chapter's मात्रा principle to judge this.
   (A) Correct: only आ/अ carries meaning because आ is the sole vowel with two मात्राः; इ, ई, उ, ऊ all share one मात्रा.
   (B) Wrong, but only because इ and उ have no long counterparts, so the pairs the learner names do not exist.
   (C) Every short/long pair differs by duration (one मात्रा vs two) and is meaning-bearing, not just आ/अ — so इ vs ई and उ vs ऊ are likewise distinct sounds that can change a word's identity, and the learner's restriction to a single pair is wrong.
   (D) Correct in spirit: length matters for आ but for इ/उ it changes only gender, never the core meaning.

17. A student splits रत्नाकरः as रत्न + आकरः and asks why the seam shows a single long आ rather than two separate vowels अ + आ. What does the chapter's view of joins predict here?
   (A) The seam shows आ because आकर actually begins with आ and रत्न ends in a consonant, so no fusion happens and the vowels never met.
   (B) The seam shows आ because अ + आ fuses to ए, which is then written as आ for short.
   (C) The seam shows आ by accident of spelling; there is no rule, so the split cannot be recovered.
   (D) At the seam रत्न(अ) + आकर, a final short अ meets an initial long आ and they coalesce into a single long आ (अ + आ → आ); so the visible long आ is the fused outcome, and to split the compound one restores the underlying boundary by undoing that vowel-fusion.

18. The poem builds Himalaya-as-crown, ocean-washing-feet, crop-green body, and holy places. A reader proposes: 'these are four independent compliments that could be reordered or dropped without affecting the central figure.' Why does the chapter's reading resist this?
   (A) Because the four images are arranged in strict chronological order narrating Bharat's history, so reordering would falsify the timeline.
   (B) The four images cohere as one extended personification — the land IS the Mother's body — so crown-at-head, washing-at-feet, green-fertile-body and sacredness-throughout jointly compose a single head-to-foot-and-in-spirit portrait; remove or scatter them and the unified figure of the living Motherland is diminished, not merely shortened.
   (C) Because each image names a different deity, so dropping one would omit a god from the hymn.
   (D) Because the images are literal geography only, so the reader is right that they are independent and interchangeable.

20. मातृ (kinship, feminine ऋ-stem) makes accusative singular मातरम् with short अ in the strong stem, while दातृ (agent, masculine ऋ-stem) makes दातारम् with long आ. A student must form the accusative singular of नप्तृ ('grandson', an agent-type masculine ऋ-stem like दातृ) and of दुहितृ ('daughter', a kinship feminine ऋ-stem like मातृ). Which pair is right?
   (A) नप्तरम् (short, kinship pattern) and दुहितारम् (long, agent pattern), reversing which type lengthens.

21. A learner writes नमः मातरम् for 'homage to the Mother', copying वन्दे's accusative onto नमः, AND writes वन्दे मात्रे for 'I salute the Mother', copying नमः's dative onto वन्दे. The learner has made a symmetrical pair of mistakes. State the correction for BOTH at once.
   (A) Both are correct as written, because नमः and वन्दे may freely exchange their cases.
   (B) Both should take the genitive (नमः मातुः and वन्दे मातुः), since salutation always implies 'of'.
   (C) Only the first is wrong: नमः मातरम् should be नमः मात्रे, but वन्दे मात्रे is acceptable.
   (D) The constructions are crossed: नमः is naturally construed with the dative मात्रे ('homage TO the Mother'), and वन्दे takes the accusative मातरम् (the direct object) — so each word's complement-case was wrongly swapped onto the other.

22. The chapter places systematic study of vowel length in the वर्णमात्रा chapter (Ch. 13), yet Ch. 1 raises आनन्द's long आ. A student infers: 'so Ch. 1 must itself be the chapter on vowel length, and the cross-reference is an error.' Give the better account of why Ch. 1 mentions length at all.
   (A) Because vowel length is unique to Ch. 1 and never revisited, the cross-reference to Ch. 13 is indeed a misprint.
   (B) Because Ch. 1 is the chapter on conjugations, vowel length is the only grammar topic it is permitted to mention.
   (C) Because Ch. 13 actually teaches compounds, the length material truly belongs in Ch. 1, confirming the student.
   (D) Ch. 1 flags vowel length only because a concrete word in this very lesson (आनन्द in आनन्दमठः) depends on the long आ for its meaning, so the lesson notes the principle in context and points forward to Ch. 13, where it is treated systematically — Ch. 1 is not itself the length chapter.

23. Reading कृषकबान्धवाः (who water with their sweat) together with सस्यश्यामला (green with crops), the poet invites an inference about the SOURCE of Bharat's greenery. A careless reader, however, credits the greenery to a different cause in the poem. Which option pairs the intended inference with the specific careless error?
   (A) Intended: the greenery is purely a gift of the ocean; careless error: crediting it to the farmers' sweat, which the poem never mentions.

24. In वन्दे मातरम् the two words sit side by side with no vowel-fusion at the seam. A student claims: 'therefore no two adjacent Sanskrit words ever fuse, and sandhi is only a within-word phenomenon.' Use the chapter's own observation to correct this.
   (A) वन्दे sits unchanged before मातरम् only because मातरम् begins with a consonant; had a vowel-initial word followed (e.g. वन्दे इह), the seam could trigger vowel-sandhi — so word-boundary sandhi is real and depends on the sounds meeting, not absent everywhere.
   (B) Correct: sandhi never operates across word boundaries, so वन्दे can never change before any following word.
   (C) Correct: मातरम् blocks all sandhi because it is an accusative, and accusatives are sandhi-proof.
   (D) The student is wrong only because वन्दे is indeclinable and indeclinables alone undergo external sandhi.

25. A student renders सस्यश्यामला as 'the Mother who possesses dark crops' (a बहुव्रीहि, 'one whose crops are dark'), instead of the तत्पुरुष 'dark-green BY crops'. Both readings touch 'crops' and 'dark'. What decisive difference in what the compound DENOTES separates the two analyses, and which does the chapter intend?
   (A) There is no difference in denotation; तत्पुरुष and बहुव्रीहि always denote the same thing, so either label is fine.
   (B) The chapter intends the तत्पुरुष 'dark-green by/with crops', which denotes the very quality (the Mother is dark-green because of crops); the बहुव्रीहि reading would denote an outside possessor 'one whose crops are dark', shifting the focus from the colour-quality caused by crops to mere ownership of dark crops — a different referential structure the chapter does not use here.
   (C) The बहुव्रीहि is intended, because सस्यश्यामला must name an outside person, and तत्पुरुष compounds can never describe the Mother.
   (D) The difference is only in gender: the तत्पुरुष is masculine and the बहुव्रीहि feminine, with identical meaning otherwise.

26. वन्दे मातरम् is described as a complete sentence, while मातरम् alone is not. A student counters: 'but माता (the nominative) names the subject, so माता alone should be a complete sentence even if मातरम् is not.' Resolve this.
   (A) Neither माता nor मातरम् alone is a complete sentence, because a finite verb is what makes a Sanskrit clause complete by asserting an action; माता is only a subject-naming noun with no predicated action, whereas वन्दे, a finite verb, already contains subject ('I') and asserts the act, so it alone is complete.
   (B) Correct: माता alone is a complete sentence because the nominative subject by itself states a complete thought.
   (C) Neither is complete because every Sanskrit sentence needs at least one noun and one adjective.
   (D) मातरम् alone is in fact complete, since the accusative implies the verb वन्दे and so carries it silently.

27. पर्वतराजः (तत्पुरुष, 'king of mountains') and चन्द्रमुखी (बहुव्रीहि, 'moon-faced one') were contrasted earlier. Now a NEW compound राजपुत्रः must be classified. As a तत्पुरुष it means 'king's son'; as a बहुव्रीहि it could mean 'one whose son is a king'. For the ordinary sense 'a prince (the king's son)', which analysis applies and what fixes it?

   (B) Yes — अहम् is accusative, so it would clash with मातरम् as a second object.
   (C) No — the subject is built into वन्दे, but adding the explicit pronoun अहम् ('I') for emphasis is permissible, not ungrammatical: अहम् वन्दे मातरम् is acceptable, with अहम् reinforcing the already-implied subject rather than conflicting with it.
   (D) No — but only because अहम् actually means 'the Mother', so it merely repeats मातरम्.
