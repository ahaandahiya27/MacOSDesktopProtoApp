Worked Solutions

1. (B) Ignore the points: 3 × 4 = 12. Factors have 1 + 1 = 2 decimal places, so the product has 2: 0.12. (1.2 misplaces the point by one; 0.012 by adding a place too many.)

2. (A) 6 × 4 = 24; total decimal places 1 + 1 = 2 → 0.24. Both factors are below 1, so the product is small. ✓

3. (C) Multiplying by 100 shifts the point 2 places right: 4.56 → 45.6 → 456. (4.5600 is the "add two zeros" trap — multiplying does not just tack on zeros after the decimals.)

4. (D) ×1000 shifts the point 3 places right: 3.45 → 34.5 → 345 → 3450 (a zero is added to make room for the third shift). The trap 34500 over-shifts; 345 under-shifts.

5. (B) Dividing by 10 shifts the point 1 place left: 45.6 → 4.56.

6. (A) ÷100 shifts the point 2 places left: 57.3 → 5.73 → 0.573. (5.73 shifts only once; 0.0573 shifts three times.)

7. (C) Make the divisor whole: multiply both by 10 → 45 ÷ 5 = 9. Estimate "about 4 or 5 ÷ a half ≈ 8–10", so 9 fits; 0.9 and 90 are point-shift traps.

8. (B) 25 × 14 = 350; 1 + 1 = 2 decimal places → 3.50 = 3.5 m². Estimate "≈ 2.5 × 1.4 ≈ 3–4" confirms it; 35 and 0.35 are off by a factor of ten.

9. (C) 1.23 has 2 decimal places, 0.4 has 1; the product carries 2 + 1 = 3 decimal places. (1.23 × 0.4 = 0.492.)

10. (D) Scale both by 10: 48 ÷ 6 = 8. Dividing by 0.6 (< 1) makes the answer bigger than 4.8 — so 0.8 and 0.08 are wrong by size.

11. (A) 2.5 × 3.6 has 1 + 1 = 2 decimal places, so the digits 900 become 9.00. Estimate "≈ 2.5 × 3.6 ≈ 9", confirming 9.00, not 90.0 or 0.900.

12. (D) 8550 × 24 = 205200; 85.50 (2 dp) × 2.4 (1 dp) → 3 decimal places → 205.200 = ₹205.20. Estimate "₹85 × 2.4 ≈ ₹200" rules out ₹2052 and ₹20.52.

13. (A) Evaluate each: 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25; 0.5 ÷ 0.5 = 1; 0.5 × 5 = 2.5; 0.5 ÷ 0.1 = 5. The smallest is 0.25, option (A). (Multiplying two numbers below 1 gives the only value under 1.)

14. (B) 12 × 5 = 60; 0.12 (2 dp) × 0.5 (1 dp) → 3 decimal places → 0.060 = 0.06. (0.6 keeps only 1 place; 0.006 keeps too many.)

15. (C) Scale both by 100: 25 ÷ 5 = 5. Dividing by 0.05 (< 1) makes the result far bigger than 0.25, so 0.5 and 0.05 are size errors.

16. (A) 0.07 must grow to 70 — that is ×1000 (point shifts 3 places right: 0.07 → 0.7 → 7 → 70). Check: 0.07 × 1000 = 70. ✓ (×100 only reaches 7.)

17. (D) 125 × 8 = 1000; 12.5 (1 dp) × 0.8 (1 dp) → 2 decimal places → 10.00 = 10. Estimate "≈ 12.5 × 0.8 ≈ 10" rules out 100 and 1.0.

18. (B) Round: 2.9 × 5.1 ≈ 3 × 5 = 15. (The exact value is 14.79; the nearest listed estimate is 15.)

19. (A) (0.2)² = 0.2 × 0.2: 2 × 2 = 4, with 1 + 1 = 2 decimal places → 0.04. (0.4 is the "just square the digit, keep one place" trap.)

20. (D) Number of pieces = 0.75 ÷ 0.15; scale both by 100 → 75 ÷ 15 = 5 pieces. (11.25 is the multiply-instead-of-divide slip.)

21. (C) 6.3 ÷ 0.9; scale by 10 → 63 ÷ 9 = 7. Dividing by 0.9 keeps the result close to but larger than 6.3 ÷ 1, and 7 fits; 0.7 and 70 are point errors.

22. (B) Test each: 8 × 0.9 = 7.2 (smaller); 8 ÷ 0.5 = 16 (LARGER); 8 × 0.1 = 0.8; 8 ÷ 2 = 4. Dividing by 0.5 (< 1) is the only one that exceeds 8.

23. (C) ×1000 shifts the point 3 places right: 0.025 → 0.25 → 2.5 → 25. (2.5 shifts only twice; 250 over-shifts.)

24. (A) Lengths = 9.6 ÷ 0.8; scale by 10 → 96 ÷ 8 = 12. (7.68 multiplies instead of dividing; 120 misplaces the point.)

25. (B) 15 × 15 = 225; 1.5 (1 dp) × 1.5 (1 dp) → 2 decimal places → 2.25. Estimate "≈ 1.5 × 1.5, between 1 and 4" confirms 2.25 over 22.5 or 0.225.

26. (D) ×100 shifts the point 2 places right: 0.008 → 0.08 → 0.8. (8 over-shifts by one; 0.08 shifts only once.)

27. (C) 102.50 × 0.5 = half of 102.50 = 51.25 → ₹51.25. (₹512.50 misplaces the point; ₹205 doubles instead of halving.)

28. (D) 7 ÷ 0.25; scale by 100 → 700 ÷ 25 = 28. "How many quarters in 7?" = 28. Dividing by 0.25 (< 1) makes the answer much bigger than 7, so 1.75 and 2.8 are wrong.

29. (A) Other factor = 0.018 ÷ 0.06; scale by 1000 → 18 ÷ 60 = 0.3. Check: 0.06 × 0.3 = 0.018. ✓ (0.03 and 3 are point-placement traps.)

30. (C) 0.36 ÷ 1.2; scale by 10 → 3.6 ÷ 12 = 0.3. Estimate "less than 1, since 0.36 < 1.2" rules out 3 and 30.

31. (B) Weight = 1.2 m × 0.6 kg/m: 12 × 6 = 72, with 1 + 1 = 2 decimal places → 0.72 kg. (7.2 misplaces the point; 0.072 adds a place too many.)

32. (D) 9 × 9 = 81; 0.9 (1 dp) × 0.9 (1 dp) → 2 decimal places → 0.81. (8.1 keeps only one place; the product of two numbers near 1 stays near 1.)

33. (A) 144 ÷ 1.2; scale by 10 → 1440 ÷ 12 = 120. Estimate "144 ÷ a bit more than 1 ≈ just over 100" confirms 120, not 12 or 1200.

34. (C) 1.25 × 8 = 10.00 = 10 litres. (1.0 misplaces the point; 0.156 divides instead of multiplying.)

35. (B) 4 × 5 = 20; 0.04 (2 dp) × 0.05 (2 dp) → 4 decimal places → 0.0020 = 0.002. (0.02 keeps only 2 places; the answer must be tiny since both factors are tiny.)

36. (C) 1.5 × 2.5 has 1 + 1 = 2 decimal places, so the digits 375 become 3.75. Estimate "≈ 1.5 × 2.5 ≈ 3–4" confirms 3.75, not 37.5 or 0.375.

37. (D) 5.6 ÷ 0.07; scale by 100 → 560 ÷ 7 = 80. Dividing by 0.07 (much less than 1) makes the answer far bigger than 5.6, so 8 and 0.8 are too small.

38. (A) Test each by shifting the point: (A) 0.456 × 10 = 4.56 ✓ (point moves 1 right); (B) 456 ÷ 1000 = 0.456 (point moves 3 left); (C) 45.6 ÷ 100 = 0.456 (point moves 2 left); (D) 0.0456 × 10 = 0.456 (point moves 1 right). Only (A) gives 4.56; the other three all land on 0.456.

39. (A) 24 × 5 = 120; 2.4 (1 dp) × 0.05 (2 dp) → 3 decimal places → 0.120 = 0.12. (1.2 keeps only one place; the product of 2.4 by a tiny 0.05 must be small.)

40. (D) Mileage = 0.8 ÷ 0.05; scale by 100 → 80 ÷ 5 = 16 km/litre. Dividing by 0.05 (< 1) makes the result much larger than 0.8, so 1.6 and 0.16 are too small.

41. (B) 15 × 2 = 30; 0.15 (2 dp) × 0.2 (1 dp) → 3 decimal places → 0.030 = 0.03. (0.3 keeps only one place; 3 ignores the points entirely.)

42. (C) 250 must shrink to 2.5 — that is ÷100 (point shifts 2 places left: 250 → 25 → 2.5). Check: 250 ÷ 100 = 2.5. ✓ (÷10 reaches only 25.)

43. (A) 8.1 ÷ 0.027; scale by 1000 → 8100 ÷ 27 = 300. Dividing by 0.027 (very small) gives a large quotient, so 3 and 30 are far too small.

44. (A) 4650 × 35 = 162750; 46.50 (2 dp) × 3.5 (1 dp) → 3 decimal places → 162.750 = ₹162.75. Estimate "≈ ₹46 × 3.5 ≈ ₹160" rules out ₹16.275 and ₹1627.50. (₹139.50 uses 3 m, not 3.5 m.)

45. (D) 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25, then × 0.5 = 0.125. Counting places: 1 + 1 + 1 = 3, and 5 × 5 × 5 = 125 → 0.125. (0.15 wrongly adds; multiplying three numbers below 1 gives a still-smaller value.)

46. (B) Round: 0.49 ÷ 0.07 ≈ 0.49 ÷ 0.07; scaling by 100 gives 49 ÷ 7 = 7 exactly, so the estimate is 7. (0.7 and 70 are point-shift errors.)

47. (D) 32 × 25 = 800; 3.2 (1 dp) × 2.5 (1 dp) → 2 decimal places → 8.00 = 8. Estimate "≈ 3.2 × 2.5 ≈ 8" confirms it over 0.8 or 80.

48. (D) Cartons = 3 ÷ 0.2; scale by 10 → 30 ÷ 2 = 15. (1.5 forgets to scale fully; 0.6 multiplies instead of dividing.)

49. (B) 1 × 1 = 1; 0.001 (3 dp) × 0.1 (1 dp) → 4 decimal places → 0.0001. (0.001 keeps only 3 places; 0.01 far too few.)

50. (D) 6 ÷ 0.15; scale by 100 → 600 ÷ 15 = 40. Dividing by 0.15 (< 1) makes the answer much larger than 6, so 4 and 0.4 are too small.

51. (A) ₹90 buys 0.25 kg, so 1 kg costs 90 ÷ 0.25; scale by 100 → 9000 ÷ 25 = ₹360. (Multiplying 90 × 0.25 = ₹22.50 is the inverted-operation trap; 1 kg is 4 times 0.25 kg, so the cost is 4 × ₹90 = ₹360.)

52. (D) 1.44 ÷ 0.12; scale by 100 → 144 ÷ 12 = 12. Estimate "≈ 1.44 ÷ 0.12, more than 1" confirms 12 over 1.2 or 0.12.

53. (C) 0.6 × 0.5 = 0.30; 0.6 ÷ 100 = 0.006. Ratio = 0.30 ÷ 0.006; scale by 1000 → 300 ÷ 6 = 50. So the product is 50 times the quotient. (5 and 500 are point-placement traps.)

55. (B) 12.50 × 60: 1250 × 60 = 75000; 12.50 (2 dp) → 2 decimal places → 750.00 = ₹750. Estimate "₹12.5 × 60 ≈ ₹750" rules out ₹75 and ₹7500.

56. (C) First 3.6 × 0.5 = 1.8 (half of 3.6); then 1.8 ÷ 0.9 = 18 ÷ 9 = 2. (0.2 and 20 are point errors; estimate "1.8 ÷ ~1 ≈ 2".)

57. (B) Number = 0.10 ÷ 0.4; scale by 10 → 1 ÷ 4 = 0.25. Check: 0.25 × 0.4 = 0.10. ✓ (2.5 inverts the size — multiplying 2.5 × 0.4 gives 1.0, not 0.10.)
