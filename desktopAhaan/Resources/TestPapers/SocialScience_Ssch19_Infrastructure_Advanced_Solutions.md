# Solutions — Social Science (Class 7) · Paper 2 (Advanced)
## Chapter 19: Infrastructure: Engine of India's Development

Each answer is keyed, then worked. The recurring moves are to sort an example
into **physical** (transport, utilities, communication, energy) or **social**
(schools, hospitals, courts, parks) infrastructure, and to trace a facility to
the development it unlocks. Three traps to pin down once: infrastructure is far
more than "roads and buildings"; the British built the railways in **1853 for
their own trade**, and the line became India's lifeline only *later*; and
caring for public infrastructure is a **shared** duty of government **and**
citizens.

---

**1. (C)** Infrastructure is the **basic facilities and systems a country needs to function** — far more than just roads and buildings.

**2. (A)** Its two broad types are **physical** (transport, utilities, communication, energy) and **social** (schools, hospitals, courts, parks).

**3. (D)** A **government hospital** is social infrastructure; highways, power grids and ports are physical.

**4. (B)** A telecom tower, water pipeline and solar park are all **physical infrastructure** (communication, utilities, energy).

**5. (B)** Infrastructure is the "backbone" because it **supports and connects all other activity** — moving goods, people, power and information.

**6. (D)** The tomato reaches the city through a *chain*: **roads, cold storage, the internet and trucks together** link production to the market — no single piece does it alone.

**7. (A)** The claim is wrong because **infrastructure has two arms**; social infrastructure (schools, hospitals) is as essential as physical.

**8. (C)** Real infrastructure also includes **invisible systems — power grids, water pipelines, communication networks — and social facilities**, not just roads and buildings.

**9. (A)** Energy and utilities infrastructure **quietly powers and supplies all the other infrastructure and activity**.

**10. (C)** A steady electricity supply is **energy/utilities (physical) infrastructure**.

**11. (D)** Development needs both because **roads and power move the economy, while schools and hospitals build the healthy, educated people who run it**.

**12. (B)** *Infra* ("below") + *structura* ("building") = **the structure that lies beneath and supports everything else**.

**13. (D)** India has the world's **second-largest road network, after the United States**.

**14. (B)** The Golden Quadrilateral is a **highway network connecting Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai and Kolkata**.

**15. (C)** An expressway is a **high-speed national road joining cities across states, built by the central government**.

**16. (A)** The living root bridges of Meghalaya were made by the **Khasi and Jaintia tribes**, who guide tree roots across streams.

**17. (C)** Before the Dhola Sadiya Bridge people **relied on ferries that failed during floods**; the 9.15-km bridge cut hours off the journey and made the crossing reliable.

**18. (A)** The British introduced railways in 1853 mainly to **serve their own colonial interests — exporting raw materials, selling British goods and moving troops**.

**19. (B)** Correctly stated: the railways were **built for British benefit first, and only *later* became India's lifeline**.

**20. (D)** The railways are a "lifeline" because they **carry over 20 million passengers a day and move cargo across the country**.

**21. (A)** Rail moves the same goods using **about 75–90 per cent less energy** than road transport.

**22. (D)** Choosing rail for heavy freight works because railways **move large loads far more energy-efficiently and are switching to clean electric power**.

**23. (B)** A metro mainly **carries many commuters quickly while easing road congestion and pollution**.

**24. (C)** Bridges and tunnels exist so transport infrastructure can **overcome natural barriers** — rivers, hills — so people and goods still move.

**25. (B)** National highway → physical (transport); Bhakra Nangal Dam → physical (energy); School → social; Telecom tower → physical (communication).

**26. (C)** A highway "lifts" its area because it **connects local production to wider markets, drawing trade, jobs and services along its path**.

**27. (A)** With 159 airports reaching places like Leh, air transport is valued as the **fastest way to connect distant people and places**.

**28. (D)** "Aviation," from *avis* ("bird"), is **the activity of flying aircraft and running air transport**.

**29. (D)** Cochin International Airport was the first in the world to run entirely on **solar power**.

**30. (A)** Cochin's feat proves that **even huge, energy-hungry facilities can run on clean energy** — a model for sustainable infrastructure.

**31. (C)** Most of India's heavy *international* trade by weight moves by **ships through sea ports**.

**32. (B)** TEU = **Twenty-foot Equivalent Unit**, a measure of container/port capacity.

**33. (C)** A deep-water port lets **the biggest cargo ships dock, so exporters and importers gain, and warehouses, factories and jobs grow around it**.

**34. (B)** "Port," from *portus* ("harbour, gateway"), is **a town with a harbour where ships load and unload cargo**.

**35. (D)** **Jagadish Chandra Bose** pioneered wireless transmission, demonstrating in 1895 that signals could pass through a wall.

**36. (A)** The misconception is corrected because Bose **demonstrated wireless communication and built an early coherer before such work became famous abroad — and chose not to patent much of it**.

**37. (A)** A patent is a **government-granted right to be the only one who may make, sell or use an invention for a number of years**.

**38. (B)** From ₹17 a minute to among the world's cheapest rates with ~1,160 million connections shows communications becoming **cheaper and near-universal**.

**39. (D)** "Telecommunication," from *tēle* ("far") + *communicare* ("to share"), means **sending information over long distances by phone, radio or internet**.

**40. (C)** Social infrastructure **builds healthy, educated and safe communities**.

**41. (D)** Collective responsibility means **citizens too must use it well and report problems, while the government builds and maintains it**.

**42. (C)** Sustainable infrastructure **uses clean energy and eco-friendly materials, minimising pollution and harm to biodiversity**.

**43. (A)** Inclusive infrastructure is designed for **children, the elderly and people with disabilities**, so everyone can use it.

**44. (B)** Kautilya's *Arthashastra* laid out **rules for roads, irrigation canals and rest-houses, with penalties for damaging public works**.

**45. (B)** Roads were given different widths based on **the type of traffic they carried**.

**46. (D)** Penalties for damaging public works show the ancient idea that infrastructure is **a shared public resource the state must protect and citizens must respect**.

**47. (C)** A road and a power line are physical; a health centre is social — so the village received **a mix of physical and social infrastructure**.

**48. (A)** "Utility," from *utilis* ("useful"), is a basic public service such as **water, electricity or gas supplied to homes**.

**49. (A)** "Logistics," from *logistikē* ("skill in calculating"), is **the detailed organising of moving, storing and supplying goods**.

**50. (D)** A first airport in a remote region most likely **brings tourists, medical help, supplies and trade that road and rail could not provide quickly**.

**51. (C)** Infrastructure is the "engine" because, **like an engine driving a vehicle, it powers and supports all other economic and social activity**.

**52. (B)** The cleanest contrast: **physical carries goods, people, power and information; social builds healthy, educated, safe communities**.

**53. (C)** Ports, an airport and expressways are all ways of **strengthening physical (transport) infrastructure suited to each state's geography**.

**54. (D)** Reporting a broken streetlight and not defacing a carriage is **sharing the collective duty of caring for public infrastructure**.

**55. (B)** The railways are both history and lifeline because they were **built by the British in 1853 for colonial trade, yet grew into a network carrying over 20 million people a day**.

**56. (A)** Ramps and wide footpaths make it **inclusive**; clean solar lighting makes it **sustainable** — the plan is both.

**57. (B)** Electric trains and a solar airport are steps toward **sustainable infrastructure that cuts pollution**.

**58. (A)** The lesson of the Bose story is that **Indians have contributed to world science from early on — his 1895 wireless work came before such work became famous abroad**.

**59. (C)** The "highways only" argument is **flawed, because development needs *both* physical and social infrastructure**.

**60. (D)** Infrastructure is the engine of development because it **provides the physical and social foundations on which all other progress is built, and is a shared, sustainable, inclusive duty**.
