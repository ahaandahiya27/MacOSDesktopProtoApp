Worked Solutions

1. (D) नित्यम् = daily/always, पिबामः = we drink, सुभाषित-रसम् = the rasa (essence/nectar) of subhashitas. The verb पिबामः is first-person plural ('we drink'); 'sometimes/forget', 'never/fruit-juice' and 'six songs/night' all mistranslate the words.

2. (B) सु- as a prefix means 'well/good', and सुभाषितरसम् itself carries that सु. 'Six' is षष्/षड् (षड्दोषाः), not सु; 'water' is जल (जलबिन्दुः); the सूत्र in दीर्घसूत्रता means 'thread', unrelated to the prefix सु.

3. (A) Gender drives the ending. सुभाषितम् (-अम्, neuter, like ज्ञानम्) → plural सुभाषितानि. नरः (-अः, masculine) → plural नराः. The -आः plural belongs to masculines; applying it to a neuter is the error. सुभाषितम् is neuter (not feminine), is not a verb, and नरः is masculine (not neuter).

4. (A) Neuter -अम् stems form the nominative plural in -आनि: सुभाषितम् → सुभाषितानि. सुभाषिताः is a masculine-style plural, सुभाषिता is a stem/feminine-looking form, and सुभाषितेन is the instrumental singular ('by a saying'), not a plural.

5. (C) Calling subhashitas a 'rasa' we drink नित्यम् (daily) frames them as daily, enjoyable nourishment for the mind. They are not bitter/avoided, not single-use, and the 'drinking' is figurative (mental nourishment), not literal water for the body.

6. (A) पञ्चभिः वकारैः is instrumental plural = 'by five va-words', the means by which a person (नरः) is honoured. 'Of' would be genitive (पञ्चानां वकाराणाम्), 'to/for' dative, and an address vocative — none match the means-sense of तृतीया here.

7. (B) The exactly five are विद्या, वपुस्, वाणी, वस्त्र, विनय. The first distractor swaps विनय for विवेक (an intruder); the second drops वस्त्र (only four); the third adds विवेक as a sixth. Counting exactly five and naming all of them is the test.

8. (D) The five are 'va' qualities — विद्या, वपुस्, वाणी, वस्त्र, विनय — all starting with व; that is exactly why नरः is honoured 'पञ्चभिः वकारैः'. They do not start with न, their base (dictionary) forms are not instrumental, and only वपुस् (body) relates to the body.

9. (D) वपुस् praises good physique as something that wins respect; निद्रा and तन्द्रा are faults because they are surrenders of the will. So the body is a virtue while indulgence in sleep/drowsiness is a fault — mastery over the body, not the body, is the point. The body is not always a fault, sleep/drowsiness are faults (not virtues), and वपुस् ≠ निद्रा.

10. (C) Each form (विद्यया, वपुषा, वाचा, वस्त्रेण, विनयेन) is instrumental — the means by which a person is पूजितः (honoured). They are not the subject (nominative), not addresses (vocative), and not possessives (genitive). The differing endings (-या, -आ, -ेण, -ेन) all realise the same तृतीया meaning across different stems.

11. (C) वाणी/वाच् → instrumental वाचा ('by speech') is correct. विद्या (feminine ā-stem) → विद्यया, not विद्येन; वस्त्र (neuter) → वस्त्रेण, not वस्त्रया; वपुस् (neuter s-stem) → वपुषा, not वपुना. Matching each stem to its proper तृतीया ending is the skill.

12. (C) षड् दोषाः = six faults (nominative, the subject), हातव्याः = to-be-abandoned (gerundive agreeing with दोषाः), भूतिम् इच्छता = by one desiring prosperity (इच्छता is instrumental, भूतिम् its object). So: the six faults must be given up by a prosperity-seeker. The distractors invert 'give up' to 'collect', swap faults for virtues, or reverse the cause–effect.

13. (D) A gerundive (कृत्य) expresses obligation/necessity — 'ought to / must be done'. हातव्याः = 'are to be given up'. It is not a past tense ('were discarded'), not a present progressive ('being discarded now'), and certainly not praise — the faults are to be abandoned, not commended.

14. (D) With a gerundive (हातव्याः), the agent who is obliged to act stands in the instrumental: हातव्याः ... इच्छता = 'to be given up by the one desiring (prosperity)'. इच्छता is not a nominative subject of a finite verb, the line affirms (not negates) desire for prosperity, and the instrumental is not a vocative address.

15. (D) The six are निद्रा, तन्द्रा, भयम्, क्रोधः, आलस्यम्, दीर्घसूत्रता. The first distractor drops दीर्घसूत्रता (five only); the second replaces आलस्यम् with विनय, a virtue intruder; the third adds विद्या (a virtue) as a seventh. Exactly six, all faults.

16. (B) Each of the six is a separate fault: निद्रा (sleep) vs तन्द्रा (drowsiness) are different states, and आलस्यम् (general laziness) vs दीर्घसूत्रता (procrastination of specific tasks) are different vices. Merging them wrongly yields four; there are six, not four or eight, and निद्रा ≠ आलस्यम्.

17. (B) आलस्यम् = general laziness/inactivity; दीर्घसूत्रता = procrastinating specific tasks ('long-thread-ness'). They are distinct yet confusable. निद्रा/क्रोधः and भयम् are unrelated to each other in that contrast; वपुस्, विद्या and विनय are virtues, not faults, so they cannot be the confused fault-pair.

18. (B) निद्रा = sleep proper; तन्द्रा = drowsiness, the heavy-eyed sluggish state short of true sleep. The reverse mapping is wrong; neither means anger (that is क्रोधः); and निद्रा is not fear (भयम्) nor तन्द्रा laziness (आलस्यम्).

19. (C) आलस्यम् = laziness (subject), मनुष्याणाम् = of men (genitive plural), शरीरस्थः = dwelling in the body, महान् रिपुः = a great enemy. So laziness is the great inner enemy of men. It is not a small/external friend, men's body is not the enemy of laziness, and the subject is laziness — not external enemies.

20. (C) By placing the रिपुः inside the शरीर, the verse says the real obstacle is one's own laziness, to be conquered by self-effort. It does not redirect blame to outside enemies, does not condemn the body itself (cf. वपुस् as a virtue), and is motivational — the inner enemy can and must be overcome.

21. (A) मनुष्याणाम् (-आणाम्) is the genitive plural, expressing 'of men / whose enemy'. Instrumental plural would be मनुष्यैः ('by men'), accusative plural मनुष्यान् ('the men' as object), and dative singular मनुष्याय ('for a man'). The 'enemy of whom' sense is genitive.

22. (C) दीर्घसूत्रता (delaying tasks) reinforces आलस्यम् (laziness) — both keep work undone, so it is the natural ally. विनय (humility) and विद्या (knowledge) are virtues, and सत्यम् (truth) is the means that purifies the mind — none are faults allied with laziness.

23. (B) The verse cleans the body (गात्राणि) by water (जलेन), the mind (मनः) by truth (सत्येन), the soul (आत्मा) by austerity (तपसा), and the intellect (बुद्धिः) by knowledge (ज्ञानेन). Each distractor scrambles at least one pair — e.g. swapping water onto the intellect, or truth onto the soul. Only the first keeps all four correct.

24. (B) The intellect is purified by knowledge (ज्ञानेन). जलेन (water) belongs to the body (गात्राणि), so applying it to बुद्धिः is the classic mismatch. तपसा (austerity) cleans the आत्मा, and सत्येन (truth) cleans the मनः — neither cleans the intellect.

25. (C) By matching each part to a distinct means, the verse teaches that different ends require different, fitting methods — you cannot scrub the intellect with water. So neither water alone nor knowledge alone suffices for all four, and the verse plainly asserts each CAN be purified by its proper means.

26. (A) गात्राणि is neuter plural ('limbs/body-parts'), so the present-tense verb agrees in the plural: शुध्यन्ति. The singular शुध्यति suits a singular subject like बुद्धिः शुध्यति. शुध्यन्तु is an imperative ('let them...'), and अशुध्यत् is past tense — neither matches a simple present statement.

27. (C) Water (जल) purifies गात्राणि (the body), so his hands are clean; but the mind (मनः) is purified by truth (सत्येन), and his lying leaves it impure. Water does not clean the mind, the soul needs तपस् (not water), and washing is not ज्ञान, so the intellect is untouched too.

28. (A) जलबिन्दु-निपातेन = by the falling of water-drops (instrumental, the means), क्रमशः = gradually/step by step, पूर्यते = is filled (passive), घटः = the pot. So the pot fills slowly, drop by drop. It is not a single flood, not emptying, and not breaking — the point is gradual accumulation.

29. (B) पूर्यते is passive ('is filled'); the grammatical subject घटः (pot) is the patient that gets filled, while जलबिन्दुनिपातेन (instrumental) is the means. The pot does not fill something else, the drops are not filled by the pot, and it is a statement, not a question.

30. (B) The verse says विद्या (knowledge), धर्म (virtue) and धन (wealth) accumulate gradually, just as the pot fills drop by drop. The faults (sleep/anger/fear; drowsiness/laziness) are not what one builds up; water/pot/drop are the image itself, not the things being taught to grow.

31. (D) क्रमशः = 'gradually, step by step', so it directly contradicts the idea of instant, effortless gain. It AFFIRMS gradual steady effort (so that is not ruled out), does not deny that means are needed, and does not contradict the literal water-fills-pot image — it builds on it.

32. (D) जलबिन्दुनिपातेन क्रमशः पूर्यते घटः praises gradual, regular accumulation — Aarav's daily study. Bina's last-minute cramming is closer to neglect-then-rush, which the gradual-effort image rejects; the verse is a metaphor (not only about literal water); and it explicitly values the manner (steady) over mere total time.

33. (C) Delaying tasks until the last moment is दीर्घसूत्रता (procrastination), one of the six faults. क्रमशः is the opposite (gradual effort), विनय is humility, and सङ्गतिः is keeping good company — none describes last-minute delay.

34. (A) The pot verse stresses steady, gradual effort; the purification verse stresses using the FITTING means for each part. Combined: steady effort + right means. So 'any means' is wrong (the purification verse insists on the right one), speed is not the lesson, and effort plainly matters (against fatalism).

35. (A) The -अक (agent) suffix names the DOER: प्रेरकः = 'one who impels/inspires'. The abstract act would be प्रेरणा, the one inspired (patient) would be a different formation (e.g. प्रेरितः), and a place would need a locative-type derivation — none of which -अक gives.

36. (D) A motivating coach inspires → प्रेरकः; the laziness that blocks the student is the inner enemy → रिपुः (cf. शरीरस्थः रिपुः). Calling the coach a रिपुः or laziness a प्रेरकः inverts the meanings, and the two cannot both be the same term since one helps and one harms.

37. (C) A रिपुः (enemy) harms; the beneficial counterpart the chapter recommends keeping is good सङ्गतिः (company), which improves character. दीर्घसूत्रता, निद्रा and क्रोधः are faults — themselves enemy-like, not opposites of an enemy.

38. (B) सङ्गतिः teaches that the company one keeps moulds one's character, so one should choose diligent, virtuous companions. The teaching is not anti-friendship, does not endorse lazy company, and weighs character over wealth — only the first option applies it correctly.

39. (B) Bad company (poor सङ्गतिः) has spread the fault दीर्घसूत्रता (procrastination) to the child — exactly the influence the chapter warns of. It is not good company nor knowledge-building, it disproves the 'no influence' claim, and copying procrastination is no act of humility (विनय).

40. (D) दीर्घ-सूत्र-ता literally 'long-thread-ness' → dragging a task out → procrastination (a fault). दीर्घ-आयुष् = 'long life-span' (a blessing). The reverse mapping is wrong, and they are not synonyms — the shared दीर्घ does not make their meanings the same.

41. (D) Although both share दीर्घ, सूत्र ('thread' → dragging out) vs आयुष् ('life') flip the meaning. So one must read the whole compound; the first word alone does not fix the sense, same first words need not match, and the second member clearly matters here.

42. (D) दीर्घायुष् = 'long life', a blessing — it is not among the six faults (the procrastination fault is दीर्घसूत्रता, a different word). It is not a synonym for laziness (आलस्यम्) nor the name of the pot (घटः).

43. (A) सुभाषित-रसम् means 'the rasa (essence) of well-spoken sayings' — the first member stands in a genitive-type relation to the second, i.e. a tatpurusha. It is not 'X and Y' (that would be dvandva), not mere apposition, and a compound's type is not a vocative.

44. (A) 'By six' needs the instrumental of षष् (six) → षड्भिः, replacing पञ्चभिः ('by five'). It cannot stay पञ्चभिः (that is 'by five'); षष्ठी names the genitive CASE (not a number-form here); and षट्सु is locative ('in six'), not the 'by' (instrumental) sense.

45. (C) पञ्चभिः = instrumental plural of पञ्चन् (five) — 'by five (qualities)'. 'Six' is षष् → षड्भिः, 'ten' दश → दशभिः, 'three' त्रि → त्रिभिः. The number must match the count of five va-qualities.

46. (B) विद्या (feminine ā-stem) → instrumental विद्यया; वस्त्र (neuter a-stem) → instrumental वस्त्रेण. Different stem-classes give different endings (-या, -ेण) but the SAME case and meaning ('by'). They are not different cases, both are singular here, and विद्यया is a noun-form, not a verb.

47. (B) 'By humility' is the instrumental विनयेन (neuter/masc a-stem → -ेन), matching वस्त्रेण/ज्ञानेन. विनयः is nominative ('humility is...'), विनयाय is dative ('for humility'), and विनयस्य is genitive ('of humility') — none expresses the means 'by'.

48. (A) 'By water' (the means) is the instrumental जलेन — exactly as the verse cleans गात्राणि जलेन. जलम् is nominative ('water is purified'), जलाय dative ('for water'), and जले locative ('in water', a place sense) — none gives the 'by/with means' force.

49. (C) केन ('by what') is instrumental, so the answer naming the MEANS is also instrumental: ज्ञानेन, जलेन, सत्येन, तपसा. A nominative (बुद्धिः) would name the thing purified, a genitive (मनुष्याणाम्) shows possession, and a vocative is an address — none answers 'by what'.

50. (C) कतिभिः ('by how many', instrumental) expects an instrumental-plural number → पञ्चभिः. केन expects an instrumental (not nominative), केषाम् ('of whom', genitive) expects a genitive (मनुष्याणाम्, not instrumental), and कस्मै ('for whom', dative) expects a dative (मनुष्याय, not genitive). Only the first pairing is right.

51. (A) नरः (nominative) is the subject: 'the man is honoured'. The means is the instrumental (पञ्चभिः वकारैः), the recipient would be dative, and an address would be vocative (हे नर). Subject = nominative.

52. (D) A copula-style equation ('X is Y') puts both X (आलस्यम्) and the predicate noun (रिपुः) in the nominative: 'laziness is an enemy'. Neither is the other's object (no transitive verb), रिपुः is not a means (that would be instrumental), and possession would be genitive.

53. (B) The MEANS of purifying takes the instrumental (जलेन, ज्ञानेन). The POSSESSOR ('whose limbs') takes the genitive (e.g. मनुष्यस्य गात्राणि). Swapping them, or using nominative/dative for the means, misassigns the case roles — only the first mapping matches Sanskrit usage.

54. (A) सङ्गतिः = company. प्रेरकः is an inspirer (not an enemy), रिपुः is an enemy (not an inspirer — the two distractors swap each other), and घटः is a pot (not procrastination, which is दीर्घसूत्रता).

55. (B) The six faults are निद्रा, तन्द्रा, भयम्, क्रोधः, आलस्यम्, दीर्घसूत्रता. आलस्यम्, तन्द्रा and क्रोधः are all faults; विनय (humility) is one of the five 'va' VIRTUES, so it is the odd one out.

56. (D) The four means are जल (water), सत्य (truth), तपस् (austerity), ज्ञान (knowledge). वस्त्रम् (dress) is one of the five 'va' qualities, not a purification means, so it is the odd one out; water, truth and austerity all purify a part.

57. (A) नरः is honoured by the five va-qualities (पञ्चभिः वकारैः पूजितः), and the six faults are to be given up by one desiring भूति (prosperity). The distractors swap virtues for faults, mix in purification means, or replace prosperity with sleep/anger/enemy.

58. (A) Three separate errors: (1) there are SIX faults; (2) the intellect (बुद्धिः) is cleaned by ज्ञानेन, not जलेन (water cleans the body); (3) दीर्घसूत्रता is procrastination, while 'long life' is दीर्घायुष्. So it is three, not one, two, or zero.

59. (C) The chapter joins gaining the five virtues with abandoning the six faults, and joins steady gradual effort (drop fills pot) with the right means for each goal (purification verse) — a 'both/and'. It is not an either/or, not a single method with no effort, and good company alone does not give instant results.

60. (D) नित्यम् ('daily') + पिबामः ('we drink') + सुभाषितरसम् ('nectar of wise sayings') urges DAILY absorption of wisdom, mirroring क्रमशः पूर्यते घटः (gradual accumulation). Once-in-a-lifetime or exam-only reading contradicts नित्यम्, and water purifies the body — knowledge (not water) feeds the intellect.
