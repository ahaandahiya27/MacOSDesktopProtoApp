Set 1 · Q1–Q10

Q1

Heat always flows on its own from a body that is:

  • Alarger to smaller
  • Blighter to heavier
  • Chotter to colder✓
  • Dcolder to hotter

Working

✓

Heat always flows on its own from the hotter body to the colder one, until both reach the same temperature.

Q2

The unit in which temperature is measured here is the:

  • Adegree Celsius✓
  • Bjoule
  • Cnewton
  • Dkelvin only

Working

✓

Everyday temperature here is measured in degrees Celsius (°C).

Q3

The measuring range of a clinical thermometer is about:

  • A0 °C to 100 °C
  • B−10 °C to 110 °C
  • C10 °C to 50 °C
  • D35 °C to 42 °C✓

Working

✓

A clinical thermometer reads only the narrow range around body temperature, about 35 °C to 42 °C.

Q4

The normal temperature of a healthy human body is:

  • A35 °C
  • B37 °C✓
  • C40 °C
  • D42 °C

Working

✓

A healthy human body is about 37 °C.

Q5

The kink in a clinical thermometer prevents the mercury from:

  • Arising at all
  • Bbreaking the glass
  • Cflowing back on its own✓
  • Dcooling down

Working

✓

The kink (a narrow bend) stops the mercury from running back so the reading holds.

Q6

A laboratory thermometer typically measures from about:

  • A−10 °C to 110 °C✓
  • B35 °C to 42 °C
  • C0 °C to 50 °C
  • D100 °C to 200 °C

Working

✓

A laboratory thermometer covers a wide range, roughly −10 °C to 110 °C.

Q7

To measure the temperature of boiling water you should use a:

  • Aclinical thermometer
  • Bdoctor's thermometer
  • Cforehead strip
  • Dlaboratory thermometer✓

Working

✓

Boiling water (100 °C) is far above a clinical thermometer's range, so a laboratory thermometer is used.

Q8

Temperature tells how hot or cold a body is, while heat is a form of:

  • Amatter
  • Benergy✓
  • Cforce
  • Dpressure

Working

✓

Heat is a form of energy; temperature only tells how hot or cold something is.

Q9

A body temperature reading of 39 °C indicates:

  • Anormal health
  • Bhypothermia
  • Ca fever✓
  • Dfreezing

Working

✓

39 °C is above the normal 37 °C, indicating a fever.

Q10

The kink lets a clinical thermometer:

  • Ahold its reading after it is removed from the body✓
  • Bmeasure very high temperatures
  • Ccool faster
  • Dmeasure room temperature

Working

✓

Thanks to the kink, the reading stays put after the thermometer is taken out.

Set 2 · Q11–Q20

Q11

In a solid metal rod, heat travels mainly by:

  • Aradiation
  • Bconvection
  • Cevaporation
  • Dconduction✓

Working

✓

In a solid such as a metal rod, heat passes by conduction.

Q12

In liquids and gases, heat is carried mainly by:

  • Aconduction
  • Bconvection✓
  • Cradiation
  • Dreflection

Working

✓

Liquids and gases carry heat mainly by convection (the fluid itself moves).

Q13

Heat from the Sun reaches the Earth by:

  • Aconduction
  • Bconvection
  • Cradiation✓
  • Da copper wire

Working

✓

Sunlight crosses empty space, so the Sun's heat reaches us by radiation.

Q14

Materials that let heat pass through them easily are called:

  • Aconductors✓
  • Binsulators
  • Creflectors
  • Dabsorbers

Working

✓

Conductors let heat pass through easily.

Q15

Materials that do NOT let heat pass easily are called:

  • Aconductors
  • Bradiators
  • Cemitters
  • Dinsulators✓

Working

✓

Insulators resist the flow of heat.

Q16

Which of these is a good conductor of heat?

  • Awood
  • Bcopper✓
  • Cplastic
  • Dair

Working

✓

Copper is a metal and a good conductor; wood, plastic and air are poor conductors.

Q17

Which of these is a good insulator of heat?

  • Airon
  • Baluminium
  • Cwool✓
  • Dcopper

Working

✓

Wool is a good insulator (it traps air).

Q18

In convection, heated liquid rises because it becomes:

  • Aless dense✓
  • Bheavier
  • Cmore dense
  • Dsolid

Working

✓

Heated liquid expands and becomes less dense, so it rises.

Q19

During the day, a sea breeze blows from the:

  • Aland to the sea
  • Bground upward only
  • Chills to the valley
  • Dsea to the land✓

Working

✓

By day the land warms faster, the air over it rises and cooler sea air moves in — a sea breeze (sea → land).

Q20

At night, a land breeze blows from the:

  • Asea to the land
  • Bland to the sea✓
  • Csky to the ground
  • Dvalley to the hills

Working

✓

At night the land cools faster, so the breeze reverses and blows land → sea.

✓

  • Aceramic cup
  • Bneither
  • Cboth equally
  • Dmetal cup✓

Metal conducts heat to the hand faster, so the metal cup feels hotter sooner.

Q32

  • Agood conductor
  • Bpoor conductor✓
  • Cperfect reflector
  • Dliquid

Working

✓

Air is a poor conductor of heat (which is why trapped air insulates).

Q33

The vacuum between the walls of a thermos flask mainly stops:

  • Aradiation only
  • Bsound
  • Cconduction and convection✓
  • Dlight

Working

✓

The vacuum has no particles, so it stops conduction and convection (both need matter).

  • Aradiation✓
  • Bconduction
  • Cconvection
  • Devaporation

✓

The silvered, shiny surfaces reflect heat radiation back, cutting radiative loss.

  • Amakes heat

  • Cis heavier
  • Dconducts heat to your skin faster✓

✓

Metal conducts heat to your skin faster than wood, so it feels hotter.

  • Aconduction

  • Dreflection

  • Aradiation
