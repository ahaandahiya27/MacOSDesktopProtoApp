# Sanskrit — Chapter 14: शब्दरूपाणि (Noun Declensions) · Paper 2 (Advanced) Solutions

**1. (C)** The instrumental plural of देव ('by the gods') is **देवैः** (-ऐः). देवेन is the instrumental *singular*; देवेभ्यः is dative/ablative plural; देवानाम् is genitive plural.

**2. (A)** देवान् is the **accusative plural** — the gods as the object of the verb. The nominative plural is देवाः.

**3. (D)** The dative and ablative plural of देव coincide in **देवेभ्यः** ('to the gods' / 'from the gods').

**4. (B)** The genitive plural of देव ('of the gods') is **देवानाम्**.

**5. (B)** The locative plural of देव ('in/among the gods') is **देवेषु**.

**6. (D)** The instrumental, dative and ablative dual all fall together in **देवाभ्याम्** — one form does three jobs in the dual.

**7. (A)** The genitive and locative dual of देव share the single form **देवयोः**.

**8. (C)** देवौ is the nominative dual AND the **accusative dual** ('the two gods', as object) — the two are identical in the dual.

**9. (A)** In रामः देवम् नमति, देवम् is the **accusative (object)** — what the bowing is directed at; रामः is the nominative subject.

**10. (C)** 'By means of the god' (instrumental singular) is **देवेन**. देवाय is 'to/for', देवात् is 'from', देवस्य is 'of'.

**11. (D)** The dative singular 'to/for the god' is **देवाय**.

**12. (B)** The ablative singular 'from the god' is **देवात्**.

**13. (D)** The locative singular 'in/on the god' is **देवे**.

**14. (B)** The genitive देवस्य answers **'whose? / of what?'** — it marks possession.

**15. (C)** देवेभ्यः, out of context, is the dative OR the **ablative** plural — both share this form.

**16. (A)** कवि is इकारान्त; its accusative plural ('the poets', object) is **कवीन्** (long ई + -न्). कवयः is the nominative plural.

**17. (C)** The instrumental singular of कवि ('by the poet') is **कविना**.

**18. (A)** The dative singular of कवि ('to/for the poet') is **कवये**.

**19. (B)** The genitive singular of कवि ('of the poet') is **कवेः** (also the ablative singular).

**20. (D)** The locative singular of कवि ('in the poet') is **कवौ**.

**21. (A)** The genitive plural of कवि ('of the poets') is **कवीनाम्**.

**22. (D)** The locative plural of कवि ('in/among the poets') is **कविषु**.

**23. (B)** The instrumental plural of कवि ('by the poets') is **कविभिः**.

**24. (C)** The nominative dual of कवि ('the two poets') is **कवी** — NOT कवयः, which is the plural.

**25. (B)** गुरु is उकारान्त; its nominative plural ('the teachers') is **गुरवः**.

**26. (C)** The accusative plural of गुरु ('the teachers', object) is **गुरून्**. गुरवः is the nominative plural.

**27. (A)** The instrumental singular of गुरु ('by the teacher') is **गुरुणा** (-णा after the र).

**28. (D)** The dative singular of गुरु ('to/for the teacher') is **गुरवे**.

**29. (D)** The genitive and ablative singular of गुरु share the form **गुरोः**.

**30. (A)** The locative singular of गुरु ('in the teacher') is **गुरौ**.

**31. (C)** The genitive plural of गुरु ('of the teachers') is **गुरूणाम्** (long ऊ + -णाम्).

**32. (B)** The locative plural of गुरु ('in/among the teachers') is **गुरुषु**.

**33. (C)** The dative/ablative plural of गुरु is **गुरुभ्यः**.

**34. (B)** देवैः, कविभिः and गुरुभिः are all the **instrumental** plural ('by ...') — the -ऐः/-भिः family of endings.

**35. (D)** देवेषु, कविषु and गुरुषु are all the **locative** plural ('in/among ...') — the -सु family.

**36. (A)** देवानाम्, कवीनाम् and गुरूणाम् are all the **genitive** plural ('of ...') — the -नाम् ending.

**37. (A)** गुरवे नमः: नमः ('salutation') governs the **dative**, so गुरवे is the dative ('to the teacher').

**38. (B)** हस्त declines like देव; हस्तेन ('with the hand') is the **instrumental singular** — the means of writing.

**39. (D)** ग्रामात् (like देवात्) is the **ablative singular** ('from the village').

**40. (C)** वने (like देवे) is the **locative singular** ('in the forest').

**41. (D)** नृपस्य (like देवस्य) is the **genitive singular** ('of the king / the king's').

**42. (C)** The vocative dual of गुरु ('O you two teachers!') is **हे गुरू!** (same as the nominative dual). हे गुरो! is the vocative singular.

**43. (A)** The vocative plural of देव ('O gods!') is **हे देवाः!** (same shape as the nominative plural).

**44. (B)** हे कवे! is formed from कवि by changing the ending -इः to **-ए**.

**45. (B)** A -भ्याम् form (देवाभ्याम्, कविभ्याम्, गुरुभ्याम्) is always a **dual** — the shared instrumental/dative/ablative dual.

**46. (D)** A -योः form (देवयोः, कव्योः, गुर्वोः) is always the **genitive/locative dual**.

**47. (C)** The accusative singular of गुरु ('the teacher', object) is **गुरुम्**.

**48. (A)** The singular declension of देव (प्रथमा→सप्तमी) is **देवः, देवम्, देवेन, देवाय, देवात्, देवस्य, देवे** — nominative, accusative, instrumental, dative, ablative, genitive, locative.

**49. (A)** The wrong pairing is **देवाय → genitive**: देवाय is the *dative* ('to/for'); the genitive is देवस्य. The others are correct.

**50. (D)** कवीन् and गुरून् are both the **accusative** plural of their words (the poets / the teachers, as object).

**51. (C)** The object of a verb takes the accusative, so देवः (nominative) must become **देवम्**.

**52. (B)** देवौ is exactly two; for three or more gods as the subject use the nominative plural **देवाः**.

**53. (C)** नमः governs the dative, so देवेभ्यः here is the **dative plural** ('to all the gods').

**54. (D)** The -णा ending marks गुरुणा as the **instrumental singular** ('by the teacher').

**55. (B)** The nominative singular/dual/plural of कवि is **कविः, कवी, कवयः**.

**56. (A)** हरि, मुनि and कवि all decline alike because all three are **इकारान्त (i-ending) masculine** nouns.

**57. (B)** साधु, विष्णु and गुरु decline alike because all three are **उकारान्त (u-ending) masculine** nouns.

**58. (A)** देवानाम् is a single word that does the work of the English **'of the gods' (genitive plural)** — Sanskrit needs no separate 'of'.

**59. (C)** The genitive-plural triple is **देवानाम्, कवीनाम्, गुरूणाम्** (all -नाम्). The -ऐः/-भिः set is instrumental, -सु is locative, -न् is accusative.

**60. (D)** देवाय is the **dative ('to/for')** and देवात् is the **ablative ('from')** — the student has them swapped.
