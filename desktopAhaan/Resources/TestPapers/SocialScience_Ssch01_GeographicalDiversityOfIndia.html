Worked Solutions

1. (C) A "subcontinent" is a large, distinct landmass that is part of a continent but set apart from the rest by natural features. India is part of Asia, yet the Himalayas, the Thar and Arabian Sea, and the surrounding oceans wall it off — so it qualifies. It is NOT a separate continent (A) nor cut off entirely, and it is one of the largest, not the smallest (B), countries in Asia.

2. (B) The five physiographic regions are the Himalayan mountain zone, the Northern Plains, the desert region, the peninsula, and the islands — all real divisions geographers use (A, C, D). A "frozen polar continent" is not part of India at all, so it is the odd one out.

3. (D) Going UP from the southern plains you first meet the lowest hills (Shivalik), then the gentler middle range (Himachal), then the highest snow range (Himadri): Shivalik → Himachal → Himadri. Option (A) is the reverse — the order you would meet them coming DOWN from the peaks.

4. (A) India is still drifting north into Asia at about 5 cm a year, so the land keeps folding upward and the peaks keep rising. Glaciers (B) erode rock rather than add it; silt (C) builds the plains, not the summits; rain (D) does not lift mountains.

5. (B) A desert is defined by very LOW rainfall, not by heat. Ladakh gets almost no rain (it lies in the Himalayan rain shadow), so it is a cold desert. Ravi wrongly assumes "desert = hot." Ladakh does not have heavy rain (A) nor lie below sea level (C), and not all deserts are sandy (D).

6. (C) India's natural walls are the Himalayas to the north, the Thar Desert with the Arabian Sea to the west, and the Indian Ocean with the Bay of Bengal to the south and east. Option (A) lists interior features (Aravallis, Deccan, Ganga) that do not frame the whole country.

7. (A) Himalayan rivers slow on the flat land and drop deep, mineral-rich alluvium year after year; level, well-watered, fertile ground supports a huge population. The plains are alluvial, not volcanic lava (B); they are low and warm, not high and cool (C); and they are inland, not ringed by reefs (D).

8. (B) Before the Himalayas existed, the Tethys Sea lay between India and Asia. When India collided, the old sea floor — with its shells — was squeezed and folded high into the rock. People (A), frozen fish (C) and birds (D) cannot explain marine fossils kilometres above any ocean.

9. (A) The cause-effect chain runs: the collision raises the Himalayas → rivers grind down the young rock → rivers carry the silt south → the silt settles on the flat plains as fertile alluvium. The other orderings scramble cause and effect (e.g. B has silt moving before the mountains exist).

10. (D) The Himachal (Lower Himalayas) has a moderate climate and the hill stations such as Shimla and Darjeeling. (A) wrongly gives the Himadri the lowest hills (it is the highest, snow-covered range); (B) and (C) swap the ranges' characters.

11. (B) The Himalayas store winter snow and ice and release it slowly in summer, feeding the Ganga, Indus and Brahmaputra all year — hence "Water Tower of Asia." They do not pump sea water (C), and they are far from the only rainy part of India (D).

12. (C) The taller-means-older idea is backwards. The Aravallis are among the OLDEST ranges (about 2.5 billion years) and billions of years of erosion have worn them low; the Himalayas are YOUNG and still rising. So (A) reverses their ages, and rock hardness (B) is not the reason.

13. (A) The Aravallis run as a ridge along the eastern edge of the Thar and physically slow the wind-blown sand. Remove them and the desert's sand would spread further east toward Delhi. They have nothing to do with the Himalayas' growth (B), the Ganga's course (C), or the west coast (D).

14. (D) In the Thar, water is extremely scarce, so people scour utensils with sand and store rainwater in taanka/kunds — every drop is saved. Sand is not used for shine (A), there is no such law (B), and the customs are about saving water, not avoiding cold nights (C).

15. (C) The Western Ghats are taller and almost unbroken, running like a wall near the west coast; the Eastern Ghats are lower and broken into chunks where east-flowing rivers cut through them. (B) swaps them, (A) treats them as identical, and (D) puts each on the wrong coast.

16. (A) The Deccan tilts slightly to the east, and rivers flow downhill along the slope — so the Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri run east into the Bay of Bengal. Saltiness (B) does not block rivers, the Bay is not higher (C), and the Ghats' heights do not set river direction (D).

17. (D) "Peninsular plateau" packs two ideas: a plateau (a raised, flat-topped landform) and a peninsula (land surrounded by water on three sides). Southern India is both. The other options describe deserts, deltas or sea-level flats, not this landform.

18. (B) Long east-flowing peninsular rivers gather lots of sediment and meet the calm Bay of Bengal, so they build big deltas; short, swift west-flowing rivers carry little sediment into the open Arabian Sea, so they form estuaries instead. The West Coast does have rivers and rain (A, C), and deltas are not about saltiness (D).

19. (C) A delta is fertile land BUILT UP where a river drops heavy sediment into a calm sea; an estuary is a funnel-shaped mouth where fresh and salt water mix without building much land. They are not split by mountain-vs-desert (A), by size (B), or by being salty-vs-fresh (D).

20. (A) Lakshadweep (Arabian Sea) is built of coral; the Andaman & Nicobar Islands (Bay of Bengal) are volcanic. Option (B) reverses this — the single most common island mix-up — and (C), (D) wrongly make both groups the same type.

21. (B) "Low, flat, coral-ringed" describes the Lakshadweep Islands, which lie in the Arabian Sea to the west; the "steep, volcanic, forested" group is the Andaman & Nicobar Islands in the Bay of Bengal. (A) names the volcanic group, and the Sundarbans (C) is a delta, not an island chain.

22. (C) The Sundarbans is a mangrove forest in the delta of the Ganga and Brahmaputra. It is neither a cold Himalayan desert (A), a coral chain (B), nor a lava plateau (D).

23. (D) The Sundarbans, home of the Royal Bengal Tiger, lies in the Ganga–Brahmaputra delta and is shared roughly half-and-half by India and Bangladesh. Pakistan, Myanmar and Sri Lanka (A, B, C) do not share this delta.

24. (A) Moist monsoon winds hitting the Northeast hills are forced to rise sharply, cool, and dump heavy rain (orographic rainfall), making the region among the wettest on Earth. The hills are high, not low (B); rivers do not flow uphill (C); and the sea is not on top of them (D).

25. (C) The chapter's theme — different regions still forming one country — is "unity in diversity." The other phrases describe competition, control or neglect, not unity.

26. (D) India's Subcontinent neighbours include Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal, Bhutan and Sri Lanka. (A) lists distant East/Southeast Asian states, (B) West Asian/African states, and (C) countries on other continents.

27. (B) A huge, varied country cannot be understood as one flat space; grouping land by height, slope, rock and water lets geographers compare regions and explain why people farm, build and live so differently. The regions are not all alike (A), drawn by kings (C), or defined by single languages (D).

28. (D) As you go higher the air gets colder and thinner, so the three ranges sit at three different temperatures — hence three climates, plants and settlement patterns. They were not built in different centuries (A), all get sunlight (B), and rock colour (C) does not set climate.

29. (B) Himalayan rivers are fed by melting snow and glaciers that release water even in the dry season (perennial), while many peninsular rivers depend on the rains and shrink afterward. Length (A) and the Aravallis (C) are irrelevant, and the Ganga is fed by the mountains, not the sea (D).

30. (A) Perennial rivers flow all year because snow and glaciers keep feeding them; rain-fed rivers swell during the rains and shrink in the dry season. The other options misdescribe perennial rivers as desert-only, monsoon-only, or claim rain-fed rivers never carry water.

31. (B) Neha is right: a desert is any place with very low rainfall — hot OR cold — as Ladakh proves. Aarav (A) repeats the "deserts must be hot and sandy" misconception, so they are not both correct (C), and being below sea level (D) has nothing to do with it.

32. (D) A sand dune is a ridge or hill of sand built up and shaped by the wind (in the Thar, up to about 150 m). It is not dried-river rock (A), river silt (B), or coral (C).

33. (A) Because rivers flow downhill, the fact that the Deccan's major rivers run eastward into the Bay of Bengal is the clearest sign the plateau tilts east. It clearly has rivers (B), they do not flow in circles (C), and they do not run north into the Himalayas (D).

34. (C) Most peninsular rivers flow east, but the Narmada and Tapti are the exceptions that flow WEST into the Arabian Sea, against the general eastward slope. They do not join the Ganga (A), flow uphill (B), or fail to reach the sea (D).

35. (A) The Deccan's dense forests shelter tribal communities such as the Santhal, Gond, Baiga, Bhil and Korku. The others name people of Ladakh, Lakshadweep and the Sundarbans — different regions entirely.
