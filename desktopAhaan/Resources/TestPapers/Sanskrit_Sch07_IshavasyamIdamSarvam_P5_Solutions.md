## Worked Solutions

**1. (D)** A gerundive carries the force 'fit/about to be acted upon'. Only the root meaning 'to dwell in / clothe' produces the sense 'to be dwelt-in, enveloped, pervaded', which matches 'pervaded by the Lord'. The wind and speech roots give meanings absent from the verse, and the word is a participle, not a finite verb.

**2. (A)** Because a gerundive is passive, the patient (the thing pervaded) becomes the nominative subject while the agent stands in the instrumental. So 'all this' is the subject and the Lord (instrumental) the agent; the participle is the predicate, not the subject.

**3. (C)** A gerundive is patient-oriented and modal: 'that which is to be dwelt-in'. An agent noun like वासी names the doer. The difference is voice and orientation (patient vs agent), not tense or place.

**4. (C)** To express the active 'one who dwells/pervades' you need an agentive form (a participle like वसन् or an agent noun like वासी), not the passive gerundive. Prefixing अ- would negate it, and the question asks for a noun/participle naming the Lord as agent.

**5. (B)** Passive constructions (including gerundives) put the agent in the instrumental ('by X'). The gloss confirms this with instrumental ईश्वरेण = ईशा. The accusative marks an object, the dative a recipient, the genitive a possessor — none is the passive agent.

**6. (B)** The specific suffix on वास्यम् is the gerundive, whose force is 'fit/ought to be acted upon' — passive and verbal, not a concrete location. A different (krit) suffix could make a place-noun, but that is not the form here.

**7. (C)** Pronouns agree in case with the noun they qualify. तेन (instrumental of तद्) agrees with instrumental त्यक्तेन: 'by that renunciation'. It is not a nominative subject, not a dative, and carries no negation.

**8. (A)** The instrumental marks both agent (in passives) and means/instrument. Here ईशा is the divine agent of the gerundive 'pervaded', while त्यक्तेन is the means of the action 'enjoy'. The roles are not swapped, and neither is a location.

**9. (D)** A past passive participle names the patient of a completed action: 'that which has been abandoned/renounced'. It is passive (patient-oriented) and perfective, not an active present, a future, or an imperative.

**10. (C)** The accusative would make 'the renounced (thing)' the object enjoyed, losing the intended 'by means of renunciation'. Only the instrumental gives the 'by/through' sense; the case is not interchangeable, and it yields neither a purpose nor an 'inside' reading.

**11. (D)** Middle (ātmanepada) signals the fruit of the action accrues to the agent — here 'you should enjoy (for yourself)'. The passive is a distinct formation. So the active 'you should enjoy' is right; 'you are enjoyed' confuses middle with passive.

**12. (A)** भुञ्जीत is the 3rd-singular optative middle ('he/she should enjoy'). भुञ्जीध्वम् is 2nd-plural optative, भोक्ष्यते is future, and अभुङ्क्त is a past form — none is the 3rd-singular optative.

**13. (D)** The optative is built with the modal -ई-/-यात्/-इय- element; भुञ्जीथाः shows it (-ई- before -थाः). It has no -स्य- (so not future), no अ- augment (so not past-imperfect), and no reduplication marking a present-class peculiarity that would settle tense.

**14. (D)** The gerundive भोक्तव्यम् carries the same 'ought/should' modality as the optative. Future, past, and present forms each report a certainty or a fact, losing the counselled 'should'.

**15. (C)** The optative expresses what one ought to do — counsel, not a blunt order. That gentler modality fits sapiential teaching. It is not stronger than the imperative, not a past report, and not an expression of doubt about whether enjoyment is real.

**16. (B)** Sanskrit futures are marked by -स्य-/-ष्य- (भोक्ष्यते etc.). भुञ्जीथाः has no such infix, so it cannot be a future. The augment marks a past, reduplication marks a perfect/some presents, and the nasal infix marks certain present classes — none is a future marker.

**17. (D)** The classical prohibitive is मा + augmentless aorist. So गृधः is an aorist of गृध् minus the अ- augment. It is not a present, has no -स्य- future, and is not a reduplicated perfect.

**18. (C)** Restoring the augment अ- yields the ordinary aorist past 'you coveted'. The prohibition lives in the combination मा + augmentless stem; remove मा and restore the augment and you get a plain past statement, not a prohibition, future, or command.

**19. (B)** The personal ending of गृधः is 2nd-singular, addressing 'you'. So the prohibition is to the listener, supporting Q. It is not 3rd or 1st person, and the ending is not ambiguous across persons.

**20. (A)** The fixed idiom is मा + augmentless aorist. मा गृह्णीहि mixes in the present-imperative stem, which the strict construction avoids. The forms are not equivalent, and the rule has nothing to do with future meaning.

**21. (A)** The root sense is craving/coveting, an inner desire; गृध्नु 'greedy' confirms it. So the prohibition targets covetousness itself, of which theft is merely one outward fruit; it is not about ignorance or anger.

**22. (B)** मा is the particle of prohibition (a command not to act); न negates declarative statements of fact. The roles are not reversed, they do not mark number, and न is not restricted to nouns.

**23. (D)** The particle स्वित् attaches to interrogatives to make them indefinite; कस्य + स्वित् = 'of anyone whatever'. It stays a genitive (it does not become instrumental or vocative) and is the opposite of pinning down one named person.

**24. (C)** The indefinite 'whose-ever' invites the answer 'ultimately no one's', because the first line has placed all things within the Lord. That dissolves the premise of private ownership behind coveting. It does not award wealth to the strong, the priests, or the king.

**25. (D)** Making 'whose' indefinite covers every possible owner, leaving no exception. A specific 'of whom' could be read as banning only one person's wealth. The indefinite does not narrow, neutralise, or weaken the prohibition — it universalises it.

**26. (D)** The genitive marks possession or relation ('whose wealth'). Agency and means are the work of the instrumental (ईशा, त्यक्तेन); the recipient is the dative. The contrast is exactly what makes the verse's 'whose wealth?' a question of ownership.

**27. (D)** Sandhi between vowels needs a vowel on each side of the seam. Here the second word begins with व (a consonant), so there is no vowel-junction at all; the long आ is simply the instrumental case-ending of ईश. There is no आ+अ merger to invoke.

**28. (C)** A word-final म् before a consonant within the same utterance is regularly written as anusvāra: इदम् → इदं. It does not become visarga, double, or drop with compensatory lengthening.

**29. (C)** A final unvoiced stop assimilates in voicing to a following voiced sound: त् + ध → द्ध (the त् voices to द्). It does not aspirate to थ्, become a visarga, or nasalise to न्.

**30. (A)** Undoing the voicing assimilation restores the unvoiced final त्: कस्यस्वित् + धनम्. The form is not underlyingly कस्यस्विद् (the द् is the sandhi product), nor does it split at a visarga or after कस्य alone — स्वित् is a unit attached to कस्य.

**31. (B)** The anusvāra change applies to the FINAL म् of the first word (इदम्) because a consonant (स) follows. सर्वम् is that following word: it conditions the change but is not itself altered at this seam. Its exemption is positional, not a matter of case, and it does not begin with a vowel.

**32. (A)** Exactly as इदम् → इदं before स, सर्वम् → सर्वं before य: a final म् before a consonant becomes anusvāra. It does not become visarga, the velar nasal ङ्, or get dropped.

**33. (B)** यत् (relative) + किम्/किञ्च (indefinitised) yields 'whatsoever', sweeping in everything and reinforcing सर्वम्. It does not narrow to one thing, ask a question, or mark time.

**34. (A)** जगत्याम् is locative — the world as the encompassing place; the second जगत् is nominative — each moving thing within it. The contrast is container vs content. Neither form is instrumental, dative, vocative, genitive, or a finite verb.

**35. (A)** The locative answers 'where/in what': 'in the world'. 'By what means' is the instrumental, 'to whom' the dative, 'whose' the genitive.

**36. (C)** The figure sets the world-as-place against each moving thing in it, driving home that absolutely everything is included. It is not empty duplication, not a tense sequence, and not two different worlds.

**37. (C)** Built on गम् 'to move', जगत् literally means 'the moving (thing)', i.e. the transient world. It does not mean the fixed, the owned, or the spoken.

**38. (C)** Lines 1–2 turn on the gerundive 'all this is to-be-pervaded by the Lord'; lines 3–4 turn on two finite moods — the optative 'you should enjoy' and the prohibitive 'do not covet'. They are not past/future, not imperative/indicative, and not a question/answer pair.

**39. (A)** तेन त्यक्तेन (both instrumental) name the means; भुञ्जीथाः is the optative verb. The clause is 'enjoy by that renunciation'. तेन is not a nominative subject, त्यक्तेन is not an accusative object, and the verb is not passive.

**40. (B)** इदम्, सर्वम्, and जगत् are nominatives naming what is pervaded; ईशा stands apart as the instrumental agent. They do not all share one case — the agent/patient split is precisely the verse's point.

**41. (B)** Line 1 states a fact (all is pervaded by the Lord) that GROUNDS the duties of lines 3–4; this is the classic 'is→ought' move. The order is fact-then-duty, the lines are related, and line 1 is not itself a command.

**42. (C)** The bridge is the denial of absolute private ownership: if all is the Lord's, no wealth is truly 'mine' or 'another's' to covet. Remove that and 'all is the Lord's' alone does not forbid coveting. Punishment, unreality, and pleasantness are not the load-bearing premise.

**43. (C)** त्यक्तेन is the instrumental of means: one enjoys THROUGH renunciation (non-clinging). It is not a temporal 'after', not the object 'of' enjoyment, and not 'despite'. So grasping is precisely excluded.

**44. (A)** If the verse said to enjoy the world, the world is real and good to live in — only not to be grasped. त्यक्तेन qualifies HOW one holds it (non-clinging), not that it is illusory; ईशा and गृधः do not assert illusion.

**45. (D)** All-pervasion plus omnipotence make the Lord the real owner of all; that dissolves the private-ownership basis of greed and places the duty of non-coveting on the person. The other options misread the epithet as a threat, an excuse, or a spatial limit.

**46. (D)** Continuing rightful livelihood while releasing attachment is exactly 'enjoyment through renunciation'. The verse does not demand abandoning livelihood; सर्वशक्तिमान् describes the Lord, and the merchant (acting rightly) is not a कुलकलङ्क.

**47. (C)** The root denotes inner craving; forbidding it reaches the desire behind every grasping act, theft included but not limited to it. गृध् does not mean 'give' or 'guard', and the prohibition is universal (कस्यस्वित्), not priest-specific.

**48. (B)** The ending -थाः is the 2nd-singular ātmanepada (middle) personal ending; it fixes person (2nd), number (singular), and pada (middle, fruit to the agent). It is not 3rd-plural, not 1st-singular, not passive, and not the 2nd-plural form.

**49. (A)** The instrumental -एन on ध्यान/नामकीर्तन names the MEANS of remembering; the Lord is the accusative object of the remembering. So one worships the Lord BY meditation and Name-chanting — the chant is the instrument, not the object, place, or recipient.

**50. (B)** ईश is an independent noun 'Lord' (root ईश् 'to rule/own'); '-ish' is an English bound suffix of resemblance. They are unrelated except by accidental sound. ईश is not a suffix and is not cognate with '-ish'.

**51. (A)** Gender is meaning-bearing here: the neuter ब्रह्म is the impersonal Absolute, matching the verse's all-pervading Supreme. ब्रह्मन् (masc.) is a priest and ब्रह्मा (masc.) the creator-god — both personal, hence not the referent. Gender is decisive, not irrelevant.

**52. (A)** World-creation belongs to the personal deity ब्रह्मा; the impersonal ब्रह्म does not 'create' in this narrative sense. ब्रह्म is neuter (not feminine) and means the Absolute (not a priest); the words are not interchangeable.

**53. (D)** The possessive -मत्/-वत् attaches to a quality-noun (शक्ति) to mean 'possessing that quality' = 'all-powerful'. It is not a plural, a tense, or a case-ending; 'machines' misreads quality-possession as object-ownership.

**54. (D)** The -मत् vs -वत् choice is conditioned by the preceding sound: after -इ/-उ stems like शक्ति-, the -मत्/-मान् allomorph appears. Both mean 'possessing'; neither is a tense marker, and the choice is rule-governed, not random.

**55. (B)** The कुलकलङ्क — one whose grasping, hoarding conduct shames his family — is the inverse of the verse's ideal: the reverent, detached, non-coveting person. It is not 'a worse relative', not a deity, and not a verb.

**56. (B)** Only the first option gets every element right: instrumental ईशा with gerundive वास्यम्, anusvāra sandhi इदं सर्वम्, optative भुञ्जीथाः, prohibitive मा गृधः, and the detached-enjoyment/non-greed ethic. Each other option contains at least one false case, sandhi, mood, meaning, or ethical inversion.

**57. (D)** Ātmanepada is middle, not passive: the agent enjoys for himself ('you should enjoy'). Q confuses middle with passive; the renderings are not equivalent; and the ending is 2nd-singular optative, not a 3rd-person past.

**58. (B)** Detached enjoyment-as-trusteeship — using and benefiting without claiming absolute ownership — is the verse's direct application. It rules out possessive seizure, total abdication, and negligent indifference alike.

**59. (B)** Both the call to enjoy and the ban on coveting flow from non-attachment: full engagement without grasping. It is neither world-rejecting asceticism, nor acquisitiveness, nor indifference.

**60. (A)** The valid chain runs: instrumental ईशA = agent → 'all this' is pervaded (gerundive) → no exclusive ownership → optative 'should enjoy' detached + prohibitive 'do not covet' anyone's wealth. The other chains miscase ईशा/त्यक्तेन, mis-mood भुञ्जीथाः/मा गृधः, and invert the ethic.
