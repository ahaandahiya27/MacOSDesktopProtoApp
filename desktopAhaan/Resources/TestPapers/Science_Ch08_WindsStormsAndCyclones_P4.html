1. (D) Air pressure acts equally in every direction, so the large downward force on the roof is matched by an upward force from below and by the indoor air pushing out; the pushes nearly cancel, which is why the roof is not crushed even though the air column genuinely is heavy. The idea that the column is too light to matter is wrong because the load really is large; it is the balancing, not the smallness, that saves the roof. No material is immune to air pressure, and air presses on solids just as it does on liquids and bodies.

2. (C) The trapped air is the same throughout; heating expands it so some is pushed out as bubbles, and cooling contracts it, lowering the inside pressure so the surrounding pressure drives water up the tube. No air is created or destroyed by temperature. The bubbles are escaping air, not boiling water, and the slight change in the rigid glass cannot account for the effect.

3. (A) Fast-moving air exerts lower pressure, so whichever side the fast stream is on becomes the low-pressure side and the still higher-pressure air on the opposite side pushes the ball toward the stream; with the fan on the outer face, the ball is pushed outward. Moving air does not 'pull' by attraction, and a side stream certainly does affect a light hanging ball. The motion is caused by the pressure difference, not by the fan's air physically shoving the ball.

4. (D) Between adjacent equally spaced towns, the P-to-Q drop is 4 units while the Q-to-R drop is 12 units, so the steeper gradient Q-to-R drives the fastest wind, blowing from higher (Q) to lower (R). Merely having the highest pressure does not make P's gradient the steepest. Wind always moves from high to low pressure, so it cannot blow from R toward Q or from R toward P.

5. (A) Steam condensing to liquid removes nearly all the gas, producing a far larger inside-pressure drop than merely cooling air, so A crushes hard while B, whose air only contracts a little, barely dents. The metal is not softened by boiling; both cans are equally rigid. B does not start with extra air, nor does A start with a vacuum, and ice water cools air just as readily as steam.

6. (B) Both the planetary circulation and the local breeze arise because more-heated air expands, becomes less dense, rises, and leaves a low-pressure region that denser cooler air flows into. Warm air is lighter, not heavier, so it rises rather than sinks; sunlight warms air rather than shoving it sideways; and although the Earth turns, the question's scope attributes both flows to uneven heating, not to rotation alone.

7. (B) Just as deeper water carries a taller column of water pressing down, a low valley has more air above it than a peak does, so the valley feels a heavier air column and higher pressure while the peak feels less. Nearness to the Sun does not 'thin air away,' air does not lose its weight at altitude, and the effect is about the height of the air column, not the spin of the Earth.

8. (B) Cooling makes the air in the bag contract and become denser, so it is heavier than the surrounding air and the bag sinks, the opposite of the candle case where warmed, less-dense air made the bag rise. Cold air is denser and heavier, not lighter; temperature clearly does change the balance as the candle case proves; and ice does not emit a lifting vapour.

9. (C) Where air or water moves fast, its pressure drops, so the still higher-pressure fluid on the outer sides pushes the two bodies toward each other across the low-pressure gap. There is no total removal of air, the moving fluid's pressure is lower (not higher) in the gap so it cannot push them apart, and no magnetic attraction is involved.

10. (C) The sea breeze is driven by the land heating faster than the sea, which lowers the land's surface pressure; if the heating briefly stops, that pressure difference fades and the breeze weakens. Darkness does not by itself increase wind, the land's stored warmth means it cannot become colder than the sea within mere minutes to drive an immediate reversal, and the breeze certainly does depend on differential heating.

11. (A) The moisture-laden sea-to-land summer wind blows because the strongly heated land develops lower pressure than the sea; without that heating, the driving pressure difference is gone and the rains fail or weaken. Cool land does not attract more moist wind, the monsoon direction is not fixed but follows the heating, and land carries little moisture so a reversed land wind would be dry, not rainy.

12. (A) A thunderstorm needs warm air that rises rapidly carrying abundant vapour to condense into tall clouds; removing the warmth and moisture removes the engine and the storm cannot form. Adding more vapour, making the air warmer, or giving more room overhead all encourage the storm rather than preventing it.

13. (B) Light reaches the eye almost instantly while sound lags, so the time gap measures how far the sound travelled; a shorter gap means the storm is nearer. Closer storms give shorter gaps, not longer; the brightness or strength of the lightning does not set the delay; and the speed of sound does not change to close the gap, the distance does.

14. (D) Because the land air is rising it has left lower pressure at the land surface, and since wind blows from high to low pressure the surface wind must blow from the higher-pressure sea toward the lower-pressure land. Rising air cannot leave higher pressure behind, a sea-to-land breeze cannot come from a land-to-sea arrow, and equal pressures would produce no definite breeze at all.

15. (C) Fast wind across the chimney top lowers the pressure there, so the higher pressure below pushes the smoke up; remove the wind and that low-pressure draw disappears, so the upward pull weakens. Still air does not suck harder than moving air, the wind plainly does affect the smoke, and calm air does not actively push smoke back down the chimney.

16. (B) Wind speed depends on how steeply pressure changes with distance; the same pressure difference packed into a shorter gap gives a steeper gradient and faster wind, so Map 1's winds are stronger. More room does not let wind 'speed up' more; spacing matters as much as the values; and centres need not touch for wind to blow.

17. (B) The sea's slow heating and cooling, combined with the daily exchange of sea and land breezes, moderates the coast's temperatures while the inland desert, far from any such buffer, swings widely. Same-latitude places receive comparable sunlight, sea air does have weight and does store heat, and the sea's moderating influence is real, not coincidental.

18. (B) The cycle is fed by warm, moist air from the sea; over cooler water less warm vapour is supplied, less condensation heat is released, the low deepens more slowly and the storm weakens. Cool water gives the storm less, not more, energy; a cyclone keeps needing the warm sea to sustain itself; and the wind is not 'frozen,' it simply loses its energy source.

19. (D) A tornado is a narrow violent column, the eye is a small calm core perhaps tens of kilometres across, a thunderstorm cell is larger, and the whole cyclone spans hundreds of kilometres, so smallest to largest runs tornado, eye, thunderstorm cell, cyclone. The other orders wrongly make the cyclone smallest, place the tornado as larger than the eye is meant to be reversed, or scramble the relative sizes.

(C) With trapped air above, that air presses down on the water and adds to the downward load, so the upward air pressure on the card may be overcome and the card falls; in the full case the upward air pressure alone exceeds the water's weight. 21. (D) Modern satellite and radar warnings give enough advance notice for people to evacuate to shelters, which is the chief reason cyclone deaths have fallen even when storm strength is similar.
