# Science — Chapter 11: Transportation in Animals and Plants · Paper 2 (Advanced) Solutions

**1. (C)** The human heart has four chambers — two atria and two ventricles.

**2. (A)** The two upper chambers are the atria (auricles).

**3. (D)** The two lower, thicker-walled chambers are the ventricles.

**4. (B)** Arteries carry blood away from the heart.

**5. (B)** Veins carry blood back to the heart.

**6. (D)** Exchange of gases and nutrients between blood and cells happens in the thin-walled capillaries.

**7. (A)** Capillary walls are only one cell thick, which lets substances pass easily.

**8. (C)** Plasma is the fluid part of blood that carries dissolved nutrients and wastes.

**9. (A)** Red blood cells carry oxygen.

**10. (C)** Haemoglobin, the iron-containing pigment of red cells, binds oxygen.

**11. (D)** White blood cells fight infection.

**12. (B)** Platelets help the blood clot at a wound.

**13. (D)** Arteries have thick elastic walls because they carry high-pressure blood pumped from the heart.

**14. (B)** Valves in veins prevent the backward flow of blood.

**15. (C)** The rhythmic expansion of an artery felt at the wrist is the pulse.

**16. (A)** A resting adult heart rate is about 70–80 beats per minute.

**17. (C)** In double circulation the blood passes through the heart twice per complete body circuit.

**18. (A)** The pulmonary circuit carries blood between the heart and the lungs.

**19. (B)** Oxygen is loaded into the blood in the lungs.

**20. (D)** The aorta is the largest artery, carrying oxygen-rich blood from the heart to the body.

**21. (A)** Oxygen-rich blood from the lungs enters the left atrium.

**22. (D)** Removing harmful metabolic wastes is excretion.

**23. (B)** The kidneys are the bean-shaped organs that filter blood to make urine.

**24. (C)** The microscopic filtering units of the kidney are nephrons.

**25. (B)** Each kidney contains about a million nephrons.

**26. (C)** Urine flows kidney → ureter → bladder → urethra.

**27. (A)** The ureter carries urine from a kidney to the bladder.

**28. (D)** Urea is the main nitrogenous waste removed in urine.

**29. (D)** When kidneys fail, the blood is cleaned artificially by dialysis.

**30. (A)** The skin also excretes salts and water as sweat.

**31. (C)** Xylem carries water and minerals upward from the roots.

**32. (B)** Phloem carries food (sugar) made in the leaves to the rest of the plant.

**33. (C)** Xylem water movement is essentially one-way: root to leaf (upward).

**34. (B)** Loss of water vapour from leaves through stomata is transpiration.

**35. (D)** The upward pull of water is generated mainly by transpiration from the leaves.

**36. (A)** Transpiration occurs mainly through the leaf pores called stomata.

**37. (A)** Phloem can move food in both directions, up and down, as the plant needs.

**38. (B)** Mature xylem cells are dead, forming hollow conducting tubes.

**39. (D)** Arteries carry blood away from the heart; veins carry it back.

**40. (C)** The sugar mainly transported in phloem is sucrose.

**41. (D)** Cohesion keeps the water column unbroken as transpiration pulls it up the xylem.

**42. (C)** "120/80" is a blood-pressure reading (systolic over diastolic).

**43. (A)** White blood cells defend the body against germs, unlike oxygen-carrying red cells.

**44. (B)** Without working platelets, a person would bleed for a long time from small cuts.

**45. (B)** Capillaries connect the smallest arteries to the smallest veins.

**46. (D)** In a nephron, useful substances such as glucose and most water are reabsorbed into the blood.

**47. (C)** Double circulation keeps oxygen-rich and oxygen-poor blood separate, delivering oxygen efficiently.

**48. (A)** Water travels soil → root → xylem → stem → leaf → out via stomata.

**49. (A)** On a hot day a plant wilts because transpiration outpaces the water the roots absorb.

**50. (D)** Deoxygenated blood from the body first enters the right atrium.

**51. (C)** The excretory system (kidneys) removes nitrogenous waste such as urea.

**52. (B)** In dialysis, small waste molecules diffuse across a semipermeable membrane from blood into the dialysate.

**53. (C)** Thin capillary walls allow easy exchange of gases and nutrients with the cells.

**54. (D)** Root hairs increase the surface area for absorbing water and minerals from the soil.

**55. (B)** Sealing the stomata would reduce transpiration, slowing the upward pull of water.

**56. (A)** Blood is red because of the haemoglobin in red blood cells.

**57. (B)** The systemic circuit carries oxygen-rich blood from the heart to the body tissues.

**58. (A)** Animals use a heart and blood vessels; plants use xylem and phloem with no pump.

**59. (C)** When dehydrated, the kidneys conserve water and make less, more concentrated urine.

**60. (D)** Large bodies need dedicated transport systems to move materials to and from every cell.
