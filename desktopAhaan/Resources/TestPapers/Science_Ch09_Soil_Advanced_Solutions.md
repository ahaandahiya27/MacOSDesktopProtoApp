# Science — Chapter 9: Soil · Paper 2 (Advanced) Solutions

**1. (B)** Soil is a living mixture of broken-down rock, decayed organic matter (humus), water, air and micro-organisms.

**2. (B)** The vertical section from surface to bedrock is the soil profile.

**3. (B)** The dark, fertile organic matter from decayed remains is humus.

**4. (C)** The surface organic layer of dead leaves and humus is the O horizon.

**5. (A)** The dark topsoil where most roots grow is the A horizon.

**6. (B)** The B horizon (subsoil) is lighter, holding minerals leached down from above.

**7. (C)** The weathered parent-rock layer is the C horizon.

**8. (B)** A fertile soil is roughly 45% minerals, 25% air, 25% water and 5% humus.

**9. (B)** Rock is broken into soil particles by weathering — water, wind, ice and biological action.

**10. (B)** The three main soil types are classified by mineral-particle size.

**11. (B)** Sandy soil is mostly large particles with big gaps between them.

**12. (B)** Big gaps make sandy soil drain quickly and hold little water.

**13. (A)** Clayey soil is mostly very small particles with tiny gaps.

**14. (B)** Clayey soil holds water well and can become waterlogged.

**15. (C)** A balanced mix of sand, silt and clay with humus is loamy soil.

**16. (C)** Loam is the best soil for growing most crops.

**17. (B)** Percolation is the downward movement of water through the soil.

**18. (B)** Percolation rate = volume of water ÷ percolation time.

**19. (B)** 200 mL ÷ 20 min = 10 mL/min.

**20. (C)** Sandy soil, with its large gaps, has the highest percolation rate.

**21. (B)** Clayey soil, with tiny gaps, has the lowest percolation rate.

**22. (B)** Faster drainage means higher percolation, so Sample X is more sandy than Y.

**23. (B)** Cactus, watermelon and groundnut suit well-drained sandy soil.

**24. (B)** Paddy needs standing water, so it grows best in water-holding clayey soil.

**25. (B)** Clayey soil has the highest water-holding capacity.

**26. (B)** Soil erosion is the removal of topsoil by water, wind or human action.

**27. (C)** The fertile topsoil (A horizon) is the most valuable and most easily eroded layer.

**28. (B)** Deforestation and overgrazing are major human causes of erosion.

**29. (B)** Cutting a hillside into flat stepped strips is terracing.

**30. (B)** Ploughing along the slope's elevation lines is contour ploughing.

**31. (B)** A plant grown to cover bare soil, stop erosion and add nutrients is a cover crop.

**32. (B)** Growing different crops in successive seasons is crop rotation.

**33. (B)** Legumes host Rhizobium bacteria that fix atmospheric nitrogen into the soil.

**34. (A)** The nitrogen-fixing bacteria in legume root nodules are Rhizobium.

**35. (A)** Cereals such as wheat, rice and maize are heavy nitrogen feeders.

**36. (B)** Tree and grass roots bind the soil and slow runoff, reducing erosion.

**37. (B)** Top to bottom the horizons run O → A → B → C → bedrock.

**38. (B)** Desert soils are sandy, drain too fast, and are low in water and nutrients, so few crops grow.

**39. (B)** Besides minerals and humus, fertile soil holds millions of micro-organisms.

**40. (B)** Clay is useful for pots and bricks because it is sticky and mouldable when wet, then hard when dried or baked.

**41. (B)** With its high water retention, a clayey field is more likely to become waterlogged after heavy rain.

**42. (B)** Loam suits most crops because it holds enough water and air while staying nutrient-rich.

**43. (A)** Soil supplies essential nutrients such as nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium.

**44. (B)** Weathering that makes soil involves water, wind, ice and biological action together.

**45. (B)** Mixing sand and organic matter into heavy clay improves its drainage.

**46. (B)** Over-ploughing and bare fields expose loose topsoil to wind and rain, worsening erosion.

**47. (B)** Percolation rate is expressed in mL/min (or mm/hour).

**48. (A)** 150 mL ÷ 30 min = 5 mL/min.

**49. (B)** Humus improves both fertility and water-holding capacity.

**50. (B)** The A horizon is dark topsoil rich in humus and roots, unlike the lifeless weathered C horizon.

**51. (B)** Terraces make water move sideways instead of rushing downhill, so soil is not washed away.

**52. (B)** Continuous wheat (a heavy feeder) without rotation drains soil nitrogen and fertility.

**53. (B)** Sandy soil is well aerated; clay holds more water but less air.

**54. (B)** Forming a few centimetres of topsoil naturally takes hundreds to thousands of years.

**55. (B)** Gully erosion during heavy rain on bare slopes is a form of water erosion.

**56. (B)** Windbreaks/trees and keeping the soil covered reduce wind erosion.

**57. (B)** Healthy soil matters above all because it supports the plants that feed us and most land life.

**58. (B)** The subsoil (B horizon) is lighter in colour and poorer in humus than the topsoil.

**59. (B)** Terracing, contour ploughing, cover crops and crop rotation together best conserve soil.

**60. (B)** Soil is a living, layered, renewable-but-easily-lost resource worth conserving.
