**Marking scheme:** **+4** for each correct answer · **−1** for each wrong answer · **0** if unattempted.

**Instructions:** Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. Reason from the physical geography of India — its five regions, rivers, and how landforms shape life. Watch the classic traps: "all deserts are hot" (a desert means low rainfall, not heat), "tall mountains must be old" (the young Himalayas are still rising; the worn Aravallis are ancient), and confusing a delta with an estuary or the coral Lakshadweep with the volcanic Andamans. Do not write on this paper — mark answers on your answer sheet.

1. India is called a "subcontinent" mainly because it is:
   (A) a separate continent entirely cut off from Asia
   (B) the smallest country in Asia
   (C) a large, distinct landmass that is part of Asia but set apart by natural features
   (D) an island lying in the middle of the Indian Ocean

2. A geographer lists India's five physiographic regions. Which of the following does NOT belong on that list?
   (A) the great mountain zone of the Himalayas
   (B) a frozen polar continent
   (C) the desert region
   (D) the islands

3. Climbing from the southern plains up to the highest snow peaks, in what order do you cross the three Himalayan ranges?
   (A) Himadri → Himachal → Shivalik
   (B) Himachal → Shivalik → Himadri
   (C) Himadri → Shivalik → Himachal
   (D) Shivalik → Himachal → Himadri

4. The Himalayas are still growing taller today. The best reason is that:
   (A) the Indian landmass keeps pushing north into Asia at about 5 cm a year
   (B) glaciers add new rock to the peaks each winter
   (C) rivers pile up silt on the mountain tops
   (D) the monsoon rains make the soil swell

5. Ravi insists Ladakh cannot be a desert "because deserts are hot and Ladakh is freezing." His mistake is that:
   (A) Ladakh actually has heavy rainfall
   (B) a desert is defined by very low rainfall, not by heat
   (C) Ladakh lies below sea level
   (D) deserts must always be covered in sand dunes

6. Which natural features together act as India's "walls" to the north, west, and south?
   (A) the Aravallis, the Deccan Plateau, and the Ganga
   (B) the Eastern Ghats, the Thar, and Chilika Lake

   (D) the Shivaliks, the Sundarbans, and the Lakshadweep islands

7. The Northern Plains can support a very large population mainly because:
   (A) Himalayan rivers spread deep, mineral-rich alluvial soil over flat, well-watered land

   (C) they sit at a very high, cool altitude
   (D) they are ringed by coral reefs that protect the farms

8. Seashell fossils are sometimes found high in the Himalayan rock. This is best explained by the fact that:
   (A) ancient people carried shells up the mountains
   (B) the floor of an old sea (the Tethys) was folded upward when India collided with Asia
   (C) glaciers froze fish into the rock
   (D) the shells fell from passing birds over millions of years

9. Trace the chain that links a slow plate collision to the rich soil of the Northern Plains. Which ordering is correct?
   (A) collision raises the Himalayas → rivers grind the rock → rivers carry silt south → silt settles on the flat plains as fertile alluvium
   (B) rivers carry silt south → collision raises the Himalayas → silt settles → rock grinds away
   (C) silt settles on the plains → the Himalayas rise → rivers form → collision happens
   (D) the Himalayas erode away completely → a sea floods the plains → silt sinks → rock rises

10. Which pairing of a Himalayan range with its character is correct?
    (A) Himadri → lowest hills with dense forests
    (B) Himachal → snow-covered all year with the giant peaks
    (C) Shivalik → moderate climate with famous hill stations
    (D) Himachal → moderate climate with hill stations like Shimla and Darjeeling

11. The Himalayas are nicknamed the "Water Tower of Asia" because they:
    (A) collect monsoon rain in giant natural reservoirs of stone
    (B) store water as snow and ice and release it in summer to feed rivers like the Ganga, Indus and Brahmaputra

12. The Aravalli Hills are low and rounded today, whereas the Himalayas are towering and jagged. The reason is that:
    (A) the Aravallis are far younger and have not yet risen
    (B) the Himalayas are made of much harder rock
    (C) the Aravallis are among the oldest ranges and billions of years of erosion have worn them down
    (D) the Aravallis were never tall to begin with

13. If the Aravalli Hills were completely flattened by mining, the most likely consequence would be that:
    (A) the Thar Desert's sand would spread further east toward cities like Delhi
    (B) the Himalayas would stop growing
    (C) the Ganga would change direction
    (D) India would lose its western coastline

14. People of the Thar Desert scrub their utensils with sand and store rainwater in tanks called taanka or kunds. These customs arise mainly because:
    (A) sand is believed to make utensils shinier than water
    (B) the law forbids the use of river water there
    (C) the nights are too cold to wash with water

15. Which statement correctly contrasts the Western Ghats with the Eastern Ghats?
    (A) Both are equally tall, unbroken walls of the same rock
    (B) The Eastern Ghats are taller and unbroken; the Western Ghats are low and broken

    (D) The Western Ghats lie on the east coast and the Eastern Ghats on the west coast

16. Most major rivers of the Deccan Plateau — the Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri — empty into the Bay of Bengal in the east because:
    (A) the plateau tilts slightly toward the east, and rivers flow downhill along the slope
    (B) the Arabian Sea is too salty for rivers to enter
    (C) the Bay of Bengal is at a higher level and pulls the water in
    (D) the Western Ghats are lower than the Eastern Ghats

17. Southern India is called a "peninsular plateau." The two ideas in that phrase are that it is:
    (A) flat at sea level and surrounded by mountains on all four sides
    (B) a sandy desert AND a chain of islands

18. The East Coast has wide river deltas (Godavari, Krishna), but the West Coast mostly does not. The geographical reason is that:
    (A) the West Coast gets no rain at all

19. A delta and an estuary are NOT the same. The key difference is that:
    (A) a delta forms in the mountains while an estuary forms in a desert
    (B) an estuary is always larger than a delta
    (C) a delta is fertile land built up where a river drops heavy sediment in a calm sea, while an estuary is a funnel-shaped mouth where fresh and salt water mix without building much land

20. Which pairing of India's two island groups with how they formed is CORRECT?
    (A) Lakshadweep — coral; Andaman & Nicobar — volcanic
    (B) Lakshadweep — volcanic; Andaman & Nicobar — coral
    (C) Both groups are coral islands
    (D) Both groups are volcanic islands

21. A ship's log notes "low, flat islands ringed by coral reefs" near one Indian group and "steep, forested hills of volcanic rock" near another. The first group and its sea are:
    (A) the Andaman & Nicobar Islands, in the Bay of Bengal

24. The hills of the Northeast (Garo, Khasi, Jaintia) receive some of the highest rainfall on Earth because:

    (B) they are the lowest land in India, so rain collects there
    (C) rivers flow uphill there and evaporate into clouds

25. The big lesson of the chapter — that India's very different regions still form one country — is summed up by the phrase:
    (A) survival of the fittest

27. Why do geographers group a huge country like India into physical regions rather than treating it as one flat space?

29. Why do Himalayan rivers like the Ganga keep flowing even in the dry season, while many peninsular rivers shrink?
    (A) the Ganga is shorter, so it dries up more slowly
    (B) Himalayan rivers are fed by melting snow and glaciers that release water all year, while peninsular rivers depend mainly on the rains

30. "Perennial" rivers and "rain-fed" rivers differ in that:
    (A) perennial rivers flow all year (snow-fed), while rain-fed rivers swell in the rains and shrink in the dry season
