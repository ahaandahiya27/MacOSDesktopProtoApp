1. A reader argues that वन्दे मातरम् could equally be glossed 'May the Mother be saluted' (a wish/passive). Within the meaning the chapter teaches, why is the chapter's own gloss 'I salute the Mother' more accurate?
   (A) वन्दे is genuinely passive, so 'may the Mother be saluted' is the literal sense and the chapter's gloss is a loose paraphrase.
   (B) Both readings are identical in Sanskrit because वन्दे has no person built into it.
   (C) वन्दे is an active, first-person-singular finite verb carrying the agent 'I' inside it, so the speaker performs the salutation now — it is neither a passive ('be saluted') nor an optative wish ('may ... be').
   (D) वन्दे is an imperative addressed to the Mother, so 'be saluted, O Mother' is the precise sense.

2. Two students debate the emotional register of वन्दे मातरम्. One says it expresses reverence; the other says affection. Using the verb the chapter actually uses, which judgement is right and why?
   (A) Reverence — वन्दे is a verb of bowing/saluting, a different lexical root from any verb of love or fondness, so the line conveys honour rather than tender affection.
   (B) Affection — वन्दे is the standard Sanskrit verb for loving someone dearly.
   (C) Neither, because वन्दे only means 'to look at', so the line is merely descriptive.
   (D) Both equally, because in Sanskrit a single verb covers reverence and affection without distinction.

3. The chapter notes that the indeclinable नमः can be construed with a dative ('homage TO X'), while वन्दे takes an accusative object. From this, what general principle about how a word governs its case can a student infer?
   (A) Any word meaning 'salute' must take the accusative, so नमः with a dative is simply an error in the chapter.
   (B) Case-government depends only on the case of the previous word in the sentence, not on the saluting word.
   (C) Both वन्दे and नमः are indeclinables and therefore neither can govern any case at all.
   (D) A word's case-government is a property of that particular word, not of the meaning 'salute' in general — a transitive verb like वन्दे demands a direct object (accusative), whereas the indeclinable नमः licenses a dative of the one honoured.

4. Consider the invented line वन्दे गङ्गाम् हिमालयम् च ('I salute the Ganga and the Himalaya'). What does this show about how many objects a single वन्दे can govern, and in what case?
   (A) वन्दे can govern only one object, so the second word हिमालयम् must be nominative.
   (B) When two objects are joined by च, the second one switches to the instrumental as the 'means'.
   (C) वन्दे can govern more than one object joined by च, and every such object stands in the accusative — here both गङ्गाम् and हिमालयम् are accusative singular.
   (D) Both गङ्गाम् and हिमालयम् become genitive because च forces possession.

5. A learner writes नमः भारतमातरम् intending 'homage to the Motherland', reasoning that since वन्दे takes the accusative, नमः must too. Evaluate the construction the chapter would endorse.
   (A) The sentence is fully correct: नमः always governs the accusative exactly like वन्दे.
   (B) नमः should take a genitive, giving नमः भारतमातुः.
   (C) The natural construction is नमः with a dative (e.g. भारतमात्रे) meaning 'homage TO the Motherland'; copying वन्दे's accusative onto नमः misapplies one word's government to a different word.
   (D) नमः should take the instrumental, giving नमः भारतमात्रा.

6. Why can the single word वन्दे count as a complete Sanskrit sentence, while the single word मातरम् cannot — even though both are 'about' saluting?
   (A) मातरम् is the real verb and वन्दे the noun, so मातरम् alone is the complete sentence.
   (B) Both are complete sentences, since any single Sanskrit word is a sentence.
   (C) Neither is complete, because a Sanskrit sentence needs at least three words.
   (D) वन्दे is a finite verb that already contains a subject ('I') and an action ('salute'), so it states a complete event; मातरम् is only an accusative noun naming a would-be object, with no verb or subject of its own.

7. In वन्दे सुजलाम् सुफलाम् मातरम् the three accusatives agree. If a student instead wrote वन्दे सुजला सुफला माता, what single underlying mistake explains ALL three altered words at once?
   (A) Each word has a different, unrelated error, so there is no single explanation.
   (B) All three words have been put back into the nominative (the dictionary/subject form) instead of the accusative the object of वन्दे requires, so the same case-error repeats across the adjective–adjective–noun group.
   (C) The words were changed to the instrumental to show the means of saluting.
   (D) Only माता is wrong; the two adjectives सुजला and सुफला were correct as the object form.

8. The chapter uses the accusative of वन्दे's object. A student claims 'the accusative is just whatever ending a noun happens to have after वन्दे'. Choose the most precise correction of this idea.
   (A) Correct as stated: the accusative is defined simply as 'any ending appearing after वन्दे'.
   (B) The accusative is a grammatical role (the direct object / thing acted upon) that the noun is put INTO because वन्दे governs it; the ending is the marker of that role, not the cause of it.
   (C) The accusative is defined by position only — the word immediately after the verb is accusative regardless of its ending.
   (D) The accusative is the same as the nominative, so वन्दे makes no real demand on the noun.

9. मातृ is an ऋकारान्त feminine. Its accusative singular is मातरम्. The agent-noun दातृ ('giver', masculine ऋ-stem) forms its accusative singular as दातारम् with a LONG आ in the suffix, unlike मातरम्. What best explains why kinship मातृ keeps a short अ while agent दातृ lengthens it?
   (A) There is no difference; मातरम् should really be मातारम् and the chapter form is a misprint.
   (B) ऋ-stems split into two sub-patterns: kinship terms like मातृ/पितृ take the short (गुण) form मातरम्/पितरम्, while agent nouns like दातृ/कर्तृ take the lengthened (वृद्धि) form दातारम्/कर्तारम् in the strong cases.
   (C) The length depends only on gender: all feminine ऋ-stems are short and all masculine ones are long.
   (D) दातृ is not an ऋ-stem at all, so the comparison is invalid.

10. By the declension of मातृ (acc. sg. मातरम्, gen. sg. मातुः), the kinship stem दुहितृ ('daughter') should form its accusative singular and genitive singular respectively as:
   (A) दुहित्रम् (accusative) and दुहित्रस्य (genitive)
   (B) दुहिता (accusative) and दुहितृम् (genitive)
   (C) दुहितरम् (accusative singular) and दुहितुः (genitive singular)
   (D) दुहितारम् (accusative) and दुहितारः (genitive)

11. A learner produces वन्दे मातृम् for 'I salute the Mother', simply tacking -म् onto the dictionary stem मातृ. Why is this wrong even though -म् is an accusative marker?
   (A) मातृम् is actually correct, and मातरम् is an alternative poetic spelling.
   (B) The error is the case, not the form: मातृम् is a fine accusative but वन्दे wants the nominative.
   (C) ऋ-stems do not form the accusative by adding -म् to the bare stem; the stem first changes grade (मातृ → मातर) and then takes -म्, giving मातरम् — so मातृम् skips the required stem-change.
   (D) मातृ never takes -म् in any case, so any -म् form is impossible.

12. Given मातृ → माता (nom. sg.), मातरम् (acc. sg.), मातरौ (acc. dual), मातरः (nom. pl.), which single statement about the SHAPE of these endings is correct?
   (A) माता and मातरम् share a stem, but मातरौ and मातरः belong to a completely different noun.
   (B) The same surface piece -रम्/-रौ/-रः rides on a strengthened stem मातर-, so number and case are told apart by the ending attached to that one strong stem, not by changing the root vowel each time.
   (C) All four are the singular of मातृ, differing only in spelling.
   (D) मातरः is the accusative singular and मातरम् is the nominative plural.

13. मातरः is nominative PLURAL ('mothers, as subjects'). In the song's vocative-like address to the one Motherland, why would मातरः be doubly wrong?
   (A) It is wrong in both number and role: मातरः is plural ('mothers') whereas the song addresses one Mother, and it is nominative (subject) whereas the object of वन्दे must be accusative — so the form misses on count and on case.
   (B) It is wrong only in number: मातरः is the correct accusative but happens to be plural.
   (C) It is wrong only in case: मातरः is correctly singular but is nominative.
   (D) It is entirely correct, since मातरः is the accusative singular of मातृ.

   (A) A catalogue of unrelated geographical facts with no figurative unity.

15. पर्वतराजः is read as पर्वतानाम् राजा ('king OF mountains'), where the first member hides a genitive — a तत्पुरुष. By contrast, चन्द्रमुखी ('moon-faced [woman]') means 'one WHOSE face is like the moon', pointing outside itself to a person. What is the structural difference?
   (A) Both are बहुव्रीहि, since each points to an outside thing.
   (B) पर्वतराजः is a तत्पुरुष naming one of its own members (a king, who IS a mountain-king), while चन्द्रमुखी is a बहुव्रीहि naming an outside referent (a woman) characterised by the compound — the whole denotes something not literally named inside it.
   (C) Both are तत्पुरुष, since each simply names its head member.
   (D) पर्वतराजः is बहुव्रीहि and चन्द्रमुखी is तत्पुरुष — the labels are the other way round.

16. रत्नाकरः stands for the ocean, analysed as रत्नानाम् आकरः ('mine of jewels'). The compound names the ocean WITHOUT using the word समुद्र. What does this reveal about how such epithets work?
   (A) रत्नाकरः literally contains the letters of समुद्र inside it, which is how it points to the sea.
   (B) रत्नाकरः is just an alternative spelling of समुद्र with the same meaning and no descriptive content.
   (C) It is a descriptive epithet whose members ('jewels' + 'mine/store') describe a property of the ocean, letting the reader decode the intended referent from sense and context rather than from a hidden copy of the word समुद्र.
   (D) Sanskrit bans the plain word समुद्र in poetry, so a substitute compound is legally required.

18. देशभक्तः ('country-devoted') is a तत्पुरुष whose head is भक्तः. Now consider चन्द्रशेखरः ('moon-crested', a name of Shiva) meaning 'one ON WHOSE crest the moon sits'. Which compound is बहुव्रीहि and why?
   (A) देशभक्तः is बहुव्रीहि because it points to a country outside the compound.
   (B) चन्द्रशेखरः is बहुव्रीहि: it does not denote a 'crest' or a 'moon' but an outside person (Shiva) characterised by having the moon on his crest; देशभक्तः, by contrast, denotes its own head (a devotee) and is तत्पुरुष.
   (C) Both are बहुव्रीहि because both contain two nouns.
   (D) Neither is बहुव्रीहि; both simply name their first member.

19. कृषकबान्धवाः is glossed 'farmer-brethren'. A student insists the head (the word that fixes what the whole compound IS) is कृषक. Why is that backwards, and which member is the head?
   (A) In such a तत्पुरुष the LAST member is the head, so बान्धव ('brethren/kinsmen') is the head and कृषक merely qualifies it — the compound denotes brethren (who are farmers), and the plural ending sits on बान्धवाः.
   (B) कृषक is the head, so the compound denotes farmers qualified as 'brotherly'.
   (C) Neither member is the head; both are equal as in a द्वन्द्व 'farmers and brethren'.
   (D) The head is whichever member is shorter, here कृषक.

20. आनन्द ('bliss') begins with a long आ; carelessly shortening it risks producing the spurious अनन्द. A learner says 'इ and ई are written differently but sound the same, so swapping them is harmless'. Apply the chapter's vowel-length principle.
   (A) Correct — only आ and अ differ in meaning; इ and ई are truly interchangeable.
   (B) Wrong — इ (short, one मात्रा) and ई (long, two मात्राः) are distinct sounds of different duration, and swapping them can change a word's identity, exactly as आ↔अ can; length is meaning-bearing for every vowel pair.
   (C) Correct — vowel length never affects meaning, so any swap is harmless.
   (D) Wrong, but only because इ has no long counterpart, so the swap is simply impossible.

   (C) Because length changes only the gender of a word, never its meaning.
   (D) Because Sanskrit words are all so short that any change at all makes them unpronounceable.

25. The phrase सस्यश्यामला arises from joining सस्य + श्यामला with no extra sound inserted at the seam. By contrast, joining स + ईश would, under vowel-sandhi, fuse to सेश-type forms (अ + ई → ए). What does this teach about reading compounds back into their parts?
   (A) All compounds are formed by fusing the seam vowels, so सस्यश्यामला must also hide a fused vowel.
   (B) No compound ever changes sounds at the seam, so अ + ई never becomes ए.
   (C) Splitting is impossible once words are joined, because sandhi is irreversible.
   (D) Some joins simply place members side by side (सस्य + श्यामला) while others fuse vowels at the seam (अ + ई → ए); to split a compound you must know whether a sandhi change occurred, then undo it to recover the original members.

26. In recovering members from a compound, a student undoes a seam where ए appears and proposes the originals were अ + इ or अ + ई. Why is this a reasonable inference about the join?
   (A) Because the vowel-sandhi rule अ/आ + इ/ई → ए runs in one direction when forming words, so an ए at a seam can be traced back to a preceding अ/आ followed by इ/ई as the likely originals.
   (B) Because ए can only ever come from joining two ए vowels.
   (C) Because ए at a seam always means the members were अ + उ.
   (D) Because ए never results from sandhi, so the student must be inventing the rule.

27. वन्दे मातरम् as printed shows no vowel-fusion between the two words. If a poet instead wrote वन्द इह (वन्दे + इह), why might the seam look different from वन्दे मातरम्?

   (B) Because sandhi happens only before consonants, so मातरम् would change but इह would not.

   (D) Because मातरम् begins with a consonant, so वन्दे sits beside it unchanged; but इह begins with a vowel इ, which can trigger vowel-sandhi at the seam (ए + इ interacting), altering the written join.

32. स्वेदबिन्दुभिः सिञ्चन्ति ('they water with sweat-drops') uses -भिः. A learner glosses it 'OF the sweat-drops'. Identify the case actually present and the case the learner confused it with.
