1. A devotee insists that since सुभाषितानि are called 'rasa' (nectar) that we 'drink' (पिबामः), the truest way to honour them is to recite each one aloud exactly once at a festival and never repeat it. Why does the title's own word-choice undercut this?
(A) The verse uses पठामः ('we read'), not पिबामः, so reciting once is exactly what it prescribes.(B) रसम् means literal fruit-juice, so the saying is about drinking at festivals, making the devotee correct.(C) नित्यम् means 'never', so the verse actually forbids drinking the sayings at all.(D) नित्यम् ('daily') paired with the drinking-metaphor demands repeated, habitual intake; a one-time festival recitation contradicts the 'every day' force the verse builds in.
2. In नित्यं पिबामः सुभाषितरसम्, suppose we change पिबामः to पिबामि while leaving everything else. What single grammatical fact changes, and does the chapter's communal sense survive?
(A) The subject shifts from 'we' (first person plural) to 'I' (first person singular); the shared, communal 'we drink together daily' sense is lost.(B) Nothing changes; पिबामि and पिबामः are both 'we drink' and equally plural.(C) The tense shifts from present to past, so it would mean 'I drank'.(D) The verb becomes an imperative 'drink!', addressed to a single listener.
3. सुभाषितरसम् carries the accusative -अम्. A student argues: 'Since रसः (nectar) is masculine and सुभाषितम् is neuter, the compound सुभाषितरसम् must be neuter, which is why it ends in -अम्.' Evaluate.
(A) The reasoning is correct: a compound always takes the gender of its first member, so सुभाषित makes it neuter.(B) The reasoning is correct because -अम् is exclusively a neuter ending in Sanskrit.(C) The compound is feminine, agreeing with सुभाषितरसा, and the -अम् is a typographical accusative.(D) The reasoning is wrong: in a tatpurusha the gender follows the LAST member (रस, masculine), and -अम् here is simply the masculine accusative singular of रस, marking it as the object drunk — not a neuter ending.
4. Why is the rasa-metaphor for subhashitas a deliberately better choice than calling them, say, 'a treasure to be locked away'?
(A) Because rasa is more valuable than treasure, so the metaphor simply exaggerates their worth.(B) Because treasure can be stolen but rasa cannot, making the saying about security.(C) There is no real difference; both metaphors mean the sayings should be hidden and protected.(D) A treasure stored away is merely kept; rasa is something one drinks and absorbs, so the metaphor stresses that wise sayings must be taken in and internalised, not just possessed.
5. A teacher says the title verse is itself an example of what it praises. What is the subtlest sense in which this is true?
(A) The verse IS a subhashita — a compact, well-spoken saying — so in advising us to drink subhashitas daily it models the very thing it recommends.(B) Because the verse literally contains the word रसः meaning the fruit-juice it tells us to drink.(C) Because the verse lists all five va-qualities, demonstrating the honour it promises.(D) Because the verse is a command to the teacher, who then obeys it by teaching.
6. If a friend renders the title as 'We always drink the taste of bad speech', exactly which two word-analyses has he botched?
(A) He has read सु- as 'bad' (it means 'well/good') and treated रसम् as a negative 'taste of speech' rather than the prized 'nectar/essence' of WELL-spoken sayings.(B) He has misread नित्यम् as 'always' (it means 'never') and पिबामः as 'we drink' (it means 'we sing').(C) He has misread सु- as 'six' and भाषित as 'fault', turning the phrase into 'six faults'.(D) Only one error: रसम् should be 'juice', but 'bad speech' for सुभाषित is correct.
7. A pupil writes the honouring verse as 'नरः विद्यया वपुषा वाचा वस्त्रेण सत्येन पूजितः' and counts six va-qualities. Pinpoint the precise defect.
(A) There is no defect; truth is one of the va-qualities, so six is correct.(B) The defect is only that वस्त्रेण should be dropped, leaving the correct five.(C) सत्येन ('by truth') is not a va-quality at all — it is a purification MEANS (मनः सत्येन शुध्यति); the genuine fifth quality is विनयेन (humility), so the list is wrong, not merely over-counted.(D) The defect is that विद्यया is the intruder, since knowledge belongs to the purification verse as ज्ञानेन.
8. विद्यया, वपुषा, वाचा, वस्त्रेण, विनयेन: a learner says 'three end in -आ/-ा-ish vowels and two in -एन, so they must be in two different cases.' Correct this.
(A) Correct — the -आ endings are nominative and the -एन endings are instrumental, so there are two cases.(B) All five are in the SAME case — the instrumental (तृतीया, 'by/with') — the endings differ only because the words belong to different stem-classes (feminine ā-/ī-stems vs neuter/consonant stems), not because the case differs.(C) Correct — the differing endings show three are singular and two are plural.(D) All five are genitive ('of knowledge', etc.), and the endings differ by gender.
9. Build the instrumental phrase for a NEW honouring quality not in the verse — say 'by good conduct', using आचार (masculine a-stem, 'conduct') on the verse's pattern (नरः ... पूजितः).
(A) नरः आचारया पूजितः, taking the -या ending seen in विद्यया.(B) नरः आचारस्य पूजितः, marking 'of conduct'.(C) नरः आचारेण पूजितः ('a man is honoured by conduct') — the masculine a-stem takes -एण in the instrumental, just like वस्त्रेण and विनयेन.(D) नरः आचारम् पूजितः, putting the means in the accusative.
10. Why does the chapter group exactly these five qualities — knowledge, body, speech, dress, humility — and what unifying idea (beyond all starting with व) justifies the set?
(A) They are grouped only because they share the letter व; there is no further connecting idea.(B) They are grouped because all five are bodily organs that perform actions.(C) Together they cover the whole person — inner faculty (विद्या), physical presence (वपुस्), expression (वाणी), outward appearance (वस्त्र), and character (विनय) — so honour is earned across mind, body, and conduct, not by any one alone.(D) They are grouped because each one, by itself, is fully sufficient to earn complete honour.
11. A man is admired purely for his fine clothes though he is rude and idle. Judged strictly by the five-quality verse, how complete is the honour he receives?
(A) Fully justified, since वस्त्र alone is the single quality that earns all honour.(B) Completely baseless, because वस्त्र (dress) is not actually one of the five qualities.(C) Based on a fault, because fine dress falls under the six दोषाः to be shed.(D) Only partial — वस्त्र (dress) is genuinely one of the five, so the honour is not baseless, but विद्या, वाणी and विनय are also required, and his rudeness and idleness fail those.
12. वाणी (good speech) is a praised quality, yet क्रोधः (anger) is a fault. A pupil who normally speaks well but lashes out in anger has, by the chapter's logic:
(A) gained a virtue, since forceful angry speech shows command of वाणी.(B) undermined his own virtue — indulging the fault of anger corrupts the very speech (वाणी) that was his quality, so a fault can spoil a virtue.(C) stayed neutral, because वाणी and क्रोधः are unrelated and cannot affect each other.(D) proved that वाणी is itself a fault, since speech can carry anger.
13. A pupil reasons: 'वपुस् (a good body) is a quality, so resting the body with plenty of निद्रा and तन्द्रा must also be virtuous.' Why does the chapter refute this?
(A) Because the body is itself a fault, so वपुस् was wrongly listed as a quality.(B) Because निद्रा and तन्द्रा are virtues that rest the body, so the pupil is correct.(C) A healthy body (वपुस्) is a virtue, but निद्रा (sleep) and तन्द्रा (drowsiness) are listed as faults; the chapter prizes MASTERY over the body, not indulgence of it.(D) Because वपुस् and निद्रा mean the same thing, so resting and fitness are identical.
14. नरः पञ्चभिः वकारैः पूजितः. Change ONLY the number to 'a man is honoured by three va-qualities'. Which two words must change, and how?
(A) Only पञ्चभिः changes, to त्रयः (the nominative 'three'), and वकारैः stays unchanged.(B) पञ्चभिः → त्रीणि and वकारैः → वकारम् (singular), since three is treated as a unit.(C) Nothing changes; पञ्चभिः already covers any number of qualities.(D) पञ्चभिः → त्रिभिः (instrumental of त्रि, 'three'), and the list would shrink to three qualities; वकारैः itself stays plural since three is still plural.
15. Which statement about the relationship between वकारैः and पञ्चभिः is exactly right?
(A) पञ्चभिः is instrumental but वकारैः is genitive, so they do not agree.(B) वकारैः is the subject in the nominative and पञ्चभिः modifies the verb as an adverb.(C) They agree as a numeral-noun pair in case and number — both instrumental plural — so पञ्चभिः ('by five') quantifies वकारैः ('by va-qualities').(D) पञ्चभिः is singular and वकारैः is plural, so the numeral and noun disagree in number.
16. नरः पञ्चभिः वकारैः पूजितः — a reader translates 'the man worships five va-things.' Two errors combine here. Name them.
(A) पूजितः is an active present verb ('worships'), and वकारैः is correctly the object; the only error is the number.(B) पूजितः is a PAST PASSIVE participle, so the man is the one BEING honoured, not the worshipper; and पञ्चभिः वकारैः is instrumental ('BY five'), the means of honour, not the accusative object of worship.(C) नरः is accusative (the man worshipped), and पञ्चभिः is the subject; both case-readings are simply reversed.(D) There is no error; 'the man worships five va-things' is the verse's meaning.
17. Among the five qualities, which pairing of a quality with the FAULT that most directly negates it is correct?
(A) विनय (humility) is most directly negated by क्रोधः (anger) — anger overthrows the calm humility that विनय names.(B) विद्या (knowledge) is most directly negated by वस्त्र (dress).(C) वपुस् (a fit body) is most directly negated by वाणी (speech).(D) वाणी (good speech) is most directly negated by दीर्घायुष् (long life).
18. Suppose someone has विद्या, वपुस्, वाणी and वस्त्र but is utterly arrogant. By the verse, is his honour secure?
(A) Yes — four out of five qualities guarantees full honour regardless of arrogance.(B) Yes — arrogance is itself a sign of विद्या, so it adds to his honour.(C) No — he is missing विनय (humility), the fifth quality; arrogance fails it, so his honour is incomplete despite the other four.(D) No — but only because arrogance is one of the six named faults that erases all qualities.
19. A reader counts the faults as 'sleepiness (निद्रा+तन्द्रा as one), idleness (आलस्यम्+दीर्घसूत्रता as one), भयम्, क्रोधः' = four. Give the precise rebuttal.
(A) The reader is right — there really are only four faults once duplicates are merged.(B) The verse names SIX distinct faults: निद्रा (full sleep) ≠ तन्द्रा (drowsy half-sleep), and आलस्यम् (general laziness) ≠ दीर्घसूत्रता (delaying particular tasks); merging each pair undercounts to four.(C) There are five faults, because भयम् and क्रोधः are also a single 'emotion' fault.(D) There are eight faults, since each merged pair actually splits into three.
20. Which of the following correctly contrasts आलस्यम् with दीर्घसूत्रता — the pair most often wrongly fused?
(A) आलस्यम् is delaying specific tasks, while दीर्घसूत्रता is a general unwillingness to act (the reverse).(B) आलस्यम् is full sleep, while दीर्घसूत्रता is drowsiness.(C) आलस्यम् is a general unwillingness to act (doing nothing at all), while दीर्घसूत्रता is dragging out and postponing PARTICULAR tasks one has undertaken.(D) आलस्यम् is fear of work, while दीर्घसूत्रता is anger at work.
21. तन्द्रा and निद्रा are kept separate. A student says 'निद्रा must be the milder of the two because it has fewer syllables.' Evaluate the claim and give the real distinction.
(A) Correct — निद्रा, being shorter, is the milder drowsiness and तन्द्रा is full sleep.(B) The distinction does not exist; निद्रा and तन्द्रा are perfect synonyms.(C) The syllable reasoning is irrelevant; in fact निद्रा is the deeper state (full sleep) and तन्द्रा the milder one (drowsy half-sleep), so even the implied ranking is reversed.(D) निद्रा means fear and तन्द्रा means anger, so neither is about sleep.
22. षड् दोषाः हातव्याः भूतिम् इच्छता. A learner makes भूतिम् इच्छता the SUBJECT, reading 'the desirer of prosperity gives up six faults.' Where exactly does the grammar resist this?
(A) Nothing resists it; इच्छता is nominative and is the subject.(B) हातव्याः is a gerundive ('to-be-abandoned') whose agent stands in the instrumental, so इच्छता is the instrumental agent ('BY the one desiring prosperity'), not a nominative subject — the faults दोषाः are the nominative.(C) भूतिम् is the subject (nominative), so 'prosperity gives up the faults'.(D) हातव्याः is an active verb 'gives up', so a nominative subject is exactly what it needs.
23. Which single transformation would turn 'षड् दोषाः हातव्याः भूतिम् इच्छता' into a grammatical ACTIVE sentence with the same meaning?
(A) Make the agent nominative and use an active verb: भूतिम् इच्छन् नरः षड् दोषान् त्यजेत् ('one desiring prosperity should give up the six faults') — agent now subject, faults now accusative object.(B) Keep हातव्याः but change दोषाः to the instrumental दोषैः.(C) Change इच्छता to the genitive इच्छतः, leaving everything else the same.(D) Replace भूतिम् with the nominative भूतिः, keeping the gerundive हातव्याः.
24. आलस्यं हि मनुष्याणां शरीरस्थो महान् रिपुः. The label शरीरस्थः ('seated in the body') is doing real philosophical work. What is it?
(A) It marks laziness as an INNER enemy that lives within oneself, so the chief obstacle to achievement is internal and must be conquered within, not blamed on outside foes.(B) It means the body itself is the enemy and ought to be neglected or harmed.(C) It means external enemies are far more dangerous than one's own laziness.(D) It means the enemy lives only in other people's bodies, never one's own.
25. मनुष्याणाम् in 'आलस्यं हि मनुष्याणां ... महान् रिपुः' answers which question, and in what case?
(A) It answers 'by whom?' and is instrumental plural — 'by men'.(B) It answers 'the men did what?' and is nominative plural — the subject.(C) It answers 'for whom?' and is dative singular — 'for a man'.(D) It answers 'an enemy OF WHOM?' and is genitive plural — 'of men' — showing whose enemy laziness is.
26. In 'आलस्यं ... महान् रिपुः', what is the grammatical relationship between आलस्यम् and रिपुः, and why are both nominative?
(A) रिपुः is the object that laziness acts upon, hence both are accusative.(B) रिपुः is the means by which laziness operates, hence instrumental.(C) रिपुः possesses laziness, so रिपुः is genitive.(D) It is predicate identification — 'laziness IS a great enemy' — so subject (आलस्यम्) and predicate noun (रिपुः) both take the nominative and agree.
27. गात्राणि जलेन शुध्यन्ति but बुद्धिः ज्ञानेन शुध्यति. A student says the verb-ending differs because the MEANS differ (जलेन forces plural, ज्ञानेन forces singular). Correct him.
(A) He is right — instrumental जलेन triggers a plural verb and ज्ञानेन a singular one.(B) The difference is tense: शुध्यन्ति is past and शुध्यति is present.(C) गात्राणि takes a command form while बुद्धिः takes a statement form.(D) The verb agrees with its SUBJECT, not its means: गात्राणि (limbs) is plural so the verb is शुध्यन्ति, while बुद्धिः (intellect) is singular so the verb is शुध्यति; the means is irrelevant to number.
28. The purification verse uses four DIFFERENT means for four parts. A boy says 'water cleans, so washing my body with water also cleans my mind.' What law of the verse does this violate?
(A) It violates nothing; water cleans the body and the mind alike in the verse.(B) The verse assigns a distinct means to each goal — water for the body, truth for the mind — so washing cleans only गात्राणि; the mind (मनः) needs सत्य (truth), and one means does not serve all goals.(C) It violates the rule that knowledge (ज्ञानेन) is what cleans the mind, not truth.(D) It violates the rule that the mind cannot be cleaned by anything at all.
29. Given the set 'गात्राणि तपसा; मनः सत्येन; आत्मा जलेन; बुद्धिः ज्ञानेन', which single SWAP makes all four correct?
(A) Swap the means of मनः and बुद्धिः, giving मनः ज्ञानेन and बुद्धिः सत्येन.(B) Change मनः सत्येन to मनः जलेन.(C) Leave it unchanged; the set is already fully correct.(D) Swap the means of गात्राणि and आत्मा, giving गात्राणि जलेन (body by water) and आत्मा तपसा (soul by austerity); मनः–सत्येन and बुद्धिः–ज्ञानेन are already right.
30. बुद्धिः केन शुध्यति? A learner answers जलेन, reasoning 'water cleans everything physical, and the brain is a physical organ.' Diagnose the deepest flaw.
(A) The verse treats बुद्धिः as the intellect (a non-physical faculty) cleaned by ज्ञानेन (knowledge); water cleans only the physical body (गात्राणि), so equating the intellect with a washable organ misreads the metaphor.(B) The flaw is none — water does clean the intellect in the verse.(C) The flaw is only that he should have said तपसा, the means for the intellect.(D) The flaw is that the intellect needs no cleaning at all, so the question is meaningless.
31. Which part-and-means pairing exposes the SUBTLEST trap, by attaching to the intellect a means that genuinely purifies a DIFFERENT inner faculty?
(A) बुद्धिः जलेन ('intellect by water'), because water purifies a different inner faculty.(B) बुद्धिः सत्येन ('intellect by truth') is the subtle trap, because truth (सत्येन) really is a purifying means — but of the MIND (मनः), not the intellect, which needs knowledge (ज्ञानेन).(C) बुद्धिः वस्त्रेण ('intellect by dress'), because dress is a purification means for another faculty.(D) बुद्धिः क्रोधेन ('intellect by anger'), because anger purifies the soul.
32. Why is सत्य (truth), not water, the fitting means for cleaning the मनः (mind)?
(A) Because water is too scarce to spare for the mind, so truth is used as a cheaper substitute.(B) The mind's impurities are inner ones — deceit and crookedness — which truthfulness removes; water can rinse the body but cannot reach or cleanse the inner faculty, so each impurity needs a matching remedy.(C) Because truth and water are the same purifier under different names.(D) Because the mind is a physical organ that truth physically washes.
33. A pupil washes his hands carefully but tells lies all day and stays angry. By the purification verse, what has he actually purified, and what remains impure?
(A) He has purified everything, since washing with water cleans body and mind alike.(B) He has purified the soul, which water alone suffices to clean.(C) He has purified the intellect, since careful washing equals knowledge.(D) He has purified the body (गात्राणि, by water) but left the mind (मनः) impure, since the mind is cleaned by truth (सत्येन), and his lying and anger leave it unpurified.
34. Across the two verses, विद्या appears as a honouring quality while ज्ञानेन is the intellect's purifier. A friend says 'so knowledge is double-listed.' Resolve this precisely.
(A) Correct — विद्या and ज्ञानेन are the identical word appearing in both verses.(B) Correct — विद्या cleans the body in one verse and honours the person in the other.(C) Wrong — विद्या appears in neither verse at all.(D) They are NOT the same entry: विद्या (learning) is one of the five honouring qualities, while the intellect's purifying means is ज्ञानेन (knowledge) — distinct words in distinct verses, so there is no overlap to count twice.
35. गात्राणि जलेन शुध्यन्ति. Which feature of गात्राणि determines that the verb is शुध्यन्ति rather than शुध्यति?
(A) गात्राणि is treated as one whole body, so it should logically take the singular शुध्यति.(B) The means जलेन is plural, and the verb agrees with the means, not the subject.(C) गात्राणि is neuter PLURAL ('the limbs'), and a finite verb agrees with its subject in number, so a plural subject takes the plural verb शुध्यन्ति.(D) गात्राणि is feminine singular, which happens to take the -अन्ति ending.
36. जलबिन्दुनिपातेन क्रमशः पूर्यते घटः. A student claims क्रमशः means 'the pot fills suddenly at the very end, once almost full.' Refute using the verse's own image.
(A) क्रमशः means 'gradually, step by step'; the WHOLE filling is incremental — each drop adds a little — so there is no sudden final jump, only steady accumulation throughout.(B) Correct — क्रमशः specifically marks the sudden last-moment completion.(C) क्रमशः means 'all at once', so the pot truly fills instantly.(D) क्रमशः means 'backwards', so the pot is gradually emptied.
37. पूर्यते in जलबिन्दुनिपातेन क्रमशः पूर्यते घटः is passive. What three roles does the passive voice fix, and which word fills each?
(A) घटः is the agent actively filling something; जलबिन्दुनिपातेन is the object filled.(B) घटः (nominative) is the patient being filled; जलबिन्दुनिपातेन (instrumental) is the means doing the filling; पूर्यते is the passive verb 'is filled' — patient + means + passive action.(C) जलबिन्दुनिपातेन is the patient being filled and घटः is the means.(D) There is no patient or means; पूर्यते is an active verb and घटः is its object.

Worked Solutions

1. (D) The title is नित्यं पिबामः सुभाषितरसम् — 'daily we drink the nectar of well-spoken sayings.' Two words fix the meaning together: नित्यम् ('daily, always') and पिबामः ('we drink'). The drinking-metaphor already implies regular nourishment, and नित्यम् pins it to an everyday habit, not a single festive performance. A once-a-year recitation honours the form but defeats the 'daily' intake the title insists on. The claim that the verb is पठामः is false — the chosen verb is पिबामः precisely to evoke absorbing wisdom like a sustaining drink. रसम् here is metaphorical nectar/essence, not literal juice, so the festival-drink reading collapses. And नित्यम् means 'daily/always', the opposite of 'never', so the idea that the verse forbids the practice inverts it.

2. (A) पिबामः is first person plural present — 'we drink'; the ending -आमः marks 'we'. पिबामि is first person singular present — 'I drink', ending -आमि. So the swap changes only person/number from plural to singular, and the warm communal idea (a whole class or family drinking the nectar of wise sayings together every day) is replaced by a solitary 'I'. The claim that both are 'we drink' confuses the two endings -आमः (we) and -आमि (I). There is no tense change — both are present; -आमि is not a past marker. And neither form is an imperative: the imperative 'drink!' to one person would be पिब, not पिबामि.

3. (D) सुभाषितरसम् is a tatpurusha (सुभाषित + रस = 'nectar OF well-spoken sayings'). In such compounds the gender is taken from the final member, here रस, which is masculine. The form सुभाषितरसम् is the masculine accusative singular of रस-stem (रसः → रसम्), marking it as the direct object of पिबामः ('we drink the nectar'). So -अम् here is a masculine accusative ending, not a sign of neuter gender. The student's rule is backwards: gender follows the last member, not the first, so सुभाषित (neuter) does not dictate the compound's gender. The claim that -अम् is 'exclusively neuter' is false — masculine a-stems take -अम् in the accusative singular (नरम्) too; only the nominative -अम् signals neuter. And the word is not feminine.

4. (D) The chapter calls the sayings रस ('nectar/juice') that we पिबामः ('drink'), and adds नित्यम् ('daily'). The point of an ingestible image over a stored-treasure image is that drinking is active internalisation: wisdom changes you only when absorbed, like nourishment, not when merely hoarded. A locked treasure stays external and unused; the rasa-image insists on daily uptake. The 'more valuable' reading misses the active-absorption point — the metaphor is about HOW wisdom helps (by being taken in), not merely how precious it is. The 'security against theft' reading imports an idea the verse never raises. And the two metaphors are not equivalent: hiding/protecting is the opposite of the daily drinking the title commands.

5. (A) The chapter's whole genre is the subhashita — a wise, well-spoken saying. The title-verse नित्यं पिबामः सुभाषितरसम् is itself such a saying: concise, pointed, memorable. So it is self-exemplifying — recommending the daily intake of subhashitas while being a subhashita worth drinking. The 'literal fruit-juice' reading mistakes the metaphor for a literal drink. The verse does not list the five va-qualities (that is a different subhashita), so it cannot 'demonstrate' them. And the verse is a first-person-plural inclusive statement ('we drink'), not a command issued to a teacher.

6. (A) सुभाषित = सु (well/good) + भाषित (spoken) = 'well-spoken'. Reading सु- as 'bad' is the first error, turning a praised saying into 'bad speech.' रस here is 'nectar/essence' — something delightful and nourishing — so calling it a neutral or negative 'taste' is the second error. The distractor that 'corrects' नित्यम् to 'never' and पिबामः to 'we sing' invents new errors: नित्यम् does mean 'always/daily' and पिबामः does mean 'we drink', so those renderings were actually right. The सु='six' reading belongs to a different compound (षड् = six in षड्दोषाः), not to सुभाषित. And the claim that 'bad speech' is acceptable for सुभाषित is precisely the mistake being diagnosed.

7. (C) The five honouring qualities are विद्या, वपुस्, वाणी, वस्त्र, विनय — all beginning with व, and in the verse all instrumental (विद्यया, वपुषा, वाचा, वस्त्रेण, विनयेन). The pupil dropped विनयेन and inserted सत्येन. But सत्य is a purification means (the mind, मनः, is cleaned by truth, सत्येन), not a va-quality — it does not even begin with व. So the list both smuggles in an outsider and loses the real fifth member; the count of six is a symptom of inserting सत्येन for विनयेन. Saying truth is a va-quality endorses the error. Dropping वस्त्रेण would wrongly cut a genuine quality (dress IS one of the five). And विद्या is genuinely a va-quality; it is not the intruder, even though the intellect's purification means happens to be ज्ञानेन.

8. (B) Case is a function (here 'by means of'), but its surface ending depends on the noun's stem-class. विद्या (feminine ā-stem) → विद्यया; वाणी (feminine ī-stem) → वाचा; वपुस् (consonant stem) → वपुषा; वस्त्र (neuter a-stem) → वस्त्रेण; विनय (masculine a-stem) → विनयेन. Different endings, one and the same instrumental meaning 'by/with'. So the learner mistakes spelling variety for case variety. The -आ forms here are NOT nominatives — विद्या's nominative and instrumental look different (विद्या vs विद्यया); the listed forms are all means-of-honour, hence instrumental. The ending differences track stem-class, not singular-vs-plural (all five are singular). And they are not genitive: 'honoured by knowledge' is means (instrumental), not 'of knowledge' (genitive).

9. (C) The verse honours 'by' a quality, which is the instrumental case. आचार is a masculine a-stem, so its instrumental singular is आचारेण (the same -एन/-एण family as वस्त्रेण, विनयेन). Hence नरः आचारेण पूजितः. The -या ending (आचारया) belongs to feminine ā-stems like विद्या → विद्यया; applying it to a masculine a-stem is a stem-class error. आचारस्य is genitive ('of conduct'), which would mean 'honoured of conduct', not 'by conduct.' आचारम् is accusative (the object), but the means of honouring is never the object — it must be instrumental.

10. (C) The five span the dimensions of a respectable person: विद्या (learning, the inner faculty), वपुस् (a fit body), वाणी (good speech), वस्त्र (appropriate dress), विनय (humility/conduct). The व-alliteration is a mnemonic, but the deeper logic is comprehensiveness — honour rests on mind, body, expression, appearance, and character together. The 'only the letter व' reading ignores this rounded coverage. They are not all bodily organs — विद्या, वस्त्र and विनय are not organs. And the point is precisely that no single quality suffices: a person dressed well but ignorant, or learned but arrogant, is not fully honoured, which is why all five are named.

11. (D) The verse honours a man 'by five va-qualities', dress (वस्त्र) being one of them — so admiration for good dress rests on a real quality and is not baseless. But the verse names five together; full honour needs विद्या (learning), वाणी (good speech), विनय (humility) and वपुस् too. His rudeness corrupts वाणी/विनय and his idleness undercuts the active life behind the qualities, so the honour is partial at best. Saying वस्त्र alone earns ALL honour ignores the other four named qualities. Claiming dress is not one of the five contradicts the verse. And dress is a quality, not a fault — the six faults are sleep, drowsiness, fear, anger, laziness, procrastination; dress is not among them.

12. (B) वाणी (good, measured speech) is one of the five honouring qualities; क्रोधः (anger) is one of the six faults. The two meet in the same channel — the mouth — so yielding to anger turns good speech into harsh speech, and the fault corrupts the virtue. Thus indulging क्रोधः costs him वाणी. Forceful angry speech is not 'command of वाणी' — वाणी praises good, controlled speech, not loud rage. They are not unrelated: anger expresses itself through speech, which is exactly why one spoils the other. And speech itself is not a fault — the chapter lists वाणी among virtues; it is anger, not speech, that is the दोष.

13. (C) वपुस् names a fit, capable body — a quality. निद्रा (full sleep) and तन्द्रा (drowsy half-sleep) are two of the six faults. The chapter's logic is that the body is to be commanded, not pampered: keeping it fit honours you, but surrendering to sleep and sluggishness wastes you. So a fit body and indulgent rest are opposites, not the same. The body is not itself a fault — वपुस् is correctly a virtue. Sleep/drowsiness are not virtues here; they are explicitly faults. And वपुस् ≠ निद्रा: one is fitness, the other is yielding to sleep — confusing them is the very slip the pupil makes.

14. (D) पञ्चभिः is the instrumental plural of पञ्चन् ('five') and means 'by five'. To say 'by three' you need the instrumental of त्रि, which is त्रिभिः; the noun वकारैः ('by va-things', instrumental plural) stays plural because three is still more than one. The nominative त्रयः would be wrong — the means of honouring must stay instrumental, and the construction needs the instrumental of three, त्रिभिः. त्रीणि is the neuter nominative/accusative of three, not the instrumental, and वकारैः should not collapse to singular for a count of three. And 'nothing changes' is false: पञ्चभिः specifically encodes 'five' and cannot silently mean 'three'.

15. (C) In नरः पञ्चभिः वकारैः पूजितः, पञ्चभिः (instrumental plural of पञ्चन्) and वकारैः (instrumental plural, 'by va-things') stand in agreement — same case, same number — so the numeral quantifies the noun: 'by five va-qualities.' This is how a numeral-adjective agrees with the noun it counts. They are not in different cases: both are instrumental, not genitive. वकारैः is not the nominative subject — that role belongs to नरः ('the man'), the one honoured; वकारैः is the means. And they do not disagree in number: पञ्चभिः is plural (five things), matching the plural वकारैः.

16. (B) Two grammatical facts overturn the active reading. First, पूजितः is a past passive participle ('honoured/worshipped'), so नरः (nominative) is the PATIENT — the one who IS honoured — not an agent doing worship. Second, पञ्चभिः वकारैः is instrumental, expressing the MEANS ('by five va-qualities'), not an accusative object. Correct sense: 'a man is honoured by five va-qualities.' The claim that पूजितः is an active present verb is the core mistake. नरः is nominative, not accusative — it is the subject of the predicate 'honoured', not an object. And 'the man worships five things' is exactly the misreading the question exposes.

17. (A) विनय is humility — composed, self-effacing conduct. The fault that most squarely destroys it is क्रोधः (anger), since an angry person abandons humility. So विनय ↔ क्रोधः is the natural quality-fault opposition. The other pairings mix unrelated items: वस्त्र (dress) is itself a quality, not a fault, so it cannot 'negate' विद्या. वाणी (speech) is a quality, not a fault, so it cannot negate वपुस्. And दीर्घायुष् (long life) is a blessing, not even one of the six faults, so it cannot negate वाणी. Only क्रोधः is a genuine fault paired against the virtue it most clearly undermines.

18. (C) The verse names five qualities together; विनय (humility) is the fifth. Arrogance is precisely the absence of विनय, so the man lacks one of the five and his honour is incomplete. Four out of five does not 'guarantee' full honour — the verse treats the set as a whole, and humility rounds out character. Arrogance is not a sign of विद्या; genuine learning is usually paired with humility, not pride. And while arrogance fails विनय, arrogance is not itself one of the six named faults (those are sleep, drowsiness, fear, anger, laziness, procrastination); the reason his honour falters is simply the missing fifth quality, not a listed दोष erasing the rest.

19. (B) The six faults are निद्रा, तन्द्रा, भयम्, क्रोधः, आलस्यम्, दीर्घसूत्रता — six, deliberately distinct. निद्रा is full sleep; तन्द्रा is drowsiness/half-sleep — related but separate states. आलस्यम् is a settled disinclination to act; दीर्घसूत्रता is the specific habit of dragging out and postponing tasks. Treating each pair as one fault is exactly the undercount the chapter warns against, giving four instead of six. The reader is therefore not right. Merging भयम् and क्रोधः into one 'emotion' fault would undercount even further — they are two separate faults. And nothing splits into three; the count is fixed at six.

20. (C) आलस्यम् = laziness in the broad sense — a settled state of inactivity, doing nothing. दीर्घसूत्रता literally 'long-thread-ness' = procrastination — taking up tasks but spinning them out and delaying them. So the first is general, the second is task-specific delay. The reverse mapping swaps the two definitions and is wrong. Full sleep/drowsiness describe निद्रा/तन्द्रा, a different pair, not these. And neither is about fear (भयम्) or anger (क्रोधः) — those are two separate faults; fusing them in would confuse four distinct items.

21. (C) Syllable count has nothing to do with meaning. The real distinction is that निद्रा is full sleep — the deeper, complete state — while तन्द्रा is drowsiness, a sluggish half-awake condition — the milder one. So the student's word-length 'rule' is baseless, and his implied ranking (निद्रा milder) is actually backwards. They are not synonyms — the chapter lists them as two separate faults precisely because they differ. And neither means fear (भयम्) or anger (क्रोधः); both are sleep-related states, just at different depths.

22. (B) हातव्याः is a gerundive/future-passive participle ('to be given up'). Such forms are passive: the thing-to-be-done (faults, दोषाः) is the grammatical subject in the nominative, and the agent who must do it stands in the INSTRUMENTAL. भूतिम् इच्छता ('by one desiring prosperity') is that instrumental agent. So the literal force is 'the six faults are-to-be-abandoned by one desiring prosperity', not 'the desirer abandons them' as an active subject. इच्छता is instrumental, not nominative, so it cannot be the plain subject. भूतिम् is accusative ('prosperity', the object of desiring), not the subject. And हातव्याः is not an active verb — its very passive/gerundive nature is what forces the instrumental agent.

23. (A) To convert a gerundive-passive into an active sentence you flip the roles: the instrumental agent becomes the nominative subject, the nominative patient becomes the accusative object, and the gerundive becomes a finite active verb. Hence भूतिम् इच्छन् नरः (nom. subject) षड् दोषान् (acc. object) त्यजेत् (active 'should give up'). Merely changing दोषाः to instrumental दोषैः leaves a passive gerundive and breaks agreement — the faults are the patient, not the means. Changing इच्छता to genitive इच्छतः keeps the passive frame and just mis-cases the agent. Replacing भूतिम् with nominative भूतिः would make 'prosperity' a subject, which is not the active restructuring required while हातव्याः (still passive) remains.

24. (A) शरीरस्थः = 'standing/seated in the body', i.e. resident within oneself. Applied to आलस्यम् (laziness), it dramatises that the great enemy (महान् रिपुः) is not external but lives inside, so the battle for achievement is self-mastery. The body is not itself the enemy — laziness is; the body is merely where this inner foe dwells, so neglecting or harming the body misreads the image. The verse does not rank external enemies above laziness — it elevates this inner one to 'great enemy'. And the enemy is precisely in one's OWN body, not only in others — the whole point is that you must face it within yourself.

25. (D) रिपुः (enemy) raises the relation 'enemy of WHOM?' That possessive relation is the genitive (षष्ठी): मनुष्याणाम् is genitive plural, 'of men'. So laziness is the great enemy OF men. It is not 'by men' — that would be the instrumental मनुष्यैः, and laziness is not done by men, it afflicts them. It is not the nominative subject — the subject is आलस्यम्. And it is plural ('men'), not a dative singular 'for a man'; the form ends in -आणाम्, the genitive plural marker.

26. (D) When a sentence equates two things with 'is' (an unstated copula here), both the subject and the predicate noun stand in the nominative and agree: आलस्यम् (subject) = रिपुः (predicate noun), 'laziness is an enemy.' That is why both are nominative. रिपुः is not an object laziness acts on — there is no transitive action; it is an identity statement, so neither is accusative. It is not the means (instrumental) — laziness does not 'work by means of an enemy'; it IS the enemy. And रिपुः does not possess laziness — that would need a genitive; here the two simply name the same thing.

27. (D) A finite verb agrees in number with its grammatical subject. गात्राणि ('limbs') is neuter plural → शुध्यन्ति (3rd person plural). बुद्धिः ('intellect') is singular → शुध्यति (3rd person singular). The instrumental means-word (जलेन, ज्ञानेन) plays no role in determining verb number — that is the student's error. The endings do not signal a tense difference: both शुध्यन्ति and शुध्यति are present; the contrast is number, not time. And neither is an imperative/command form — both are plain present statements; the difference is solely singular vs plural subject.

28. (B) The verse pairs गात्राणि–जलेन (body–water), मनः–सत्येन (mind–truth), आत्मा–तपसा (soul–austerity), बुद्धिः–ज्ञानेन (intellect–knowledge). The governing principle is 'right means for each goal': no single means serves all four. So washing with water purifies only the body; the mind is cleaned by truth, not water. The boy collapses four means into one, violating that one-to-one matching. Water does NOT clean the mind here — that is the error itself. The mind's means is truth (सत्येन), not knowledge — knowledge is the intellect's means; so 'mind by knowledge' is also a mismatch. And the mind certainly can be cleaned — by truth — so 'cannot be cleaned at all' is wrong.

29. (D) Correct pairs: body–water, mind–truth, soul–austerity, intellect–knowledge. In the given set, मनः सत्येन ✓ and बुद्धिः ज्ञानेन ✓ are already right. Only गात्राणि and आत्मा are crossed: the set has गात्राणि तपसा and आत्मा जलेन, which are exactly swapped. Exchanging just those two means restores गात्राणि जलेन and आत्मा तपसा, fixing everything with one swap. Swapping मनः and बुद्धिः would BREAK two pairs that were already correct. Changing मनः सत्येन to मनः जलेन wrecks a correct pair and leaves the body/soul cross unfixed. And the set is not already correct — body and soul are mismatched.

30. (A) In this verse बुद्धिः is the intellect/judgement, a non-material faculty, and its purifier is knowledge (ज्ञानेन) — clear understanding refines judgement. Water (जलेन) is the means for गात्राणि, the physical limbs. The learner's slip is to treat the intellect as a washable physical organ, importing a literal-water logic where a metaphorical means is intended. Water does NOT clean the intellect here — that is the misconception. तपसा (austerity) is the means for the soul (आत्मा), not the intellect, so that 'correction' is also wrong. And the intellect DOES get a means (knowledge), so the question is meaningful, not empty.

31. (B) The four means are water (body), truth (mind), austerity (soul), knowledge (intellect). The subtlest mismatch is बुद्धिः सत्येन: truth IS a genuine purifier in the verse, but of the MIND, not the intellect — so it is tempting precisely because सत्य belongs to the system, just to the wrong slot; the intellect's correct means is knowledge. बुद्धिः जलेन is wrong but cruder — water purifies the body, a PHYSICAL part, not an inner faculty, so it is a more obvious mismatch. वस्त्र is not a purification means at all (it is a honouring quality), so बुद्धिः वस्त्रेण is plainly off-system. And anger (क्रोधः) is a fault, never a purifier of the soul or anything else, so that pairing is the least subtle of all.

32. (B) The verse's logic is that each part needs a means suited to its kind of impurity. The mind's stains are moral/mental — falsehood, crookedness — and the remedy that dissolves those is truth (सत्य). Water addresses physical dirt on the body but has no purchase on inner deceit, so it is the wrong tool for the mind. Scarcity has nothing to do with it — the verse is about appropriateness, not cost. Truth and water are not the same purifier renamed — they clean different kinds of part (inner vs physical). And the mind is treated as a non-physical faculty here, so truth works morally, not by physical washing.

33. (D) Washing with water purifies गात्राणि — the physical body — and that part he has indeed cleaned. But the mind (मनः) is purified by truth (सत्येन); persistent lying and anger leave the mind unpurified, since water cannot touch inner impurity. So body clean, mind dirty. He has NOT purified everything — water does not reach the mind. The soul (आत्मा) is cleaned by austerity (तपसा), not water, so 'soul purified by water' is wrong. And washing is not knowledge — the intellect (बुद्धिः) is cleaned by ज्ञानेन, so careful hand-washing does nothing for it.

34. (D) Two different words sit in two different verses. विद्या is a va-quality that earns honour ('a man is honoured by knowledge/learning'). ज्ञानेन (instrumental of ज्ञान) is the means that purifies the intellect (बुद्धिः ज्ञानेन शुध्यति). Even though both touch the idea of knowing, they are separate lexical items in separate roles, so nothing is double-listed. They are not the identical word — विद्या ≠ ज्ञान in form. विद्या does not clean the body — the body's means is water (जलेन). And विद्या certainly does appear — it is one of the five honouring qualities — so 'neither verse' is false.

35. (C) गात्राणि is the neuter nominative plural of गात्र ('limb/body-part') — 'the limbs'. Verb-subject agreement in number means a plural subject requires the plural finite verb, here शुध्यन्ति ('are purified'). So it is number agreement that picks शुध्यन्ति over the singular शुध्यति. Treating गात्राणि as 'one whole body' to justify a singular verb ignores that the form is grammatically plural and governs a plural verb. The means जलेन does not control verb number — agreement is with the subject, not the instrument. And गात्राणि is neuter plural, not feminine singular; the -आणि ending is the neuter plural marker.

36. (A) क्रमशः = 'in order, gradually, step by step.' The drop-by-drop image (जलबिन्दुनिपातेन, 'by the falling of water-drops') is the very picture of incremental growth: every drop adds a small amount, and the pot fills over the whole process, not in one final burst. So there is no 'sudden jump at the end' — that contradicts the gradual image. क्रमशः is not 'all at once' — that is its opposite. And it is not 'backwards/emptying' — the verb पूर्यते means 'is FILLED', so the pot is gaining, not losing, content gradually.

37. (B) In a passive, the patient (the thing acted upon) takes the nominative and the means/agent takes the instrumental. Here घटः ('pot', nominative) is what gets filled; जलबिन्दुनिपातेन ('by the falling of water-drops', instrumental) is the means; पूर्यते ('is filled') is the passive verb. So: patient = pot, means = falling drops, action = passive 'is filled'. The pot is not the agent — it does not fill something else; it is filled. The drops are not the patient — they are the means; the pot is what is filled. And पूर्यते is not active with घटः as object — passive constructions put the patient in the nominative, which घटः is.

38. (B) The verse uses the gradual pot-filling to teach that विद्या (knowledge), धर्मः (virtue/duty) and धनम् (wealth) accumulate incrementally — steady effort over time, not a single windfall. The point is encouragement: small consistent efforts compound into great results. Slow growth does not imply low value — the chosen trio is precisely the most prized goods, so the 'worthless' reading inverts the message. The image insists they are NOT got all at once — that is the misconception the verse refutes. And these three are virtues/goods, not faults; the six faults are sleep, drowsiness, fear, anger, laziness, procrastination, none of which is विद्या, धर्म or धनम्.

39. (B) The accumulation principle — small repeated inputs producing a large result — is mechanism-neutral: it works for whatever is repeated. So just as daily study builds knowledge, a daily small lie builds a large habit of dishonesty, drop by drop. The 'never accumulates' reading wrongly restricts the law to good things; the verse's logic of accumulation applies to any repeated act. Lies do not 'cancel out' — repetition reinforces, it does not erase. And while the literal image is water and a pot, its very purpose is metaphorical extension (to knowledge, virtue, wealth — and by the same logic, to habits), so 'only literal water' misses the point of the subhashita.

40. (A) Meera's daily 20 minutes is exactly the drop-by-drop, क्रमशः pattern — small steady inputs accumulating like drops filling a pot. Arjun's pattern of doing nothing and then a single last-night marathon is दीर्घसूत्रता (procrastination — delaying the task until forced). Arjun does NOT embody the drop-pot verse — that verse praises gradual repetition, the opposite of one burst. They are not equal under क्रमशः — क्रमशः specifically means gradual/stepwise, which only Meera does; raw total hours is not the verse's measure. And the labels are not reversed: Meera's diligence is the opposite of आलस्यम् (laziness), and Arjun's cramming does not exemplify विद्या (knowledge) as a process.

41. (C) जलबिन्दुनिपात is one compound: जल (water) + बिन्दु (drop) + निपात (falling) = 'the falling of water-drops', and the single instrumental ending -एन makes the whole compound one means: 'by the falling of water-drops.' Splitting it into three separate instruments misreads a unified compound as a list. It is not a purpose ('for the sake of') — the ending is instrumental (means), not dative; the pot is filled BY this, not FOR it. It is not a location — that would be locative (-e), not instrumental. And the members are not unrelated — they combine into one coherent image; they do not merely 'share an ending' by accident.

42. (A) प्रेरकः means 'one who impels/inspires' — that is the coach, who drives the student forward. रिपुः ('enemy'), and specifically the शरीरस्थः रिपुः (inner enemy), is laziness dwelling within the student. So coach = प्रेरकः, laziness = inner रिपुः. Calling the coach a रिपुः and laziness a प्रेरकः reverses helper and obstacle. Both cannot be प्रेरकाः — laziness impels nothing forward; it holds back. And both cannot be रिपवः — the coach helps, so he is not an enemy; only the laziness is.

43. (D) रिपुः is an enemy — something to overcome and keep away. Its functional opposite is something beneficial to keep near, and the chapter holds up सङ्गतिः (good company) as exactly that: the company you keep moulds your character for the better. So good company is the 'keep-close' counterpart to the 'keep-away' enemy. दीर्घसूत्रता (procrastination) is itself a fault, not a benefit to keep close. निद्रा (sleep) is a fault too, not a friend to indulge. And क्रोधः (anger) is a fault, certainly not an ally to nurture — so none of these is the beneficial opposite; only good company is.

44. (A) Two ideas combine: सङ्गतिः (the company one keeps shapes character) and दीर्घसूत्रता (procrastination — putting tasks off). The friends' habit of skipping and delaying work is procrastination, and through bad company it spreads to her. So: bad सङ्गतिः → the fault of दीर्घसूत्रता. It is not GOOD company strengthening विद्या — the company is harmful and breeds a fault, not knowledge. It is not harmless — the chapter's whole point about सङ्गतिः is that company DOES shape character. And copying idle friends is not विनय (humility) — humility is a virtue; here a fault is being acquired.

45. (C) दीर्घ ('long') opens both compounds, but the second member decides the sense. दीर्घ + सूत्र ('thread') + -ता gives दीर्घसूत्रता, 'long-thread-ness' = dragging tasks out = procrastination (a fault). दीर्घ + आयुस् ('life') gives दीर्घायुष्, 'long-lived' = a long lifespan (a blessing). So same first word, opposite valence. The lesson is therefore to read the whole compound including the second member. The reversal (दीर्घसूत्रता = long life) is wrong. Neither means literally 'a long thread' — दीर्घायुष् is about life, not thread. And they are not interchangeable 'long life' synonyms — one is a fault, the other a blessing, which is precisely why the parts matter.

46. (A) The six faults (sleep, drowsiness, fear, anger, laziness, procrastination) are all things one should give up. दीर्घायुष् ('long-lived') names a desirable blessing — a long life — which one would WANT, not shed. A blessing is categorically not a fault-to-abandon, so it cannot belong on the list. The shared दीर्घ prefix is a red herring — meaning, not spelling, determines membership. It is not a 'seventh fault' held back for counting reasons — it simply is not a fault at all. And it is not another name for आलस्यम् — laziness and a long lifespan are unrelated; one is a fault, the other a blessing.

47. (B) Both cases show a word-final म् followed by a consonant (प in नित्यं पिबामः, ह in आलस्यं हि), and in both the म् is written as anusvāra ं. The unifying rule: word-final म् → anusvāra ं before a following consonant. It is not visarga sandhi — there is no final ः here; the input is म्, not ः. It is not vowel-lengthening — no two vowels are meeting; a consonant follows. And it is not consonant-doubling — प and ह are not doubled; the change is in the preceding nasal, not the following consonant.

48. (C) The sandhi rule converts final म् to anusvāra ं specifically when a CONSONANT follows. A following vowel does not trigger it, so नित्यम् + एव stays नित्यम् एव — the म् is retained. So the absence of a following consonant is exactly why the rule lapses. म् does not 'always' become anusvāra at word-end — only before consonants. The म् is not deleted before vowels — it remains. And there is no doubling of the vowel — that is not what this rule does.

But the faults-verse says the six faults must be given up by one who desires भूति (prosperity); keeping anger and procrastination therefore endangers his prosperity. 52. (C) Two complementary metaphors: rasa is to be drunk (an active intake of nourishment — wisdom), and रिपुः is to be conquered (an active resistance to harm — laziness). Their common thread is that self-improvement is active on both sides: cultivate the good AND defeat the bad, neither passively. 'Leave both alone' contradicts both verbs (drink, conquer). 'Avoid the nectar, welcome the enemy' inverts both — you welcome the nectar and resist the enemy. And wisdom and laziness are opposites here, not 'the same force' — one nourishes, the other obstructs.

53. (D) A question-word's case signals the case of the expected answer. केन is the instrumental of किम् ('by what?'), so the answer must be an instrumental naming a means: जलेन (by water), सत्येन (by truth), तपसा (by austerity), ज्ञानेन (by knowledge). A nominative like बुद्धिः answers 'what is purified?' (the subject), not 'by what?'. A genitive like मनुष्याणाम् answers 'of whom?'. And a vocative like हे बुद्धे is a form of address, not an answer to a means-question.

54. (A) Two distinct verses are joined. 'Pot fills drop by drop' is the जलबिन्दुनिपातेन verse — gradual accumulation. 58. (A) आलस्यम् is a broad disinclination to act; दीर्घसूत्रता is the habit of dragging out and postponing specific tasks. 59. (D) Sanskrit case can echo meaning. The qualities appear in the instrumental because they are the MEANS of being honoured ('honoured BY them'); the faults appear with a gerundive (हातव्याः, 'to-be-given-up') because they are patients the person must act upon by discarding. So the grammar tracks the moral structure: cultivate-as-means vs discard-as-object. This is far from random — the case choices encode the roles. The roles are not reversed — qualities are means (not objects to abandon), and faults are objects to abandon (not the means of honour). And the person is not passive — he actively uses qualities and actively gives up faults; both verses imply an agent doing something.

60. (B) The drop-pot verse supplies persistence (steady repetition); the purification verse supplies appropriateness (water for body, truth for mind, austerity for soul, knowledge for intellect — the matched means). Each is incomplete on its own: tireless effort with the wrong method wastes itself, and the perfect method applied once or half-heartedly achieves little. So the pairing insists on BOTH effort and the right means. The verses do NOT say the same thing — they contribute different halves, so neither is a mere backup. Gradual effort alone is not 'always enough' — without the right means it can misfire. And the right means alone is not enough either — without sustained effort it produces no accumulation. Only the both/and reading honours why the chapter keeps both verses.
