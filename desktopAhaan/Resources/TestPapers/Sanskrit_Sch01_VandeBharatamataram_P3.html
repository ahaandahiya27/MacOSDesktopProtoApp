Worked Solutions

1. (C) वन्दे is the active first-person singular of root वन्द् ('to salute/revere'), and मातरम् is its accusative object. Rendering it 'I offer salutation to the Mother' preserves both the active sense and the reverence. The idea that वन्दे is passive or that the Mother is the agent is wrong; वन्दे does not mean romantic love (that is कामये); and मातरम् is accusative (the object), not nominative.

2. (A) वन्दे expresses reverential salutation, distinct from affection (कामये / स्निह्यामि). The reversed pairing that calls वन्दे 'love' is the classic error the lesson warns against; वन्दे is neither 'see' (दृश्) nor 'give' (दा).

3. (D) An adjective agrees with the noun it qualifies in case, number and gender. सुजलाम् and सुफलाम् describe मातरम् (accusative singular feminine, object of वन्दे), so they too are accusative singular feminine. They are not subjects (nominative), nor means (instrumental), nor possessors (genitive).

4. (C) वन्दे, being a finite transitive verb, takes its object in the accusative (वन्दे मातरम्). The indeclinable नमस् ('homage') is commonly construed with the dative of the one honoured (नमो मात्रे, 'homage to the mother'). The chapter pairs वन्दे + accusative; the claim that they are reversed, identical, or caseless is false.

5. (C) Feminine ऋ-stems of relationship (मातृ, स्वसृ, दुहितृ) take guṇa of the ऋ to अर् before the accusative-singular ending -अम्: मातृ → मातरम्, so स्वसृ → स्वसारम्. The forms स्वसृम् (no guṇa), स्वस्राम् (a wrong ā-stem ending) and स्वसाः (nominative-like) are all incorrect.

6. (D) The accusative singular of the r-stem मातृ is मातरम्. The form मात्राम् would be an accusative of मात्रा ('a measure'), a different word — so the writer has both used the wrong word and the wrong declension. वन्दे correctly governs the accusative (so nominative is wrong), and वन्दे is itself a valid first-person form (no need for वन्दामि here).

7. (B) हिमालयः is an अकारान्त masculine noun; as the accusative-singular object of वन्दे it ends in -म् (हिमालयम्). It is masculine, not feminine; -म् here is accusative, not genitive (which would be हिमालयस्य); and वन्दे governs the accusative, not the nominative (हिमालयः).

8. (B) मातृ → मातरौ shows the dual ending -औ, and as the object of वन्दे it is the accusative dual ('the two mothers'). The singular accusative is मातरम्; the nominative plural is मातरः; the genitive singular is मातुः. So -औ here is accusative dual.

9. (B) श्यामला is a feminine आ-stem adjective; its accusative singular is श्यामलाम् (-आम्), agreeing in gender, number and case with the feminine मातरम्. Masculine/neuter accusative singulars end in -अम्, not -आम्; -आम् here is singular, not plural; and the long आ is correct, not a sandhi error.

10. (C) The object of वन्दे must be accusative, and the feminine adjective must agree: सुफलाम् मातरम् (both feminine accusative singular). सुफला माता is nominative (wrong as object); सुफलायाः मातुः is genitive; and सुफलम् would be a masculine/neuter ending that fails to agree with the feminine मातरम्.

11. (C) रत्नाकरः = रत्न ('jewel') + आकर ('mine/store'), 'store of jewels'. Replacing the first member with जल ('water') gives जलाकरः, 'a store/reservoir of water'. It does not mean 'king' (that is राज), nor 'water-less' (which would need a negating prefix), nor 'jewel-carrier'.

12. (B) पर्वतराजः is a षष्ठी-तत्पुरुष: पर्वतानाम् राजा. देवराजः = देवानाम् राजा has the identical genitive-tatpurusha structure. महाराजः is a कर्मधारय (adjective + noun, 'great king'), not a genitive relation; 'king is a son' mis-reads राजपुत्रः; and 'a king and a mountain' is a द्वन्द्व (coordinative), a different compound type.

13. (A) श्यामला ('dark/green') is qualified by सस्य as the cause/means: 'dark-green by reason of (its) crops' — a तृतीया (instrumental) tatpurusha. It is not a द्वन्द्व ('crops and darkness'), and although the whole word describes the Mother, the internal relation is instrumental, not a self-contained bahuvrīhi reading; nor is it a reduplication.

14. (C) In these compounds the SECOND member भक्तः ('devoted to') is the head, and the FIRST member names the object of devotion. विद्या ('knowledge') as the first member yields विद्याभक्तः, 'devoted to learning'. देश would give देशभक्तः, धर्म would give धर्मभक्तः, and समुद्र is unrelated to विद्या.

15. (B) बान्धव is an अकारान्त masculine; -आः is its nominative-plural ending, so कृषकबान्धवाः means '(the) farmer-brethren' as plural subjects. The accusative singular would be -अम् (बान्धवम्), the instrumental plural -ऐः (बान्धवैः), and the genitive singular -अस्य (बान्धवस्य).

16. (C) रत्नाकरः splits cleanly into रत्न ('jewel') + आकर ('mine'), a poetic name for the ocean. The other splits are spurious: पर्वतराजः is पर्वत + राजः ('king of mountains'), not पर्व + तराज; सस्यश्यामला is सस्य + श्यामला ('crop-dark'), the negating split is invented; and देशभक्तः is देश + भक्तः, not देशभ + क्तः.

17. (A) The genitive singular of the r-stem मातृ is मातुः (as in भारतमातुः, 'of the Motherland'). मातरम् is the accusative, मातरि the locative, and मात्रा the instrumental — none of which expresses 'of the mother'.

18. (B) The genitive marks possession independent of the possessed noun's case or number; 'the Motherland's hands' keeps भारतमातुः genitive just as 'the Motherland's feet' did. The dual हस्तौ does not change the possessor's case; possession is shown by the genitive, not the locative; and swapping the body-part does not turn the possessor accusative.

19. (A) कस्याः is feminine genitive singular ('of whom/whose', feminine). A grammatical answer must agree, so भारतमातुः is feminine genitive singular. It is not nominative (the subject is समुद्रः), not accusative (the object is चरणौ), and not instrumental (no 'means' is asked).

20. (B) चरण is an अकारान्त masculine; -औ marks the dual, and as the object of प्रक्षालयति it is accusative dual ('the two feet'). The accusative and nominative dual look alike for such stems, but the role here is object; the genitive dual would be चरणयोः and the instrumental dual चरणाभ्याम्.

21. (D) 'The Himalaya's' (possessor) needs the genitive: हिमालयस्य. With चरणौ as the object washed, the correct phrase is समुद्रः हिमालयस्य चरणौ प्रक्षालयति. हिमालयम् is accusative (wrong for the possessor), हिमालयः is nominative (would make it the subject), and हिमालयेन is instrumental ('by the Himalaya').

22. (A) Neuter अ-stems take -ऐः in the instrumental plural, so जल → जलैः ('with waters'). जलानि is the nominative/accusative plural, जलेभ्यः the dative/ablative plural, and जलानाम् the genitive plural — none expresses 'by means of'. (The -भिः of स्वेदबिन्दुभिः is the u-stem instrumental-plural; both -भिः and -ऐः render the same instrumental 'with'.)

23. (A) The ending -भिः marks the instrumental plural, the means: 'with sweat-drops'. 'Of the drops' (possession) would be the genitive plural स्वेदबिन्दूनाम्. -भिः is neither vocative ('O drops!') nor accusative (object) — those are different endings and meanings.

24. (B) Just as स्वेदबिन्दुभिः ('with sweat-drops') is instrumental of means, अश्रुभिः ('with tears') would also be instrumental, expressing the means of watering. It is not the subject (the farmers act), not a recipient (dative), and not a place (locative).

25. (D) The verb's doers, the farmers, stand in the nominative (कृषकाः); the means by which they water, the sweat-drops, stand in the instrumental (स्वेदबिन्दुभिः). Reversing the roles, calling both instrumental, or making the farmers an object misreads the sentence's structure.

26. (A) Sweat is shed in many drops, so the plural स्वेदबिन्दुभिः ('with sweat-drops') fits the sense. The instrumental does have a singular (-ना type), so it is not plural-only; the means need not match the number of the agent automatically — it reflects the actual multiplicity of drops.

27. (D) आ (दीर्घ, two mātrās) and अ (ह्रस्व, one mātrā) are separate vowel sounds; substituting one for the other can change or destroy a word's meaning (आनन्द 'bliss' vs the non-word/altered अनन्द). They are not the same sound, not consonant-vs-vowel, and not a nasal/oral pair.

28. (A) मात्रा here means the unit of pronunciation-time of a vowel: a short (ह्रस्व) vowel is one mātrā, a long (दीर्घ) vowel two. It is not a noun case, not a verb class, and not a name of Bharat (note मात्रा 'measure' ≠ मातृ 'mother').

29. (D) The basic vowels pair up by length: अ/आ, इ/ई, उ/ऊ, with the first of each pair short (ह्रस्व, one mātrā) and the second long (दीर्घ, two mātrās). So अ इ उ are short and आ ई ऊ are long. The other groupings mis-assign or flatten this distinction.

30. (B) Just as आ/अ differ, इ (ह्रस्व) and ई (दीर्घ) are distinct vowels; substituting one for the other can alter meaning. इ does have a long counterpart ई; the length principle is not limited to आ; and इ and ई are NOT identical in duration.

31. (D) आनन्दमठः is the novel ('monastery/abbey of bliss') in which वन्दे मातरम् appears; आनन्द ('bliss') needs the long आ, which is why it doubles as a vowel-length illustration. It is not the author's name (that is बङ्किमचन्द्रः), not a case of मातृ, and not a verb.

32. (C) वन्द् is an ātmanepada root: first-person singular वन्दे ('I salute'), third-person singular वन्दते ('he/she salutes'). वन्दे is the 'I' form, वन्दन्ते is third-person plural ('they salute'), and वन्दसे is second-person singular ('you salute').

33. (A) सिञ्चन्ति ends in -अन्ति, the third-person plural active ending: 'they water' (the farmers). First-person singular would be सिञ्चामि, second-person plural सिञ्चथ, and third-person singular सिञ्चति.

34. (C) A finite verb agrees with its SUBJECT. समुद्रः ('the ocean') is third-person singular, so प्रक्षालयति (3rd sg.) is correct. The subject is singular, not plural (so not -अन्ति); प्रक्षालयति is third person, not first; and the verb agrees with the subject's number, not the object's dual.

35. (B) वन्द् is ātmanepada; first-person plural is वन्दामहे ('we salute'). वन्दे is first-person singular ('I'), वन्दन्ते is third-person plural ('they'), and वन्देथे is second-person dual. So 'we salute' = वन्दामहे.

36. (C) A finite verb like वन्दे encodes person, number and action ('I salute') and can stand as a minimal sentence. मातरम् is an accusative noun (an object) with no verb, so it cannot. The roles are not reversed (मातरम् is not a verb), they are not both verbs, and वन्दे is a fully inflected form, not a bare stem.

37. (C) सिञ्चन्ति uses the -अन्ति (parasmaipada) third-plural ending, while वन्दते uses the -ते (ātmanepada) third-singular ending; Sanskrit roots take different ending-sets (pada) by class. Verbs do not all share one ending-set, they DO mark person and number, and that marking is on the verb, not the noun.

38. (D) Bharat's Himalaya is in the north (imagined as the high 'head/crown') and the oceans wash the southern coasts (the 'feet') — the imagery mirrors real geography. The reversed (Himalaya-south) claim is false, both are not east, and the placement is deliberately geographic, not random.

39. (A) Both names elevate natural features with grand titles ('king of mountains', 'mine of jewels'), praising Bharat's landscape. They are not neutral facts, not criticism, and not the names of human kings — they are poetic honorifics for nature.

40. (C) सस्यश्यामला ('dark-green with crops') paints Bharat's fertile midland, complementing the crown (head) and ocean (feet) to give a complete, abundant figure. It does not call her barren, does not introduce cities, and describes the cultivated LAND, not the ocean.

41. (D) Linking सस्यश्यामला (crop-green) with कृषकबान्धवाः watering the soil by sweat shows the greenery as earned by farmers' labour. The ocean and the Himalaya beautify her but are not credited with the crops, and the imagery explicitly ties abundance TO human effort.

42. (C) Naming तीर्थक्षेत्राणि ('places of pilgrimage') adds holiness to the catalogue of mountains, seas and fields, praising Bharat's sacredness alongside her beauty. It does not imply a lack of rivers, is not a neutral tourist list, and does not suggest smallness.

43. (B) The hymn's controlling image is Bharat-as-mother whose body is the land: Himalaya-crown, ocean-feet, crop-green skin, holy sites. She is not depicted as a warrior-queen, nor as detached from the soil (she IS the soil), nor as a merchant city.

44. (B) रत्नाकरः ('store of jewels') is an epithet: by analysing रत्न + आकर and using context (the thing washing Bharat's feet) the reader identifies the ocean. It is not a respelling of समुद्र, does not contain समुद्र, and समुद्र is a perfectly usable word — the epithet is a poetic choice.

45. (A) The chapter credits बङ्किमचन्द्रः चट्टोपाध्यायः with the song, embedded in his 1882 novel आनन्दमठः. It is not Tagore's, not composed by the lesson's children, and not an anonymous Vedic hymn.

46. (C) Being part of the novel आनन्दमठः situates the song within a patriotic narrative rather than as an isolated verse. It does not make the work anonymous, is certainly not a recipe, and does not predate Sanskrit literature — the novel is a 19th-century work.

47. (D) जिज्ञासा means the desire to know (curiosity); the children's जिज्ञासा prompts the mother's explanation, framing the lesson. It is not salutation (वन्दनम्), not sweat (स्वेदः), and not crown (मुकुटः).

48. (A) The mother's explanation is triggered by the children's जिज्ञासा (curiosity); without it the explanatory dialogue would not happen. जिज्ञासा does not position the Himalaya, does not govern any case, and does not name the author — those are separate content elements.

49. (A) Here भारतमातुः ('of the Motherland') depends on वन्दनम् as a possessive/objective genitive, the same -उः genitive form. It is not accusative (which would be भारतमातरम्), not instrumental (भारतमात्रा), and not nominative (भारतमाता).

50. (B) मातरम् is the accusative object; सस्यश्यामलाम् and सुजलाम् are adjectives agreeing with it, all feminine accusative singular (note the -आम्/-अम् accusative endings). The other counts wrongly treat the adjectives as nominative or genitive, or deny that वन्दे takes an object.

51. (D) समुद्रः ('the ocean') is the nominative subject that does the washing; चरणौ ('the two feet') is the accusative object washed; भारतमातुः ('of the Motherland') is the genitive possessor of those feet. The other mappings scramble subject, object and possessor.

52. (D) The doers are nominative (कृषकाः), the fields are the accusative object (क्षेत्राणि, neuter pl.), and the means is instrumental (जलैः, 'with water'): कृषकाः क्षेत्राणि जलैः सिञ्चन्ति. Making the farmers instrumental (कृषकैः) or accusative (कृषकान्), the fields instrumental (क्षेत्रैः), or the water accusative (जलानि) breaks the role-to-case mapping.

53. (D) For मातृ, the nominative singular is माता and the accusative singular is मातरम् — so the -म् is the accusative marker, not nominative. -म् does not always mark nominative; it is not the genitive (मातुः) nor the vocative (मातः) here.

54. (A) The instrumental स्वेदबिन्दुभिः signals 'by means of (sweat-drops)', which presupposes an action carried out using them — the farmers' watering (सिञ्चन्ति). The instrumental is not the subject (the drops do not act alone), not the object (they are not watered), and not possession (that is genitive).

55. (D) Sanskrit verbs encode person and number, so वन्दे already contains its subject 'I'; no separate pronoun is required. The sentence is not subject-less; मातरम् is the object, not 'I'; and वन्दे is a verb, not a second subject.

56. (A) Every clause must hold: वन्दे is first-person singular of वन्द् ('I salute'); मातरम् is the accusative singular of मातृ (the object); the line is from Bankim Chandra's 1882 novel आनन्दमठः. The distractors err on person/number, case, meaning, authorship, or date.

57. (C) वन्दे needs an accusative object, so माता (nominative) must be मातरम्. And पर्वतराजेन is instrumental ('by the king of mountains'), which has no sensible role in 'I salute Mother' — there is no instrument of saluting intended. So there are two faults, not zero or one; and वन्दे itself is a correct verb (no need for नमः).

58. (B) Both principles hold together: split देश + भक्तः for 'devoted to country', and treat आ (दीर्घ) vs अ (ह्रस्व) as meaning-bearing so आनन्द ('bliss') must keep its long आ. Claims that length is irrelevant, that compounds cannot be split, or that आ→अ merely changes gender are all false.

59. (B) For the r-stem मातृ: nominative singular = माता (subject), accusative singular = मातरम् (object), genitive singular = मातुः ('of'). The correct pairing is accusative → मातरम् → object. The distractors mis-assign मातरम् to the nominative, माता to the genitive, or मातुः to the accusative.

60. (D) All three layers must be right: meaning (reverence for the mother), grammar (वन्दे + accusative मातरम्; genitive भारतमातुः with चरणौ), and imagery (Himalaya = crown/पर्वतराजः at the head, ocean = रत्नाकरः washing the feet). The distractors invert the imagery, mis-gloss वन्दे, mis-case मातृ, or wrongly credit Tagore.
