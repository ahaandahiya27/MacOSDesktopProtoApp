## Worked Solutions

**1. (B)** The period saw Turkic invasions reaching much deeper than the earlier Arab raids, a major turning point. India was not peaceful, the Guptas had long gone, and the Arabs had arrived centuries earlier.

**2. (A)** Turkic invasions penetrated far deeper than the Arab raids that had been confined near Sindh. They did cross into India, went beyond Sindh, and were not peaceful trade.

**3. (B)** The period's lesson is resilience and the lasting power of knowledge, with scholars, saints and temples flourishing. It was not total defeat, the end of culture, or all-India foreign rule.

**4. (D)** The Khyber Pass through the Hindu Kush served as both trade and invasion route into India. It was not a sea route, southern, or a Chola base.

**5. (C)** The Hindu Shahis defended the north-western frontier against early Turkic invaders. They did not rule the south, build Angkor Wat, or found the Sultanate.

**6. (D)** The north-west, with passes like the Khyber, was again a gateway for invaders. It was not sea-sealed, the safest frontier, or a southern port.

**7. (C)** Mahmud of Ghazni led about 17 plundering raids. He did not make a peaceful trade mission, build Nalanda, or found the Chola navy.

**8. (C)** Mahmud's raids destroyed the Somnath temple. Angkor Wat (Cambodia), Brihadishvara (Chola) and the Anubhava mandapa (Basava's hall) were not destroyed by him.

**9. (A)** Mahmud raided about 17 times for plunder and withdrew each time; he did not rule India. The Sultanate came later under others, he did reach India, and he did not rule the south.

**10. (D)** Booty or plunder is goods seized by force in war. It is not a treaty, a philosophy, or a temple tower (a vimana).

**11. (B)** Mahmud raided for treasure and returned home, not building a lasting empire. It was not empire-building, pilgrimage, or naval expedition.

**12. (B)** Mahmud raided and withdrew; the Ghurids came to stay and govern, founding lasting rule. The reversal, the temple claim and the 'never reached India' option are wrong.

**13. (C)** A raider takes loot and leaves; a conqueror stays to rule - very different. They are not the same, raiders need not build empires, and conquerors do keep land.

**14. (C)** Lasting foreign rule began with the Ghurids and generals like Aibak, not Mahmud's raids. The Chola navy and al-Biruni were not foreign rulers of north India.

**15. (A)** Al-Biruni was a scholar who learned Sanskrit and studied India's sciences, religion and customs. He was not a general, an admiral, or a poet-saint.

**16. (D)** Al-Biruni learned Sanskrit and wrote a careful study of India, showing deep two-way learning. He did not refuse Sanskrit, destroy books, or write nothing.

**17. (D)** Knowledge flowed both ways: scholars studied India, and Indian science such as sine tables spread outward. It was not one-way or absent.

**18. (D)** Al-Biruni's careful study shows scholarship continued amid the storms. Learning had not stopped, India had much worth studying, and books were written.

**19. (C)** Indian sine tables spreading abroad show India contributing knowledge to the world. It is not pure borrowing, mathematical ignorance, or knowledge that stayed put.

**20. (D)** Bhaskaracharya was a great mathematician and astronomer, showing the endurance of Indian science. He was not an invader, an admiral, or merely a builder.

**21. (B)** Bhaskaracharya shows Indian science endured and advanced despite invasions. It did not vanish, come only from abroad, or never exist.

**22. (C)** Algebra is from Arabic al-jabr ('reunion of broken parts'), al-Khwarizmi's book title. The cosmic mountain is Mount Meru, a fleet is a navy, and a short Kannada poem is a vachana.

**23. (B)** Trigonometry (Greek trigonon 'triangle' + metron 'measure') is the maths of triangles and angles. Temple towers are vimanas, ships make a navy, and Kannada poems are vachanas.

**24. (B)** The Kakatiyas built the torana (decorative gateway) at Warangal. A vimana is a temple tower, a sabha a village assembly, and a samanta a subordinate ruler.

**25. (C)** The Hoysalas were a flourishing southern and Deccan power. They were not north-western Sultanate founders, steppe nomads, or Arabs.

**26. (B)** The thriving Kakatiyas and Hoysalas show the south stayed free and flourishing while the north faced invasions. India had not all fallen, the south had kingdoms, and it was not Ghazni-ruled.

**27. (B)** Rajaraja Chola I built powerful Chola sea power. He was not a Turkic raider, a Ghurid general, or a poet-saint.

**28. (D)** A navy (Latin navis, 'ship') is a country's fighting ships and crews. A gateway is a torana, an ideas-hall the Anubhava mandapa, and the cosmic mountain Mount Meru.

**29. (C)** Rajaraja I built the Brihadishvara temple with its towering vimana. Somnath was raided by Mahmud, Nalanda was a university, and Angkor Wat is in Cambodia.

**30. (C)** Rajendra Chola I led a naval expedition to Srivijaya in Southeast Asia. The Khyber Pass, Ghazni and Kannauj are northern or overland, not naval targets.

**31. (A)** The Srivijaya expedition shows the Cholas as a far-reaching sea power. They were not landlocked, Turkic invaders, or rulers of Ghazni.

**32. (D)** Chola sea power reaching Srivijaya shows the south flourishing independently. India had not all fallen, the Cholas were not Ghurid servants, and the sea was vital.

**33. (A)** The Paramaras are linked to the scholar-king Bhoja. Prithviraj Chauhan, Muhammad Ghuri and Rajendra Chola were other figures.

**34. (A)** A scholar-king like Bhoja ruled while championing learning. He was not a non-ruler, a foreign invader, or a Chola admiral.

**35. (A)** The Ghurids were led by Muhammad Ghuri. Mahmud of Ghazni led the earlier Ghaznavid raids; Rajaraja was a Chola and al-Biruni a scholar.

**36. (B)** Ghuri lost the First Battle of Tarain (1191) to Prithviraj Chauhan. He won the Second (1192); he did not conquer all India at Tarain, and Nalanda was destroyed by Bakhtiyar Khilji.

**37. (A)** Ghuri won the Second Battle of Tarain (1192), defeating Prithviraj Chauhan and opening the way to lasting rule. He was not defeated, Chola-helped, or the destroyer of Somnath (that was Mahmud).

**38. (A)** Prithviraj Chauhan was defeated at the Second Battle of Tarain (1192). Rajaraja Chola, Bhoja and Harshavardhana were other rulers.

**39. (C)** Ghuri lost the First Battle of Tarain (1191) and won the Second (1192). The reversal, the 'Prithviraj won both' and the 'at sea' options are wrong.

**40. (A)** 'From plunder to rule' marks the shift from raiding for treasure (Mahmud) to staying to govern (the Ghurids). It is not the reverse, a peace-to-war shift, or about Ghazni.

**41. (B)** Aibak founded the Delhi Sultanate. He did not lead the Chola navy, write on algebra, or build Angkor Wat.

**42. (B)** A sultanate is the rule or territory of a sultan (an Arabic word for 'authority'). A gateway is a torana, a monastery a vihara, and a Kannada poem a vachana.

**43. (D)** Bakhtiyar Khilji destroyed Nalanda and Vikramashila. He did not found the Chola navy, write the Rajatarangini, or build Brihadishvara.

**44. (C)** Nalanda and Vikramashila were great Buddhist universities, so their destruction was a heavy loss to learning. They were not naval bases, forts, or trading ports.

**45. (D)** Ramanuja's Vishishtadvaita holds the world and souls real, with bhakti and surrender as the path. It does not say the world is unreal, that sacrifice alone frees, or that there are no gods.

**46. (B)** Basavanna founded the Lingayat movement, rejecting caste and ritual and teaching devotion, honest work and equality. It was not a sultanate, a navy, or the Tripartite Struggle.

**47. (C)** A vachana is a short Kannada poem; Basavanna used vachanas to express his spiritual and ethical vision. A gateway is a torana, a tower a vimana, and a ship part of a navy.

**48. (D)** The Anubhava mandapa was Basavanna's hall in Kalyani where people of all backgrounds exchanged ideas. It was not a warship, a fort, or a destroyed temple.

**49. (A)** The Lingayats rejected caste and welcomed all, valuing equality and open dialogue. They did not uphold caste, sultan-rule, or conquest.

**50. (D)** Ramanuja and Basava show vibrant new religious thought during the period. It was not thought-free, only war, or foreigners-only.

**51. (A)** Khajuraho and Brihadishvara show the era's rich temple art. It was not building-free, only war, or Ghazni-ruled.

**52. (C)** Angkor Wat's five towers symbolise Mount Meru, the cosmic mountain. They do not stand for oceans, generals, or Chola kings.

**53. (B)** Angkor Wat shows India's cultural influence reaching far abroad, peacefully. It was not conquest, isolation, or a sign of no temples.

**54. (D)** A vimana is the tall tower over a South Indian temple's main shrine. A ship is part of a navy, a gateway a torana, and a Kannada poem a vachana.

**55. (A)** Great temples at home and influence abroad show India as a thriving, outward-reaching cultural centre. It was not barren, cut off, or wholly foreign-ruled.

**56. (D)** Large parts of the north and all the south stayed free, with southern powers flourishing. There were invaders, but they did not conquer everything, and it was the north that mainly faced them.

**57. (A)** The age also produced al-Biruni and Bhaskaracharya, Ramanuja and Basava, and temples from Khajuraho to Angkor Wat. It was not only ruins, learning-free, or foreigners-only.

**58. (C)** Mahmud raided for plunder and left; Ghuri came to stay and rule. The reversal, the 'both only raided' and the 'Chola navy' options are wrong.

**59. (A)** Mahmud's raids came first (early 11th century), then the First (1191) and Second (1192) Battles of Tarain. The other orders scramble these.

**60. (B)** Mahmud only raided (the Ghurids ruled later), much of India remained free, and the age was rich in scholarship, devotion and temple art - correcting all three errors. The other options keep one or more misconceptions.
