Set 1 · Q1–Q10

Q1

During respiration, the food broken down to release energy in our cells is mainly:

  • Aprotein
  • Bfat
  • Cglucose✓
  • Dvitamins

Working

✓

Respiration breaks down food to release energy, and the chief fuel is glucose (a simple sugar). Proteins and fats can be used in special situations, but the everyday fuel of the cell is glucose.

Q2

Which statement correctly separates breathing from respiration?

  • Abreathing releases energy; respiration exchanges gases
  • Bbreathing is the exchange of gases; respiration releases energy inside cells✓
  • Cbreathing and respiration mean exactly the same thing
  • Drespiration happens only in the lungs, breathing in the cells

Working

✓

Breathing is only the physical exchange of air (taking in oxygen-rich air, giving out carbon-dioxide-rich air). Respiration is the chemical process inside the cells that breaks down glucose to release energy. They are not the same — breathing supplies the oxygen that respiration uses.

Q3

A jar of exhaled air is bubbled through clear limewater. What is observed, and why?

  • Ait stays clear, because exhaled air has no carbon dioxide
  • Bit turns blue, because of extra oxygen
  • Cit turns red, because of water vapour
  • Dit turns milky, because exhaled air is rich in carbon dioxide✓

Working

✓

Carbon dioxide turns limewater milky. Exhaled air is rich in CO₂, so the limewater goes milky. ("Stays clear" wrongly assumes exhaled air has no CO₂.)

Q4

The correct word equation for aerobic respiration is:

  • Aglucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy✓
  • Bglucose → alcohol + carbon dioxide + energy
  • Ccarbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen
  • Dglucose + carbon dioxide → oxygen + water + energy

Working

✓

Aerobic respiration: glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy. Option (C) is photosynthesis written backwards, and (B) is the anaerobic (yeast) equation.

Q5

A yeast cell respiring in dough without oxygen produces:

  • Alactic acid and energy
  • Bcarbon dioxide and water only
  • Coxygen and glucose
  • Dalcohol, carbon dioxide and energy✓

Working

✓

Without oxygen (anaerobic), yeast carries out fermentation: glucose → alcohol + carbon dioxide + energy. Lactic acid is what *our muscle* cells make, not yeast.

Q6

After sprinting hard, an athlete's leg muscles ache and cramp. The substance responsible is:

  • Alactic acid✓
  • Balcohol
  • Ccarbon dioxide
  • Dglucose

Working

✓

When muscles run short of oxygen during a sprint, they respire anaerobically and make lactic acid, which builds up and causes the ache and cramp.

Q7

Anaerobic respiration is best described as the breakdown of glucose:

  • Aonly inside the lungs
  • Busing large amounts of oxygen
  • Cin the absence of oxygen✓
  • Donly in green plants

Working

✓

"An-aerobic" literally means "without air (oxygen)". So anaerobic respiration is the breakdown of glucose in the absence of oxygen.

Q8

During inhalation, the diaphragm:

  • Aflattens and moves down, and the chest cavity gets larger✓
  • Bdomes up, and the chest cavity gets smaller
  • Cstays still while only the ribs move
  • Dflattens and moves down, and the chest cavity gets smaller

Working

✓

On inhalation the diaphragm contracts, flattens and moves down; together with the ribs swinging up and out, this enlarges the chest cavity so air flows in. (Option D pairs the right diaphragm move with the wrong volume change — a trap.)

Q9

The correct path of air entering the human body is:

  • Anostrils → lungs → trachea → nasal cavity
  • Btrachea → nostrils → nasal cavity → lungs
  • Cnostrils → nasal cavity → trachea → lungs✓
  • Dnasal cavity → nostrils → lungs → trachea

Working

✓

Air travels: nostrils → nasal cavity → trachea (windpipe) → lungs. The other orders scramble this path.

Q10

Insects such as cockroaches take in air through small openings called:

  • Aspiracles✓
  • Bgills
  • Cstomata
  • Dlenticels

Working

✓

Insects such as cockroaches take in air through tiny holes called spiracles. (Gills are for fish, stomata and lenticels are for plants.)

Set 2 · Q11–Q20

Q11

A fish kept in a sealed bag of boiled (air-free) water soon dies. The best reason is that:

  • Agills extract dissolved oxygen from water, and boiled water has lost it✓
  • Bfish breathe by swallowing water into their lungs
  • Cfish need carbon dioxide dissolved in the water
  • Dboiled water is too heavy for the gills to pump

Working

✓

Fish do not have lungs; their gills extract oxygen *dissolved* in the water. Boiling drives the dissolved oxygen out, so the fish suffocates even though it is surrounded by water.

Q12

A person breathes 18 times per minute at rest. About how many breaths is that in 5 minutes?

  • A36
  • B23
  • C360
  • D90✓

Working

✓

18 breaths/minute × 5 minutes = 90 breaths. (36 would be 18 × 2; 360 multiplies by 20 by mistake.)

Q13

Which organ takes in oxygen in an earthworm?

  • Agills
  • Bits moist skin✓
  • Clungs
  • Dspiracles

Working

✓

An earthworm has no lungs or gills; it takes in oxygen through its moist skin, which is why it must stay damp.

Q14

The part of the respiratory system commonly called the windpipe is the:

  • Atrachea✓
  • Bdiaphragm
  • Clarynx
  • Dbronchus

Working

✓

The trachea is the windpipe — the tube carrying air from the throat toward the lungs.

Q15

During heavy exercise our breathing rate rises mainly because:

  • Athe lungs shrink and need refilling
  • Bmuscles need more oxygen and more carbon dioxide must be removed✓
  • Cthe diaphragm has stopped working
  • Dthe body is trying to cool the blood

Working

✓

Hard exercise makes muscles respire faster, so they need more oxygen and produce more carbon dioxide. Breathing speeds up to supply the extra O₂ and clear the extra CO₂.

Q16

A frog can take in oxygen through:

  • Agills only
  • Bspiracles only
  • Cstomata in its skin
  • Dboth its moist skin and its lungs✓

Working

✓

A frog is special: it can take in oxygen both through its moist skin and through its lungs.

Q17

In plants, the tiny pores on the surface of leaves used for gas exchange are the:

  • Alenticels
  • Bspiracles
  • Calveoli
  • Dstomata✓

Working

✓

Leaves exchange gases through tiny pores called stomata. (Lenticels are on stems, spiracles on insects, alveoli in human lungs.)

Q18

The breakdown of glucose with the release of energy takes place:

  • Aonly in the lungs
  • Bin the cells of the body✓
  • Conly in the blood
  • Donly in the nostrils

Working

✓

Respiration — the actual breakdown of glucose to release energy — happens inside the body's cells, not in the lungs or blood (those only transport the gases).

Q19

Which of these is the correct comparison of the energy released per glucose molecule?

  • Aanaerobic respiration releases more energy than aerobic respiration
  • Baerobic respiration releases more energy than anaerobic respiration✓
  • Cboth release exactly the same energy
  • Danaerobic respiration releases no energy at all

Working

✓

Aerobic respiration breaks glucose down completely, releasing much more energy per glucose molecule than anaerobic respiration, which only breaks it down partly (into alcohol or lactic acid).

Q20

The muscle that lies below the lungs and helps in breathing is the:

  • Adiaphragm✓
  • Btrachea
  • Cbronchus
  • Dheart

Working

✓

The diaphragm is the dome-shaped sheet of muscle below the lungs that drives breathing movements.

Set 3 · Q21–Q30

Q21

A muscle cramp after running is usually relieved by:

  • Arunning even faster
  • Bholding your breath
  • Crest, massage or a hot bath✓
  • Ddrinking less water

Working

✓

Lactic-acid cramps ease once oxygen returns and the acid is cleared; rest, gentle massage or a hot bath all help, so the body can finish breaking down the lactic acid.

Q22

Compared with breathing in air, a fish must pump water over its gills almost continuously because:

  • Awater has no oxygen at all
  • Bgills cannot move on their own
  • Cfish do not respire
  • Dwater contains far less dissolved oxygen than air does✓

Working

✓

Water holds far less dissolved oxygen than the same volume of air, so a fish must keep passing a steady stream of water over its gills to gather enough oxygen.

Q23

During exhalation, the ribs and the chest cavity respectively:

  • Amove up and out; cavity gets larger
  • Bmove down and in; cavity gets smaller✓
  • Cstay still; cavity gets larger
  • Dmove down and in; cavity gets larger

Working

✓

On exhalation the diaphragm relaxes (domes up) and the ribs move down and in, so the chest cavity gets smaller and air is pushed out.

Q24

Which set lists three organs/structures used for taking in oxygen by three DIFFERENT organisms?

  • Agills (human), lungs (fish), spiracles (earthworm)
  • Bstomata (fish), lungs (earthworm), gills (human)
  • Cspiracles (fish), gills (cockroach), lungs (frog)
  • Dgills (fish), lungs (human), moist skin (earthworm)✓

Working

✓

A correct set must match each organ to the right organism: gills (fish), lungs (human), moist skin (earthworm). The other sets mismatch organs and organisms.

Q25

When you exhale onto a cold mirror, it fogs up. This shows exhaled air contains more:

  • Aoxygen
  • Bwater vapour✓
  • Cnitrogen
  • Dglucose

Working

✓

The mirror fogs because exhaled air carries extra water vapour, which condenses on the cold glass.

Q26

A swimmer's breathing rate is measured as 15 per minute before a race and 35 per minute just after. The rise happens because:

  • Athe lungs have become smaller
  • Bthe heart has stopped beating
  • Cthe body needs to supply more oxygen and remove more carbon dioxide✓
  • Dless energy is needed during the race

Working

✓

Swimming hard makes the muscles respire faster, so the body must take in more oxygen and remove more carbon dioxide — hence the jump from 15 to 35 breaths per minute.

Q27

Yeast is used to make bread rise. The gas that forms the bubbles in the dough is:

  • Aoxygen
  • Bwater vapour
  • Ccarbon dioxide✓
  • Dnitrogen

Working

✓

Yeast fermenting in dough gives off carbon dioxide; the trapped bubbles of CO₂ make the bread rise.

Q28

Roots of a plant obtain oxygen for respiration:

  • Afrom sunlight falling on the leaves
  • Bfrom air present in the spaces between soil particles✓
  • Cfrom carbon dioxide in the air
  • Donly from water in the soil

Working

✓

Soil has air in the spaces between its particles; roots take in oxygen from this trapped air for their own respiration.

Q29

Which statement about breathing rate is correct?

  • Ait is lower at rest and rises during exercise✓
  • Bit is highest while sleeping and lowest while running
  • Cit stays exactly the same whatever you do
  • Dit only changes if you hold your breath

Working

✓

Breathing rate is lowest at rest and rises during exercise (the opposite of option B, which is the trap).

Q30

The thin-walled, balloon-like sacs in the lungs where oxygen passes into the blood are the:

  • Abronchi
  • Balveoli✓
  • Cspiracles
  • Dbronchioles

Working

✓

The tiny balloon-like air sacs in the lungs are the alveoli; their thin walls let oxygen pass into the surrounding blood.

Set 4 · Q31–Q40

Q31

A plant carries out respiration:

  • Aboth day and night✓
  • Bonly during the day
  • Conly at night
  • Dnever, because it makes its own food

Working

✓

Plants respire both day and night. (They photosynthesise only in light, but respiration never stops — the trap is thinking plants only respire at night.)

Q32

Which is the most complete statement about gas exchange in respiration?

  • Awe take in carbon dioxide and give out oxygen
  • Bwe take in and give out only oxygen
  • Cwe take in oxygen and give out carbon dioxide✓
  • Dwe take in nitrogen and give out carbon dioxide

Working

✓

In respiration we take in oxygen and give out carbon dioxide. (Taking in CO₂ and giving out O₂ is photosynthesis in plants — a common mix-up.)

Q33

In the stem of a woody plant, gas exchange takes place through openings called:

  • Astomata
  • Bspiracles
  • Calveoli
  • Dlenticels✓

Working

✓

Woody stems exchange gases through small openings called lenticels. (Stomata are on leaves.)

Q34

The air we breathe out, compared with the air we breathe in, has:

  • Amore oxygen and less carbon dioxide
  • Bmore oxygen and more carbon dioxide
  • Cthe same amounts of both gases
  • Dless oxygen and more carbon dioxide✓

Working

✓

Compared with inhaled air, exhaled air has less oxygen (some was used up) and more carbon dioxide (a product of respiration).

Q35

During the rising of bread dough, yeast is carrying out:

  • Aanaerobic respiration, producing alcohol and carbon dioxide✓
  • Baerobic respiration, producing only water
  • Cphotosynthesis, producing glucose
  • Dbreathing through tiny lungs

Working

✓

In the dough, yeast lacks oxygen and respires anaerobically, producing alcohol and carbon dioxide — the CO₂ makes the dough rise.

Q36

Cockroaches and other insects carry air to their body tissues through a network of tubes called:

  • Abronchi
  • Bgills
  • Calveoli
  • Dtracheae✓

Working

✓

From the spiracles, air travels through a branching network of fine air tubes called tracheae that carry it directly to the insect's tissues.

Q37

Which sequence shows the breathing movements in the correct order during one inhalation?

  • Adiaphragm contracts → ribs move up and out → chest expands → air rushes in✓
  • Bdiaphragm relaxes → ribs move down → chest shrinks → air goes out
  • Cribs move down → diaphragm domes up → chest expands → air rushes in
  • Dair rushes in → diaphragm relaxes → ribs move down → chest shrinks

Working

✓

One inhalation, in order: diaphragm contracts → ribs move up and out → chest expands → air rushes in. The other orders describe exhalation or scramble the steps.

Q38

A burning candle and a respiring animal both:

  • Arelease oxygen and use carbon dioxide
  • Bneed no oxygen at all
  • Cuse oxygen and release energy and carbon dioxide✓
  • Dproduce only water and no gases

Working

✓

A burning candle and a respiring animal both take in oxygen and release energy plus carbon dioxide — respiration is like a slow, controlled "burning" of glucose.

Q39

Which organism does NOT use lungs to take in oxygen?

  • Ahuman
  • Bfrog
  • Cfish✓
  • Ddog

Working

✓

A fish does not have lungs; it uses gills. Humans, frogs and dogs all use lungs (a frog also uses its skin), so the fish is the odd one out.

Q40

After a fizzy fermentation experiment, a candle lowered into the gas above the liquid goes out. The gas is most likely:

  • Aoxygen
  • Bhydrogen
  • Cnitrogen
  • Dcarbon dioxide✓

Working

✓

A flame needs oxygen; if the candle goes out, the gas does not support burning. Fermentation gives off carbon dioxide, which puts the flame out.

Q41

The number of breaths a person takes in one minute is called the:

  • Aheart rate
  • Bpulse
  • Cbreathing rate✓
  • Drespiration product

Working

✓

The number of breaths taken in one minute is the breathing rate. (Heart rate and pulse count heartbeats, not breaths.)

Q42

Mountaineers at very high altitude often feel breathless. The best Class-7 reason is that:

  • Athe air there has more carbon dioxide
  • Btheir lungs stop working in the cold
  • Cthe air there is thinner, so each breath supplies less oxygen✓
  • Dthere is no air at all on mountains

Working

✓

High up, the air is thinner (lower pressure), so each breath carries less oxygen; the body feels short of oxygen and breathing speeds up — that breathless feeling.

Q43

Which pairing of organism and breathing organ is INCORRECT?

  • Acockroach — gills✓
  • Bfish — gills
  • Cearthworm — moist skin
  • Dhuman — lungs

Working

✓

The incorrect pair is "cockroach — gills". A cockroach breathes through spiracles and tracheae, not gills. The other three pairings are all correct.

Q44

During vigorous exercise, when oxygen supply to muscles cannot keep up, the muscles partly switch to:

  • Aphotosynthesis
  • Bbreathing through the skin
  • Caerobic respiration only
  • Danaerobic respiration, forming lactic acid✓

Working

✓

When oxygen can't reach the muscles fast enough during vigorous exercise, they switch partly to anaerobic respiration, forming lactic acid.

Q45

A balloon attached to the mouth of a flask of sugar solution with yeast slowly inflates. The gas inflating it is:

  • Aoxygen made by the yeast
  • Bwater vapour
  • Cnitrogen from the air
  • Dcarbon dioxide from anaerobic respiration✓

Working

✓

Yeast in sugar solution respires anaerobically, releasing carbon dioxide that collects in and inflates the balloon. (Yeast does not *make* oxygen.)

Q46

Which statement is TRUE?

  • Aevery living cell, plant or animal, respires✓
  • Bonly animals respire; plants only photosynthesise
  • Crespiration stops in animals while they sleep
  • Donly the lungs respire in a human

Working

✓

Every living cell — in plants as well as animals, awake or asleep — respires to release the energy it needs to stay alive.

Q47

Two test tubes of limewater are set up: tube X receives air breathed OUT, tube Y receives ordinary room air pushed in by a pump for the same time. Which is correct?

  • AY turns milky much faster than X
  • BX turns milky much faster than Y✓
  • Cneither changes at all
  • Dboth turn milky equally fast

Working

✓

Tube X receives *exhaled* air (CO₂-rich) and tube Y receives ordinary room air. The exhaled-air tube (X) turns milky far faster because exhaled air carries much more carbon dioxide. So "X turns milky much faster than Y" is the correct statement.

Q48

The voice box, located in the throat above the windpipe, is the:

  • Abronchus
  • Bdiaphragm
  • Calveolus
  • Dlarynx✓

Working

✓

The voice box sitting at the top of the windpipe is the larynx.

Q49

A student says, "Fish breathe water." The most accurate correction is:

  • Afish do breathe water directly into their lungs
  • Bfish make their own oxygen from water
  • Cfish take in oxygen dissolved in water using their gills✓
  • Dfish do not need oxygen at all

Working

✓

The accurate correction to "fish breathe water" is that fish take in oxygen *dissolved* in water using their gills — they do not breathe water into lungs, nor make their own oxygen.

Q50

At rest a person breathes about 15–18 times per minute. Which value would you most expect just after climbing several flights of stairs?

  • A5 per minute
  • B16 per minute
  • C30 per minute✓
  • D0 per minute

Working

✓

Resting rate is about 15–18 per minute; after climbing stairs it rises well above that, so ~30 per minute is the expected value. (16 is a resting value; 0 and 5 are impossible after exertion.)

Set 6 · Q51–Q60

Q51

Which best explains why air rushes INTO the lungs during inhalation?

  • Athe lungs pull the air in like a magnet
  • Bthe chest cavity expands, lowering the pressure inside the lungs✓
  • Cthe diaphragm pushes the air in from below
  • Dthe air is heavier than the lungs

Working

✓

When the chest cavity expands, the same amount of air now fills a bigger space, so the pressure inside the lungs drops below outside pressure and air rushes in. (Lungs do not "pull" air like a magnet.)

Q52

Earthworms come to the surface of waterlogged soil after heavy rain mainly because:

  • Athey want to drink the rainwater
  • Bthe water fills the soil spaces, leaving too little air for skin breathing✓
  • Cthe soil becomes too cold for them
  • Dthey breathe better when fully underwater

Working

✓

Earthworms breathe through moist skin using oxygen from soil air. Heavy rain fills the soil spaces with water, leaving too little air, so the worms surface to get oxygen.

Q53

A green plant in a sealed dark box for a night will:

  • Arelease oxygen and use carbon dioxide
  • Bneither take in nor give out any gas
  • Cuse oxygen and release carbon dioxide through respiration✓
  • Dcarry out photosynthesis as usual

Working

✓

In the dark a plant cannot photosynthesise, but it still respires: it uses oxygen and releases carbon dioxide.

Q54

Which of these is a product common to BOTH aerobic respiration and the fermentation of sugar by yeast?

  • Acarbon dioxide✓
  • Blactic acid
  • Coxygen
  • Dalcohol

Working

✓

Aerobic respiration gives carbon dioxide + water; yeast fermentation gives alcohol + carbon dioxide. The product common to both is carbon dioxide.

Q55

The main job of the hair and mucus inside the nasal cavity is to:

  • Aadd oxygen to the air
  • Btrap dust and germs and clean the incoming air✓
  • Ccool the lungs down
  • Dproduce energy

Working

✓

The hairs and sticky mucus in the nasal cavity trap dust and germs, cleaning (and warming and moistening) the air before it reaches the lungs.

Q56

During exhalation, air leaves the lungs because:

  • Athe chest cavity expands and pressure drops
  • Bthe ribs move further up and out
  • Cthe diaphragm relaxes and the chest cavity shrinks, raising the pressure inside✓
  • Dthe lungs make extra oxygen that must escape

Working

✓

On exhalation the diaphragm relaxes and the chest cavity shrinks; squeezing the air into a smaller space raises the pressure inside the lungs above outside pressure, so air flows out.

Q57

A jar holds a small germinating-seed setup that uses up the oxygen; a candle lowered in goes out and limewater turns milky. This shows the seeds were:

  • Aphotosynthesising, giving out oxygen
  • Brespiring, using oxygen and giving out carbon dioxide✓
  • Cnot alive
  • Dgiving out only water vapour

Working

✓

The seeds used up the oxygen (candle goes out) and released carbon dioxide (limewater turns milky) — the signs of living seeds respiring, not photosynthesising.

Q58

Which statement comparing breathing in different animals is correct?

  • Aa cockroach uses gills while a fish uses spiracles
  • Ba fish uses gills while a cockroach uses tracheae✓
  • Can earthworm uses lungs while a human uses skin
  • Da frog uses spiracles while an earthworm uses gills

Working

✓

The correct comparison is "a fish uses gills while a cockroach uses tracheae." The other options swap the breathing organs between animals.

Q59

A bicycle pump and the human breathing system are alike because both:

  • Achange the volume of a space to move air in and out✓
  • Bburn glucose to release energy
  • Cmake oxygen from water
  • Dturn limewater milky

Working

✓

Both a bicycle pump and the breathing system work by changing the volume of a space, which changes the pressure and so moves air in and out. (The pump does not burn glucose; that part is unique to respiration.)

Q60

Why must even a sleeping person keep breathing, although the muscles are barely moving?

  • Bbreathing only happens during exercise
  • Cthe lungs need air to make glucose at night
