## Worked Solutions

**1. (D)** A neighbour can be a land neighbour (shared border) or a maritime neighbour (shared sea). Language and currency are not what define a neighbour.

**2. (A)** A maritime neighbour is linked by a shared sea, needing no land border. A land-border country is a land neighbour, and landlocked or coastless describes the opposite.

**3. (D)** Sri Lanka and the Maldives, with no land border, are among India's closest neighbours via the sea. So not every neighbour shares land, India has maritime neighbours, and the sea does connect countries.

**4. (B)** Pakistan, Nepal, Bhutan and Bangladesh share land borders with India. The other lists include maritime neighbours like Sri Lanka, the Maldives, Indonesia, Thailand, Oman, Iran and Singapore.

**5. (C)** Sri Lanka and the Maldives are maritime neighbours, linked by sea. They are not land or landlocked neighbours, and they are certainly neighbours.

**6. (D)** India sits at the centre of South Asia and the Indian Ocean. It is not at the heart of the Pacific, Atlantic, or Arctic regions.

**7. (D)** The chapter gives India's land boundary as about 15,100 km. The other figures are far too small or too large.

**8. (C)** The chapter gives India's coastline as about 11,100 km. The other figures are wrong.

**9. (C)** A long coastline and central Indian Ocean position link India by sea to many maritime neighbours. India is not cut off, unable to trade by sea, or landlocked.

**10. (C)** Regionalism is neighbours cooperating for peace, stability and progress. It is not war, mutual neglect, or merging into one nation.

**11. (A)** Being neighbours means cooperating for the whole region's good. It is not wall-building, distant-only trade, or cultural separation.

**12. (A)** Shared religion, trade and festivals bind the neighbourhood through deep cultural and historical ties. It is not disconnected, plane-only, or king-united.

**13. (B)** The Himalayas separate India and China. The Indian Ocean, Thar Desert and Palk Strait separate India from other places.

**14. (C)** China is a northern land neighbour across the Himalayas. It is not a Pacific maritime neighbour, an open-border partner like Nepal, or an island chain.

**15. (B)** The Himalayas show geography shaping a border. Borders are not always coastal, mountains did not stop all trade (routes crossed them), and geography clearly affects neighbours.

**16. (A)** India and Pakistan share heritage but have a divided history. Their pasts are not unrelated, their border is not the open Nepal-type, and they are land neighbours.

**17. (C)** India and Pakistan share cultural roots but became separate nations - shared heritage, divided history. They are not unrelated, the same country, or unconnected.

**18. (B)** Afghanistan is an ancient land-linked neighbour and friend. It is not maritime, an archipelago, or historically unconnected.

**19. (D)** The Uttarapatha was the northern trade route from the Ganga plains to Central Asia through Afghanistan. It was not a southern sea route, a Buddhist school, or a peak.

**20. (A)** The Uttarapatha through Afghanistan made it a key overland trade link for India. It was not cut off, sea-only, or an island.

**21. (D)** Bangladesh is a relatively new nation, hence 'newborn neighbour'. It is not the world's oldest, ancient and unchanged, or a non-country.

**22. (C)** A transboundary river crosses the borders of two or more countries (India and Bangladesh share several). It does not stay within one country, flow only underground, or flow uphill.

**23. (C)** Shared transboundary rivers require cooperation on water management. The neighbours cannot simply ignore each other, halt all flow, or deny the rivers.

**24. (D)** India and Nepal share an open border, crossable without a visa or passport. It is not sealed, maritime, or a gateless wall.

**25. (B)** An open border still exists - people simply cross without a visa or passport, while both countries cooperate to keep it safe. Nepal is a separate country, and the border is not sealed.

**26. (B)** Nepal is landlocked, nestled in the Himalayas. It is not an island, a coastal maritime neighbour, or a seaside desert.

**27. (D)** A landlocked country has no coastline, ringed by land (like Nepal and Bhutan). It is not an island group, a long-coast country, or mid-ocean.

**28. (A)** Even landlocked Nepal depends on the ocean for world trade, with goods passing through Indian ports - so India is its gateway to the sea. It does trade, has no coast of its own, and the sea is vital for trade.

**29. (C)** Bhutan is the 'Land of the Thunder Dragon'. The 'Nation of Islets' is the Maldives, the 'golden land' is Suvarnabhumi, and Myanmar is the gateway to Southeast Asia.

**30. (D)** Gross National Happiness measures wellbeing, sustainability, governance and culture, going beyond money. It is not money-only, army size, or island count.

**31. (D)** Bhutan generates hydroelectricity from its flowing mountain water. It does not burn sea water, rely only on tides, or depend solely on imported coal.

**32. (B)** Bhutan, like Nepal, is landlocked, with no coastline. It is not maritime, an island, or coastal.

**33. (A)** Gross National Happiness shows Bhutan valuing wellbeing and the environment, not just wealth. It is not about riches alone, city size, or military strength.

**34. (C)** Myanmar is India's land gateway to Southeast Asia. The nearest maritime neighbour is Sri Lanka; Myanmar is neither landlocked-Himalayan nor an archipelago.

**35. (C)** As a gateway, Myanmar connects India by land toward Southeast Asia. It does not block contact, sit in the Pacific, or lack an Indian border.

**36. (D)** Sri Lanka is India's nearest maritime neighbour, just across a narrow strait. It is not a land neighbour, landlocked, or the farthest.

**37. (B)** A strait is a narrow sea channel between larger waters, often dividing two lands. A mountain range, island chain and desert are different features.

**38. (B)** The Maldives is an archipelago of many small islands. It is not landlocked, a continent, or a mountain kingdom.

**39. (B)** An archipelago is a large group or chain of islands (like the Maldives and Indonesia). A coastless country is landlocked, and the others are a mountain and a strait.

**40. (A)** Sri Lanka and the Maldives are maritime neighbours connected by sea. They share no land border, no Himalayan link, and are not on the overland Uttarapatha.

**41. (A)** Small island neighbours like the Maldives matter for regional security and friendship. They are not worthless, ignorable, or non-countries.

**42. (D)** The three schools are Theravada, Mahayana and Vajrayana. The others name trade routes, cropping seasons, or Preamble words.

**43. (B)** Theravada means 'the teaching of the elders'. 'Great vehicle' is Mahayana, 'diamond/thunderbolt vehicle' is Vajrayana, and 'golden land' is Suvarnabhumi.

**44. (C)** Mahayana means 'the great vehicle'. 'Teaching of the elders' is Theravada, 'diamond/thunderbolt vehicle' is Vajrayana, and an open border is a Nepal-style border.

**45. (A)** Buddhism spreading from India in three schools shows India as a source of shared ideas and religion. It was not cut off, a borrower of Buddhism, or uninterested in neighbours.

**46. (C)** Suvarnabhumi (suvarna 'gold' + bhumi 'land') means 'the golden land'. The thunder dragon is Bhutan, the nation of islets the Maldives, and 'teaching of the elders' is Theravada.

**47. (A)** Such names reflect the peaceful spread of Indian culture and the Ramayana to Thailand. India did not conquer Thailand, Thailand did not conquer India, and the echoes are not coincidence.

**48. (B)** Indian scripts, religions and stories spread mainly through trade, travel and teachers, not conquest, and locals reshaped them. So it was not by armies, the links are real, and they are ancient.

**49. (A)** The Ramakien is Thailand's own reshaped version of the Ramayana, showing local adaptation. It is not an unchanged copy, a trade route, or a Buddhist school.

**50. (A)** Borobudur, a vast Buddhist stupa, is in Indonesia and shows India's peaceful cultural influence. It is not in Sri Lanka, Bhutan, or Afghanistan.

**51. (D)** Malaysia, Singapore and Indonesia are maritime neighbours in the wider Southeast Asian neighbourhood. They are not land, landlocked-Himalayan, or Arctic neighbours.

**52. (A)** Iran and Oman are ancient western neighbours connected by sea and old trade. They are not eastern islands, Himalayan land neighbours, or Arctic.

**53. (A)** Uttarapatha (north) and Dakshinapatha (south) were ancient Indian trade routes. They are not Buddhist schools, cropping seasons, or neighbours.

**54. (B)** The Silk and spice routes carried goods, ideas and culture, linking India with its neighbours. They did not seal India off, carry only soldiers, or belong only to modern times.

**55. (C)** These ancient routes show India's neighbourly ties are centuries old. They are not brand-new, purely military, or conquest-based.

**56. (D)** SAARC was formed in 1985 to promote cooperation among South Asian nations. 1947 is India's independence, 1971 Bangladesh's birth, and 2010 is unrelated.

**57. (B)** SAARC promotes regional cooperation among South Asian nations. It is not for conquest, ending trade, or replacing governments.

**58. (C)** A diaspora (Greek diaspeirein, 'to scatter') is a community settled far from its homeland. A sea channel is a strait, an island chain is an archipelago, and Buddhist schools are Theravada, Mahayana and Vajrayana.

**59. (D)** Bilateral (Latin bi- 'two' + latus 'side') means between two countries. It is not global, single-country, or country-and-sea.

**60. (B)** Sri Lanka is a close maritime neighbour, and Indian culture reached Thailand peacefully via trade, travel and religion, not armies - correcting both errors. The other options repeat the misconceptions.
