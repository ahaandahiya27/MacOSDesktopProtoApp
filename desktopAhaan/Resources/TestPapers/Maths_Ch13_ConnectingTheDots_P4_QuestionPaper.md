**Paper 4 — (progressively harder)**

**Marking scheme:** **+4** for each correct answer · **−1** for each wrong answer · **0** if unattempted.

**Instructions:** Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. Classic traps: averaging two means without weighting by group size, forgetting to re-sort after editing a value, confusing the change in the mean with the new data point itself, treating an unchanged median as proof nothing moved, and trusting a bar's apparent height across a truncated axis or a switched scale. Do not write on this paper — mark answers on your answer sheet.

1. The mean of six numbers is 40. Two of the numbers are 30 and 50. If those two are removed, the mean of the remaining four numbers is:
   (A) 38
   (B) 42
   (C) 55
   (D) 40

2. The mean of 8 numbers is 15. A 9th number is added and the mean becomes 16. A 10th number is then added and the mean returns to 15. The 10th number is:
   (A) 15
   (B) 24
   (C) 6
   (D) 16

3. Every number in a data set of 5 values is first doubled and then 3 is subtracted from each result. If the original mean was 10, the new mean is:
   (A) 20

   (C) 10
   (D) 23

4. Class A (mean 60) and Class B (mean 80) are combined and the overall mean is exactly 68. What is the ratio of the number of students in Class A to Class B?
   (A) 1 : 1
   (B) 3 : 2
   (C) 2 : 3
   (D) 3 : 1

5. Seven numbers in increasing order are 6, 9, 13, x, 22, 27, 31. It is required that the median equals one quarter of the range. The value of x:
   (A) 6.25
   (B) 25
   (C) does not exist, because the median is the 4th value x (which must be between 13 and 22), but range ÷ 4 = 25 ÷ 4 = 6.25 is too small to sit there
   (D) 18

6. A list of five distinct whole numbers has median 12. The two largest are each increased by 10 and the smallest is decreased by 4 (the order is preserved). What is the new median?

7. A data set is 3, 3, 5, 7, 7, 7, 9. The fewest single-entry edits (each changes one number) needed to make the data bimodal with modes 3 and 7 is:
