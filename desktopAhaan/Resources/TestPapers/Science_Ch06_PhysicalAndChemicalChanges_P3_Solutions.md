## Worked Solutions

**1. (C)** Dissolving is physical: the sugar is still chemically sugar and can be fully recovered by evaporating the water, so no new substance was made — that is the decisive test, not taste. A change is chemical only when a substance of new chemical identity appears. Recovery by a physical means (evaporation) is the proof here. Colourlessness is irrelevant — many solutions of new compounds are colourless. Heat absorption does not decide the issue: some physical changes absorb or release heat. 'Too small to see' is wrong; the particles are merely dispersed, not destroyed.

**2. (B)** Burning magnesium combines it with oxygen to make magnesium oxide (MgO), so the ash contains the original magnesium PLUS the added oxygen — it is heavier than the ribbon. This is a chemical change (a new substance with new properties). 'Burning destroys metal as smoke' is the common misconception; here the product is a solid that gained oxygen. 'Same mass' confuses the conservation idea (total mass including the air's oxygen is conserved, but the solid alone gains mass). Light does not remove mass.

**3. (D)** The only dependable signature of a chemical change is the formation of a new substance with new properties, not recoverable by a physical step. Heat exchange happens in many physical changes too (dissolving washing soda warms; melting ice cools). Colour change can be physical (heating a wire glows; some indicators are merely diluted). Irreversibility alone is false — breaking glass is irreversible yet physical. So only the new-substance criterion holds up alone.

**4. (A)** The rule 'irreversible ⇒ chemical' is false, and the cleanest counter-example is a smashed cup: you cannot reassemble it, yet the pieces are still the same material — no new substance, so it is physical. Boiling and freezing are reversible examples that do not test the irreversible-half claim. Cooking an egg is genuinely irreversible AND chemical, so it agrees with the false rule rather than refuting it. Only the broken cup shows irreversible-but-physical.

**5. (C)** CO₂ reacts with lime water to form insoluble calcium carbonate, whose fine white particles make the liquid look milky — a precipitate is a classic chemical-change clue. The new solid has different properties from the starting materials. Scattering by gas bubbles is not the cause; the cloudiness is a solid product. No freezing occurs at room temperature. Dust is irrelevant — the milkiness appears the instant CO₂ reacts, in a sealed clean tube too.

**6. (C)** Displacement depends on reactivity: iron is more reactive than copper, so it pushes copper out of copper sulphate, depositing copper on the nail and turning the liquid green (iron sulphate). A copper coin cannot displace copper from copper sulphate — a metal cannot push itself out of its own salt — so the coin is unchanged. Cleanliness is not the deciding factor. Copper sulphate does not react with every metal; only one more reactive than copper will react. The coin's lack of change is real, not merely invisible.

**7. (A)** Rusting needs iron, oxygen (from air) AND moisture together. Tube A has water but the boiling removed dissolved air and the oil seals air out — no oxygen, so no rust. Tube C has air but no moisture — no rust. Only Tube B supplies both, so only B rusts. 'Iron always rusts' ignores that removing either condition stops it. Dry air alone (C) cannot rust iron because moisture is missing.

**8. (B)** Galvanising works partly as a barrier but crucially because zinc is more reactive than iron: at a scratch the zinc is attacked first and keeps protecting the bare iron (sacrificial action). Paint is only a barrier — once breached, the exposed iron rusts. Zinc does not magically regrow. Hardness is not why it protects; even a deep scratch is still guarded by the surrounding zinc's chemistry. Air entry is similar for both scratches, so that is not the reason.

**9. (D)** Crystallisation separates the dissolved copper sulphate as crystals of the very same substance — no new substance forms, so it is physical, used to obtain pure crystals from a solution. The colour deepens only because solid crystals concentrate the blue; no new compound appears. Heating to dissolve does not make it chemical — that step is also physical (dissolving). It is definitely a change (a state/phase change of the solute), so 'neither' is wrong.

**10. (B)** A bursting balloon is loud and sudden, but the rubber is merely torn — it is still the same rubber, so no new substance forms and the change is physical. Burning magnesium forms a new substance (magnesium oxide) with light and heat — chemical. Milk souring into curd makes new substances — chemical. Coal glowing and turning to ash is combustion forming new substances — chemical. Only the balloon, despite the bang, keeps the same substance throughout.

**11. (A)** Burning a candle is a chemical change that needs a steady supply of oxygen from the air. Under the inverted jar, the trapped air holds only a limited amount of oxygen; once that is used up, the flame goes out — showing burning consumes oxygen. The glass is nowhere near hot enough to melt. Covering the flame does not switch the change from chemical to physical. The flame is extinguished by lack of oxygen, not by being cooled below a 'melting point'.

**12. (D)** Melting wax keeps the same substance (physical); a burning wick makes new substances such as carbon dioxide and water (chemical); souring milk forms new acidic substances (chemical) — so physical, chemical, chemical. The option starting with a burning wick puts chemical first. 'Dissolving, crystallising, baking' is physical, physical, chemical. 'Souring, melting, rusting' is chemical, physical, chemical. Only the first option matches the required order.

**13. (B)** Some dissolving processes release heat yet form no new substance — the solid can be recovered by evaporation, proving the warm change was physical. That defeats 'heat ⇒ chemical'. Burning magnesium and burning wood are chemical (new substances), so they support Riya rather than refute her. Quicklime plus water forms a brand-new substance (slaked lime) with heat — that is chemical, so it cannot serve as the physical counter-example.

**14. (B)** Adding water to quicklime produces slaked lime — a brand-new substance with properties unlike quicklime — together with a strong release of heat, so it is a chemical change. It is more than 'just adding water': the substance's identity changes. It is not crystallisation, which gives crystals of the same substance from a solution. And it cannot be undone by evaporating, because a new compound has formed; the change is not a simple, recoverable dissolving.

**15. (D)** Burning LPG combines the fuel with oxygen to make new substances — carbon dioxide and water vapour — whose properties differ from the original gas, and the change cannot be reversed by simple physical means; that is the hallmark of a chemical change. The liquid-to-gas step at the cylinder is a separate physical change, not the reason burning is chemical. The blue flame is not 'just a colour change'. Cooling the kitchen cannot turn the products back into LPG.

**16. (A)** Salt water in open air supplies oxygen and abundant moisture and the dissolved salt makes the water conduct better, all of which speed up rusting most. Silica-gel removes moisture, so rusting is slowed, not sped up. Dry air lacks the needed moisture, again slowing rust. Grease seals out air and moisture, the slowest case. So salty water in open air is the fastest-rust condition.

**17. (C)** Camphor subliming is a state change of the same substance (physical); a mango ripening develops new substances such as sugars and aroma compounds (chemical) — both correct. Curdling is chemical and melting ghee is physical, so that pair is swapped. Rusting is chemical and cutting cloth is physical, also swapped. Digestion is chemical and freezing is physical, again swapped. Only the camphor/mango pair is right.

**18. (D)** Beating gold changes only its shape; it is still gold, so the change is physical. Hammering does not make gold react with anything — no new substance forms. Any hardness change is a physical property shift, still not a new substance. Crystallisation specifically means crystals coming out of a solution, which has nothing to do with shaping solid metal by hammering.

**19. (C)** A hot solution can dissolve and hold more solid than a cold one. As the saturated solution cools, it can no longer keep all the copper sulphate dissolved, so the excess separates out as solid crystals — this is why cooling triggers crystal growth. Cooling is a physical change, not a chemical one, so it does not 'convert water into crystals' or create a new compound. No fresh copper sulphate is added. The deepening colour is a consequence of crystals forming, not the cause.

**20. (D)** Burning bonds magnesium with oxygen to give magnesium oxide — a new white powder that no longer behaves like the shiny metal, so it is chemical. Cleaning, by contrast, only removed a surface layer (physical). The ribbon does not simply melt back into the same magnesium; it is chemically converted. No mere cutting is involved; the substance's identity itself changes.

**21. (B)** Precipitate formation, gas evolution, and the giving out of heat or light are recognised clues of a chemical change — that is three. Cutting an object into pieces only changes its size and shape (physical), so it is NOT a clue of a chemical change. Hence three, not four. It is more than two and far more than one, because three of the four items are valid chemical-change indicators.

**22. (D)** Burning magnesium is a chemical change: oxygen combines with the metal to make a new substance, magnesium oxide, so cooling cannot give the ribbon back. Freezing water is only a physical change of state — the same water — so warming the ice easily reverses it. That difference (new substance vs same substance) is the real reason, not the ash's temperature. Cooling does not reverse burning, and the white powder is not merely powdered magnesium — its chemical identity has changed.

**23. (A)** Marble chips reacting with dilute acid produce a brand-new gas (carbon dioxide) as a chemical reaction takes place, turning the marble into new substances — true gas evolution from chemistry. A flat fizzy drink and a freshly opened soda bottle merely release CO₂ that was already dissolved in them — no new substance forms, so those are physical. Boiling water gives steam, which is the same water as a gas (physical). Only the marble-and-acid case makes a gas by a chemical change.

**24. (B)** A precipitate is a brand-new insoluble substance created when two solutions react chemically — it did not exist before mixing. Sand stirred into water is an already-existing solid that merely sinks; nothing new is made, so that settling is purely physical. They are therefore not 'both unchanged solids'. Neither forms because of cooling. And a true precipitate, being a new insoluble product, generally cannot simply be stirred back into solution the way one might re-suspend sand.

**25. (D)** Paint is purely a barrier — break it and the bare iron rusts. Galvanising adds a barrier of zinc AND, because zinc is more reactive, the zinc corrodes preferentially and shields the iron even at a scratch. So a scratch does not ruin both equally; galvanising still protects. It is paint that is the simple barrier, not the reverse. Galvanising does not transmute iron into zinc; it merely coats it.

**26. (C)** Both rusting and burning are chemical changes that involve combining with oxygen — burning is rapid (oxidation with heat and light), rusting is slow oxidation. Rusting forms a new oxide, not a mere coating of the same iron. Neither is physical; in both, new substances appear. Burning is not a change of state — petrol vapour reacts to form new gases, so it is chemical, not just evaporation.

**27. (A)** Drying is water leaving the cloth as vapour — same water, so physical. The sour smell comes from new substances produced as the stains spoil — that is chemical. They are not both physical: souring makes new compounds. They are not both chemical: evaporation forms no new substance. The labels are not reversed; drying is the physical one and souring the chemical one.

**28. (A)** The browning of cut apple is a chemical change driven by contact with air. Coating the surface with lemon juice or keeping it under water keeps the air away, so the chemistry is slowed and the slices stay pale. Lemon juice does not freeze the apple. The browning is caused by air, not by light, so blocking light is not the point. It is not mere drying — wetting the apple does not hide a stain but actually slows a real chemical change.

**29. (D)** Melting ice, dissolving salt, and cutting paper each keep the same substance — all physical. The wood-burning set contains burning (chemical), so it fails. The rice set contains cooking (chemical). The rusting set contains rusting (chemical). Only the first set is entirely physical, with no new substance formed in any of the three.

**30. (B)** Dissolving sugar is physical: the sugar keeps its identity and can be recovered by evaporating the water. The fizzing tablet is different — it bubbles because a new gas is produced, a sign that a chemical reaction is taking place, so that change is chemical. They are therefore not both physical. Looking or tasting different does not by itself make dissolving chemical. The labels are not reversed: sugar = physical, fizzing tablet = chemical.

**31. (B)** Filing produces smaller and smaller bits of iron, but each filing is still iron — no new substance forms — so it is a physical change, like cutting or grinding. It is not chemical: the material's identity is unchanged. It is not rusting; brief filing in air does not corrode the metal. It is not crystallisation, which means crystals separating from a solution, not metal being scraped into dust.

**32. (D)** Digestion breaks food down into new substances the body can absorb, so it is a chemical change. Chopping a vegetable only changes its size and shape (physical). Dissolving honey disperses it in water, recoverable in principle (physical). Melting chocolate is a change of state of the same chocolate (physical). Only digestion forms new substances, so it is the chemical change among the four.

**33. (D)** In this displacement reaction the more reactive iron pushes copper out of copper sulphate, so two new substances appear: copper metal, which deposits on the nail as a reddish-brown layer, and iron sulphate, which dissolves to give the pale-green solution. Rust and zinc are not involved. No extra copper sulphate or pure water is produced. The products are not oxides (no oxide forms here) — they are copper metal and iron sulphate.

**34. (C)** Rust is loose and flaky and holds moisture and air against the iron, so painting straight over it traps exactly the conditions rusting needs; the chemical change simply carries on beneath the paint. Paint cannot turn rust back into iron. Paint does not add oxygen to speed rusting. And painting over rust does not stop further change — the rust must first be removed and the bare metal cleaned for paint to protect properly.

**35. (B)** Frying an egg turns the runny, clear white into a firm, opaque solid — a new substance that cannot be turned back into raw egg, so it is a chemical change. Grating cheese only changes its size and shape (physical). Melting butter is a change of state of the same butter (physical). Freezing curry just turns liquid to solid, the same curry (physical). Only the frying egg forms new substances, making it the chemical change.

**36. (C)** Iodine sublimes: it goes straight from solid to vapour on warming and re-forms as solid crystals on a cool surface, but it is iodine throughout — no new substance forms — so the change is physical. The purple colour is just the colour of iodine vapour, not proof of a new substance. Warming does not split the iodine chemically. The crystals that re-form are chemically identical to the original ones; only the state has changed.

**37. (A)** Stretching a rubber band and letting it spring back changes only its shape; the rubber is the same substance before and after, with no new substance formed, so it is a physical change (and a reversible one). The slight warmth on stretching does not make it chemical — physical changes can involve small heat effects. It is not crystallisation, and nothing about stretching makes it irreversible or chemical.

**38. (C)** Softening hot glass and reshaping it into a bottle changes only its shape; the material is still glass, so it is a physical change. Being heated does not by itself make a change chemical — no new substance forms here. Hardening on cooling is not crystallisation in the chemical sense (crystals from a solution). And glass does not 'rust'; rusting is corrosion specific to iron, not a result of being hot.

**39. (A)** Souring milk into curd produces new substances, giving curd a taste and texture quite unlike milk, and the curd cannot be turned back into milk — the marks of a chemical change. It is not a mere change of state from thin to thick liquid; the milk's substances are chemically altered. Cooling does not turn curd back into milk. New substances certainly do form, so 'only the colour changes' is false.

**40. (C)** The sparkler is chemical because new substances form when its contents burn; light and heat are supporting clues, not the defining test — some physical processes (a glowing filament, dissolving washing soda) also give light or heat. So the correct reasoning rests on the new-substance test. Light/heat alone is not always proof. They certainly CAN accompany chemical changes, so 'never' is wrong. Burning a sparkler is chemical, not a change of state.

**41. (B)** Pumping air into a tyre only squeezes the same air into a smaller space, raising its pressure; letting it out gives back the same air. No new substance forms, so it is a physical change. The warmth felt on compression is a physical effect and does not make it chemical. Air does not 'crystallise', and the change has nothing to do with the valve rusting.

**42. (A)** A burning incense stick forms new substances — smoke gases and ash — and cannot be put back together into a whole stick, so it is a chemical change. It is not merely the stick changing shape as it shortens; its very substance is consumed and converted. Ash gathering in a heap is not crystallisation. And the smoke is a new product of burning, not just the solid stick changing state.

**43. (B)** Chrome plating coats the iron with rust-resistant chromium, and stainless steel is an iron alloy containing chromium that resists corrosion — both keep a protective chromium-bearing surface against air and moisture. Neither turns iron to gold. They do not speed iron's reaction with oxygen — the opposite, they slow corrosion. They protect in ordinary air, not only underwater (water would in fact promote rusting of unprotected iron).

**44. (C)** A heater coil glows when hot and returns to dull grey when it cools, but it is the same metal throughout — the glow is just hot metal giving off light, with no new substance formed — so this colour change is a physical effect. The coil does not 'react with electricity' to make a new substance, heat does not always make new substances, and a colour change alone is not crystallisation. The key point is that no new substance forms.

**45. (B)** Subliming camphor (solid to gas), melting ice (solid to liquid), and bending a spoon (shape change) all leave the substance chemically unchanged — they are all physical. No new substance forms in any, so 'all chemical' is wrong. Only camphor 'gives off a gas' and even that is its own vapour, not a chemical gas; ice and the spoon give off no gas. None forms a precipitate. The shared feature is unchanged identity.

**46. (D)** Rust is a genuinely new substance — a reddish-brown oxide of iron — made when iron reacts with oxygen and moisture together; being flaky and weak, it does not protect the metal. It is not dried mud stuck on the surface. It is not 'the same iron, only a different colour' — its chemical identity has changed. And it is not harder or stronger than iron; rust weakens iron, which is why rusting is so damaging.

**47. (D)** Rusting needs moisture, so storing tools in a dry place — for instance in a box with a silica-gel sachet that soaks up moisture — removes one of the two essentials and slows rusting, with no coating needed. A damp cellar, weekly salt water, or a wet cloth would all supply the moisture (and salt) that speed rusting, doing the opposite of protecting the tools.

**48. (D)** Melting wax and letting it set again just moves the wax between solid and liquid — the same wax throughout — so it is a reversible physical change. Burning a candle's wick and wax makes new substances that cannot be turned back into a candle (chemical, irreversible). Cooking rice changes it into a new, softened food (chemical, irreversible). Souring makes new acidic substances in curd (chemical, irreversible). Only the wax melting can be reversed to give back the original material.

**49. (A)** Exhaled breath is rich in carbon dioxide, which reacts with limewater to form an insoluble white solid, turning it milky — the same chemical test, demonstrating a new substance. It does not turn blue; oxygen does not produce that reaction. Breath is not only water vapour — its CO₂ is what reacts. The nitrogen in breath does not react with limewater, so 'clear and fizzing' is wrong; the milkiness is the key result.

**50. (C)** Caramelising heats sugar until it forms new brown, bitter substances — chemical and not reversible. Dissolving merely disperses sugar in water, and evaporating the water gives the sugar back — physical. They are not both physical: caramelising makes a new substance. They are not both chemical: dissolving makes none. The labels are not reversed; caramelising is the chemical one.

**51. (A)** To test moisture alone, both tubes must have air and differ only in moisture: a nail in dry air (drying agent) versus one in moist air does exactly that, isolating moisture as the variable. Boiled-water-under-oil versus dry air changes both air and water, so it is not a clean single-factor test. Salty versus plain water both have moisture, so it tests salt, not moisture. Painted versus greased both block air, testing neither factor cleanly.

**52. (A)** Growing clean sugar-candy (mishri) crystals from a concentrated sugar solution is crystallisation — pure, well-shaped crystals of the same substance separate out of the solution, a physical change. Burning wood to ash forms new substances (chemical). Rusting an iron nail to brown powder forms a new substance (chemical). Cutting a fruit into slices is just a shape change, not crystallisation. Only the sugar-candy case is true crystallisation.

**53. (A)** Cooking an egg and curdling milk are chemical because each forms new substances — that is the valid reason, not the mere fact of irreversibility. Irreversibility does NOT guarantee chemistry (breaking glass is irreversible yet physical), so the student's reasoning is flawed even though the verdict is correct. Both changes are chemical, not physical. Both DO form new substances, so the last option is false.

**54. (B)** Tube A has air but no moisture and does not rust; tube B has moisture but no air and does not rust — so removing EITHER factor stops rusting, proving both air and moisture are needed. 'Only air' is wrong because tube A had air yet stayed shiny. 'Only moisture' is wrong because tube B had water yet stayed shiny. Iron certainly does rust when both factors are present (e.g., open tap water), so the last option is false.

**55. (A)** A chemical change forms new substances with new properties, so getting the original substances back needs more than a simple physical step like cooling or filtering — that is why most chemical changes cannot be reversed physically. A physical change keeps the same substance, so it can often be restored. Heating-vs-cooling is not the dividing line, chemical changes do often show clues, and forming a precipitate is a chemical clue, not a feature of physical changes.

**56. (C)** Dissolving salt is physical — the salt is recoverable by evaporating the water. Burning a candle is chemical — the wax vapour forms new substances such as carbon dioxide and water. 'Both chemical because they disappear' is wrong: disappearing into solution is physical. The labels are not swapped; dissolving is the physical one. They are not both physical, because burning cannot be reversed and makes new substances.

**57. (B)** The decisive test is whether the original substances can be got back by a physical means: if they cannot, a new substance has formed and the change is chemical. Measuring heat alone does not decide it, since physical changes can exchange heat. Brightness of a colour change is not a reliable criterion. Whether a smell is pleasant or not says nothing about whether a new substance formed; only the recovery test settles it.

**58. (C)** Writing with chalk only rubs tiny bits of chalk onto the board; the marks are still chalk — the same substance — so no new substance forms and the change is physical. A new white substance is not created; it is simply chalk deposited on the surface. It is not crystallisation, and the chalk does not react chemically with the board. Only its position and size change, which is the signature of a physical change.

**59. (C)** A new-smelling gas plus a colour change plus failure to recover the starting material together strongly indicate a new substance — chemical. Sawing a block only changes its size and shape (physical). Bending metal changes only shape (physical). Diluting a coloured liquid with water makes it paler but forms no new substance, and the original can be recovered by evaporation (physical). So only the first observation points to chemistry.

**60. (D)** Water cycling between steam and liquid is a pure change of state — the same water all along, reversible, physical — supporting the claim. Wax vapour burning makes new substances (CO₂ and water), a chemical change, not a simple change of state. Wood charring forms new substances (charcoal, smoke gases), chemical. Heating limestone produces a new gas and a new solid, chemical. Only the steam-water case is a reversible state change.
