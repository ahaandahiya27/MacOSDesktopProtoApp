Instructions: Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. Paper 3 is the first hard tier: every item is multi-step. Do brackets first, then × and ÷, then + and −; never strictly left-to-right. A minus sign before a bracket flips every inner term's sign; distributing means multiplying every term inside; only addition and multiplication may be freely reordered or regrouped — subtraction and division may not. Worked Solutions

1. (C) Innermost first: 2 × 3 = 6, so the bracket is 10 − 6 = 4. Then 3 × 4 = 12. Now 48 − 12 + 5; do the additions/subtractions left to right: 48 − 12 = 36, then 36 + 5 = 41. The 131 answer comes from doing 10 − 2 = 8 then ×3 inside (wrong order) and mishandling; 29 comes from 48 − 12 − 5 (treating +5 as −5); 215 comes from multiplying 3 × the whole 48-... chain. Correct value is 41.

2. (B) Take 4 × (30 − 5) ÷ 1: bracket 30 − 5 = 25, then 4 × 25 = 100, then ÷ 1 = 100. The option 4 × 30 − 5 = 120 − 5 = 115 (no bracket so ×4 hits only 30). (4 × 30) − 5 × 1 = 120 − 5 = 115 as well. 4 + 30 − 5 = 29. Only the first equals 100.

3. (D) A term is a part joined by + or −, and × binds tighter than these. So 6 × 4 = 24 is one term, carrying the minus in front: −24. The terms are 50, −24, and +9. Splitting 6 and 4 apart breaks the product that must stay together as one term, and making the last term −9 wrongly attaches a minus to the +9.

4. (A) A minus before a bracket flips the sign of every term inside, so 200 − (60 + 40) = 200 − 60 − 40 = 100; check directly: 200 − 100 = 100. ✓. The option 200 − 60 + 40 = 180 (it kept +40, a sign error). 200 − (60 − 40) flips to 200 − 60 + 40 = 180, not 200 − 60 − 40. 200 + 60 − 40 wrongly flips the leading 200 too. Only the first is true.

5. (D) Bracket first: 4 + 2 = 6. Then × and ÷ left to right: 72 ÷ 6 = 12, then 12 × 3 = 36. Finally 36 − 5 = 31. Doing 72 ÷ (6 × 3) = 72 ÷ 18 = 4 then 4 − 5 = −1-ish gives the wrong 1; treating ÷ as last gives 9; multiplying everything gives 139. Correct is 31.

6. (A) By the distributive idea, 38 × 24 + 38 = 38 × 24 + 38 × 1 = 38 × (24 + 1) = 38 × 25. So both name the same number (950). They are equal — no need to multiply out. Picking either side as 'greater' misses that the extra one 38 exactly rebuilds the 25th group.

7. (B) The ₹50 start is paid once; the ₹12 is paid per km for 9 km, i.e. 12 × 9 = 108, then add the start: 50 + 108 = 158. So the fare is 50 + 12 × 9. (50 + 12) × 9 wrongly charges the start fee per km. 50 × 9 + 12 multiplies the wrong number. 50 + 12 + 9 just adds raw numbers. Correct is 50 + 12 × 9.

8. (B) Multiplication is associative and commutative, so pair the round-number factors: 4 × 25 = 100, then 100 × 17 = 1700. The other groupings give the same value 1700 but are harder (e.g. 4 × 17 = 68 then × 25). The options showing 425 are arithmetic errors (17 × 25 = 425 is correct but then × 4 gives 1700, not 425). Best and correct: (4 × 25) × 17 = 1700.

9. (D) Innermost bracket: 5 − 2 = 3. Then 4 × 3 = 12, so the outer bracket is 3 + 12 = 15. Then 2 × 15 = 30. Finally 100 − 30 = 70. Doing 3 + 4 = 7 then × (5−2)=3 gives 21 → 100 − 42 = 58-type errors; 94 comes from 100 − 2 × 3 only; −202 from multiplying everything left to right. Correct is 70.

10. (C) Write 96 = 100 − 4, so 7 × 96 = 7 × (100 − 4) = 7 × 100 − 7 × 4 = 700 − 28 = 672. Check: 7 × 96 = 672. ✓. The option 7 × 100 − 4 = 696 forgets to multiply the 4 by 7. 7 × 90 + 6 = 636 (the 6 wasn't multiplied). Adding instead of subtracting gives 728. Correct is 7 × 100 − 7 × 4.

11. (A) Removing a bracket after a minus flips inner signs: 30 − (8 + 5) = 30 − 8 − 5 = 17. ✓. 30 − 8 + 5 = 27 ≠ 17. 30 − (8 − 5) = 30 − 3 = 27 ≠ 17. 40 ÷ 4 ÷ 2 = 5 but 40 ÷ (4 ÷ 2) = 40 ÷ 2 = 20 — division isn't associative. Only 30 − 8 − 5 and 30 − (8 + 5) are equal.

12. (D) 5 × (60 − 10) = 5 × 50 = 250, then ÷ 1 leaves 250. The other three are all 5 × 60 − 10 = 300 − 10 = 290 (the bracket is missing so ×5 hits only 60). So the only one equal to 250 is 5 × (60 − 10) ÷ 1.

13. (B) Total pencils = 6 packs × 8 = 48; give away 5, leaving 48 − 5 = 43, modelled by 6 × 8 − 5. The give-away is once, not per pack, so 6 × (8 − 5) = 18 wrongly removes 5 from every pack. 6 + 8 − 5 = 9 just adds raw numbers. Correct is 6 × 8 − 5 = 43.

14. (D) Do × and ÷ first: 5 × 6 = 30, 48 ÷ 6 = 8, 2 × 7 = 14. Now 30 − 8 + 14, left to right: 30 − 8 = 22, then 22 + 14 = 36. Treating +14 as −14 gives 8; left-to-right from the start gives wrong values like 52 or 44. Correct is 36.

15. (A) The chain 24 ÷ 6 × 2 is one term (× and ÷ bind together, left to right): 24 ÷ 6 = 4, then 4 × 2 = 8. It sits behind a minus sign, so as a signed term it is −8. Doing 6 × 2 = 12 first gives 24 ÷ 12 = 2 (wrong order, gives −2); −4 stops at 24 ÷ 6; +8 forgets the leading minus. The term is −8.

16. (A) 52 × 19 + 19 = 52 × 19 + 1 × 19 = (52 + 1) × 19 = 53 × 19 by the distributive idea. Both equal 1007. They are equal; the extra one 19 supplies exactly the 53rd group of 19.

17. (D) Addition is commutative and associative, so reorder to make round hundreds: 27 + 73 = 100 and 46 + 54 = 100, total 200. The other pairings give 73 + 127, 81 + 119 etc., not round. Addition order is definitely not fixed. Best grouping: (27 + 73) + (46 + 54) = 200.

18. (C) 100 ÷ (10 ÷ 2) = 100 ÷ 5 = 20, while (100 ÷ 10) ÷ 2 = 10 ÷ 2 = 5. These differ (20 ≠ 5), so division is NOT associative and the bracket changes the value. The plain 100 ÷ 10 ÷ 2 means (100 ÷ 10) ÷ 2 = 5, again unequal to 20. The true statement is that the two bracketings have different values.

19. (D) Left bracket: 4 × 2 = 8, then 6 + 8 = 14. Right bracket: 2 × 3 = 6, then 10 − 6 = 4. Product: 14 × 4 = 56. Doing 6 + 4 = 10 then ×2 (wrong order) gives 20 → wrong products like 20 or 140. Correct is 56.

20. (C) Factor out the common 8: 8 × 47 + 8 × 53 = 8 × (47 + 53) = 8 × 100 = 800. Just adding 47 + 53 = 100 and forgetting the ×8 gives 100; 376 is 8 × 47 alone; 8000 multiplies by an extra 10. Correct is 800.

21. (B) Each friend spends 40 + 15 = 55, and there are 3 of them: 3 × (40 + 15) = 3 × 55 = 165. Equivalently 3 × 40 + 3 × 15 = 120 + 45 = 165. The option 3 × 40 + 15 = 135 charges the snack only once. 3 + 40 × 15 and (3 + 40) × 15 mismodel entirely. Correct is 3 × (40 + 15).

22. (A) 100 − 18 − 27 = 55. Factoring the subtracted parts: 100 − 18 − 27 = 100 − (18 + 27) = 100 − 45 = 55. ✓. 100 − (18 − 27) = 100 − (−9) = 109. (100 − 18) + 27 = 109. 100 + (18 − 27) = 91. Only 100 − (18 + 27) equals 55.

23. (C) Compare by shifting: from 64 + 39 to 66 + 38, the first number went up by 2 (+2) and the second down by 1 (−1), a net change of +1. So 66 + 38 is greater by 1. Check: 64 + 39 = 103, 66 + 38 = 104. ✓. They are not equal. Greater is 66 + 38.

24. (C) Bracket: 2 × 3 = 6, then 3 + 6 = 9. Now × and ÷ left to right: 90 ÷ 9 = 10, then 10 ÷ 2 = 5. Finally 5 + 4 = 9. Doing 90 ÷ (9 ÷ 2) gives 20 (wrong, division not associative) → wrong answers; ignoring the +4 gives 5. Correct is 9.

25. (B) Inside, 15 − 5 = 10, so 60 − (15 − 5) = 60 − 10 = 50. Removing the bracket flips EVERY inner sign: 60 − (15 − 5) = 60 − 15 + 5 = 50. The student kept the inner −5 as −5 (only flipped the 15), getting 40. So the error is the unflipped 5; correct value 50.

26. (D) × and ÷ bind into single terms: 12 × 3 = 36 (term +36) and 40 ÷ 5 = 8, which sits behind a minus so it's −8; then +7. Terms: 36, −8, 7. Splitting the products into separate numbers is wrong; making +7 into −7 attaches a sign that isn't there; leaving the 8 positive ignores the minus in front. Correct: 36, −8, 7.

27. (B) Pair to round tens: 8 + 12 = 20 and 17 + 23 = 40, plus 35: 20 + 40 + 35 = 95. The other pairings reach the same 95 (addition is associative) but aren't round (e.g. 8 + 17 = 25). Addition certainly can be regrouped. Best round grouping gives 95.

28. (A) Each day he saves 500 − 350 = 150, over 6 days: 6 × (500 − 350) = 6 × 150 = 900. Equivalently 6 × 500 − 6 × 350 = 3000 − 2100 = 900. The option 6 × 500 − 350 = 2650 subtracts the spending only once. Correct is 6 × (500 − 350).

29. (B) By distributivity, 25 × 30 + 25 × 2 = 25 × (30 + 2) = 25 × 32. Both equal 800. They are equal — the right side is just 25 × 32 split into two pieces.

30. (C) Innermost: 3 + 1 = 4. Then 2 × 4 = 8, so the outer bracket is 12 − 8 = 4. Then 4 × 4 = 16. Finally 200 − 16 = 184. Doing 12 − 2 = 10 then × 4 = 40 (wrong order) gives 200 − 160 = 40-type errors; 152 from a different misorder. Correct is 184.

31. (A) Bracket: 9 − 4 = 5. Products/quotients: 7 × 8 = 56, 6 × 5 = 30, 12 ÷ 4 = 3. Now 56 − 30 + 3, left to right: 56 − 30 = 26, then 26 + 3 = 29. Treating +3 as −3 gives 23; ignoring the divide term gives 26. Correct is 29.

32. (A) 'Difference of 20 and 8' is 20 − 8, and that whole difference is multiplied by 5: (20 − 8) × 5 = 12 × 5 = 60. Without the bracket, 20 − 8 × 5 = 20 − 40 = −20 multiplies only the 8. 20 − (8 × 5) is the same wrong reading. 5 × 20 − 8 = 92 doesn't take the difference first. Correct is (20 − 8) × 5.

33. (D) Factor out 7: 7 × 100 − 7 × 2 = 7 × (100 − 2) = 7 × 98 = 686. Adding instead gives 7 × 102 (= 714). 7 × 100 − 2 = 698 multiplies only the 100 by 7. So the equal one is 7 × 98.

34. (B) Inside the bracket do ÷ first: 48 ÷ 6 = 8, then 8 + 2 = 10. Then 10 × 3 = 30. Separately 4 × 2 = 8. Finally 30 − 8 = 22. Doing 48 ÷ (6 + 2) = 6 inside gives a wrong bracket; forgetting the −4×2 gives 30. Correct is 22.

35. (A) The amount subtracted (26) is the same in both, but the starting number is larger in 83 − 26 (83 > 81). Starting bigger and taking away the same amount leaves more. Check: 81 − 26 = 55, 83 − 26 = 57. Greater is 83 − 26.

36. (B) Write 25 = 20 + 5, so 12 × 25 = 12 × (20 + 5) = 12 × 20 + 12 × 5 = 240 + 60 = 300. Check: 12 × 25 = 300. ✓. The option 12 × 20 + 5 forgets to multiply the 5 by 12; multiplying the pieces (12 × 20 × 5) is wrong. Correct split: 12 × 20 + 12 × 5.

37. (D) Both multiply by 7, but 99 > 98, so 99 sevens is one extra 7 more than 98 sevens; 99 × 7 = 98 × 7 + 7, hence larger. Check: 99 × 7 = 693, 98 × 7 = 686. Greater is 99 × 7.

38. (C) × and ÷ form single terms: 3 × 6 = 18 behind a minus → −18; 24 ÷ 8 = 3 with a plus → +3. Terms: 100, −18, 3. Making the last −3 wrongly flips the +; splitting into single numbers breaks the products; leaving 18 positive ignores its minus sign. Correct: 100, −18, 3.

39. (B) A minus before the bracket flips EVERY inner sign: −40 stays −40, −15 becomes +15, +5 becomes −5. So 120 − (40 − 15 + 5) = 120 − 40 + 15 − 5 = 90. Check the bracket value: 40 − 15 + 5 = 30, and 120 − 30 = 90. ✓. The other options flip only some signs. Correct: 120 − 40 + 15 − 5.

40. (B) Bracket: 7 − 3 = 4. Then × and ÷ left to right: 64 ÷ 8 = 8, 8 × 2 = 16; and 5 × 4 = 20. Now 16 + 20 = 36. Doing 64 ÷ (8 × 2) = 4 (wrong, ÷ and × go left to right) gives 4 + 20 = 24. Correct is 36.

41. (B) Multiplication and division share the same level and are done left to right: 24 ÷ 6 = 4, then 4 × 2 = 8. So the teacher's 8 is correct. Reena multiplied 6 × 2 = 12 first (treating × as higher than ÷), giving the wrong 2. There is no 'right to left' rule, and the answer is not a matter of choice.
