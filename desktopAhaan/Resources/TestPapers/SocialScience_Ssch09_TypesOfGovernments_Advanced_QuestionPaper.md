**Marking scheme:** **+4** for each correct answer · **−1** for each wrong answer · **0** if unattempted.

**Instructions:** Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. This paper goes deeper than Paper 1: what a government does through its three functions, what democracy means and its principles, the forms of representative democracy, the main alternatives to democracy, and the long history behind "from the rulers to the ruled." Reason about who holds power and how it is checked. This paper does not repeat any question from Paper 1.

   (B) trades goods
   (C) makes, carries out and enforces the rules of a country
   (D) builds private houses

5. The function of **interpreting** laws and ensuring they are followed is the:
   (A) legislative function

   (A) separation of powers
   (B) theocracy

9. "Accountability" of a government means that it:
   (A) must answer to the people for its actions

10. The principle of dividing power among the legislative, executive and judicial organs so none becomes too strong is called:
   (A) universal franchise
   (B) monarchy

   (D) oligarchy

11. A "monarchy" is a government headed by a:
   (A) committee of priests
   (B) group of generals

12. In an **absolute** monarchy, the monarch:

13. In a **constitutional** monarchy, the monarch's powers are:
   (A) unlimited
   (B) the same as an absolute monarch's

14. A "theocracy" is a government in which power is held by:
   (A) elected representatives
   (B) religious leaders or rule in the name of a deity

   (D) traders

15. A "dictatorship" is a form of government in which:
   (A) power is shared by all citizens

   (C) one ruler (or small group) holds total power, usually unelected

16. An "oligarchy" is rule by:
   (A) a small group of powerful persons

   (C) one king

17. A "republic" is a state in which the head of state is:
   (A) a hereditary monarch
   (B) a foreign ruler
   (C) not a hereditary monarch but usually elected
   (D) the army chief by birth

18. The two main forms of representative democracy discussed are:
   (A) parliamentary and presidential
   (B) absolute and constitutional
   (C) theocracy and oligarchy

19. In a **parliamentary** democracy, the head of government (e.g. the Prime Minister) is:
