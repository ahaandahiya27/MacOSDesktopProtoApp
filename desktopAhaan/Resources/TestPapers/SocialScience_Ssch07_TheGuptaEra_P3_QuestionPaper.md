**Instructions:** Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. Chandragupta II (Gupta) is a different ruler from Chandragupta Maurya centuries earlier; the Iron Pillar resists rust because of its phosphorus-rich iron forming a protective layer, not because iron 'cannot rust'; Faxian mostly described prosperous cities and must be cross-checked; a 'Golden Age' means cultural brilliance, not equality for everyone. 1. A student lists the Gupta rulers in the order Samudragupta → Chandragupta I → Chandragupta II. What single correction makes the chronology right?

   (B) Chandragupta I must come first, before his son Samudragupta, who is then followed by Chandragupta II.
   (C) Samudragupta is correctly first; only Chandragupta I and Chandragupta II need to be swapped.
   (D) No correction is needed because all three names refer to the same king at different ages.

2. Why is it a factual error to credit the conquests recorded in the Prayaga (Allahabad) Prashasti to Chandragupta II rather than Samudragupta?

   (B) The prashasti is a praise-inscription of Samudragupta's military campaigns; Chandragupta II was his successor and is associated with later cultural splendour, not that inscription.
   (C) Chandragupta II composed the prashasti himself, so the conquests it lists are his own.
   (D) The prashasti actually praises Chandragupta Maurya, so neither Gupta ruler should be credited.

3. The 'Chandra' inscription on the Delhi Iron Pillar is generally linked to Chandragupta II. Which reasoning best shows why it cannot belong to Chandragupta Maurya?

   (B) Chandragupta Maurya never used the name element 'Chandra', so no inscription could ever refer to him.
   (C) The Mauryas worked only in gold, so they could not have raised an iron pillar at all.
   (D) Both kings share the title Vikramaditya, so the inscription must mean whichever ruled in Delhi.
