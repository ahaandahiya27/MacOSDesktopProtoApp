Set 1 · Q1–Q10

Q1

Two angles of a triangle are 50° and 60°. The third angle is:

  • A90°
  • B110°
  • C70°✓
  • D60°

Working

✓

Angle sum: 180° − 50° − 60° = 70°.

Q2

The angles of a triangle are in the ratio 1 : 2 : 3. The largest angle is:

  • A90°✓
  • B60°
  • C30°
  • D120°

Working

✓

Parts 1 + 2 + 3 = 6 → one part = 30°; the largest is 3 × 30° = 90°.

Q3

A right triangle has one angle of 35° (besides the right angle). The third angle is:

  • A35°
  • B65°
  • C90°
  • D55°✓

Working

✓

180° − 90° − 35° = 55°.

Q4

Each angle of an equilateral triangle measures:

  • A90°
  • B60°✓
  • C45°
  • D30°

Working

✓

All three equal and summing to 180° gives 60° each.

Q5

Two angles of a triangle are equal, each 70°. The third angle is:

  • A70°
  • B140°
  • C40°✓
  • D50°

Working

✓

180° − 70° − 70° = 40°.

Q6

The angles of a triangle are 3x, 4x and 5x. The largest angle is:

  • A75°✓
  • B60°
  • C45°
  • D90°

Working

✓

3x + 4x + 5x = 180 → 12x = 180 → x = 15; the largest is 5 × 15 = 75°.

Q7

A triangle with angles 90°, 45°, 45° is best described as:

  • Aequilateral
  • Bscalene
  • Cacute isosceles
  • Dright isosceles✓

Working

✓

A right angle plus two equal 45° angles is a right isosceles triangle.

Q8

Can a triangle have two right angles?

  • Ayes, always
  • Bno✓
  • Conly if it is isosceles
  • Donly if equilateral

Working

✓

Two right angles already total 180°, leaving nothing for the third angle — impossible.

Q9

The angles of a triangle are x, x + 20 and x + 40. The largest angle is:

  • A40°
  • B60°
  • C80°✓
  • D100°

Working

✓

x + (x+20) + (x+40) = 180 → 3x + 60 = 180 → x = 40; the largest is x + 40 = 80°.

Q10

Two angles of a triangle are 48° and 67°. The third is:

  • A65°✓
  • B75°
  • C115°
  • D55°

Working

✓

180° − 48° − 67° = 65°.

Set 2 · Q11–Q20

Q11

An exterior angle of a triangle has remote interior angles 50° and 60°. The exterior angle is:

  • A70°
  • B60°
  • C130°
  • D110°✓

Working

✓

Exterior angle = sum of remote interiors = 50° + 60° = 110°.

Q12

An exterior angle is 120°; one remote interior angle is 70°. The other remote interior angle is:

  • A60°
  • B50°✓
  • C190°
  • D70°

Working

✓

120° − 70° = 50°.

Q13

An exterior angle of a triangle is 130°. The interior angle adjacent to it is:

  • A130°
  • B65°
  • C50°✓
  • D60°

Working

✓

The interior angle adjacent to a 130° exterior angle is its linear-pair partner: 180° − 130° = 50°.

Q14

An exterior angle equals 100°, and its two remote interior angles are equal. Each of them is:

  • A50°✓
  • B100°
  • C80°
  • D40°

Working

✓

The two equal remote interiors sum to the exterior angle 100°, so each is 50°.

Q15

An exterior angle of an equilateral triangle is:

  • A60°
  • B90°
  • C60° less than a right angle
  • D120°✓

Working

✓

Each interior angle is 60°, so each exterior angle is 180° − 60° = 120°.

Q16

The sum of the three exterior angles of a triangle (one at each vertex) is:

  • A180°
  • B360°✓
  • C540°
  • D270°

Working

✓

The exterior angles of any triangle total 360°.

Q17

An exterior angle 3x equals the sum of remote interiors x and 80°. The exterior angle is:

  • A40°
  • B80°
  • C120°✓
  • D160°

Working

✓

3x = x + 80 → 2x = 80 → x = 40, so the exterior angle 3x = 120°.

Q18

An exterior angle of a triangle is always greater than:

  • Aeither remote interior angle✓
  • Bthe sum of all three interior angles
  • C180°
  • Da straight angle

Working

✓

By the exterior-angle theorem it is the sum of the two remote interiors, hence larger than either of them.

Q19

An exterior angle of 110° has remote interior angles 2y and 3y. The larger remote interior angle is:

  • A22°
  • B44°
  • C110°
  • D66°✓

Working

✓

2y + 3y = 110 → 5y = 110 → y = 22; the larger remote interior is 3y = 66°.

Q20

If an exterior angle equals its adjacent interior angle, each measures:

  • A45°
  • B90°✓
  • C60°
  • D180°

Working

✓

They form a linear pair and are equal, so each is 90°.

Set 3 · Q21–Q30

Q21

Can sides of length 3, 4 and 8 form a triangle?

  • Ayes
  • Bonly if right-angled
  • Cno, since 3 + 4 < 8✓
  • Donly if isosceles

Working

✓

3 + 4 = 7, which is less than 8, so the triangle inequality fails — no triangle.

Q22

Do sides 5, 6 and 10 form a valid triangle?

  • Ayes✓
  • Bno
  • Conly if equilateral
  • Dcannot tell

Working

✓

5 + 6 = 11 > 10 (and the other pairs clearly exceed the third), so it is valid.

Q23

For a triangle with two sides 7 and 9, the third side must lie between:

  • A7 and 9
  • B0 and 16
  • C1 and 7
  • D2 and 16✓

Working

✓

The third side lies strictly between 9 − 7 = 2 and 9 + 7 = 16.

Q24

Can sides 2, 2 and 5 form a triangle?

  • Ayes
  • Bno, since 2 + 2 < 5✓
  • Cyes, isosceles
  • Donly if right-angled

Working

✓

2 + 2 = 4 < 5, so no triangle is possible.

Q25

With two sides 6 and 8, the smallest whole-number length for the third side is:

  • A1
  • B2
  • C3✓
  • D14

Working

✓

The third side must exceed 8 − 6 = 2, so the smallest whole number is 3.

Q26

With two sides 6 and 8, the largest whole-number length for the third side is:

  • A13✓
  • B14
  • C2
  • D48

Working

✓

The third side must be less than 6 + 8 = 14, so the largest whole number is 13.

Q27

Which set of lengths CANNOT form a triangle?

  • A3, 4, 5
  • B2, 3, 4
  • C5, 5, 5
  • D1, 2, 3✓

Working

✓

1 + 2 = 3 is not greater than 3, so 1, 2, 3 cannot form a triangle.

Q28

Can a triangle with all three sides 5 exist?

  • Ano
  • Byes, it is equilateral✓
  • Conly if right-angled
  • Donly if scalene

Working

✓

5 + 5 > 5, so an equilateral triangle of side 5 exists.

Q29

With two sides 10 and 4, the third side must be greater than:

  • A14
  • B4
  • C6✓
  • D10

Working

✓

The third side must be greater than 10 − 4 = 6.

Q30

Do sides 8, 15 and 17 form a triangle?

  • Ayes (in fact right-angled)✓
  • Bno
  • Conly if isosceles
  • Donly if drawn small

Working

✓

8 + 15 = 23 > 17, so it is a valid triangle — and since 8² + 15² = 64 + 225 = 289 = 17², it is right-angled.

All sides different means the triangle is scalene.

Q32

A triangle whose three angles are all less than 90° is:

  • Aright-angled
  • Bacute✓
  • Cobtuse
  • Dreflex

Working

✓

All angles below 90° defines an acute triangle.

Q33

A triangle with one angle greater than 90° is:

  • Aacute
  • Bright-angled
  • Cobtuse✓
  • Dequilateral

Working

✓

One angle above 90° makes the triangle obtuse.

Q34

An isosceles triangle has, at the least:

  • Atwo equal sides✓
  • Bthree equal sides
  • Ca right angle
  • Dno equal sides

Working

✓

An isosceles triangle has at least two equal sides.

Q35

In any triangle, the angles opposite to two equal sides are:

  • Asupplementary
  • Bcomplementary
  • Cright angles
  • Dequal✓

Working

✓

The base-angles theorem: angles opposite equal sides are equal.

Q36

An equilateral triangle is also:

  • Aright-angled
  • Bequiangular✓
  • Cobtuse
  • Dscalene

Working

✓

Equal sides force equal angles, so an equilateral triangle is also equiangular.

Q37

The longest side of a right triangle is the:

  • Abase
  • Baltitude
  • Chypotenuse✓
  • Dmedian

Working

✓

The side opposite the right angle, the hypotenuse, is the longest.

Q38

Can a triangle be both right-angled and obtuse?

  • Ano✓
  • Byes
  • Conly if isosceles
  • Donly if equilateral

Working

✓

A right angle already uses 90°; an obtuse angle would push the sum past 180° — impossible together.

Q39

The number of acute angles in a right triangle is:

  • A1
  • B3
  • C0
  • D2✓

Working

✓

The right angle uses 90°, leaving two angles that must each be acute — two acute angles.

Q40

A triangle can never have two:

  • Aequal sides
  • Bobtuse angles✓
  • Cacute angles
  • Dequal angles

Working

✓

Two obtuse angles would exceed 180° on their own, so a triangle can't have two.

Q41

An isosceles triangle has a vertex angle of 40°. Each base angle is:

  • A40°
  • B100°
  • C70°✓
  • D140°

Working

✓

Base angles share the remaining 180° − 40° = 140°, so each is 70°.

Q42

An isosceles triangle has a base angle of 50°. Its vertex angle is:

  • A80°✓
  • B50°
  • C100°
  • D130°

Working

✓

Vertex angle = 180° − 50° − 50° = 80°.

Q43

The two equal angles of an isosceles right triangle each measure:

  • A90°
  • B30°
  • C60°
  • D45°✓

Working

✓

A right angle leaves 90° split equally between two base angles: 45° each.

Q44

If the vertex angle of an isosceles triangle equals each base angle, the triangle is:

  • Aright-angled
  • Bequilateral✓
  • Cobtuse
  • Dscalene

Working

✓

If the vertex angle equals each base angle, all three are equal — the triangle is equilateral.

Q45

A triangle has all three angles equal to 2x. Then x is:

  • A60°
  • B90°
  • C30°✓
  • D45°

Working

✓

2x + 2x + 2x = 180 → 6x = 180 → x = 30°.

Q46

The exterior angle at the base of an isosceles triangle whose base angle is 65° is:

  • A115°✓
  • B65°
  • C130°
  • D50°

Working

✓

The exterior angle is the linear-pair partner of the 65° base angle: 180° − 65° = 115°.

Q47

The angles of a triangle are x, x + 10 and x + 20. The middle angle is:

  • A50°
  • B70°
  • C80°
  • D60°✓

Working

✓

x + (x+10) + (x+20) = 180 → 3x + 30 = 180 → x = 50; the middle angle is x + 10 = 60°.

Q48

The angles of a triangle are in arithmetic progression with common difference 20°. The middle angle is:

  • A40°
  • B60°✓
  • C80°
  • D50°

Working

✓

In an AP the middle term is the mean; the three angles average 180° ÷ 3 = 60°, so the middle is 60°.

Q49

The vertex angle of an isosceles triangle is 100°. Each base angle is:

  • A80°
  • B50°
  • C40°✓
  • D100°

Working

✓

Base angles share 180° − 100° = 80°, so each is 40°.

Q50

In triangle ABC, ∠A = 90° and ∠B = ∠C. Each of ∠B and ∠C is:

  • A45°✓
  • B60°
  • C30°
  • D90°

Working

✓

90° leaves 90° split equally: ∠B = ∠C = 45°.
