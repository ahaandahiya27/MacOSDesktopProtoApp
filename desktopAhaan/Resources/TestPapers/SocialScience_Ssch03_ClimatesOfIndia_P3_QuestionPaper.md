1. A newspaper headline reads, "Heavy showers lash Pune this afternoon." A textbook states, "Pune enjoys a moderate, fairly dry climate." Which word in these two lines is being used correctly, and why?
   (A) Only the headline is correct, because climate is just today's sky and a textbook cannot know it.
   (B) Only the textbook is correct, because a single afternoon of rain is itself the climate of Pune.
   (C) Both are correct: the headline reports one afternoon's condition (weather), the textbook reports the long-term average (climate).
   (D) Both are wrong, because rain can only be described as climate and dryness only as weather.

2. Two friends argue. Riya says her city's climate changed because it rained today after a dry week. Decide who is reasoning correctly.
   (A) Riya is right, because every day of rain shifts the climate by a measurable amount.
   (B) Riya is right, because climate and weather mean exactly the same thing.
   (C) Riya is wrong: a single day's rain is weather, and climate is judged only over many years, so one rainy day cannot change a climate.
   (D) Riya is wrong only because it did not rain enough; heavier rain would have changed the climate.

3. Singapore (near the Equator) and Delhi (well to the north) are compared. Singapore is warm and even all year while Delhi has scorching summers and chilly winters. The single factor most responsible for Delhi's wider swing is:
   (A) its lower altitude compared with Singapore
   (B) Singapore being far closer to the Sun than Delhi
   (C) Delhi having no atmosphere to trap heat
   (D) its higher latitude, which gives a stronger seasonal change in how directly the Sun's rays fall

   (C) the Earth's core heats the Equator from below
