Set 1 · Q1–Q10

Q1

A 'neighbour' country, in this chapter, can be one that shares with India:

  • Aonly a land border
  • Bonly the same language
  • Conly the same currency
  • Deither a land border or a sea (a land or a maritime neighbour)✓

Working

✓

A neighbour can be a land neighbour (shared border) or a maritime neighbour (shared sea). Language and currency are not what define a neighbour.

Q2

A 'maritime neighbour' is a country:

  • Aconnected to another by a shared sea or ocean, even without a land border✓
  • Bthat shares only a land border
  • Cthat is completely landlocked
  • Dthat has no coastline at all

Working

✓

A maritime neighbour is linked by a shared sea, needing no land border. A land-border country is a land neighbour, and landlocked or coastless describes the opposite.

Q3

The claim 'a neighbour must share a land border with us' is wrong because:

  • Aevery neighbour does share a land border
  • BIndia has no maritime neighbours
  • Cthe sea cannot connect countries
  • DSri Lanka and the Maldives are close neighbours linked by sea, not land✓

Working

✓

Sri Lanka and the Maldives, with no land border, are among India's closest neighbours via the sea. So not every neighbour shares land, India has maritime neighbours, and the sea does connect countries.

Q4

Which list contains only LAND neighbours of India?

  • ASri Lanka, Maldives, Indonesia and Thailand
  • BPakistan, Nepal, Bhutan and Bangladesh✓
  • CSri Lanka, Nepal, Oman and Iran
  • DMaldives, Bhutan, Singapore and Myanmar

Working

✓

Pakistan, Nepal, Bhutan and Bangladesh share land borders with India. The other lists include maritime neighbours like Sri Lanka, the Maldives, Indonesia, Thailand, Oman, Iran and Singapore.

Q5

Sri Lanka and the Maldives are best described as India's:

  • Aland neighbours
  • Blandlocked neighbours
  • Cmaritime neighbours✓
  • Dnon-neighbours

Working

✓

Sri Lanka and the Maldives are maritime neighbours, linked by sea. They are not land or landlocked neighbours, and they are certainly neighbours.

Q6

India lies at the heart of:

  • Athe Pacific Ocean and East Asia
  • Bthe Atlantic Ocean and Europe
  • Cthe Arctic and North America
  • DSouth Asia and the Indian Ocean✓

Working

✓

India sits at the centre of South Asia and the Indian Ocean. It is not at the heart of the Pacific, Atlantic, or Arctic regions.

Q8

According to the chapter, India's coastline is about:

  • A111 km long
  • B1,110 km long

  • D91,000 km long

Q9

India's long coastline and central position in the Indian Ocean make it:

  • Acut off from all other countries
  • Bunable to trade by sea
  • Cwell placed to connect with many maritime neighbours by sea✓
  • Da landlocked country

Working

✓

A long coastline and central Indian Ocean position link India by sea to many maritime neighbours. India is not cut off, unable to trade by sea, or landlocked.

Q10

'Regionalism' means neighbouring countries:

  • Afighting one another for territory
  • Bignoring each other entirely
  • Cworking together for peace, stability and shared progress✓
  • Dmerging into one single country

Working

✓

Regionalism is neighbours cooperating for peace, stability and progress. It is not war, mutual neglect, or merging into one nation.

Set 2 · Q11–Q20

Q11

The chapter says being neighbours is not just about borders but about:

  • Aworking together for the good of the whole region✓
  • Bbuilding the highest walls possible
  • Ctrading only with distant continents
  • Dkeeping every culture strictly separate

Working

✓

Being neighbours means cooperating for the whole region's good. It is not wall-building, distant-only trade, or cultural separation.

Q12

Centuries of shared Buddhism, Hinduism, trade and festivals show that India's neighbourhood is:

  • Aknit together by deep cultural and historical ties✓
  • Bcompletely disconnected
  • Clinked only by modern aeroplanes
  • Dunited only by a common king

Working

✓

Shared religion, trade and festivals bind the neighbourhood through deep cultural and historical ties. It is not disconnected, plane-only, or king-united.

Q13

India and China are separated mainly by the:

  • AIndian Ocean
  • BHimalayas✓
  • CThar Desert
  • DPalk Strait

Working

✓

The Himalayas separate India and China. The Indian Ocean, Thar Desert and Palk Strait separate India from other places.

Q14

As a land neighbour to the north, China shares with India a:

  • Amaritime border across the Pacific
  • Bvisa-free open border like Nepal's
  • Clong Himalayan land border✓
  • Dshared island chain

Working

✓

China is a northern land neighbour across the Himalayas. It is not a Pacific maritime neighbour, an open-border partner like Nepal, or an island chain.

Q15

The Himalayas acting as a natural boundary between India and China is an example of how:

  • Aborders are always drawn by the sea
  • Bgeography can shape the border between two countries✓
  • Cmountains make trade impossible forever
  • Dgeography has no effect on neighbours

Working

✓

The Himalayas show geography shaping a border. Borders are not always coastal, mountains did not stop all trade (routes crossed them), and geography clearly affects neighbours.

Q16

India and Pakistan are described as neighbours with a:

  • Ashared heritage but a divided history✓
  • Bcompletely unrelated past
  • Cshared visa-free open border like Nepal's
  • Dpurely maritime link only

Working

✓

India and Pakistan share heritage but have a divided history. Their pasts are not unrelated, their border is not the open Nepal-type, and they are land neighbours.

Q17

The phrase 'a shared heritage, a divided history' for India and Pakistan means they:

  • Ahave nothing in common at all
  • Bare actually the same country today
  • Cshare deep cultural roots yet were separated into different nations✓
  • Dnever had any connection

Working

✓

India and Pakistan share cultural roots but became separate nations - shared heritage, divided history. They are not unrelated, the same country, or unconnected.

Q18

Afghanistan is described as India's:

  • Anearest maritime neighbour
  • Bancient land-linked friend✓
  • Can island archipelago
  • Da country with no historical ties to India

Working

✓

Afghanistan is an ancient land-linked neighbour and friend. It is not maritime, an archipelago, or historically unconnected.

Q19

The 'Uttarapatha' was:

  • Aa southern sea route to Sri Lanka
  • Ba school of Buddhism
  • Ca Himalayan mountain peak
  • Dthe ancient northern trade route linking the Ganga plains to Central Asia via Afghanistan✓

Working

✓

The Uttarapatha was the northern trade route from the Ganga plains to Central Asia through Afghanistan. It was not a southern sea route, a Buddhist school, or a peak.

Q20

That the Uttarapatha ran through Afghanistan shows that, long ago, Afghanistan was:

  • Aan important link on India's overland trade routes✓
  • Bcompletely cut off from India
  • Conly ever reachable by sea
  • Dan island in the Indian Ocean

Working

✓

The Uttarapatha through Afghanistan made it a key overland trade link for India. It was not cut off, sea-only, or an island.

Set 3 · Q21–Q30

Q21

Bangladesh is called a 'newborn neighbour' because it:

  • Ais the oldest country in the world
  • Bhas existed since ancient times unchanged
  • Cis not actually a country
  • Dbecame a separate nation relatively recently✓

Working

✓

Bangladesh is a relatively new nation, hence 'newborn neighbour'. It is not the world's oldest, ancient and unchanged, or a non-country.

Q22

A 'transboundary river' is one that:

  • Anever crosses any border
  • Bflows only underground
  • Cflows across the border of two or more countries✓
  • Dflows uphill from the sea

Working

✓

A transboundary river crosses the borders of two or more countries (India and Bangladesh share several). It does not stay within one country, flow only underground, or flow uphill.

Q23

Rivers shared by India and Bangladesh are a reason the two countries must:

  • Anever speak to each other
  • Bbuild dams to stop all flow
  • Ccooperate on managing their shared waters✓
  • Dpretend the rivers do not exist

Working

✓

Shared transboundary rivers require cooperation on water management. The neighbours cannot simply ignore each other, halt all flow, or deny the rivers.

Q24

The India-Nepal border is famous for being:

  • Acompletely sealed and impossible to cross
  • Ba maritime border across the ocean
  • Ca high wall with no gates
  • Dan 'open border' that people can cross without a visa or passport✓

Working

✓

India and Nepal share an open border, crossable without a visa or passport. It is not sealed, maritime, or a gateless wall.

Q25

The claim 'the open border between India and Nepal means there is no border at all' is wrong because:

  • Athere really is no border
  • Bthe border exists; it is just open, and both countries still cooperate to keep it safe✓
  • CNepal is part of India
  • Dthe border is a high sealed wall

Working

✓

An open border still exists - people simply cross without a visa or passport, while both countries cooperate to keep it safe. Nepal is a separate country, and the border is not sealed.

Q26

Nepal lies 'in the lap of the Himalayas' and is:

  • Aan island nation in the Indian Ocean
  • Ba landlocked country with no coastline✓
  • Ca maritime neighbour with a long coast
  • Da desert country by the sea

Working

✓

Nepal is landlocked, nestled in the Himalayas. It is not an island, a coastal maritime neighbour, or a seaside desert.

Q27

A 'landlocked' country is one that:

  • Ais made up entirely of islands
  • Bhas the world's longest coastline
  • Clies in the middle of an ocean
  • Dhas no coastline and is surrounded entirely by land✓

Working

✓

A landlocked country has no coastline, ringed by land (like Nepal and Bhutan). It is not an island group, a long-coast country, or mid-ocean.

Q28

The belief 'a landlocked country like Nepal has no need for the sea' is wrong because:

  • Aits goods usually travel out through Indian ports, making India its gateway to the sea✓
  • Blandlocked countries never trade with anyone
  • CNepal has its own ocean coast
  • Dthe sea is useless for trade

Working

✓

Even landlocked Nepal depends on the ocean for world trade, with goods passing through Indian ports - so India is its gateway to the sea. It does trade, has no coast of its own, and the sea is vital for trade.

Q29

Bhutan is known as the:

  • A'Nation of Islets'
  • B'Golden Land of the South'
  • C'Land of the Thunder Dragon'✓
  • D'Gateway to Southeast Asia'

Working

✓

Bhutan is the 'Land of the Thunder Dragon'. The 'Nation of Islets' is the Maldives, the 'golden land' is Suvarnabhumi, and Myanmar is the gateway to Southeast Asia.

Q30

'Gross National Happiness' is Bhutan's way of measuring progress by:

  • Acounting only the money the country earns
  • Bmeasuring the size of its army
  • Ccounting its number of islands
  • Dwellbeing, sustainability, governance and culture, not money alone✓

Working

✓

Gross National Happiness measures wellbeing, sustainability, governance and culture, going beyond money. It is not money-only, army size, or island count.

Set 4 · Q31–Q40

Q31

Bhutan, a mountainous neighbour, produces much electricity from:

  • Aburning sea water
  • Bocean tides only
  • Cimported coal alone
  • Dhydroelectricity (the force of flowing water)✓

Working

✓

Bhutan generates hydroelectricity from its flowing mountain water. It does not burn sea water, rely only on tides, or depend solely on imported coal.

Q32

Like Nepal, Bhutan is a ____ neighbour of India.

  • Amaritime
  • Blandlocked✓
  • Cisland
  • Dcoastal

Working

✓

Bhutan, like Nepal, is landlocked, with no coastline. It is not maritime, an island, or coastal.

Q33

Bhutan's choice to measure 'Gross National Happiness' suggests it values:

  • Aoverall wellbeing and the environment alongside economic growth✓
  • Bonly how rich it can become
  • Conly the size of its cities
  • Donly its military strength

Working

✓

Gross National Happiness shows Bhutan valuing wellbeing and the environment, not just wealth. It is not about riches alone, city size, or military strength.

Q34

Myanmar is described as India's:

  • Anearest maritime neighbour
  • Blandlocked Himalayan neighbour
  • Cgateway to Southeast Asia✓
  • Dan island archipelago

Working

✓

Myanmar is India's land gateway to Southeast Asia. The nearest maritime neighbour is Sri Lanka; Myanmar is neither landlocked-Himalayan nor an archipelago.

Q35

Calling Myanmar a 'gateway to Southeast Asia' means it:

  • Ablocks all contact between India and Southeast Asia
  • Bis an island far out in the Pacific
  • Clinks India by land toward the countries of Southeast Asia✓
  • Dhas no border with India

Working

✓

As a gateway, Myanmar connects India by land toward Southeast Asia. It does not block contact, sit in the Pacific, or lack an Indian border.

Q36

Sri Lanka is India's:

  • Alargest land neighbour
  • Bonly landlocked neighbour
  • Cfarthest neighbour
  • Dnearest maritime neighbour✓

Working

✓

Sri Lanka is India's nearest maritime neighbour, just across a narrow strait. It is not a land neighbour, landlocked, or the farthest.

Q37

A 'strait', such as the one between India and Sri Lanka, is:

  • Aa tall mountain range
  • Ba narrow channel of sea connecting two larger bodies of water and often separating two lands✓
  • Ca chain of islands
  • Da dry desert

Working

✓

A strait is a narrow sea channel between larger waters, often dividing two lands. A mountain range, island chain and desert are different features.

Q38

The Maldives is described as a 'nation of islets', which makes it:

  • Aa landlocked country
  • Ban archipelago - a chain or group of many islands✓
  • Ca single large continent
  • Da high mountain kingdom

Working

✓

The Maldives is an archipelago of many small islands. It is not landlocked, a continent, or a mountain kingdom.

Q39

An 'archipelago' is:

  • Aa country with no coastline
  • Ban extensive group or chain of islands✓
  • Ca single isolated mountain
  • Da narrow channel of sea

Working

✓

An archipelago is a large group or chain of islands (like the Maldives and Indonesia). A coastless country is landlocked, and the others are a mountain and a strait.

Q40

Both Sri Lanka and the Maldives are connected to India mainly by:

  • Athe sea✓
  • Ba shared land border
  • Cthe Himalayas
  • Dthe Uttarapatha overland route

Working

✓

Sri Lanka and the Maldives are maritime neighbours connected by sea. They share no land border, no Himalayan link, and are not on the overland Uttarapatha.

India and the Maldives cooperating closely shows that even small island neighbours:

  • Bhave nothing to offer their neighbours

Small island neighbours like the Maldives matter for regional security and friendship. They are not worthless, ignorable, or non-countries.

The three great schools of Buddhism named in the chapter are:

  • DTheravada, Mahayana and Vajrayana✓

Q43

'Theravada' Buddhism means:

  • A'the great vehicle'
  • B'the teaching of the elders'✓
  • C'the diamond vehicle'
  • D'the golden land'

Working

✓

Theravada means 'the teaching of the elders'. 'Great vehicle' is Mahayana, 'diamond/thunderbolt vehicle' is Vajrayana, and 'golden land' is Suvarnabhumi.

Q44

'Mahayana' Buddhism means:

  • A'the teaching of the elders'
  • B'the diamond vehicle'
  • C'the great vehicle'✓
  • D'the open border'

Working

✓

Mahayana means 'the great vehicle'. 'Teaching of the elders' is Theravada, 'diamond/thunderbolt vehicle' is Vajrayana, and an open border is a Nepal-style border.

Q45

That Buddhism spread from India to many neighbours in three schools shows that India was:

  • Aa source of ideas and religion shared across the region✓
  • Bcut off from all its neighbours
  • Ca country that borrowed Buddhism from abroad
  • Duninterested in its neighbours

Working

✓

Buddhism spreading from India in three schools shows India as a source of shared ideas and religion. It was not cut off, a borrower of Buddhism, or uninterested in neighbours.

Q46

'Suvarnabhumi', an ancient Indian name for parts of Southeast Asia, means:

  • A'the thunder dragon'
  • B'the nation of islets'
  • C'the golden land'✓
  • D'the teaching of the elders'

Working

✓

Suvarnabhumi (suvarna 'gold' + bhumi 'land') means 'the golden land'. The thunder dragon is Bhutan, the nation of islets the Maldives, and 'teaching of the elders' is Theravada.

Q47

Thai kings are named 'Rama' and the city 'Ayutthaya' echoes 'Ayodhya' because:

  • AIndian culture and the Ramayana spread peacefully to Thailand✓
  • BIndia once conquered and ruled Thailand
  • CThailand conquered India
  • Dthe names are pure coincidence

Working

✓

Such names reflect the peaceful spread of Indian culture and the Ramayana to Thailand. India did not conquer Thailand, Thailand did not conquer India, and the echoes are not coincidence.

Q48

The claim 'India became so influential in Thailand and Indonesia because it conquered them' is wrong because:

  • AIndia did conquer them with large armies
  • BIndian culture spread mostly through trade, travel and teachers, not armies✓
  • Cthe cultures have no Indian links
  • Dthe spread happened only in modern times

Working

✓

Indian scripts, religions and stories spread mainly through trade, travel and teachers, not conquest, and locals reshaped them. So it was not by armies, the links are real, and they are ancient.

Q49

  • Ban exact copy of the Indian Ramayana with no changes
  • Ca Thai trade route

✓

The Ramakien is Thailand's own reshaped version of the Ramayana, showing local adaptation. It is not an unchanged copy, a trade route, or a Buddhist school.

Q50

The great Borobudur stupa, evidence of Indian cultural influence, is located in:

  • AIndonesia✓
  • BSri Lanka
  • CBhutan
  • DAfghanistan

Working

✓

Borobudur, a vast Buddhist stupa, is in Indonesia and shows India's peaceful cultural influence. It is not in Sri Lanka, Bhutan, or Afghanistan.

Q58

  • Da school of Buddhism

A diaspora (Greek diaspeirein, 'to scatter') is a community settled far from its homeland. A sea channel is a strait, an island chain is an archipelago, and Buddhist schools are Theravada, Mahayana and Vajrayana.

Q59
