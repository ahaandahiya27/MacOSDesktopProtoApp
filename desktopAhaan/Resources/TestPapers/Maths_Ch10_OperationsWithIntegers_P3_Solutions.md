## Worked Solutions

**1. (D)** Start 0, +7 → 7, −3 → 4. Doubling the position: 4 × 2 = 8. Then −10 → 8 − 10 = −2. Forgetting the −10 leaves 8; doubling before the −3 would give a wrong larger value; treating the final step as +10 gives 18.

**2. (C)** Group positives 8 + 11 = 19 and negatives (−15) + (−6) + (−4) = −25. Total 19 − 25 = −6. Adding all magnitudes as if negative gives −44; the opposite sign mistake gives +6.

**3. (B)** a − b and b − a differ in sign in general, so subtraction is not commutative. Subtracting a negative increases the result, so it does not always shrink. (a − b) − c ≠ a − (b − c) in general, so it is not associative. Integers ARE closed under subtraction, so that option is false.

**4. (A)** Left to right: (−54) ÷ 9 = −6, then (−6) ÷ (−2) = +3 (negative ÷ negative is positive). Stopping the sign at −3 ignores the second negative; multiplying instead of dividing the 2 gives ±27.

**5. (B)** Multiply first: 3 × (−5) = −15. Then (−4) + (−15) − (−6) = −4 − 15 + 6 = −13. Adding −4 and 3 first (wrong order) gives (−1) × (−5) … = different value; treating −(−6) as −6 gives −25.

**6. (A)** An integer plus its opposite is 0 (additive inverse). (−13) + 13 = 0 by that property. (−13) × 0 is 0 but by the zero-product rule, not additive inverse; (−13) + (−13) = −26; 13 − (−13) = 26.

**7. (C)** Multiplying by 1 leaves a unchanged; multiplying by (−1) gives the opposite. So a × 1 × (−1) × 1 = −a. It is not a itself (one negative factor flips the sign), not always negative (if a is negative, −a is positive), and not 0.

**8. (C)** Rise over 3 hours = 3 × 4 = 12 °C. So −9 + 12 = +3 °C. Subtracting 12 gives −21; counting only one hour gives −5; forgetting the start of −9 gives +12.

**9. (B)** Four negative factors → even count → product positive. Magnitude 1×2×3×4 = 24, so +24. An odd-count error gives −24; adding instead of multiplying gives ±10.

**10. (D)** Descent: 4 × 3 = 12 m down → −12 m. Rising 5 m: −12 + 5 = −7 m. Subtracting the rise gives −17; ignoring the descent rate gives −12; a sign flip gives +7.

**11. (B)** Negative ÷ negative is POSITIVE, so that statement is the false one. The other three are all correct sign facts: (−)(−)=+, a smaller minus a larger is negative, and (−)÷(+) is negative.

**12. (A)** Inside the bracket: 7 − (−3) = 10. Then (−2) × 10 = −20, and (−4) × (−5) = +20. Sum: −20 + 20 = 0. Treating 7 − (−3) as 4 changes the first term; making both products negative gives −40.

**13. (C)** A positive product needs an EVEN number of negative factors. Among 0–5, the even possibilities are 0, 2, 4. Of the listed options only 4 is even. 3, 5, and 1 are odd and would give a negative product.

**14. (B)** Division: (−72) ÷ (−8) = +9. Multiplication: (−3) × (−2) = +6. Add: 9 + 6 = 15. Subtracting the 6 gives +3; making the division negative gives −15.

**15. (B)** Credits add: 150 + 90 = 240. Debits subtract: 200 + 85 = 285. Balance 240 − 285 = −45, i.e. overdrawn ₹45. A sign slip gives +45; adding everything as same sign gives 525.

**16. (C)** 'How much greater' means (−7) − (−20) = −7 + 20 = 13. Treating the subtraction of a negative as a subtraction gives −27; a sign slip gives −13.

**17. (C)** (−2)⁴ = +16 (four negatives, positive). (−3)×(−3)×(−1) = −9; (−5)×2 = −10; (−4)−(−1) = −3. The largest is +16.

**18. (D)** Division first: 36 ÷ (−4) = −9, so the expression is 20 − (−9) + (−5) = 20 + 9 − 5 = 24. Doing 20 − 36 first breaks order of operations; treating −(−9) as −9 gives +6.

**19. (A)** a × 1 = a defines 1 as the multiplicative identity. 0 (not 1) is the additive identity. Multiplying by 1 keeps the original sign, so it is not always positive. 1 and −1 are opposites, but the identity statement is the one being asked.

**20. (A)** Five negative factors → odd → product negative. Magnitude 1×2×3×4×5 = 120, so −120. An even-count error gives +120; adding gives ±15.

**21. (B)** Total rise = (−50) − (−300) = 250 m up over 5 steps → 250 ÷ 5 = +50 m each. The +/− sign of rise must be positive (it went up); −50 reverses direction; 250 is the whole rise, not one step.

**22. (C)** (−1) × (−1) = +1, so n × 1 = n. The two negatives cancel; a single-negative slip gives −n; it is not n² (no multiplication of n by itself).

**23. (B)** Six copies of −9: 6 × (−9) = −54. Five copies would give −45; seven give −63; a sign slip gives +54.

**24. (D)** Bracket: (−2) + (−1) = −3. Then (−18) ÷ (−3) = +6. Finally 6 − (−4) = 6 + 4 = 10. Treating 6 − (−4) as 6 − 4 gives +2; making the division negative gives −10.

**25. (D)** Multiplying a factor across a sum, a × (b + c) = a×b + a×c, is the distributive property. Associativity regroups one operation; commutativity reorders; identity involves 0 — none of these matches the split shown.

**26. (B)** Left to right: (−96) ÷ 8 = −12; × (−1) = +12; ÷ (−4) = −3. Two of the three sign-flips net... counting the negatives: factors −96, −1, −4 → three negatives → odd → result negative, magnitude 96÷8÷4 = 3, so −3. An even-count error gives +3.

**27. (A)** Correct: 11 × 4 = 44. Wrong: 7 × (−2) = −14. Score 44 − 14 = 30; the 7 unattempted add 0. Ignoring penalties gives 44; counting all 25 as attempted changes the wrong count; adding the penalty as positive gives 58.

**28. (B)** We need (−6) × x = +30. Since the product is positive and one factor is negative, x must be negative: x = 30 ÷ (−6) = −5. Choosing +5 gives −30; multiplying instead of dividing gives ±180.

**29. (C)** −5² means square first then negate: −(25) = −25. (−5)² = +25. Sum: −25 + 25 = 0. Treating −5² as (−5)² gives 50; sign slips give −50 or −10.

**30. (A)** First bracket: (−8) − (−3) = −8 + 3 = −5. Second: (−2) − 4 = −6. Product (−5) × (−6) = +30 (two negatives). Treating the first bracket as −11 or making the product negative gives the wrong options.

**31. (D)** An integer plus its opposite is 0 (additive inverse), and 0 × (−17) = 0 (zero-product rule). Confusing the inverse sum with 1 gives −17; doubling gives −34.

**32. (D)** If x ÷ (−6) = +7 then x = 7 × (−6) = −42. Check: (−42) ÷ (−6) = +7. Choosing +42 gives −7; adding instead of multiplying gives −13.

**33. (A)** Net move = −8 − 3 + 9 − 6 = −8. Start +5: 5 − 8 = −3. Adding the rights and lefts with wrong signs gives the other options; +5 − 8 = −3 is correct.

**34. (D)** The first product contains a factor 0, so it is 0. The second product: (−3) × (−2) = +6. Total 0 + 6 = 6. Forgetting the second term gives 0; ignoring the 0 in the first term gives a large value; a sign slip gives −6.

**35. (A)** Multiplication and division bind tighter than subtraction and are done left to right: 6 x (−2) = −12, then −12 ÷ 3 = −4. Finally (−10) − (−4) = −10 + 4 = −6. Subtracting first, or multiplying the two leftmost terms, or turning the final subtraction into an addition of a negative, each breaks the order-of-operations rule and gives a different (wrong) value.

**36. (B)** (−5) × 8 = −40 and 5 × (−8) = −40. They are equal, so the difference is 0. Adding the two magnitudes gives 80; a sign confusion gives −80.

**37. (C)** (−3)³ = (−3) × (−3) × (−3). First (−3)×(−3) = +9, then +9 × (−3) = −27 (three negatives → odd → negative). +27 forgets the odd parity; ±9 stops after two factors.

**38. (C)** Profits: 130 + 25 = 155. Losses: 40 + 75 + 90 = 205. Net 155 − 205 = −50, a loss of ₹50. A sign slip gives a gain of 50; summing all magnitudes as loss gives 360; ignoring losses gives 155.

**39. (A)** p × q is positive (two negatives). p + q is the sum of two negatives, hence negative. Positive × negative = negative. It cannot be zero since p, q are non-zero negatives, and the sign is fully determined.

**40. (D)** Brackets: 15 − 23 = −8, so (−8) × (−5) = +40. Division: (−40) ÷ 8 = −5. Sum 40 + (−5) = 35. Treating the division as +5 gives 45; making the product negative gives −45.

**41. (D)** There are nine negative factors; nine is odd, so the product is negative. (None of the factors is 0, so it is not zero.) Mistaking nine for an even count gives positive; −45 is the sum, not the product.

**42. (A)** (−4) × x + 6 = −18 → (−4) × x = −24 → x = (−24) ÷ (−4) = +6. Check: (−4)×6 = −24, −24 + 6 = −18. Choosing −6 gives +24+6 = 30; sign/division slips give ±3.

**43. (B)** Division: (−45) ÷ (−9) = +5. Multiplication: (−2) × 3 = −6, and there is a minus before it: −(−6) = +6. So 5 + 6 = 11. Treating −(−2)×3 as −6 gives −1; a sign slip on the division gives −11.

**44. (D)** First product: (−2) × (−5) = +10. Second product: (−3) × (−4) = +12. Then 10 − 12 = −2. Adding the products gives +22; a sign slip gives +2.

**45. (B)** 2 − 5 = −3; −3 − 4 = −7; −7 + 3 = −4 °C. Treating the rise as a fall gives −14; a sign slip gives +4; ignoring the rise gives −7.

**46. (C)** (−2)×(−3)×(−4) = −24 (three negatives → negative). (−5)×(−5) = +25; (−6)+(−6) = −12; (−10)−(−1) = −9. The most negative is −24.

**47. (A)** Left to right division: (−64) ÷ (−8) = +8; +8 ÷ (−2) = −4. Then (−4) − (−3) = −4 + 3 = −1. Treating −(−3) as −3 gives −7; sign slips give ±7 or +1.

**48. (D)** 102 = 100 + 2, so (−8) × 102 = (−8)×100 + (−8)×2 = −800 + (−16) = −816. The other splits mishandle the sign of (−8) × 2, which must be −16, not +16, giving the incorrect −784.

**49. (B)** Other = 48 ÷ (−6) = −8 (positive product with a negative factor needs a negative partner). Check: (−6) × (−8) = +48. Choosing +8 gives −48; multiplying instead of dividing gives ±288.

**50. (B)** Net = −30 + 120 − 45 + 80 = +125. Twice: 2 × 125 = +250. Forgetting to double gives 125; a sign error on the net gives −250; summing magnitudes gives 550.

**51. (A)** Descending means each next number is smaller. 6 > 1 > 0 > −4 > −9 holds. The first distractor is ascending; the others are out of order (1 is not less than 0, and −4 is not less than −9).

**52. (C)** Bracket: (−2) − (−5) = −2 + 5 = 3. First term: (−6) × 3 = −18. Second: (−6) × (−2) = +12, with the minus in front: −18 − 12 = −30. Treating the second product as −12 (then subtracting) flips the result toward −6; sign slips give +30.

**53. (A)** Adding, subtracting, or multiplying two integers always gives an integer, so the integers are closed under those three. Division can give a non-integer (e.g. 7 ÷ 2), so closure fails for division — any option that includes division is wrong.

**54. (D)** (−2)³ = −8; (−3)² = +9; (−1)⁵ = −1, and minus that is +1. Sum: −8 + 9 + 1 = +2. Treating (−1)⁵ as +1 (then subtracting) gives 0; sign slips on the cube give other values.

**55. (D)** Need sum −1, product −30. Try 5 and −6: sum = −1, product = −30 ✓. (−5)+6 = +1 (wrong sum); (−10)+3 = −7; (−15)+2 = −13. Only 5 and −6 satisfy both.

**56. (C)** Multiply: 5 × (−4) = −20; before it is a minus: −(−20) = +20. Divide: (−12) ÷ 6 = −2. So (−9) + 20 + (−2) = 9. Treating −5×(−4) as −20 gives −31-type errors; ignoring the division gives +11.

**57. (C)** Net move = −7 + 3 − 5 + 6 = −3. From +2: 2 − 3 = −1. A sign slip on one of the moves gives +1 or −3; ignoring a down move gives +5.

**58. (C)** Rewrite subtractions of negatives as additions: −14 + 6 + 9 − 4 = (−14 − 4) + (6 + 9) = −18 + 15 = −3. Treating every '− (−)' as a plain subtraction gives −33; a sign slip gives +3.

**59. (A)** Each negative factor flips the sign; an odd count of flips leaves the product negative. It is never zero (all factors are non-zero), not positive (that needs an even count), and not equal to the count.

**60. (D)** Top: (−5)×(−2) = +10, then 10 − 4 = 6. Bottom: (−1) + (−2) = −3. So 6 ÷ (−3) = −2. Treating the product as −10 changes the numerator; a sign slip on the quotient gives +2.
