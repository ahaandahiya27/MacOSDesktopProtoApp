## Worked Solutions

**1. (D)** The chapter pairs twelve postures one-to-one with twelve Sun-names, and the twelve names echo the twelve months; the intended lesson is that physical practice, the Sanskrit names/mantras, and the natural year are unified into one daily routine. The Sun's name does not literally change each month — the twelve names are synonyms used together. The number is exactly twelve, not a loose 'about ten'. And one need not switch postures by month — every full cycle uses all twelve.

**2. (C)** स्वस्थं शरीरम् is physical, वीर्यम् (vigour) and तेजः (radiance) are energetic, and प्रज्ञा (wisdom) is mental — together an all-round well-being. Wealth and rank are nowhere claimed. The chapter never says one stops studying — प्रज्ञा is the fruit of, not a replacement for, learning. And the four are genuinely distinct fruits, not four labels for one thing.

**3. (C)** The false claim is that the mantras are meaningless — each is a salutation to the Sun by one of its meaningful names (e.g. मित्राय नमः). The other three are all true: twelve postures, each with a Sun-name mantra, and the practice yields a healthy body and mind.

**4. (B)** The chapter fixes the count at twelve postures, so 'about half' (six) undercounts. It is not six, not ten, and not a number that varies day to day; the twelve is fixed and matches the twelve Sun-names.

**5. (B)** मित्राय is a dative governed by नमः; explaining the dative right there lets the student understand the mantra being chanted, tying language to the practice. Postures do not require reciting endings first. The dative is one of several cases, not the only one. And the pairing is deliberate, not accidental shared space.

**6. (D)** नमः ('salutation') idiomatically takes its object in the dative, so मित्राय is 'to the Sun' and the phrase means 'salutation to the Sun'. It is not a nominative subject with a verb — नमः is an indeclinable, not a finite verb. मित्राय is a single word, not a compound. And it is dative, not vocative — a vocative would be मित्र.

**7. (C)** Here मित्र is used as a Sun-name (its sense 'friend' lies behind the name, but the referent is the Sun). It is not a classmate, not the moon, and not a river — those would all break the Sun-Salutation context.

**8. (D)** मित्राय is dative ('to the Sun'), governed by नमः; making 'the friend' the subject wrongly treats a dative as a nominative — the salutation is offered TO the Sun, not BY a friend. He did not (and should not) read मित्र as the moon, नमः is the salutation word that governs the dative, and the error is the case of मित्राय, not a tense on नमः.

**9. (C)** नमः governs the dative, so the Sun must be सूर्याय (dative). सूर्यः is nominative, सूर्यम् accusative, and सूर्येण instrumental — none of these is the case नमः requires.

**10. (C)** नमः needs the dative; the dative of the i-stem रवि is रवये, giving रवये नमः. रविः is nominative, रविम् accusative, and रवेः genitive/ablative — wrong cases after नमः.

**11. (A)** नमः is an indeclinable (अव्यय) that governs the dative — hence मित्राय, सूर्याय, रवये. It does not itself decline to match the noun, it is not a finite verb, and its position is free (मित्राय नमः or नमः मित्राय).

**12. (C)** नमः needs the dative. चन्द्राय (a-stem dative) and गुरवे (u-stem dative of गुरु) are correct datives, but सूर्यः is nominative — it should be सूर्याय. So नमः सूर्यः is the wrong one; the other two follow the rule and 'all three correct' is false.

**13. (C)** भास्कर is an अ-stem, so its dative singular is भास्कराय, matching मित्राय/छात्राय. भास्करः is nominative, भास्करेण instrumental, and भास्करस्य genitive — none works after नमः.

**14. (B)** The dative (चतुर्थी, 'to/for') marks the recipient: the salutation is offered to the Sun. 'By whom' would be instrumental (सूर्येण), 'from whom' ablative (सूर्यात्), and 'whose' genitive (सूर्यस्य) — different cases with different roles.

**15. (A)** मित्राय is the dative singular of मित्र — exactly what नमः requires. मित्रस्य is genitive, मित्रम् accusative, and मित्रेण instrumental; though each is a real form, none is the dative नमः governs.

**16. (D)** नमः governs the dative, so the saluted Sun must be मित्राय; मित्रः is the nominative and is therefore wrong. मित्रः is a noun (not a verb), it is singular (not plural), and the difference is grammatical, not merely stylistic.

**17. (A)** The male teacher आचार्य is an अ-stem; its dative singular is आचार्याय, which नमः requires. आचार्यः is nominative, आचार्यम् accusative, and आचार्या is the feminine nominative ('female teacher') — not the dative needed here.

**18. (A)** They are synonyms (एकार्थकशब्दाः) — distinct words all naming the Sun. They are not antonyms (not opposites), not pieces of one compound, and not five case-forms of a single word (each is its own word).

**19. (C)** हिमालयः is the mountain (the Himalaya), not a Sun-name. सविता, मित्रः and आदित्यः are all among the twelve names of the Sun.

**20. (A)** रत्नाकरः ('mine of jewels') is the ocean, not the Sun, so that pairing is wrong. भास्करः, आदित्यः and मित्रः are all genuine Sun-names correctly glossed as the Sun.

**21. (B)** The one-to-one scheme rests on exactly twelve names matching exactly twelve postures and twelve months; a thirteenth posture has no name to match. Sanskrit has no rule against the number thirteen, no new Sun-name appears on demand, and the year still has twelve months — so the surplus posture simply breaks the pairing.

**22. (C)** The many synonyms supply a distinct name for each of the twelve postures while all meaning the one Sun. There is only one Sun (not twelve), Sanskrit is not short of a word (it is rich in them), and synonyms agree rather than contradict.

**23. (D)** घटः is a pot, breaking the set of Sun-names आदित्यः, अर्कः, भानुः. आदित्यः is not feminine, अर्कः is not a compound, and भानुः is a Sun-name (not the moon) — so those three belong together.

**24. (D)** स्व = 'self/one's own' and स्थ = 'standing/abiding', so स्वस्थ is 'self-abiding'. The other splits invent meanings: स्वस्थ is not 'sun+salute', not 'six+fault', and not the dative gloss.

**25. (B)** नीरोगम् = 'disease-free', so स्वस्थ here means healthy. It is not 'wealthy', not merely 'standing' (that misreads the स्थ part literally), and not 'selfish' — 'self-abiding' means centred and at ease, the opposite of disturbed.

**26. (B)** स्व + स्थ pictures health as being centred and at ease in oneself — a positive state, richer than the bare गloss नीरोगम्. It is not about literally standing, not about dependence (the word stresses self), and the स्व ('self') is exactly the point.

**27. (A)** स्वस्थं शरीरम् (healthy body) plus स्वस्थं मनः (healthy mind) explicitly couples physical and mental health. It is not body-only or mind-only — both are named — and wealth (धनम्) is not in the pair.

**28. (B)** शरीरम् and मनः are both neuter nouns, so the agreeing adjective takes the same neuter form स्वस्थम् in each phrase. An adjective does change with gender/case in general (so 'never changes' is false), मनः is not borrowing anything, and they are two separate phrases, not one compound.

**29. (C)** नी-रोग means 'without disease'; its opposite must mean 'with disease' (सरोगम्/रोगयुक्तम्). नीरोगम् and स्वस्थम् are synonyms for 'healthy' (not opposites), and तेजः ('radiance') is unrelated to the contrast of healthy vs diseased.

**30. (B)** प्रज्ञा is wisdom, तेजः is radiance/brilliance, and वीर्यम् is vigour/valour. The other sets swap these around — e.g. calling तेजः 'vigour' or वीर्यम् 'radiance' mixes up the pair तेजः↔वीर्यम्, and the first option scrambles all three.

**31. (D)** वीर्यम् ('vigour/valour') is the natural opposite of आलस्यम् ('laziness'). प्रज्ञा opposes ignorance, not laziness; नीरोगम् opposes disease; and नमः ('salutation') is not a benefit-word at all.

**32. (A)** तेजः is radiance/brilliance; it does not bring sleepiness or idleness — that is the opposite of what the practice gives. The other three map correctly: प्रज्ञा→wisdom, वीर्यम्→vigour, healthy body→good health.

**33. (D)** Bright in study = प्रज्ञा (wisdom); energetic in play = वीर्यम् (vigour); glowing = तेजः (radiance); free of illness = स्वस्थं शरीरम् (healthy body). Only the first option keeps that exact order; the others mis-sequence the four.

**34. (C)** धनम् ('wealth') is never listed among the fruits of the practice — the benefits are bodily and mental, not material. प्रज्ञा, तेजः and वीर्यम् are all explicitly named benefits.

**35. (D)** तेजः = radiance/brilliance (how one shines), वीर्यम् = vigour/valour (active strength). Neither means wisdom (प्रज्ञा) or wealth (धनम्), neither means fear or laziness, and they are distinct, not identical.

**36. (B)** In a समास the members fuse and the first (सूर्य) loses its independent case ending, the whole then declining as one word (सूर्यनमस्कारः). Keeping both endings would make it two words, not a compound; it is a noun, not a verb; and it is a single word, not a sentence.

**37. (A)** In a compound only the final member takes the case ending for the whole word, so -ः sits on नमस्कार and marks सूर्यनमस्कारः as nominative singular; सूर्य has dropped its ending. The first member does not keep an ending, both do not decline separately, and there is no verb inside.

**38. (B)** नमस्कार ('salutation', cognate with नमः) governs the dative, so 'salutation to the Sun' is सूर्याय नमस्कारः. There is no rule that the first word is dative, नमस्कारः here is nominative (not dative), and many cases can follow a noun.

**39. (A)** सूर्यनमस्कारः, as one fused word, names the practice/discipline; सूर्याय नमस्कारः, two words, expresses the act of saluting the Sun. They are not identical, the compound is perfectly correct Sanskrit, and the roles are not reversed.

**40. (A)** In forming a समास the first member loses its case ending, so the correct compound is सूर्यनमस्कारः, not सूर्यायनमस्कारः. Members do not keep their endings inside a compound, compounds are very common in Sanskrit, and endings are not relocated to the front.

**41. (B)** In a compound the first member drops its case ending and appears in its bare stem रवि, giving रविनमस्कारः. रवये (dative), रविः (nominative) and रवेः (genitive) all wrongly keep a case ending inside the compound.

**42. (C)** आचार्या (ending -आ) is a female teacher and आचार्यः (ending -ः) a male teacher — a gender contrast. It is not singular vs plural, not a case contrast (both are nominative singular), and both are nouns, not verbs.

**43. (A)** आचार्या is the feminine form, so the teacher is a woman. It is not the masculine आचार्यः (a man), not plural (a group), and आचार्या still means 'teacher', not 'student'.

**44. (B)** आचार्या→आचार्यः flips the form from feminine to masculine, so the teacher reads as male. It does not make the word plural, does not convert prose to mantras, and is not a no-op — the gender genuinely changes.

**45. (D)** The feminine आ-stem आचार्या has dative singular आचार्यायै, which नमः requires. आचार्याय is the masculine dative ('to the male teacher'), आचार्या is feminine nominative, and आचार्याम् is accusative — none is the feminine dative needed.

**46. (D)** नमः, स्वाहा and स्वस्ति belong to a small group of indeclinables that idiomatically take a dative object. They are not verbs, they do not strip endings off following words, and they differ in meaning (salutation / oblation cry / well-being).

**47. (B)** स्वस्ति governs the dative, and the dative plural of प्रजा is प्रजाभ्यः, so 'well-being to the people' is स्वस्ति प्रजाभ्यः. प्रजाः is nominative, प्रजानाम् genitive, and प्रजाभिः instrumental — none is the dative स्वस्ति needs.

**48. (C)** स्वाहा takes a dative object; अग्नये is the dative singular of the i-stem अग्नि ('to Agni'). It is not the nominative (अग्निः), स्वाहा is an indeclinable not a dative, and the case here is dative, not accusative.

**49. (A)** Dative endings depend on the stem: अ-stems take -आय (मित्राय, छात्राय) and the इ-stem रवि takes -ये (रवये). All three ARE datives, just of different stem-types; -ये is not a misprint.

**50. (D)** देव is an अ-stem like मित्र/छात्र/सूर्य, so its dative singular is देवाय (ending -आय). देवः is nominative, देवेन instrumental, and देवस्य genitive.

**51. (C)** Drilling छात्राय first teaches the dative -आय ending, so the learner sees why नमः takes मित्राय. छात्र ('student') is not a Sun-name, mantras are not 'written in a paradigm', and नमः can salute any noun, not only छात्र.

**52. (A)** The -आय in मित्राय is the dative case-ending of the noun, making मित्राय 'to (the Sun) Mitra'; it is not the verb 'come'. मित्राय is a noun, not a verb, and नमः means 'salutation', not 'come'.

**53. (B)** Both halves must be right: नमः→dative→मित्राय, and सूर्यनमस्कारः is one compound. The first option errs on the case (nominative) and the compound; the second errs by calling the compound two words; the third errs on the case (accusative).

**54. (D)** एकार्थकशब्दाः are synonyms (one meaning, as with the Sun-names). समास is a compound (fused, not separate), चतुर्थी (dative) means 'to/for' (not 'from', which is ablative), and आचार्या is a FEMALE teacher.

**55. (A)** There is one Sun; सूर्याय नमः is the act 'salutation to the Sun', and सूर्यनमस्कारः is the named practice. There are not two suns, the compound does not multiply the Sun, and both expressions are perfectly grammatical.

**56. (D)** The chapter combines twelve postures, each with a dative Sun-name mantra (मित्राय नमः etc.), and lists the fruits स्वस्थं शरीरम्, वीर्यम्, तेजः, प्रज्ञा. It is not one repeated mantra, not moon-worship for wealth, and not a pot-saying.

**57. (C)** Pairing postures with meaningful dative mantras shows body and language being trained together, so health and learning advance jointly. Grammar is not kept separate here, the words' meaning is central (they salute the Sun), and the mantras are substantive, not decorative.

**58. (D)** All three claims are wrong and must each be fixed: नमः→dative (मित्राय), मित्र→a Sun-name (not the moon), सूर्यनमस्कारः→one compound (not two words). The distractors leave at least one error uncorrected, and the sentence is not already correct.

**59. (B)** नमः needs the dative. आदित्य is an अ-stem → आदित्याय; भानु is a उ-stem → भानवे. So आदित्याय नमः and भानवे नमः. The nominatives (आदित्यः, भानुः) and accusatives (आदित्यम्, भानुम्) are wrong cases, and the last option leaves भानुः in the nominative.

**60. (A)** The valid chain: नमः ('salutation') governs the dative; मित्र (a Sun-name) therefore becomes मित्राय; the phrase is 'salutation to the Sun'. The first chain wrongly makes a dative the subject; the second wrongly calls a two-word phrase a compound; the third misidentifies मित्र as the moon and मित्राय as instrumental.
