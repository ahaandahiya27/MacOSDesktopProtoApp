Set 1 · Q1–Q10

Q1

A torch is switched on in a dusty room and a sharp straight beam is seen. This is the best everyday evidence that light:

  • Abends around corners
  • Btravels in a straight line✓
  • Cis made of seven colours
  • Dalways slows down in air

Working

✓

A torch beam appears as a straight line of light because light travels in a straight line (rectilinear propagation). Light does not bend around corners, and the straight beam is not evidence about colour or speed.

Q2

A small bright lamp is placed in front of a large cardboard sheet that has a tiny hole. The patch of light on a wall behind the hole, and the sharp-edged shadow of the cardboard, both happen because light:

  • Aspreads equally in all directions through solids
  • Bis reflected by the cardboard
  • Cis refracted by the hole
  • Dtravels in straight lines✓

Working

✓

A patch of light passing through a tiny hole and a sharp-edged shadow both arise because light moves in straight lines: rays that are blocked make the shadow, rays that pass straight through the hole make the bright patch. It is not reflection or refraction here.

Q3

An opaque ball is held between a single small lamp and a screen. As the ball is moved **closer to the lamp** (away from the screen), its shadow on the screen will:

  • Abecome smaller
  • Bdisappear completely
  • Cbecome larger✓
  • Dchange colour

Working

✓

Moving the opaque ball closer to the small lamp (and farther from the screen) makes it block a wider cone of the diverging rays, so the shadow on the screen grows **larger**. (Moving it closer to the screen would shrink the shadow — the trap.)

Q4

A shadow is always:

  • Adark and shows only the outline of the object✓
  • Bcoloured like the object
  • Clarger than the object
  • Dthe same shape and colour as the object

Working

✓

A shadow is simply the dark region where light is blocked; it shows only the **outline** of the object and has no colour of its own. It is not necessarily larger, and it is never coloured like the object.

Q5

Three opaque cards have holes punched in them. Light from a lamp reaches your eye only when the three holes are:

  • Aof exactly equal size
  • Bplaced in a curve
  • Ccoloured the same
  • Darranged in a straight line with your eye and the lamp✓

Working

✓

You see the lamp only when light reaches your eye, which (since light goes in straight lines) requires the three holes, your eye and the lamp to all lie on one straight line. Equal size, colour or a curved arrangement do not matter.

Q6

Light falls on a smooth polished mirror and on a sheet of white paper. Compared with the mirror, the paper:

  • Aabsorbs all the light and reflects none
  • Breflects a sharp clear image like the mirror
  • Creflects light in many scattered directions (diffuse reflection)✓
  • Dlets all the light pass straight through

Working

✓

A polished mirror reflects rays in one regular direction (regular reflection), giving a clear image. Rough white paper reflects light in many scattered directions — **diffuse (irregular) reflection** — so no image forms, though plenty of light still bounces off.

Q7

You can see a clear image of yourself in a still pond but not in a pond rippled by wind. The reason is that ripples cause:

  • Arefraction of the image
  • Bregular reflection
  • Cdiffuse (irregular) reflection✓
  • Dtotal absorption of light

Working

✓

A still water surface acts like a plane mirror (regular reflection) and forms a clear image. Wind makes ripples — an uneven surface — so the reflection becomes scattered, i.e. **diffuse (irregular) reflection**, and the image breaks up.

Q8

A ray of light strikes a plane mirror and the angle between the incoming ray and the mirror surface is 30°. The angle of reflection (measured from the normal) is:

  • A30°
  • B60°✓
  • C90°
  • D15°

Working

✓

The angle of incidence is measured from the **normal**, not from the surface. If the ray makes 30° with the mirror surface, it makes 90° − 30° = 60° with the normal. By the law of reflection the angle of reflection also equals **60°**. (30° is the trap of measuring from the surface.)

Q9

Which statement about the reflection of light from any surface is **always** true?

  • AOnly shiny metals can reflect light.
  • BReflection only happens with white light.
  • CLight is destroyed when it is reflected.
  • DThe angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection.✓

Working

✓

The fundamental law of reflection is that the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection — true for every reflecting surface, shiny or dull, and for light of any colour. Reflection is not limited to metals or to white light, and light is not destroyed.

Q10

We are able to read the words printed on this paper because the paper produces:

  • Aregular reflection that forms an image
  • Bdiffuse reflection that scatters light to our eyes from every part✓
  • Cits own light
  • Drefraction of the room light

Working

✓

Printed paper does not glow on its own; we read it because room light strikes it and undergoes **diffuse reflection**, scattering from every point of the page into our eyes. Regular reflection would give a mirror image, not readable text.

Set 2 · Q11–Q20

Q11

The image of your face in a flat bathroom mirror is:

  • Areal and inverted
  • Breal and magnified
  • Cvirtual, erect and the same size as you✓
  • Dvirtual and diminished

Working

✓

A plane mirror always forms a **virtual, erect** image that is the **same size** as the object and as far behind the mirror as the object is in front. It is never real, magnified or diminished.

Q12

Which one of these can a plane-mirror image **never** be?

  • Acaught on a screen✓
  • Berect
  • Cthe same size as the object
  • Dlaterally inverted

Working

✓

A plane-mirror image is virtual, so it cannot be **caught on a screen**. It is always erect, the same size, and laterally inverted — all of which a plane-mirror image genuinely is. So the one it can never be is "caught on a screen."

Q13

You stand 1.5 m in front of a plane mirror. The distance between you and your image is:

  • A1.5 m
  • B3 m✓
  • C0.75 m
  • D0 m

Working

✓

The image is as far behind the mirror as the object is in front, i.e. 1.5 m behind. The distance from you to your image is therefore 1.5 + 1.5 = **3 m**. (1.5 m forgets to add the image's distance behind.)

Q14

A plane mirror forms an image that is the same size as the object. If you walk **towards** the mirror, your image:

  • Agrows larger than you
  • Bshrinks
  • Cturns upside down
  • Dstays the same size as you✓

Working

✓

A plane mirror always forms an image the same size as the object, regardless of how close you stand. Walking towards the mirror does not change the image's size — it **stays the same size as you**. (It may *appear* to loom, but its true size is unchanged.)

Q15

A boy stands 2 m from a plane mirror and then steps back to 5 m from it. By how much does his image move away from the mirror?

  • A3 m✓
  • B6 m
  • C1.5 m
  • D10 m

Working

✓

The image is always as far behind as the object is in front. At 2 m the image is 2 m behind; at 5 m it is 5 m behind. So the image moves 5 − 2 = **3 m** farther back. (6 m is the trap of doubling the object's shift.)

Q16

In a plane-mirror image the part that appears swapped (changed) is:

  • Atop and bottom
  • Bnear and far
  • Cleft and right✓
  • Dnothing is swapped

Working

✓

A plane mirror swaps **left and right** (lateral inversion); it does not turn the image upside down or swap near and far. Your right hand appears as the image's left hand.

Q17

Held up to a plane mirror, the printed word **AMBULANCE** would look correct. This is why the word is written reversed on the front of an ambulance, so that:

  • Aa driver ahead reads it correctly in the rear-view mirror✓
  • Bit looks artistic
  • Cit reflects more light
  • Dit can be read in the dark

Working

✓

In a following driver's rear-view mirror the writing is laterally inverted; printing AMBULANCE already reversed means the mirror reverses it back, so the **driver ahead reads it correctly** and pulls over. It is not for looks, brightness or darkness.

Q18

The capital letters H, I, O, X look unchanged in a plane mirror, but the letter R does not. The reason H, I, O, X are unchanged is that:

  • Athey contain only curves
  • Bthey are the brightest letters
  • Cthey are written in red
  • Dthey are symmetric about a vertical line down the middle✓

Working

✓

Lateral inversion swaps left and right. Letters that are **symmetric about a vertical line** (H, I, O, X) look identical after this swap, while R is not vertically symmetric, so it appears reversed. Brightness or colour is irrelevant.

Q19

A wall clock shows the time 3:00. Seen in a plane mirror, the reflected clock appears to read:

  • A3:00
  • B6:00
  • C9:00✓
  • D12:00

Working

✓

A clock seen in a mirror reads "12-hour minus the real time." For 3:00 the mirror image reads 12:00 − 3:00 = **9:00**. (The hands at the 3 position appear at the 9 position.)

Q20

The number **1881** is held up to a plane mirror. Its reflection reads:

  • A8118
  • B1881✓
  • C1188
  • D8811

Working

✓

Lateral inversion reverses the left-right order and mirror-flips each digit. The digits 1 and 8 are each symmetric, and reversing the order of 1-8-8-1 gives 1-8-8-1 again, so the reflection still reads **1881** (it is a mirror palindrome here).

Set 3 · Q21–Q30

Q21

An object is placed 4 cm in front of a plane mirror. The total distance from the object to its image is:

  • A4 cm
  • B8 cm✓
  • C2 cm
  • D16 cm

Working

✓

The image lies 4 cm behind the mirror, so object-to-image distance = 4 + 4 = **8 cm**. (4 cm ignores the image distance; 16 cm wrongly squares or doubles twice.)

Q22

A candle is placed 10 cm in front of a plane mirror. Where would you have to put a screen to catch the candle's image?

  • A10 cm behind the mirror
  • Bat the surface of the mirror
  • Cnowhere — the image cannot be caught on a screen✓
  • D10 cm in front of the mirror

Working

✓

A plane mirror forms a **virtual** image, which is only an apparent point where rays seem to come from — no light actually meets there, so it **cannot be caught on any screen**, whether in front of or behind the mirror.

Q23

A man walks towards a plane mirror at 1 m/s. His image approaches the mirror at:

  • A1 m/s✓
  • B0.5 m/s
  • C2 m/s
  • Dit stays still

Working

✓

In a plane mirror the image is always as far behind as the object is in front, so the image moves exactly in step with the object. If the man approaches at 1 m/s, his image approaches the mirror at **1 m/s** too. (2 m/s is the rate at which he and his image close on each other, not the image's speed toward the mirror.)

Q24

A reflecting surface curved **inwards** (like the inside of a spoon's bowl) is a:

  • Aplane mirror
  • Bconvex mirror
  • Cconvex lens
  • Dconcave mirror✓

Working

✓

A surface that curves **inwards** (the hollow side, like the bowl of a spoon) is a **concave mirror**. The outward-bulging side would be convex.

Q25

A concave mirror is sometimes called a **converging** mirror because it:

  • Aspreads parallel rays apart
  • Bbrings parallel rays together to a point✓
  • Clets light pass through it
  • Dsplits white light into colours

Working

✓

A concave mirror is called converging because incoming parallel rays are reflected so that they **meet (converge) at a point** (the focus). Spreading rays apart describes a convex mirror; passing light through describes a lens.

Q26

Which mirror can form a **real, inverted** image of a distant object?

  • Aa plane mirror
  • Ba convex mirror
  • Ca concave mirror✓
  • Dno mirror can

Working

✓

Only a **concave mirror** can form a real, inverted image (of a distant object, the rays actually meet in front of the mirror). A plane mirror and a convex mirror only ever form virtual, erect images.

Q27

When you hold a concave (shaving) mirror **very close** to your face, the image you see is:

  • Areal and inverted
  • Bdiminished
  • Cexactly the same size
  • Dvirtual, erect and magnified✓

Working

✓

When the object is **very close** to a concave mirror (nearer than its focus), the image is **virtual, erect and magnified** — which is exactly why it is used as a shaving/make-up mirror. Far objects would give a real inverted image, but "very close" gives the enlarged erect one.

Q28

A dentist uses a concave mirror mainly because, held close to a tooth, it gives:

  • Aan enlarged, erect image of the tooth✓
  • Ba wider view of the whole mouth
  • Ca smaller image so more fits in
  • Da coloured image

Working

✓

A dentist holds a concave mirror close to a tooth so it acts like a magnifier, giving an **enlarged, erect image of the tooth** for a better look. A wider view is what a convex mirror gives, not a concave one.

Q29

Why is a concave mirror, and not a plane or convex one, placed behind the bulb in a torch or car headlight?

  • Ait makes the bulb look bigger
  • Bit spreads the light in all directions
  • Cit changes the colour of the light
  • Dit gathers the light and sends out a strong parallel beam✓

Working

✓

A bulb placed at the focus of a **concave** (converging) reflector sends out a strong, nearly parallel beam — ideal for a torch or headlight. A plane or convex mirror would scatter the light instead of focusing it into a beam.

Q30

A student claims a concave mirror "always makes things look bigger." This is:

  • Awrong — a distant object gives a small, inverted image✓
  • Bcorrect for every position of the object
  • Ccorrect only for red objects
  • Dwrong — a concave mirror always shrinks things

Working

✓

"Always bigger" is **wrong**: a concave mirror magnifies only when the object is close (nearer than the focus). For a **distant object** it gives a small, **inverted** image. So the image depends on object distance.

Set 4 · Q31–Q40

Q31

A reflecting surface curved **outwards** (bulging towards you, like the back of a spoon) is a:

  • Aconcave mirror
  • Bplane mirror
  • Cconvex mirror✓
  • Dconcave lens

Working

✓

A surface that bulges **outwards** towards you (the back of a spoon) is a **convex mirror**. The inward, hollow side is concave.

Q32

Whatever the position of the object, the image formed by a convex mirror is always:

  • Areal and inverted
  • Bvirtual, erect and diminished✓
  • Cvirtual, erect and magnified
  • Dreal and the same size

Working

✓

Whatever the object's position, a convex mirror always forms a **virtual, erect and diminished** image. It can never form a real or magnified image — a key trap.

Q33

A convex mirror is preferred as a vehicle's rear-view mirror chiefly because it:

  • Agives a wider field of view✓
  • Bmagnifies cars behind so they look close
  • Cforms a real image on the road
  • Dshows the true distance exactly

Working

✓

A convex mirror always makes a diminished image, so it packs a **wider field of view** into the glass — letting a driver see more of the road behind. It does not magnify or show true distance; objects look smaller and farther than they really are.

Q34

A salesperson says a security mirror in a shop "magnifies thieves so they look bigger." For a convex security mirror this claim is:

  • Acorrect
  • Bcorrect only in bright light
  • Cwrong — a convex mirror always gives a diminished image✓
  • Dwrong — it inverts the image instead

Working

✓

"Magnifies thieves" is **wrong**: a convex (security) mirror always gives a **diminished** image. Its value is the wide field of view, letting one mirror cover a large area, not enlargement.

Q35

In a convex mirror, a car that is actually quite close behind looks small and far away. This happens because the convex mirror always forms a:

  • Amagnified image
  • Breal image
  • Claterally upright image
  • Ddiminished image✓

Working

✓

A convex mirror always forms a **diminished** image, so a nearby car looks small and far. (This is why such mirrors carry the warning "objects are closer than they appear.")

Q36

Which single property is shared by a plane mirror **and** a convex mirror but **not** by a concave mirror used for a distant object?

  • Athey magnify the object
  • Bthey form a virtual, erect image✓
  • Cthey form a real image
  • Dthey invert the image

Working

✓

A plane mirror and a convex mirror both form **virtual, erect** images, and neither magnifies. A concave mirror used for a distant object forms a real, inverted image instead. So the shared property is "they form a virtual, erect image."

Q37

Match the use to the correct mirror: a large mirror used to focus sunlight in a **solar concentrator** is:

  • Aplane
  • Ba concave lens
  • Cconvex
  • Dconcave✓

Working

✓

A solar concentrator uses a large **concave** mirror to converge (focus) sunlight onto one spot to produce heat. A convex mirror or a lens would not focus the light to a hot point the way a concave reflector does.

Q38

At a blind T-junction a large mirror is fixed high on a wall so drivers can see a **wide stretch** of the crossing road. The right mirror for this is:

  • Aconcave, because it magnifies
  • Bconvex, because it gives a wide field of view✓
  • Cplane, because it shows the true size
  • Dconcave, because it gives a real image

Working

✓

A blind-junction safety mirror must show a **wide stretch** of road, so a **convex** mirror is used — it gives a wide field of view. A concave mirror would magnify but show only a narrow region.

Q39

Four mirrors are described: (i) gives a wide view, (ii) used in a torch reflector, (iii) gives a same-size image, (iv) can form a real image. The pair that are **both concave** is:

  • A(i) and (iii)
  • B(ii) and (iv)✓
  • C(i) and (ii)
  • D(iii) and (iv)

Working

✓

(i) wide view = convex; (ii) torch reflector = concave; (iii) same-size image = plane; (iv) can form a real image = concave. The two **concave** ones are **(ii) and (iv)**.

Q40

A shopkeeper needs a mirror that lets a small camera watch the **whole** shop floor. The best choice is a:

  • Aconvex mirror✓
  • Bconcave mirror
  • Cplane mirror
  • Dconvex lens

Working

✓

Wait — re-reading: a single mirror that lets a camera watch the **whole** shop floor needs the widest possible field of view, so a **convex mirror** is the right choice. A concave or plane mirror would not capture the whole floor in one view.

Set 5 · Q41–Q50

Q41

A lens that is **thicker in the middle** than at the edges is a:

  • Aconvex lens✓
  • Bconcave lens
  • Cplane mirror
  • Dconvex mirror

Working

✓

A lens that is **thicker in the middle** than at its edges is a **convex lens** (a converging lens). The concave lens is the one thinner in the middle.

Q42

A magnifying glass used to read tiny print is a:

  • Aconcave lens
  • Bconcave mirror
  • Cplane mirror
  • Dconvex lens✓

Working

✓

A magnifying glass is a **convex lens**: held close to small print it gives an enlarged, erect (virtual) image. A concave lens would shrink the print; mirrors are not used as hand magnifiers here.

Q43

A convex lens is called a **converging** lens because rays passing through it are:

  • Aspread apart
  • Bbrought together towards a point✓
  • Creflected back
  • Dsplit into colours

Working

✓

A convex lens is called converging because rays passing through it are **brought together towards a point** (the focus). Spreading rays apart is what a concave lens does.

Q44

Which of these can be obtained on a screen (i.e., is a **real** image)?

  • Athe image in a plane mirror
  • Bthe image in a convex mirror
  • Cthe image of a distant tree formed by a convex lens✓
  • Dthe magnified image seen through a magnifying glass held close to print

Working

✓

A **real** image can be caught on a screen. Of the four, only the **image of a distant tree formed by a convex lens** is real (the rays actually meet and can fall on a screen). Plane-mirror, convex-mirror and close-up magnifier images are all virtual.

Q45

Sunlight passing through a convex lens can be focused to a tiny bright hot spot on a sheet of paper. This shows that a convex lens:

  • Aspreads light out
  • Bonly works with coloured light
  • Cconverges light to a point✓
  • Dreflects sunlight

Working

✓

Focusing sunlight to a tiny hot spot shows the convex lens **converges light to a point** (its focus). A concave lens would spread the light out instead, and the effect works with ordinary white sunlight.

Q46

A lens that is **thinner in the middle** than at the edges is a:

  • Aconvex lens
  • Bconcave mirror
  • Cconvex mirror
  • Dconcave lens✓

Working

✓

A lens that is **thinner in the middle** than at its edges is a **concave lens** (a diverging lens). The convex lens is thicker in the middle.

Q47

Whatever the position of the object, a concave (diverging) lens always forms an image that is:

  • Areal and inverted
  • Bvirtual, erect and diminished✓
  • Cvirtual, erect and magnified
  • Dreal and magnified

Working

✓

Whatever the object's position, a concave (diverging) lens always forms a **virtual, erect and diminished** image. It can never form a real or magnified image — the lens analogue of the convex-mirror rule.

Q48

Which one of the following will spread parallel rays of light apart (diverge them)?

  • Aa concave lens✓
  • Ba convex lens
  • Ca concave mirror
  • Da magnifying glass

Working

✓

A **concave lens** is the diverging element: it spreads parallel rays apart. A convex lens and a concave mirror both converge light, and a magnifying glass is a convex lens.

Q49

A student looks at small print through a concave lens. The print appears:

  • Alarger and upside down
  • Blarger and upright
  • Csmaller and upright✓
  • Dthe same size

Working

✓

A concave lens always gives a virtual, erect, **diminished** image, so the print looks **smaller and upright**. (A convex lens would make it larger — that is the trap.)

Q50

White light from the Sun is actually a mixture of:

  • Ajust three colours
  • Bseven colours✓
  • Conly red, green and blue
  • Da single pure colour

Working

✓

White sunlight is actually a mixture of **seven colours** (the VIBGYOR spectrum). It is not a single pure colour or merely three colours.

Set 6 · Q51–Q60

Q51

The correct order of colours in the spectrum of white light, from one end to the other, is given by VIBGYOR, which stands for:

  • Aviolet, indigo, blue, green, yellow, orange, red✓
  • Bred, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet only if reversed wrongly
  • Cwhite, indigo, blue, green, yellow, orange, red
  • Dviolet, indigo, blue, green, yellow, orange, brown

Working

✓

VIBGYOR names the colours in order: **violet, indigo, blue, green, yellow, orange, red**. The other options either end in "brown," start with "white," or are deliberately garbled.

Q52

A glass prism held in sunlight throws a band of colours on a wall. The splitting of white light into its colours is called:

  • Areflection
  • Bdispersion✓
  • Clateral inversion
  • Dabsorption

Working

✓

The splitting of white light into its component colours by a prism is called **dispersion**. Reflection is bouncing back; lateral inversion is the left-right swap in a mirror; absorption is light being taken up.

Q53

A rainbow in the sky is formed when sunlight is dispersed by:

  • Aclouds reflecting white light
  • Btiny water droplets acting like little prisms✓
  • Cthe mirror-like surface of the ground
  • Da concave mirror in the sky

Working

✓

A rainbow forms when sunlight enters countless **tiny water droplets**, each acting like a small prism that disperses the light into its colours. Clouds, the ground, or a sky-mirror do not produce a rainbow.

Q54

A glass prism splits white light into seven colours. A disc painted with the seven colours and spun very fast (Newton's disc) appears:

  • Ablack
  • Bviolet
  • Cwhitish / white✓
  • Dstriped with all seven colours

Working

✓

A prism splits white light into seven colours; conversely, a fast-spinning seven-colour (Newton's) disc recombines them, so the eye blends the colours and the disc looks **whitish / white**. This is the reverse of dispersion.

Q55

The fact that a prism can split white light into colours, **and** a spinning seven-colour disc looks white, together show that:

  • Awhite light is a single pure colour
  • Bcolours are created by the prism itself
  • Cthe disc destroys the colours
  • Dwhite light is made of the seven colours combined✓

Working

✓

A prism separating white light into seven colours, plus a spinning seven-colour disc looking white, together prove that **white light is made of the seven colours combined**. The prism reveals the colours; it does not create them.

Q56

A prism produces a band of colours but a flat sheet of window glass does not. The key difference is that the prism's faces are:

  • Acoloured
  • Bmade of a special glass
  • Cslanted (not parallel), so the colours stay separated✓
  • Dcurved like a lens

Working

✓

A prism's two refracting faces are **slanted (not parallel)**, so the colours bent by the first face keep spreading and stay separated. A flat sheet of window glass has parallel faces, so any spreading is undone and no colour band appears.

Q57

If you shine pure **red** light (a single colour) through a glass prism, you will see:

  • Athe full seven-colour spectrum
  • Bwhite light
  • Conly a band of red light, slightly bent but not split✓
  • Dno light at all

Working

✓

A single pure colour like **red** cannot be split further, so a prism only **bends a band of red light slightly without splitting it** into other colours. Only white (mixed) light gives the full spectrum.

Q58

Of the colours in white light, the one usually shown bending the **most** through a prism is at the violet end, and the one bending the **least** is at the:

  • Ared end✓
  • Bgreen end
  • Cblue end
  • Dindigo end

Working

✓

In dispersion, violet (shortest path-bending the most) is bent most and **red** is bent the least. So the colour bending the least is at the **red end** of the spectrum.

Q59

A teacher shows three demonstrations: (i) a still pond reflecting a tree, (ii) a prism making a rainbow band, (iii) a magnifying glass enlarging print. These illustrate, in order:

  • Arefraction, reflection, dispersion
  • Breflection, lateral inversion, dispersion
  • Cdispersion, reflection, action of a concave lens
  • Dreflection, dispersion, action of a convex lens✓

Working

✓

(i) a still pond imaging a tree = **reflection**; (ii) a prism making a colour band = **dispersion**; (iii) a magnifying glass enlarging print = the action of a **convex lens**. In order: reflection, dispersion, action of a convex lens.

Q60

Which sequence correctly pairs each device with the kind of image it forms?

  • Aplane mirror → virtual same-size; convex mirror → virtual diminished; concave lens → virtual diminished✓
  • Bplane mirror → real; convex mirror → magnified; convex lens (distant object) → virtual
  • Cconcave lens → real; convex mirror → real; plane mirror → inverted
  • Dconvex mirror → magnified erect; plane mirror → diminished; concave mirror → never real

Working

✓

Correct pairing: a plane mirror gives a **virtual, same-size** image; a convex mirror gives a **virtual, diminished** image; a concave lens gives a **virtual, diminished** image. The other options wrongly claim real/magnified/inverted images for these devices. --- *Solutions complete: 60 / 60.*
