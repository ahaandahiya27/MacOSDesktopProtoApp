Expert traps: chaining percolation-rate arithmetic across unequal volumes AND unequal times where the slower-finishing column is actually the faster-draining one once both are normalised; correcting depth/cross-section confounds so a faster drain time never alone proves coarser texture; tracing washed-down clay and iron from the A horizon's loss to the B horizon's gain while the C horizon stays barely organic and the bedrock contributes none; never letting 'more water always helps' survive once aeration and oxygen-starved roots enter the picture; separating weathering (break-in-place) from erosion (carry-away) even when both act on the same slope; reading water-retention-versus-crop-suitability tables where the highest-retaining soil is the wrong pick; and refusing to treat percolation rate and water-holding capacity as the same property when they are inverse. 1. On a single mountain, three identical granite outcrops sit at the foot (warm, wet), mid-slope (mild, moist) and summit (cold, dry).
