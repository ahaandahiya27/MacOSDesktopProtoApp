Worked Solutions

1. (B) Both devices steer the spotlight off the fox: the agentless passive hides who failed, and the verdict 'sour' faults the fruit. They are two faces of one self-protective evasion, not honest praise, mere synonyms for fatigue, or empty repetition.

2. (A) Putting the verb in the active voice with शृगालः as subject pins the failure squarely on the fox by name. The tense stays present, आम्लम् stays an adjective, and the grapes are not made agents.

3. (C) यदि normally opens the conditional clause and pairs with तर्हि, whereas चेत् characteristically sits after a word within the clause; the faithful rewrite fronts यदि on the same न-लभ्यते condition. यदि does not mean 'sour', need not go last, and is not stacked with चेत्.

4. (D) The clause ties the label 'sour' strictly to not-obtaining, exposing that the judgement rides on failure rather than any taste. Fatigue, eating, and a 'therefore' reading all misread the conditional.

5. (B) The future is signalled by the -ष्य- element, not by word length; the past would need the augment अ-, which उत्पतिष्यति lacks. So it is neither past, present, nor imperative.

6. (A) Before the augment-vowel अ, the unvoiced त् of the prefix softens to voiced द्, yielding उद-अ-पतत् = उदपतत्. The prefix is not replaced, the root does not become पद्, and the त् does change.

7. (D) Present उत्पतति and future उत्पतिष्यति both carry उत् yet are not past, so the prefix is not a tense marker. Tense is fixed by endings/augment, not by उत्/उद्.

8. (A) लता is the climbing plant and फलम् is the fruit, so द्राक्षालता is the vine and द्राक्षाफलम् the grapes. The members are not swapped, not synonyms, and not taste-words.

9. (A) A तत्पुरुष resolves with a case relation between members; here the natural sense is 'the fruit of the grape(-vine)', a genitive. It is not a dvandva 'X and Y', not a bare identity, and not a verbal clause.

10. (B) The whole drama is about the fruit he cannot eat, so the verdict 'sour' applies to द्राक्षाफलम्. The vine can take an adjective, द्राक्षालता is the whole plant not a single grape, and it is the fruit that is out of reach.

11. (D) Reduplication in these forms signals the desiderative 'wishing to drink/eat', a desire-pattern, not plurality, past tense, or a directional prefix.

12. (A) The desiderative on दृश् yields 'wish to see' (the form दिदृक्षा), parallel to thirst and hunger. It is not a past tense, a direction adverb, or a plural.

13. (D) A desiderative means 'wish to [root-action]', so the root alone fixes whether the desire is to drink (thirst) or eat (hunger). The root carries neither number, direction, nor the feeling's tense.

14. (A) Choosing पिपासा spotlights thirst without ruling on hunger; the words name distinct desires. Foxes do feel hunger, the two words are not synonyms, and the choice implies nothing about a recent meal.

15. (C) श्रान्त/क्लान्त = physically worn out; खिन्न = low in spirit. They are not all 'sad', none means 'thirsty' or 'sour/sweet', and the body/mind labels are not reversed.

16. (D) खिन्नः alone is mental (dejection); श्रान्तः (fatigue) and पिपासायुक्तः (thirst) are bodily. So removing खिन्नः leaves only physical conditions.

17. (A) -युक्त adds 'endowed with', so the fox is 'endowed with thirst', i.e. still thirsty. It does not mean having drunk, losing thirst, or a direction.

18. (B) पृष्ठतः = behind and दक्षिणतः = on the right, so the coin is behind and the stone to the right. The other options scramble these fixed senses.

19. (C) The -तः form is a fixed direction adverb modifying the verb, while वामम् is a declined nominal. They are neither plural-of-each-other, verbs, nor freely interchangeable.

20. (D) Applying the तसिल् pattern gives a -तः adverb उपरितः. A dative, future-verb, or genitive ending would not produce a direction adverb.

21. (B) अपश्यत् carries the past augment अ- ('looked'), and वामतः is a direction adverb 'on/to the left' modifying the looking. It is neither present nor future, and वामतः is not the object, subject, or a time-word.

22. (B) दक्षिणतः = to the right, पृष्ठतः = behind. The other options swap these senses.

23. (D) By showing real, mounting effort first, the tale isolates the fault in the later excuse, not in any laziness. It does not show non-effort, prove sourness, or name directions.

24. (C) The fable faults only the sneering rationaliser, not the one who strives again. Attempting is not the fault, the persistent trainer is praised not condemned, and the sneer is dishonest, not honest.

25. (B) The exact parallel pairs real effort with a sour-grapes put-down of the goal. The other cases lack the effort, lack the failure, or lack the belittling excuse.

26. (C) The moral never forbids attempting hard things — striving can be noble. The other three correctly capture honesty about failure and the danger of prideful excuses.

27. (C) Speaking the excuse externalises the self-justifying habit at the heart of the lesson. It does not prove reachability, real sourness, or absent thirst.

28. (B) The general form makes the excuse a recognisable human pattern. It does not assert real sourness, narrate eating, or confine the point to grapes.

29. (B) The active counterpart of लभ्यते uses लभते ('obtains'). Eating (खादति), seeing (पश्यति), and wanting (इच्छति) are different roots and not what लभ् denies.

30. (A) The irony is that desirable (sweet) grapes are branded sour to excuse failure — verdict opposite to likely truth. He does not flatter them, report truly, or speak of colour.

31. (C) Word-final म् before a following consonant is regularly represented by anusvāra ं. It is not dropped, not turned to न्, and not doubled.

32. (A) The augment अ- gives past उदपतत्, plain -ति gives present उत्पतति, -ष्य- gives future उत्पतिष्यति, and -तु gives the imperative पततु. The other orderings misassign these markers.

33. (B) उत् adds 'up' to both पत् and गम्, showing prefixes are mobile meaning-adders across roots. They are not root-locked, not tense-changers, and not declined nouns.

34. (C) With उत् 'up', the verb means springing upward — the fox jumping for the grapes. It is not falling down, flying off, or sitting up.

35. (D) लोमशा means covered in hair/fur, which excludes a smooth hairless reptile while fitting the fox. It does not mean hairless, aquatic, or feathered.

36. (D) वने is the locative singular of वनम्, marking the location of the action ('in the forest'). It is not a dative, genitive, or plural.

37. (B) Agency drives protagonist-hood: the fox acts throughout; the vine merely is desired. The vine does appear, is not the fox, and does not speak.

38. (A) द्राक्षा is the grape, द्राक्षाफलम् the grape-fruit, द्राक्षालता the whole vine. The other rows scramble these senses.

39. (B) Past tense demands the augment अ- before the root; with the prefix, उत्+अ softens to उद, yielding उदपतत्. The prefix is not dropped, the root is unchanged, and -ति is not the past ending.

40. (A) Exertion → श्रान्तः; lack of drink → पिपासायुक्तः. The labels are not reversed, and neither traveller is 'sour' or specifically 'hungry'.

41. (A) Left opposes right and front opposes back, so वामतः/दक्षिणतः and अग्रतः/पृष्ठतः are the true pairs. The others mismatch axes.

42. (C) Prefixes (sense) and tense-markers (augment/endings) are distinct grammatical categories; conflating them is the error. उत् is not a tense marker on any root, and the form is present, not future.

43. (C) Both clauses misstate the lesson: it neither endorses early surrender nor sour-grapes insults. So there are two errors, not zero or one.

44. (C) Bodily exhaustion is exactly श्रान्तः. 'Sour', 'on the left', and 'only thirsty, never tired' do not fit.

45. (B) उदपतत् is PAST ('he leapt up'), so calling it present is the false statement. The other three are accurate.

46. (D) उत्पतिष्यति is indeed future (-ष्य-). उदपतत् is past not present, पिपासा is a desiderative noun not a past tense, and अग्रतः is an adverb not a dative noun.

47. (D) Only the first option gets all three right: the honest-failure moral, present उत्पतति, and पिपासा from पा. Each distractor breaks at least one of these.

48. (A) द्राक्षाफलम् is the object, न लभ्यते चेत् the condition, आम्लम् the verdict. The distractors misname all three.

49. (C) Wanting the goal and failing-despite-effort carry no moral fault; the fault is the after-the-fact dishonest downgrade. The episode is not blameworthy at every step.

50. (B) Honest admission (i) is fine; the dishonest 'sour' verdict (ii) is the target. The fable does not praise face-saving dismissal, nor condemn all speech or all reactions.

51. (A) In द्राक्षालता the second member लता is the head (it is a kind of vine), refuting a 'first-member-is-head' rule. It does not denote a grape, is not a coordinate 'grape and vine', and is genuinely a compound.

52. (D) All desideratives share the reduplicated 'wish-to' pattern but differ by their root-action; so a पठ्-desiderative shares the shape with पिपासा but names a different desire. It does not share पा's root, a direction, or a past augment.

53. (D) Sanskrit adjectives match their noun's gender, so the form shifts आम्लम् (neuter) → आम्ला (feminine). It is not a tense change, the म्→ं sandhi, or a -तः suffix.

54. (A) The pattern needs desire + failure + dishonest downgrade; the scrawny-excuse-after-failing cat matches it. Easy success, no desire, or an honest report after a real catch all break the pattern.

55. (D) An agentless passive can omit the doer, leaving the fox implicit — the very evasion the fable exposes. The passive need not name its agent, does not make grapes the doer, and लभ्यते is not active.

56. (B) The chapter's grammar centres on -तः adverbs and the desiderative/state words, and its moral on honest acceptance of failure. The other pairings name material foreign to this chapter.

57. (C) Only the first option is right throughout: श्रान्तः tired, present उत्पतति, passive न लभ्यते, आम्लम् 'sour', and the anti-excuse moral. Each distractor breaks the narrative, a tense label, the meaning of आम्लम्, or the moral.

58. (C) Desideratives express a present wish to perform an action, whereas श्रान्तः (a past participle in sense) marks an already-reached state of fatigue. They are not both conditions, not future actions, and the roles are not reversed.

59. (C) The moral lives in the spoken excuse; remove it and only blameless silent failure remains, with nothing to condemn. The fable never teaches against attempting, the -तः grammar is independent of the speech, and the jumping alone does not deliver the moral.

60. (D) The error flips न लभ्यते चेत् ('if NOT obtained') into 'because it WAS obtained' — inverting the very condition. The noun, the adjective 'sour', and the present tense are all rendered correctly.
