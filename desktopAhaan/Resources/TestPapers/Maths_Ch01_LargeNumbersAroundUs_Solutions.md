## Worked Solutions

**1. (C)** One crore = 1,00,00,000 = a 1 followed by **7** zeroes (10⁷). (A lakh has 5 zeroes; an arab has 9 — both are tempting near-misses.)

**2. (A)** 50000000 has 8 digits. Indian grouping is 3-2-2-2 from the right: 000 | 00 | 00 | 5 → 5,00,00,000 (five crore). Option D (50,000,000) is the International 3-3-3 grouping, and B/C have the wrong digit count.

**3. (D)** One million = 1,000,000 = 10,00,000 = **10 lakh**. The classic trap is treating a million and a lakh as equal — a million is ten times a lakh.

**4. (B)** Adding 1 to the largest 5-digit number: 99,999 + 1 = 1,00,000, the smallest 6-digit number (one lakh). 10,000 is only the smallest 5-digit number.

**5. (A)** Group 7,84,36,512 as 7 crore | 84 lakh | 36 thousand | 512. The digit 4 sits in the lakh place of "84 lakh," so it stands for **4 lakh**. (40 lakh would be the 8.)

**6. (D)** Three crore = 3,00,00,000; five lakh = 5,00,000; seven thousand = 7,000; two hundred = 200. Sum = 3,05,07,200. Option C (3,05,70,200) misplaces the thousand digit; B inserts an extra lakh.

**7. (B)** Strip the commas: 90706005. Re-group International 3-3-3 from the right: 005 | 706 | 90 → 90,706,005. The digit string is identical to the Indian 9,07,06,005 — only the comma positions change.

**8. (C)** The smallest 8-digit number is 1 followed by seven zeroes = 1,00,00,000 (one crore). Option B (10,00,00,000) is 9 digits; A is 7 digits.

**9. (A)** In 6,53,000 the 6 is in the lakh place → place value 6,00,000; the 3 is in the thousand place → 3,000. Difference = 6,00,000 − 3,000 = 5,97,000. (Subtracting bare digits 6 − 3 = 3 gives the trap 3,00,000.)

**10. (D)** The Indian convention puts the first comma after 3 digits from the right, then after every 2 digits: **3, then 2, then 2, then 2, …**. The 3-3-3 pattern (option A) is the International system.

**11. (C)** One arab = 100 crore, and one crore = 100 lakh, so one arab = 100 × 100 = **10,000 lakh**. (100 lakh is only one crore.)

**12. (B)** One billion = 10⁹ = 100 crore = 100 × 100 lakh = **10,000 lakh**. Estu's "one hundred lakh" equals only one crore, off by a factor of 100.

**13. (A)** Convert all to plain numbers: 9 lakh = 9,00,000; 1 million = 10,00,000; 90 lakh = 90,00,000; and 9,00,001. The smallest is **9 lakh** = 9,00,000 (just one less than 9,00,001).

**14. (B)** 4 × 10,00,000 = 40,00,000; 7 × 10,000 = 70,000; 3 × 100 = 300; plus 5. Sum = 40,70,305. Note the hundred-thousands and ten-thousands places, with zeros elsewhere.

**15. (B)** Starting from 0, each +1000 press adds 1000. To reach 53,000 you need 53,000 ÷ 1000 = **53** presses.

**16. (A)** Each +100 press adds 100. One lakh = 1,00,000, so 1,00,000 ÷ 100 = **1,000** presses. (This also shows 1,000 hundreds make a lakh.)

**17. (C)** Fewest presses = place-value digits of 40,629: 4 + 0 + 6 + 2 + 9 = **21**. The press-counts in each slot are exactly the digits.

**18. (D)** 8,300 − (7 × 1000) = 8,300 − 7,000 = 1,300, which needs 1,300 ÷ 100 = **13** presses of +100. (This is the valid non-standard decomposition 7×1000 + 13×100.)

**19. (A)** 3,07,250 has place-value digits (lakh → ones): 3, 0, 7, 2, 5, 0. The minimal press-count in each slot equals these digits, so **3, 0, 7, 2, 5, 0**. Option B drops the zero in the ten-thousand slot.

**20. (C)** Check each: A = 5000 + 70 + 2 = 5072 ✓; B = 5000 + 72 = 5072 ✓; D = 5000 + 60 + 12 = 5072 ✓; **C** = 4000 + 1000 + 70 = 5070 ≠ 5072. So C does NOT make 5,072.

**21. (D)** Group 12,78,830 as 12 lakh | 78 thousand | 830 → **twelve lakh seventy-eight thousand eight hundred thirty**. Option C wrongly says "eight hundred three"; A and B regroup the digits incorrectly.

**22. (A)** 6,72,85,183 = 6 crore | 72 lakh | 85 thousand | 183. In "72 lakh," the 7 occupies the ten-lakh place, so the answer is **7**. (The 2 is the lakh place.)

**23. (C)** In 5,35,000 the first 5 is in the lakh place (place value 5,00,000); the second 5 is in the thousand place (place value 5,000). Difference = 5,00,000 − 5,000 = **4,95,000**.

**24. (B)** "Ten crore twenty lakh thirty thousand forty" = 10,20,30,040. Strip commas: 102030040. Re-group International 3-3-3: 040 | 030 | 102 → **102,030,040**.

**25. (B)** 10,00,000 (Indian) and 1,000,000 (International) are the **same number, ten lakh** (= one million). The comma positions and spoken names differ, but the value does not. Options A and C wrongly claim one is ten times the other.

**26. (A)** One lakh = 1,00,000. So 1,00,000 − 75,000 = **25,000**. (15,000 would be the gap to 90,000; 1,25,000 wrongly adds instead of subtracting.)

**27. (D)** Nearest ten-thousand of 4,87,650: the thousands portion is 7,650, which is ≥ 5,000, so round the ten-thousands place up: 4,80,000 → 4,90,000. Answer **4,90,000**.

**28. (C)** Nearest thousand of 3,87,69,957: look at the last 3 digits, 957 ≥ 500, so round up: 3,87,69,000 → **3,87,70,000**.

**29. (A)** Total needed = 732 + 23 = 755, and she must never run short, so round **up to 800**. Ordering exactly 755 leaves no margin; rounding down (700 or 750) risks shortage.

**30. (B)** Quoting ₹470 as "about ₹450" lowers the figure, so it is rounding **down**, done to seem generous (and because customers like round numbers). It is the nearest fifty, not a "round up."

**31. (C)** Nearest lakh of 6,72,85,183: the last 5 digits form 85,183, which is ≥ 50,000, so round the lakhs up: 6,72,00,000 → **6,73,00,000**.

**32. (D)** 4,63,128 + 4,19,682 = 8,82,810 — near 8,00,000 and just above it, confirming Roxie. Answer **8,82,810**. (8,72,810 drops a carry; 9,82,810 over-carries.)

**33. (A)** A round "about one lakh" **conveys the size with less effort and no real loss** of meaning — the exact 1,02,455 isn't needed at the dinner table. The exact figure isn't "wrong"; rounding is a deliberate, honest approximation.

**34. (B)** Nearest crore of 8,49,21,000: the part below the crore is 49,21,000, which is < 50,00,000, so round **down**: **8,00,00,000**. (Reading 49,21,000 as "almost a crore" and rounding up to 9 crore is the trap.)

**35. (C)** Numbers that round to 7,00,000 at the nearest lakh lie in [6,50,000, 7,49,999]. Checking: 7,51,000 → 8 lakh; 6,40,000 → 6 lakh; 7,62,000 → 8 lakh; only **7,48,200** rounds to 7,00,000.

**36. (B)** 116 × 5 = 116 × (10 ÷ 2) = (116 × 10) ÷ 2 = 1160 ÷ 2 = **580**. (Forgetting to halve gives the trap 1,160.)

**37. (D)** 824 × 25 = 824 × (100 ÷ 4) = 82400 ÷ 4 = **20,600**. (Stopping at 82,400 forgets the ÷4; 8,240 divides by 10 instead.)

**38. (A)** 72 × 125 = 72 × (1000 ÷ 8) = 72000 ÷ 8 = **9,000**. (576 is just 72 × 8, the wrong operation.)

**39. (D)** A 3-digit × 3-digit product has (3+3−1) or (3+3) = **5 or 6** digits. Smallest 100 × 100 = 10,000 (5 digits); largest 999 × 999 = 998,001 (6 digits). Indeed 234 × 567 = 1,32,678 has 6 digits — within the range.

**40. (B)** 2-digit × 2-digit: smallest 10 × 10 = 100 (3 digits), largest 99 × 99 = 9,801 (4 digits). So the product has **3 or 4 digits** — never exactly 4 only.

**41. (C)** The smallest 3-digit × 3-digit product uses the smallest inputs: 100 × 100 = **10,000**. (9,801 is 99 × 99, the largest 2-digit case — a tempting mix-up.)

**42. (D)** m-digit × n-digit gives (m+n−1) or (m+n) digits. Here 12 + 13 = 25, so the answer has **24 or 25** digits. (156 wrongly multiplies 12 × 13.)

**43. (B)** 8,000 × 7,000 = 8 × 7 × 1000 × 1000 = 56 × 10⁶ = 56,000,000 — which has **8** digits. (Using the m+n rule: 4 + 4 = 8 here.)

**44. (D)** Roxie is **wrong; the smallest such product is 10,000**. The least 3-digit × 3-digit product is 100 × 100 = 10,000 (already 5 digits), so a 4-digit result is impossible. (Options A and B illegally use a 2-digit or 1-digit factor.)

**45. (A)** The shortcut works **because 5 is half of 10**: multiplying by 5 equals multiplying by 10 (easy) then halving. Option C is false (×10 makes a number ten times bigger), and dividing by 2 is not the same as multiplying by 2.

**46. (C)** 1 lakh buses × 50 people each = 1,00,000 × 50 = 50,00,000 = **50 lakh**. (5 lakh divides instead of placing the zero; 5 crore over-counts by ten.)

**47. (B)** Seconds in a day = 24 × 60 × 60 = 86,400. At one number per second you reach **about 86,400** — far short of a million. (One lakh, 1,00,000, is close but still more than 86,400.)

**48. (D)** 100 km/day × 365 days × 10 years = 3,65,000 km. The Moon is 3,84,400 km away, so she falls about 19,400 km short — **3,65,000 km — just short**.

**49. (C)** 5,000 ships × 2,500 passengers = 5000 × 2500 = 1,25,00,000 (1 crore 25 lakh). Answer **1,25,00,000**. (12,50,000 drops a zero.)

**50. (D)** 75 beats/min × 60 min × 24 h = 1,08,000 ≈ **about 1,00,000** (one lakh) beats a day. (10,000 and 4,500 are far too small; 10,00,000 is ten times too big.)

**51. (A)** A reasonable Fermi assumption matches everyday experience: **one barbershop serves about 5,000 people in a locality**. "1,000 haircuts a day" is impossible (each takes ~30 min); a bus holding 5,000 people and a city with exactly one barbershop are absurd.

**52. (B)** 500 lakhs = 5,00,00,000 = 5 crore = 50 million — which is far more than 5 million. So **500 lakhs is bigger (it is 5 crore)**. They are not equal; the trap is reading "500 lakh" as "5 million."

**53. (C)** 640 crore = 640 × 10⁷ = 6.4 × 10⁹ = 6.4 billion, which is less than 60 billion. So **60 billion (640 crore is only 6.4 billion)** is bigger. (Reading 640 crore as 64 billion mis-scales by a factor of ten.)

**54. (A)** As plain numbers: 90,000; 9 lakh = 9,00,000; 1 million = 10,00,000; 1 crore = 1,00,00,000. Increasing order: **90,000 < 9 lakh < 1 million < 1 crore**.

**55. (C)** One crore = 1,00,00,000. Increase = 1,00,00,000 − 5,00,000 = **95,00,000 litres**. (50,00,000 would be the gap from 50,00,000, not 5,00,000.)

**56. (B)** The smallest 9-digit number is 1 followed by eight zeroes = 10,00,00,000; the number just before it is 9,99,99,999 (the largest 8-digit number). So **10,00,00,000 and 9,99,99,999**.

**57. (D)** 12 lakh = 12,00,000 and 8 crore = 8,00,00,000. Fraction = 12,00,000 / 8,00,00,000 = 12 / 800 = **3/200**. (1/80 = 10,00,000/8,00,00,000, a near-miss from rounding 12 to 10.)

**58. (A)** 1 lakh buses × 50 = 50,00,000 = 50 lakh capacity. Mumbai ≈ 1 crore 24 lakh = 124 lakh people. 50 / 124 ≈ 0.40, so **about 40%** — the buses hold only part of the city.

**59. (C)** Compare: 1,00,01,000 (just over one crore) vs 99,99,000 (under one crore) → 1,00,01,000 > 99,99,000 ✓. The others are false: 7,05,000 < 70,50,000; 9,99,999 < 10,00,000; 8 lakh < 8 million.

**60. (D)** 49,873 to the nearest ten-thousand: the thousands portion 9,873 ≥ 5,000, so round up → 50,000. But "about a lakh" (1,00,000) is roughly double the true ~50,000, so **nearest ten-thousand is 50,000; "about a lakh" overstates by roughly double**.
