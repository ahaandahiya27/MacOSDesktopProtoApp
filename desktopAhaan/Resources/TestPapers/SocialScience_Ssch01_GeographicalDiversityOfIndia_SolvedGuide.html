Set 1 · Q1–Q10

Q1

India is called a "subcontinent" mainly because it is:

  • Aa separate continent entirely cut off from Asia
  • Bthe smallest country in Asia
  • Ca large, distinct landmass that is part of Asia but set apart by natural features✓
  • Dan island lying in the middle of the Indian Ocean

Working

✓

A "subcontinent" is a large, distinct landmass that is part of a continent but set apart from the rest by natural features. India is part of Asia, yet the Himalayas, the Thar and Arabian Sea, and the surrounding oceans wall it off — so it qualifies. It is NOT a separate continent (A) nor cut off entirely, and it is one of the largest, not the smallest (B), countries in Asia.

Q2

A geographer lists India's five physiographic regions. Which of the following does NOT belong on that list?

  • Athe great mountain zone of the Himalayas
  • Ba frozen polar continent✓
  • Cthe desert region
  • Dthe islands

Working

✓

The five physiographic regions are the Himalayan mountain zone, the Northern Plains, the desert region, the peninsula, and the islands — all real divisions geographers use (A, C, D). A "frozen polar continent" is not part of India at all, so it is the odd one out.

Q3

Climbing from the southern plains up to the highest snow peaks, in what order do you cross the three Himalayan ranges?

  • AHimadri → Himachal → Shivalik
  • BHimachal → Shivalik → Himadri
  • CHimadri → Shivalik → Himachal
  • DShivalik → Himachal → Himadri✓

Working

✓

Going UP from the southern plains you first meet the lowest hills (Shivalik), then the gentler middle range (Himachal), then the highest snow range (Himadri): Shivalik → Himachal → Himadri. Option (A) is the reverse — the order you would meet them coming DOWN from the peaks.

Q4

The Himalayas are still growing taller today. The best reason is that:

  • Athe Indian landmass keeps pushing north into Asia at about 5 cm a year✓
  • Bglaciers add new rock to the peaks each winter
  • Crivers pile up silt on the mountain tops
  • Dthe monsoon rains make the soil swell

Working

✓

India is still drifting north into Asia at about 5 cm a year, so the land keeps folding upward and the peaks keep rising. Glaciers (B) erode rock rather than add it; silt (C) builds the plains, not the summits; rain (D) does not lift mountains.

Q5

Ravi insists Ladakh cannot be a desert "because deserts are hot and Ladakh is freezing." His mistake is that:

  • ALadakh actually has heavy rainfall
  • Ba desert is defined by very low rainfall, not by heat✓
  • CLadakh lies below sea level
  • Ddeserts must always be covered in sand dunes

Working

✓

A desert is defined by very LOW rainfall, not by heat. Ladakh gets almost no rain (it lies in the Himalayan rain shadow), so it is a cold desert. Ravi wrongly assumes "desert = hot." Ladakh does not have heavy rain (A) nor lie below sea level (C), and not all deserts are sandy (D).

Q6

Which natural features together act as India's "walls" to the north, west, and south?

  • Athe Aravallis, the Deccan Plateau, and the Ganga
  • Bthe Eastern Ghats, the Thar, and Chilika Lake
  • Cthe Himalayas, the Thar Desert with the Arabian Sea, and the Indian Ocean with the Bay of Bengal✓
  • Dthe Shivaliks, the Sundarbans, and the Lakshadweep islands

Working

✓

India's natural walls are the Himalayas to the north, the Thar Desert with the Arabian Sea to the west, and the Indian Ocean with the Bay of Bengal to the south and east. Option (A) lists interior features (Aravallis, Deccan, Ganga) that do not frame the whole country.

Q7

The Northern Plains can support a very large population mainly because:

  • AHimalayan rivers spread deep, mineral-rich alluvial soil over flat, well-watered land✓
  • Bthey are made of hardened volcanic lava
  • Cthey sit at a very high, cool altitude
  • Dthey are ringed by coral reefs that protect the farms

Working

✓

Himalayan rivers slow on the flat land and drop deep, mineral-rich alluvium year after year; level, well-watered, fertile ground supports a huge population. The plains are alluvial, not volcanic lava (B); they are low and warm, not high and cool (C); and they are inland, not ringed by reefs (D).

Q8

Seashell fossils are sometimes found high in the Himalayan rock. This is best explained by the fact that:

  • Aancient people carried shells up the mountains
  • Bthe floor of an old sea (the Tethys) was folded upward when India collided with Asia✓
  • Cglaciers froze fish into the rock
  • Dthe shells fell from passing birds over millions of years

Working

✓

Before the Himalayas existed, the Tethys Sea lay between India and Asia. When India collided, the old sea floor — with its shells — was squeezed and folded high into the rock. People (A), frozen fish (C) and birds (D) cannot explain marine fossils kilometres above any ocean.

Q9

Trace the chain that links a slow plate collision to the rich soil of the Northern Plains. Which ordering is correct?

  • Acollision raises the Himalayas → rivers grind the rock → rivers carry silt south → silt settles on the flat plains as fertile alluvium✓
  • Brivers carry silt south → collision raises the Himalayas → silt settles → rock grinds away
  • Csilt settles on the plains → the Himalayas rise → rivers form → collision happens
  • Dthe Himalayas erode away completely → a sea floods the plains → silt sinks → rock rises

Working

✓

The cause-effect chain runs: the collision raises the Himalayas → rivers grind down the young rock → rivers carry the silt south → the silt settles on the flat plains as fertile alluvium. The other orderings scramble cause and effect (e.g. B has silt moving before the mountains exist).

Q10

Which pairing of a Himalayan range with its character is correct?

  • AHimadri → lowest hills with dense forests
  • BHimachal → snow-covered all year with the giant peaks
  • CShivalik → moderate climate with famous hill stations
  • DHimachal → moderate climate with hill stations like Shimla and Darjeeling✓

Working

✓

The Himachal (Lower Himalayas) has a moderate climate and the hill stations such as Shimla and Darjeeling. (A) wrongly gives the Himadri the lowest hills (it is the highest, snow-covered range); (B) and (C) swap the ranges' characters.

Q11

The Himalayas are nicknamed the "Water Tower of Asia" because they:

  • Acollect monsoon rain in giant natural reservoirs of stone
  • Bstore water as snow and ice and release it in summer to feed rivers like the Ganga, Indus and Brahmaputra✓
  • Cpump sea water uphill from the Bay of Bengal
  • Dare the only place in India where it ever rains

Working

✓

The Himalayas store winter snow and ice and release it slowly in summer, feeding the Ganga, Indus and Brahmaputra all year — hence "Water Tower of Asia." They do not pump sea water (C), and they are far from the only rainy part of India (D).

Q12

The Aravalli Hills are low and rounded today, whereas the Himalayas are towering and jagged. The reason is that:

  • Athe Aravallis are far younger and have not yet risen
  • Bthe Himalayas are made of much harder rock
  • Cthe Aravallis are among the oldest ranges and billions of years of erosion have worn them down✓
  • Dthe Aravallis were never tall to begin with

Working

✓

The taller-means-older idea is backwards. The Aravallis are among the OLDEST ranges (about 2.5 billion years) and billions of years of erosion have worn them low; the Himalayas are YOUNG and still rising. So (A) reverses their ages, and rock hardness (B) is not the reason.

Q13

If the Aravalli Hills were completely flattened by mining, the most likely consequence would be that:

  • Athe Thar Desert's sand would spread further east toward cities like Delhi✓
  • Bthe Himalayas would stop growing
  • Cthe Ganga would change direction
  • DIndia would lose its western coastline

Working

✓

The Aravallis run as a ridge along the eastern edge of the Thar and physically slow the wind-blown sand. Remove them and the desert's sand would spread further east toward Delhi. They have nothing to do with the Himalayas' growth (B), the Ganga's course (C), or the west coast (D).

Q14

People of the Thar Desert scrub their utensils with sand and store rainwater in tanks called taanka or kunds. These customs arise mainly because:

  • Asand is believed to make utensils shinier than water
  • Bthe law forbids the use of river water there
  • Cthe nights are too cold to wash with water
  • Dwater is extremely scarce, so every drop must be saved✓

Working

✓

In the Thar, water is extremely scarce, so people scour utensils with sand and store rainwater in taanka/kunds — every drop is saved. Sand is not used for shine (A), there is no such law (B), and the customs are about saving water, not avoiding cold nights (C).

Q15

Which statement correctly contrasts the Western Ghats with the Eastern Ghats?

  • ABoth are equally tall, unbroken walls of the same rock
  • BThe Eastern Ghats are taller and unbroken; the Western Ghats are low and broken
  • CThe Western Ghats are taller and almost unbroken; the Eastern Ghats are lower and broken by rivers✓
  • DThe Western Ghats lie on the east coast and the Eastern Ghats on the west coast

Working

✓

The Western Ghats are taller and almost unbroken, running like a wall near the west coast; the Eastern Ghats are lower and broken into chunks where east-flowing rivers cut through them. (B) swaps them, (A) treats them as identical, and (D) puts each on the wrong coast.

Q16

Most major rivers of the Deccan Plateau — the Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri — empty into the Bay of Bengal in the east because:

  • Athe plateau tilts slightly toward the east, and rivers flow downhill along the slope✓
  • Bthe Arabian Sea is too salty for rivers to enter
  • Cthe Bay of Bengal is at a higher level and pulls the water in
  • Dthe Western Ghats are lower than the Eastern Ghats

Working

✓

The Deccan tilts slightly to the east, and rivers flow downhill along the slope — so the Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri run east into the Bay of Bengal. Saltiness (B) does not block rivers, the Bay is not higher (C), and the Ghats' heights do not set river direction (D).

Q17

Southern India is called a "peninsular plateau." The two ideas in that phrase are that it is:

  • Aflat at sea level and surrounded by mountains on all four sides
  • Ba sandy desert AND a chain of islands
  • Ca river delta AND a mangrove forest
  • Da raised, flat-topped landform AND land surrounded by water on three sides✓

Working

✓

"Peninsular plateau" packs two ideas: a plateau (a raised, flat-topped landform) and a peninsula (land surrounded by water on three sides). Southern India is both. The other options describe deserts, deltas or sea-level flats, not this landform.

Q18

The East Coast has wide river deltas (Godavari, Krishna), but the West Coast mostly does not. The geographical reason is that:

  • Athe West Coast gets no rain at all
  • Blong east-flowing rivers carry lots of sediment into the calm Bay of Bengal, while short, swift west-flowing rivers carry little into the open Arabian Sea✓
  • Cthe East Coast has no rivers, only lakes
  • Ddeltas can form only where the sea is salty

Working

✓

Long east-flowing peninsular rivers gather lots of sediment and meet the calm Bay of Bengal, so they build big deltas; short, swift west-flowing rivers carry little sediment into the open Arabian Sea, so they form estuaries instead. The West Coast does have rivers and rain (A, C), and deltas are not about saltiness (D).

Q19

A delta and an estuary are NOT the same. The key difference is that:

  • Aa delta forms in the mountains while an estuary forms in a desert
  • Ban estuary is always larger than a delta
  • Ca delta is fertile land built up where a river drops heavy sediment in a calm sea, while an estuary is a funnel-shaped mouth where fresh and salt water mix without building much land✓
  • Da delta is salty and an estuary is fresh

Working

✓

A delta is fertile land BUILT UP where a river drops heavy sediment into a calm sea; an estuary is a funnel-shaped mouth where fresh and salt water mix without building much land. They are not split by mountain-vs-desert (A), by size (B), or by being salty-vs-fresh (D).

Q20

Which pairing of India's two island groups with how they formed is CORRECT?

  • ALakshadweep — coral; Andaman & Nicobar — volcanic✓
  • BLakshadweep — volcanic; Andaman & Nicobar — coral

  • DBoth groups are volcanic islands

Lakshadweep (Arabian Sea) is built of coral; the Andaman & Nicobar Islands (Bay of Bengal) are volcanic. Option (B) reverses this — the single most common island mix-up — and (C), (D) wrongly make both groups the same type.

Q24

The hills of the Northeast (Garo, Khasi, Jaintia) receive some of the highest rainfall on Earth because:

Moist monsoon winds hitting the Northeast hills are forced to rise sharply, cool, and dump heavy rain (orographic rainfall), making the region among the wettest on Earth. The hills are high, not low (B); rivers do not flow uphill (C); and the sea is not on top of them (D).

Q27

Why do geographers group a huge country like India into physical regions rather than treating it as one flat space?

Q28

The three Himalayan ranges have three different climates. The main reason is that:

  • Athey were formed in three different centuries
  • Bonly the lowest range ever gets sunlight
  • Ceach range is made of a different colour of rock
  • Das you go higher the air gets colder and thinner, so each range sits at a different temperature✓

Working

✓

As you go higher the air gets colder and thinner, so the three ranges sit at three different temperatures — hence three climates, plants and settlement patterns. They were not built in different centuries (A), all get sunlight (B), and rock colour (C) does not set climate.

Q29

Why do Himalayan rivers like the Ganga keep flowing even in the dry season, while many peninsular rivers shrink?

  • Athe Ganga is shorter, so it dries up more slowly

  • Cpeninsular rivers are blocked by the Aravallis
  • Dthe Ganga is fed by the sea

Working

✓

Himalayan rivers are fed by melting snow and glaciers that release water even in the dry season (perennial), while many peninsular rivers depend on the rains and shrink afterward. Length (A) and the Aravallis (C) are irrelevant, and the Ganga is fed by the mountains, not the sea (D).

Q30

"Perennial" rivers and "rain-fed" rivers differ in that:

  • Aperennial rivers flow all year (snow-fed), while rain-fed rivers swell in the rains and shrink in the dry season✓
  • Bperennial rivers exist only in deserts
  • Crain-fed rivers never carry any water
  • Dperennial rivers flow only during the monsoon

Working

✓

Perennial rivers flow all year because snow and glaciers keep feeding them; rain-fed rivers swell during the rains and shrink in the dry season. The other options misdescribe perennial rivers as desert-only, monsoon-only, or claim rain-fed rivers never carry water.
