# Sanskrit — Chapter 12: वीराङ्गना पन्नाधाया (Panna Dhai, the Brave) · Paper 2 (Advanced) Solutions

**1. (C)** The infinitive of गम् ('go') is **गन्तुम्** ('to go'). गच्छति is the present, अगच्छत् the past.

**2. (A)** The infinitive of दृश् ('see') is **द्रष्टुम्** ('to see').

**3. (D)** The infinitive of पठ् ('read') is **पठितुम्** ('to read').

**4. (B)** The infinitive of रक्ष् ('protect') is **रक्षितुम्** ('to protect') — exactly what Panna did for the prince.

**5. (B)** Every Sanskrit infinitive ends in **-तुम्** (the तुमुन् suffix): गन्तुम्, द्रष्टुम्, मारयितुम्, etc.

**6. (D)** The infinitive translates as **'to X'** and usually expresses purpose ('in order to ...').

**7. (A)** मारयितुम् expresses the assassin's **purpose** — he came 'in order to kill' — not that he succeeded.

**8. (C)** **कर्तुम्** ('to do') is the infinitive; अमारयत्, मारयति and करोति are all finite verbs.

**9. (A)** 'To kill' (infinitive) is **मारयितुम्** (matching the past अमारयत् 'he killed').

**10. (C)** अमारयत् is a **finite past tense** ('he killed'); मारयितुम् is the **infinitive** ('to kill').

**11. (D)** The initial अ- in अमारयत् is the **past-tense augment (लङ्)**, not a negative.

**12. (B)** **सः जलम् पातुम् इच्छति** ('he wishes to drink water') correctly pairs a main verb (इच्छति) with the infinitive of purpose (पातुम्).

**13. (D)** The infinitive of कृ ('do') is **कर्तुम्** ('to do').

**14. (B)** The infinitive of दा ('give') is **दातुम्** ('to give').

**15. (C)** The infinitive of ज्ञा ('know') is **ज्ञातुम्** ('to know').

**16. (A)** राजगृह = राज्ञः गृहम् — **the king's house, i.e. the palace**.

**17. (C)** राजपुत्र = राज्ञः पुत्रः — **the king's son, i.e. the prince**.

**18. (A)** राजगृह names a **place (palace)** while राजपुत्र names a **person (prince)** — they must not be swapped.

**19. (B)** शयनागारम् (शयन 'sleeping' + आगारम् 'room') literally means a **bedroom**.

**20. (D)** दुष्टबुद्धि = दुष्ट ('wicked') + बुद्धि ('intellect') = **evil-minded**.

**21. (A)** दुष्टबुद्धि means evil-minded, NOT merely **foolish (मूर्ख)** — it is a moral, not an intellectual, fault.

**22. (D)** The prefix कु- means **'bad'**, so कुतन्त्रम् is an evil scheme.

**23. (B)** The prefix सु- means **'good/well'**, so सुतन्त्रम् would be a good scheme.

**24. (C)** सर्वस्वत्याग = सर्वस्व ('one's everything') + त्याग ('giving up') = **the giving-up of one's everything**.

**25. (B)** वीराङ्गना = वीरा ('brave') + अङ्गना ('woman') = **a brave woman / heroine**.

**26. (C)** छलेन is the instrumental of छल, meaning **'by deception'**.

**27. (A)** The -एन ending marks the **instrumental** case ('by/with').

**28. (D)** स्वर्णिमाक्षरैः (golden + letters, instrumental plural) literally means **'in golden letters'**.

**29. (D)** A name written स्वर्णिमाक्षरैः is an idiom for being **permanently honoured in history**.

**30. (A)** The synonym given for धाया is **धात्री** (foster-mother / nurse).

**31. (C)** धाया (आ) vs धैर्य (ऐ) shows that **vowel quality distinguishes whole words** in Sanskrit.

**32. (B)** सर्वस्वम् (स्वीयं सम्पूर्णम्) means **'one's complete own — all that one has'**.

**33. (C)** For Panna, सर्वस्वत्याग meant giving up **her own son's life**.

**34. (B)** The one who died was **Panna's own son**, placed in the prince's bed.

**35. (D)** 'Panna gave up her own life' is **wrong — she survived; it was her son who died** in the prince's place.

**36. (A)** The prince Panna protected became the Maharana of **Mewar** (Udai Singh II).

**37. (A)** षोडशे शतके places the events in the **16th century (the 1500s)**.

**38. (B)** षोडश is the number **sixteen**.

**39. (D)** शतक (from शत 'hundred') is a span of about **one hundred years — a century**.

**40. (C)** The 16th century corresponds to the years **1501–1600 CE**.

**41. (D)** The infinitive मारयितुम् marks only **what was attempted/intended** (the purpose), not that the killing of the prince succeeded.

**42. (C)** Panna is praised because she chose **loyalty and duty over her personal loss**.

**43. (A)** The contrast is **treachery (छल) versus loyal sacrifice (त्याग)**.

**44. (B)** त्याग means **giving up / renunciation**.

**45. (B)** वीराङ्गनासु is the locative plural, **'among the brave women'**.

**46. (D)** **राजपुत्रम् रक्षितुम्** ('to protect the prince') correctly uses the accusative object with the infinitive of purpose.

**47. (C)** राजगृह is a तत्पुरुष meaning **'the king's (genitive) house'** — the first member stands in a hidden genitive relation.

**48. (A)** कुतन्त्रम् and सुतन्त्रम् differ only in their **prefix — कु- ('bad') vs सु- ('good')**.

**49. (A)** If छलेन is 'by deception', then सत्येन (instrumental of सत्य) is **'by truth'**.

**50. (D)** The moral is **supreme sacrifice and loyalty to duty**.

**51. (C)** The correct pair is **शयनागारम् = 'bedroom'**. मारयितुम् is 'to kill' (not 'he killed'); राजगृह is the palace (not the prince); धाया is the nurse (not 'courage').

**52. (B)** The correct statement: **मारयितुम् is an infinitive ('to kill'); अमारयत् is the past ('he killed')**.

**53. (C)** The correct split is **राजपुत्र = king's son (prince)**. राजगृह is the palace, दुष्टबुद्धि is evil-minded, सर्वस्वत्याग is giving up everything.

**54. (D)** The correct prefixes: **कु- = 'bad'; सु- = 'good'**.

**55. (B)** The correct detail: **the story is set in the 16th century in Mewar**.

**56. (A)** The substitution succeeded because the assassin **struck the boy in the prince's bed, mistaking him for the prince**.

**57. (B)** राजपुत्रम् द्रष्टुम् आगच्छत् means someone came **'to see the prince'** (द्रष्टुम् = infinitive of purpose).

**58. (A)** The single word मारयितुम् packs the whole English phrase **'to kill'**.

**59. (C)** पन्नाधाया is honoured स्वर्णिमाक्षरैः because she **sacrificed her own son to save the future king**.

**60. (D)** Panna gave up her own **SON's** life (she herself survived), and मारयितुम् is the **infinitive 'to kill'**, not a past tense.
