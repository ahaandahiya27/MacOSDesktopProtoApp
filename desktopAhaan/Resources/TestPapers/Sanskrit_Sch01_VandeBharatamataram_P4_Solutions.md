## Worked Solutions

**1. (C)** वन्दे ends in the first-person-singular middle ending and means 'I salute/revere'; the doer 'I' is encoded in the verb, making it an active statement performed by the speaker. The wish-reading 'may ... be saluted' would need an optative form, and the passive 'be saluted' would need a passive stem with the Mother as grammatical subject — neither is present. The claim that वन्दे carries no person is false, since the ending itself marks first-person singular. It is also not an imperative addressed to the Mother, because the Mother is the accusative object मातरम्, not the addressee.

**2. (A)** The chapter glosses वन्दे as 'I salute/bow/revere', built on the root for salutation, which is lexically distinct from any verb of affection; hence the register is reverence. Treating वन्दे as 'love' confuses two different roots. It does not mean 'look at'. And Sanskrit does keep reverence and affection in separate verbs, so they are not interchangeable.

**3. (D)** वन्दे, a finite transitive verb, takes its object in the accusative, while नमः, an indeclinable, can be paired with a dative meaning 'homage to'. So the lesson is that government attaches to the individual word, not to the gloss 'salute'. That refutes the idea that every 'salute' word must take the accusative. Case-government is not decided by the previous word's case. And वन्दे is a verb, not an indeclinable, so the claim that neither governs a case is false.

**4. (C)** A transitive verb can take coordinated objects joined by च, and each remains in the accusative as the thing acted upon; गङ्गाम् and हिमालयम् are both accusative singular. There is no rule capping वन्दे at one object, so the second is not nominative. च merely coordinates and does not convert anything to instrumental or genitive.

**5. (C)** The chapter distinguishes नमः (an indeclinable that pairs with the dative of the one honoured) from the verb वन्दे (which takes an accusative object). So 'homage to the Motherland' wants a dative, and forcing an accusative because वन्दे does so is the error. नमः is not a verb that governs the accusative. Genitive would mean 'of', and instrumental would mean 'by means of' — neither expresses 'homage to'.

**6. (D)** A finite verb like वन्दे encodes person, number and action, so 'I salute' is a whole clause. मातरम् is a case-form of a noun and supplies no verb, hence cannot stand alone. The roles are not reversed (मातरम् is not a verb). Not every single word is a complete sentence, and there is no three-word minimum in Sanskrit.

**7. (B)** मातरम्, सुजलाम् and सुफलाम् are all feminine accusative singular agreeing as the object of वन्दे; माता, सुजला and सुफला are the nominative forms. So one and the same shift — accusative back to nominative — accounts for every word. They were not given three different errors, nor turned instrumental, and the adjectives are not exempt: agreement forces them into the accusative too.

**8. (B)** Case is a role: वन्दे, being transitive, requires its object to take the accusative (the patient/direct-object role), and the ending merely signals that role. Defining the accusative as 'whatever follows वन्दे' confuses marker with cause. Position alone does not assign case in Sanskrit, which has relatively free word order. And the accusative is genuinely distinct from the nominative, so वन्दे does impose a real requirement.

**9. (B)** Among ऋ-stems, kinship nouns (मातृ, पितृ) use the गुण grade giving मातरम्, पितरम्, while agent/doer nouns (दातृ, कर्तृ) use the वृद्धि grade giving दातारम्, कर्तारम् in strong cases. So the contrast is a kinship-vs-agent distinction, not a misprint. It is not purely gender-based, since some masculine kinship stems (पितृ) are also short. And दातृ is indeed an ऋ-stem, so the comparison stands.

**10. (C)** दुहितृ is a kinship ऋ-stem patterning exactly like मातृ: accusative singular दुहितरम् (cf. मातरम्) and genitive singular दुहितुः (cf. मातुः). The forms with -त्रम्/-त्रस्य wrongly drop into a different stem class. दुहिता is the nominative, not the accusative, and दुहितृम् is not a valid genitive. The lengthened दुहितारम् belongs to the agent-noun pattern, not to kinship stems.

**11. (C)** For ऋ-stems the strong stem (मातर-) appears before the accusative-singular -म्, yielding मातरम्; मातृम् wrongly attaches -म् to the unchanged bare stem. So both forms are not equivalent — मातृम् is simply not a valid form. The case is right (वन्दे does want the accusative), so the fault is the formation, not the case. And मातृ does take -म् once it is in its strong grade, as मातरम् shows.

**12. (B)** माता, मातरम्, मातरौ, मातरः are all forms of one stem मातृ: the strong stem मातर- carries the endings (-Ø/-म्/-औ/-:) that distinguish nominative, accusative, dual and plural. They are not from different nouns, nor are they all singular (मातरौ is dual, मातरः is plural). And the labels are not swapped: मातरम् is accusative singular, मातरः is nominative plural.

**13. (A)** मातरः encodes nominative plural; the song reveres a single Motherland as the object of वन्दे, which needs accusative singular मातरम्. So मातरः errs on number (plural vs singular) AND case (nominative vs accusative). It is not merely one of those errors, and it is certainly not the correct accusative singular.

**14. (C)** Each image maps onto a body part or quality of a personified Mother (crown=head, sea=feet, crops=fertile body, holy sites=sacredness), so together they revere Bharat head-to-foot and spiritually. They are figurative, not a bare fact-list. There is no battle imagery, so 'warrior-queen with weapons' is foreign to the passage. And the tone is loving reverence for one's own land, not a cold catalogue of a foreign country.

**15. (B)** पर्वतराजः denotes a king (its own head member), so it is तत्पुरुष; चन्द्रमुखी denotes a woman who is not named inside the compound but is characterised by having a moon-like face, the hallmark of बहुव्रीहि. So they differ in type. They are not both बहुव्रीहि (पर्वतराजः names its own head) nor both तत्पुरुष (चन्द्रमुखी points outside). And the labels are not swapped — पर्वतराजः is the तत्पुरुष and चन्द्रमुखी the बहुव्रीहि.

**16. (C)** रत्नाकरः describes the sea by a property — it stores jewels (pearls, corals) — and the reader infers 'ocean' from that description plus context; it does not embed समुद्र. It is not a spelling variant, since its members carry real descriptive content. And there is no rule forbidding समुद्र; the epithet is a poetic choice, not a legal substitute.

**17. (A)** सस्यश्यामला has the relation 'dark/green by means of crops' (an instrumental-type तत्पुरुष), so जलशीतला parallels it as 'cool by means of water'. 'King of waters' would be a genitive तत्पुरुष like पर्वतराजः, a different relation. 'Water-less' would be a negative बहुव्रीहि, not this pattern. And 'water and coolness together' would be a द्वन्द्व, which this compound is not.

**18. (B)** चन्द्रशेखरः names a third party (Shiva) possessing the feature 'moon on the crest', the signature of बहुव्रीहि; देशभक्तः names its own head (the devotee) and is तत्पुरुष. देशभक्तः does not point to a country as its referent — its referent is the devotee. Merely containing two nouns does not make a compound बहुव्रीहि. And तत्पुरुष names its HEAD (second member), not its first member.

**19. (A)** In a determinative (तत्पुरुष) compound the final member is the head, here बान्धव; कृषक modifies it, and the case/number ending appears on the head बान्धवाः (nominative plural). So कृषक is not the head. It is not a द्वन्द्व of two equal nouns ('farmers and brethren'). And headedness is fixed by structure, not by which word is shorter.

**20. (B)** The chapter treats vowel length (मात्रा) as meaning-bearing: इ and ई differ in duration and can distinguish words, just as आ and अ do. So the learner is wrong, and the principle is not limited to आ/अ. Vowel length does affect meaning generally. And इ does have a long counterpart ई, so the swap is possible — which is precisely why it matters.

**21. (D)** आनन्द means 'bliss' precisely because of its long आ; with a short अ the sense collapses, so the title 'Abbey of Bliss' (आनन्दमठः) hinges on that length. The आ is not a plural marker, not a case-ending making the title an object, and it labels the meaning of the word, not the author's name.

**22. (A)** The दीर्घ (long, two-मात्रा) vowels among these are आ, ई, ऊ; their short (ह्रस्व, one-मात्रा) partners are अ, इ, उ. So अ, इ, उ is the all-short set, not all-long. अ, आ, इ mixes short and long. And इ, उ, ऋ are all short, not long.

**23. (A)** Length in Sanskrit is meaning-distinguishing (phonemic): आनन्द and अनन्द would be different items, so a slip alters the word itself. Sanskrit does have long vowels (that is the whole point). Length is not merely a gender switch; it can change the lexical meaning. And the issue is meaning, not pronounceability.

**24. (B)** Ch. 1 needs the long आ to keep आनन्द/आनन्दमठः meaningful, so it introduces the idea in context and defers the full treatment to the वर्णमात्रा chapter. Length is not unique to Ch. 1 — that is why the forward reference exists. Ch. 1 is not 'about conjugations only'. And the link to आनन्द makes the mention relevant, not an error.

**25. (D)** सस्यश्यामला shows a join with no seam change, whereas अ + ई → ए shows vowel fusion; so a reader must check for and reverse any sandhi to recover members. Not all joins fuse (this one does not), and seam fusion does occur (अ+ई→ए), so the two absolute claims are both false. Sandhi is reversible by rule, which is exactly how words are split.

**26. (A)** The standard guṇa-sandhi अ/आ + इ/ई → ए lets a reader reverse an ए at a seam into a preceding अ/आ plus इ/ई. ए does not require two ए vowels to arise. अ + उ would give ओ, not ए. And ए certainly can result from sandhi, so the rule is real, not invented.

**27. (D)** Sandhi at a word-seam is driven by the following sound: मातरम् starts with a consonant (no vowel fusion), while इह starts with a vowel इ, which can interact with the final ए of वन्दे. The two following words are not the same. Vowel-sandhi is triggered by a following vowel, not by consonants, so the rule in one distractor is inverted. And वन्दे does not lose meaning merely because a word follows it.

**28. (A)** The members are सस्य + श्यामला, giving 'dark/green by means of crops'; there is no negative अ-, so reading अश्यामला both mis-segments the sounds and reverses the meaning to 'not dark'. It is therefore not correct, not merely 'slightly off' in meaning, and श्यामला has no hidden negative.

**29. (D)** रत्नाकरः splits as रत्न + आकर ('mine/store of jewels'), the ocean — correct boundary and sense. पर्वतराजः must split पर्वत + राज ('king of mountains'), not पर्व + तराज. देशभक्तः splits देश + भक्त ('country-devoted'), not देशभ + क्त. And सस्यश्यामला splits सस्य + श्यामला, not by peeling a final आ off a coined masculine.

**30. (B)** The ocean (समुद्रः) is the nominative subject doing the washing; the two feet (चरणौ) are the accusative-dual object; भारतमातुः is genitive marking whose feet. The other mappings scramble these roles — e.g. making the ocean the object or the means, or the Motherland the subject/object — none of which fits 'the ocean washes the Motherland's two feet'.

**31. (D)** Sanskrit marks the dual distinctly, and -औ here gives accusative dual चरणौ ('the two feet' as object of प्रक्षालयति). It is not a plural (Sanskrit would use a different plural ending), not nominative (the feet are washed, not washing), and not a singular (there are two feet). It is also not genitive, which would express 'of the feet'.

**32. (C)** -भिः marks the instrumental plural, the means by which the watering is done; 'of' would be the genitive स्वेदबिन्दूनाम्. So the gloss 'of' mistakes instrumental for genitive. -भिः is not accusative (the drops are the means, not the thing watered) nor nominative (they are not the subject — the farmers are).

**33. (A)** The farmers (कृषकाः) are the nominative subject/agent; the sweat-drops (स्वेदबिन्दुभिः) are the instrumental means; the land (भूमिम्) is the accusative object. The other assignments swap agent and means, or misread the object as subject, or wrongly flatten all three into the instrumental — none matches 'the farmers water the land with sweat'.

**34. (C)** Sweat falls in many drops, so the means is naturally plural, and the plurality underlines the farmers' abundant, ongoing labour. The instrumental does have singular forms, so plural is a choice, not a necessity. Sanskrit does not force a means to copy the subject's number (means and agent are independent). And -भिः is itself a plural ending, so the claim that it is singular is false.

**35. (B)** 'With tears' is the means, so neuter अश्रु takes the instrumental plural अश्रुभिः, mirroring स्वेदबिन्दुभिः. The accusative अश्रूणि would make the tears the thing watered, the genitive अश्रूणाम् would mean 'of the tears', and a nominative would make the tears the doer — none fits the means-role 'with tears'.

**36. (C)** वन्दे (1st sg.) and वन्दते (3rd sg.) share the root वन्द् and differ only in the personal ending, which fixes person/number. They are not identical in meaning. वन्दते is third-person singular, not the plural 'we salute' (that would be वन्दामहे). And the root stays वन्द्; it is the ending, not the root, that changes.

**37. (C)** -अन्ति (parasmaipada) and -ते (ātmanepada) are from different ending-sets, chosen by the root's class/pada, illustrating that ending choice depends on the verb's type. There is no single universal ending-set, so the difference is not one of tense. Sanskrit verbs do mark person and number (the endings prove it), and person is encoded in the verb, not the noun.

**38. (D)** वन्दन्ते is third-person plural ('they salute'), while first-person plural 'we salute' is वन्दामहे; the forms are not swapped. वन्दन्ते is plural, not the singular वन्दे. And it is third person, not the second-person plural (which would be वन्दध्वे).

**39. (A)** Placing कृषकबान्धवाः (toiling with sweat) beside सस्यश्यामला (crop-green) makes the abundance the fruit of human labour, so crediting it to the ocean alone, or to snow alone, or to nothing, all miss the intended causal link the poet draws between the farmers' sweat and the green fields.

**40. (C)** तीर्थक्षेत्राणि introduces sacredness, rounding out the praise of natural features with spiritual worth, so it fits the hymn's reverent portrait. It says nothing about a lack of rivers, is not a detached tourist list, and does not imply the land is small — it adds a dimension of holiness, not a limitation.

**41. (D)** जिज्ञासा (the children's desire to know) triggers the mother's explanation, so removing it would remove the explanatory dialogue that structures the lesson. जिज्ञासा does not place the Himalaya, does not cause वन्दे's accusative government (that is grammatical), and does not identify the author — those are unrelated to the children's curiosity.

**42. (C)** Appearing within the novel आनन्दमठः sets the song inside a story, lending it narrative and patriotic context. Being in a novel does not make it anonymous — the chapter names Bankim Chandra. It is a song, not a recipe. And being set in an 1882 novel does not make it older than all Sanskrit literature.

**43. (B)** When salutation is expressed by the noun वन्दनम्, its target appears in the genitive (भारतमातुः, 'salutation OF the Motherland'); the finite verb वन्दे instead governs an accusative object (मातरम्). So भारतमातुः is genitive, not accusative. It is not the means (instrumental) nor the subject (the gods, देवाः, are the nominative subject).

**44. (B)** Adjective–noun agreement forces सस्यश्यामलाम् and सुजलाम् into the same feminine accusative singular as मातरम्, giving three accusative words. Adjectives do not stay nominative while the noun is accusative (that breaks agreement). सस्यश्यामलाम् is accusative, not genitive. And वन्दे does take an object, so 'none' is wrong.

**45. (A)** The nominative singular of मातृ is माता (ending in long आ, no -म्), and मातरम् carries the accusative-singular -म्; so -म् marks the accusative, not the nominative, and merely being word-final tells us nothing. माता is not the accusative, मातरम् is not a genitive of possession, and it is not a vocative of address — it is the accusative object.

**46. (C)** वन्दे needs its object in the accusative, so माता must be मातरम्; and the instrumental पर्वतराजेन ('by the king of mountains') supplies a 'means' that the sentence does not call for, so it is out of place — two faults. The sentence is therefore not correct as written. माता is genuinely wrong (not 'fine'), so it is not a single-fault sentence, and the fix is not merely swapping वन्दे for नमः.

**47. (D)** देशभक्तः splits as देश + भक्तः ('devoted to country'), and shortening आ to अ (आनन्द→अनन्द) can wreck meaning since length is phonemic — both rules applied correctly. आ and अ are not interchangeable, so 'length never matters' is false. देशभक्तः can be split. And its sense is 'country-devoted', not 'devotion's country', and length affects meaning, not merely gender.

**48. (A)** मातरम् is the accusative singular, the object form (e.g. the one saluted), so that pairing is right. मातरम् is not the nominative (that is माता). माता is not the genitive (that is मातुः). And मातुः is the genitive, not the accusative.

**49. (A)** The doers are nominative (कृषकाः), the fields watered are the accusative object (क्षेत्राणि), and the means 'with water' is instrumental (जलैः): कृषकाः क्षेत्राणि जलैः सिञ्चन्ति. Making कृषक instrumental (कृषकैः) wrongly demotes the agent. Putting the fields in instrumental (क्षेत्रैः) loses the object. And कृषकान् makes the farmers the object rather than the subject.

**50. (B)** A first-person-singular verb encodes its subject 'I', so वन्दे already carries the subject — it is simply unwritten as a separate pronoun. Sanskrit clauses do have subjects (encoded in the verb). मातरम् is the accusative object, not a hidden 'I'. And वन्दे is a finite verb, not a noun, so the sentence is verb + object, not two subjects.

**51. (D)** वन्दे (1st sg., root वन्द्) takes the accusative object मातरम् (stem मातृ), and the song opens Bankim Chandra's आनन्दमठः (1882) — all four facts correct. The verb is not third-person plural and मातरम् is not nominative. वन्दे does not mean 'I love', मातरम् is not genitive, and the date is 1882, not the 12th century. And वन्दे is a verb, not a noun, with a known author.

**52. (D)** The correct statement is right on all three: meaning (reverence for the Mother), imagery (Himalaya-crown, ocean-feet), and grammar (वन्दे + accusative मातरम्, genitive भारतमातुः चरणौ). The warrior-king option errs in grammar (nominative माता) and inverts the imagery. The foreign-queen option errs in meaning (वन्दे ≠ 'love') and imagery (deserts). And the trade-routes option errs in meaning, grammar (genitive object) and author (it is Bankim Chandra, not Tagore).

**53. (D)** Every adjective must agree with मातरम् in feminine accusative singular, giving सुजलाम् सुफलाम् सस्यश्यामलाम् मातरम्. The all-nominative version (सुजला ... माता) drops the object case. The -ऐः forms are instrumental plural ('by means of'), not agreeing accusatives. And the -आयाः/मातुः forms are genitive ('of'), again not the required accusative.

**54. (A)** वन्दे conveys reverence, not affection, and the 'Mother' is the personified Motherland (भारतमाता), not a household mother — two separate errors. वन्दे does not mean 'I look at', and 'love' is not the right verb. The 'Mother' is the land personified, not literally the Himalaya. So the rendering is wrong on both counts, not acceptable.

**55. (D)** श्यामला denotes dark/green colour, and सस्य ('crops') anchors that to fertile fields, giving 'dark-green with crops' — not white. So the snow-white rendering inverts the colour. श्यामला does not mean white in any reading here, and it is not 'sea-blue' either; the image is green farmland.

**56. (A)** नमः, an indeclinable, pairs with a dative ('homage TO'), while the verb वन्दे takes an accusative object (मातरम्). So the two saluting words govern different cases — they are not both accusative, the assignment is not reversed (नमः is not the accusative one), and neither takes a genitive 'of' for this meaning.

**57. (C)** The -औ ending on मातरौ marks the accusative dual ('two mothers'), so it cannot mean one Mother; the singular needs मातरम्. -औ is not the accusative singular (that is -म्). It is accusative dual here, the object of वन्दे, not a nominative subject. And it is the dual (exactly two), not a general plural for 'more than zero'.

**58. (B)** वन्दे is active first-person, takes accusative मातरम्; रत्नाकरः is the ocean; पर्वतराजः (Himalaya) is a genitive तत्पुरुष ('king of mountains') — all four facts correct. वन्दे is not passive with nominative माता, and रत्नाकरः is not the Himalaya, nor is पर्वतराजः a द्वन्द्व. वन्दे does not mean 'love' with a genitive, रत्नाकरः is not the farmers, and पर्वतराजः denotes its own head (a king), so it is तत्पुरुष, not बहुव्रीहि. And वन्दे is not third-person with a dative object, nor is रत्नाकरः the crops.

**59. (B)** Because मठ begins with the consonant म, no vowel-sandhi occurs at the seam; आनन्द ('bliss') and मठ ('abbey') stand side by side, yielding 'Abbey of Bliss'. There is no आ+अ vowel fusion to undo, since the second member starts with a consonant. The word denotes the abbey itself (its head member मठ), so it is not a बहुव्रीहि pointing to an outside person. And मठ is a noun, not a verb, so the whole is a compound noun, not a verb-phrase.

**60. (D)** Citation forms (सुजला, माता) are nominative; वन्दे demands its object in the accusative, and the adjective must agree, so both become accusative: वन्दे सुजलाम् मातरम्. The dictionary instinct fails because वन्दे does not govern the nominative. Changing only the adjective (leaving माता) or only the noun (leaving सुजला) breaks adjective-noun agreement -- both words must shift together to the accusative.
