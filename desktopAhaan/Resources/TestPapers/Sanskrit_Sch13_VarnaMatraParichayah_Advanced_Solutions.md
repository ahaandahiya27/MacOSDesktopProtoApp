# Sanskrit — Chapter 13: वर्णमात्रा-परिचयः (Vowel Quantity) · Paper 2 (Advanced) Solutions

**1. (C)** **इ** is a simple short (ह्रस्व) vowel, 1 mātrā. आ, ई and ऊ are all long (दीर्घ), 2 mātrās.

**2. (A)** **आ** is long (दीर्घ), 2 mātrās. अ, इ and उ are short, 1 mātrā each.

**3. (D)** The correctly matched short–long pair is **इ (short) — ई (long)**. The other rows reverse the lengths.

**4. (B)** The simple short vowels are **अ, इ, उ, ऋ** (each 1 mātrā). आ/ई/ऊ/ॠ are their long counterparts; ए/ऐ/ओ/औ are long; क/च/ट/त are consonants.

**5. (B)** **ए is always long (2 mātrās)** — it has no short form. So the ए in देव counts as 2 mātrās.

**6. (D)** ए, ऐ, ओ and औ **are always दीर्घ (long); they have no short version**, so they always count 2 mātrās.

**7. (A)** There is no 'short ओ' because **ओ is inherently long (2 mātrās)** in standard Sanskrit.

**8. (C)** **अ is 1 mātrā (short); आ is 2 mātrās (long)** — the same vowel-place, twice the duration.

**9. (A)** **ऋ is a short vowel (1 mātrā)**, as in ऋषि. It is a true vowel, not a consonant.

**10. (C)** The long counterpart of ऋ is **ॠ** (rarely used, 2 mātrās).

**11. (D)** **आ, ई, ऊ** are all long (दीर्घ) vowels. The अ/इ/उ row is short, and क/ख/ग are consonants.

**12. (B)** **अ, इ, उ** are all short (ह्रस्व). The आ/ई/ऊ row is long, and ए/ओ/औ are long.

**13. (D)** कमल = क(अ,1) + म(अ,1) + ल(अ,1) = **3 mātrās** (three short syllables).

**14. (B)** गीता = गी(ई,2) + ता(आ,2) = **4 mātrās**.

**15. (C)** नदी = न(अ,1) + दी(ई,2) = **3 mātrās**.

**16. (A)** देव = दे(ए,2) + व(अ,1) = **3 mātrās** — because ए is long (2).

**17. (C)** गुरु = गु(उ,1) + रु(उ,1) = **2 mātrās** (two short syllables).

**18. (A)** फलम् = फ(अ,1) + ल(अ,1) + म्(no vowel, 0) = **2 mātrās**.

**19. (B)** सेवा = से(ए,2) + वा(आ,2) = **4 mātrās** — both syllables are long (ए and आ).

**20. (D)** रमा = र(अ,1) + मा(आ,2) = **3 mātrās**.

**21. (A)** हिमालय = हि(इ,1) + मा(आ,2) + ल(अ,1) + य(अ,1) = **5 mātrās**.

**22. (D)** **गीता** counts 4 mātrās (2 + 2). कमल is 3, फलम् is 2, and गुरु is 2.

**23. (B)** **गुरु** has the fewest at 2 mātrās; गीता and सेवा are 4, हिमालय is 5.

**24. (C)** नदी (1 + 2) and रमा (1 + 2) each total **3 mātrās**, so they match.

**25. (B)** विद्या = वि(इ,1) + द्या(the cluster द्य carries आ, 2) = **3 mātrās**.

**26. (C)** Three short syllables, each 1 mātrā, total **3 mātrās** (e.g. कमल).

**27. (A)** A प्लुत vowel lasts **3 mātrās** — three times a short vowel.

**28. (D)** A प्लुत vowel is written with the numeral **३ (three)** placed after it, e.g. राम३.

**29. (D)** राम३ tells the reader to **hold the final vowel for three mātrās** — a प्लुत (prolonged) call.

**30. (A)** In ओ३म्, the ओ is held for **3 mātrās** (प्लुत) during chanting.

**31. (C)** A प्लुत vowel is three times as long as a **ह्रस्व (short)** vowel (3 mātrās vs 1).

**32. (B)** प्लुत vowels appear mainly in **chanting and long-distance calls** (e.g. भो३ः!), not in quick everyday speech.

**33. (C)** Said for only 2 mātrās, the प्लुत ओ३म् collapses into an ordinary **दीर्घ (long)** vowel.

**34. (B)** Shortest to longest: **ह्रस्व (1), दीर्घ (2), प्लुत (3)**.

**35. (D)** The mātrā ratio ह्रस्व : दीर्घ : प्लुत is **1 : 2 : 3**.

**36. (A)** A long (दीर्घ, 2-mātrā) vowel held one mātrā more becomes a **प्लुत** (3 mātrās).

**37. (A)** One rooster (एकः कुक्कुटः) is singular, so the verb is the singular **रौति** ('crows').

**38. (B)** Many roosters (बहवः कुक्कुटाः) is plural, so the verb is the plural **रुवन्ति** ('crow').

**39. (D)** Picking रौति for one and रुवन्ति for many shows subject–verb agreement in **number**.

**40. (C)** वायसः (crow) and कुक्कुटः (rooster) are both **birds**.

**41. (D)** शिखी is the **peacock** (literally 'the crested one'), so शिखी नृत्यति = 'the peacock dances'.

**50. (D)** वर्ण meaning both 'letter' and 'caste' illustrates Sanskrit **polysemy** — one word, several context-set senses.
