Watch the classic traps: Sanskrit has SEVEN cases plus the vocative (EIGHT forms), not Latin's six; there are THREE numbers, and the DUAL (द्विवचन) is for exactly TWO (देवौ = 'two gods', not 'many'); each case has its OWN ending and ONE job (देवस्य 'of', देवाय 'to/for', देवात् 'from'); the vocative is NOT always the nominative (हे देव!, हे गुरो!, हे कवे!); and Sanskrit has no Hindi-style 'oblique'. Worked Solutions

1. (B) Sanskrit has seven cases (विभक्ति). With the vocative counted separately there are eight noun-forms; six is the Latin count.

2. (D) Seven cases plus the vocative make eight forms. Six is Latin's count, seven omits the vocative, and ten is too many.

3. (C) सप्तविभक्तयः means 'the seven cases'. Not the vowels, days, or sages.

4. (B) The seven cases run from प्रथमा (1st) to सप्तमी (7th). चतुर्थी and पञ्चमी are in between, and there is no अष्टमी case.

5. (D) Each noun is declined in three numbers - singular, dual and plural. Not two, one, or seven.

6. (C) The vocative is shown alongside the seven cases but counted separately. It is not the first case, unused, or the genitive.

7. (A) प्रथमा marks the subject. The object is द्वितीया, the means तृतीया, and possession षष्ठी.

8. (A) द्वितीया marks the object. The subject is प्रथमा, 'from' पञ्चमी, and 'in/on' सप्तमी.

9. (B) तृतीया means 'by / with' (the means). 'To/for' is चतुर्थी, 'of' षष्ठी, and 'in/on' सप्तमी.

10. (D) 'By/with' (the means) is the तृतीया (instrumental). The others are nominative, genitive and locative.

11. (B) चतुर्थी means 'to / for'. 'By/with' is तृतीया, 'from' पञ्चमी, and 'in/on' सप्तमी.

12. (A) पञ्चमी means 'from'. 'To/for' is चतुर्थी, 'of' षष्ठी, and 'in/on' सप्तमी.

13. (B) सप्तमी (locative) means 'in / on'. 'By' is तृतीया, 'to' चतुर्थी, and 'from' पञ्चमी.

14. (C) The nominative singular of देव is देवः. देवम् is accusative, देवेन instrumental, and देवाय dative.

15. (A) The accusative singular is देवम्. देवः is nominative, देवात् ablative, and देवे locative.

16. (D) The instrumental singular is देवेन ('by the god'). देवाय is dative, देवस्य genitive, and देवः nominative.

17. (A) The dative singular is देवाय ('to/for the god'). देवात् is ablative, देवेन instrumental, and देवम् accusative.

18. (D) The ablative singular is देवात् ('from the god'). देवाय is dative, देवस्य genitive, and देवे locative.

19. (D) The genitive singular is देवस्य ('of the god'). देवाय is dative, देवात् ablative, and देवेन instrumental.

20. (B) The locative singular is देवे ('in/on the god'). देवः is nominative, देवम् accusative, and देवस्य genitive.

21. (C) The vocative singular drops the -ः: हे देव!. हे देवः! keeps the nominative ending, and the others are dative/genitive forms.

22. (D) The three numbers are singular, dual and plural. Not two numbers, the genders, or the tenses.

23. (A) The dual is for exactly two. Three or more take the plural, and one the singular.

24. (A) देवौ is the dual, 'the two gods'. The plural is देवाः, the singular देवः, and the vocative हे देव!.

25. (B) Three or more take the बहुवचन (plural). The dual is for two, the singular for one.

26. (A) Sanskrit's dual is for pairs and exactly-two contexts. It is not for all groups, single objects, or tenses.

27. (C) देवौ is dual (exactly two), so it cannot mean 'many'. It is not singular, plural, or vocative.

28. (A) The nominative plural of कवि is कवयः. देवाः is the अकारान्त plural, कविम् accusative singular, and कविना instrumental singular.

29. (A) The -अयः plural appears in कवयः, हरयः, मुनयः (इकारान्त). देवाः/रामाः are अकारान्त, गुरवः उकारान्त, and देवान् is accusative plural.

30. (A) कवयः and देवाः differ by declension class (इकारान्त vs अकारान्त), not tense, gender, or number (both are nominative plural).

31. (D) हरि and मुनि are इकारान्त, declining like कवि. They do not follow देव (अकारान्त) or गुरु (उकारान्त).

32. (D) गुरु is उकारान्त (ending in उ). देव is अकारान्त, कवि इकारान्त; गुरु here is masculine, not feminine.

33. (D) The vocative singular of गुरु is हे गुरो!. हे गुरुः! keeps the nominative, हे गुरुम्! is accusative-like, and हे गुरवे! dative-like.

34. (C) The उकारान्त vocative shifts -ुः to -ओ (हे गुरो!). -आय is dative, -स्य genitive, and -एन instrumental.

35. (B) साधु, like गुरु, has the vocative हे साधो!. हे साधुः! keeps the nominative, and the others are dative/instrumental-like.

36. (C) विष्णु has the vocative हे विष्णो!. हे विष्णुः! keeps the nominative, हे विष्णवे! is dative-like, and हे विष्णुम्! accusative-like.

37. (C) For अकारान्त the vocative drops the -ः (हे देव!). -ओ is for उकारान्त, -ए for इकारान्त, and -स्य is the genitive.

38. (B) For उकारान्त the vocative changes -ुः to -ओ (हे गुरो!). Dropping -ः is अकारान्त, -ए इकारान्त, and -आय the dative.

39. (C) For इकारान्त the vocative is हे कवे!. हे कविः! keeps the nominative, हे कवो! is उकारान्त-like, and हे कवाय! dative-like.

40. (B) It is false: the vocative changes (हे देव!, हे गुरो!, हे कवे!). It is not always like the nominative, nor only for plurals or the dual.

41. (C) The vocative calls or addresses someone ('O ...!'). Possession is genitive, the means instrumental, and the object accusative.

42. (A) देव and राम are the अकारान्त paradigm-words. कवि/हरि are इकारान्त and गुरु/शम्भु उकारान्त.

43. (D) कवि and हरि are इकारान्त. देव/राम are अकारान्त and गुरु/शम्भु उकारान्त.

44. (D) गुरु and शम्भु are उकारान्त. देव/राम are अकारान्त and कवि/हरि इकारान्त.

45. (C) बाल, शिव and हस्त are अकारान्त, declining like देव. They do not follow कवि or गुरु.

46. (C) The देव paradigm fits hundreds of अकारान्त masculine nouns. It is not for देव alone, feminine nouns, or verbs.

47. (D) Sanskrit has a separate ending for each case, with no single 'oblique'. It is not oblique-plus-postpositions, case-less, or two-case.

48. (B) Hindi compressed the system into 2-3 forms plus postpositions. It did not keep eight full cases, drop all cases, or add a tenth.

49. (B) Sanskrit has seven cases plus the vocative (eight forms), more than Latin's six. It is not six, three, or none.

50. (D) Sanskrit keeps a distinct ending for every case, with no oblique. It is not oblique-only, ending-less, or postposition-only.

51. (A) देवस्य means 'of the god' (genitive). 'To the god' is देवाय, 'by the god' देवेन, and 'O god!' हे देव!.

52. (B) देवाय means 'to/for the god' (dative). 'Of the god' is देवस्य, 'from the god' देवात्, and 'by the god' देवेन.

53. (B) Each case has its own ending and one job: देवस्य 'of' (genitive) vs देवाय 'to/for' (dative). They are not the same, interchangeable, or verbs.

54. (D) देवात् ('from the god') is the ablative (पञ्चमी). The genitive is देवस्य, dative देवाय, and locative देवे.

55. (C) देवः is the nominative (प्रथमा), the subject doing the eating. It is not accusative, instrumental, or genitive.

56. (A) फलम् is the accusative (द्वितीया), the object being eaten. It is not nominative, dative, or ablative.

57. (B) 'In the village' uses the locative (सप्तमी): ग्रामे. The nominative, accusative and dative forms mean other roles.

58. (C) देवेन is the instrumental ('by the god'). It is not genitive (देवस्य); देवाय is dative (not ablative); and देवे is locative (not nominative).

59. (C) कवि is इकारान्त and गुरु उकारान्त. The other options misassign their classes.

60. (A) Sanskrit has seven cases plus the vocative (eight forms), and देवौ is the dual ('two gods'). The other options keep one or both errors.
