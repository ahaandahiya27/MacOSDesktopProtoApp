Set 1 · Q1–Q10

Q1

The central value of this chapter, 'vasudhaiva kutumbakam', means:

  • A'the whole world is one family'✓
  • B'a guest is like God'
  • C'the world is one market'
  • D'India for Indians only'

Working

✓

Vasudhaiva kutumbakam means 'the whole world is one family', the chapter's core value. 'A guest is like God' is atithi devo bhava; the others are not the phrase's meaning.

Q2

'Atithi devo bhava' expresses India's tradition of:

  • Ahospitality - treating a guest like God✓
  • Bkeeping out all foreigners
  • Ctrading only with neighbours
  • Dworshipping many gods

Working

✓

Atithi devo bhava ('a guest is like God') expresses hospitality. It is not about excluding foreigners, trade, or polytheism.

Q3

The chapter says India has, for thousands of years, been:

  • Aa home to many communities from around the world✓
  • Bclosed to all outsiders
  • Chome to only one community
  • Druled by foreign refugees

Working

✓

India has long been home to many communities from around the world. It was not closed, single-community, or refugee-ruled.

Q4

Newcomers to India generally:

  • Awere forced to abandon all their traditions
  • Bnever mixed with Indian society at all
  • Cruled over the Indians
  • Dkept their own traditions while becoming part of Indian society✓

Working

✓

Communities kept their traditions while integrating into Indian society - identity-preserving integration. They were not forced to abandon traditions, kept apart, or made rulers.

Q5

The idea of vasudhaiva kutumbakam treats India's diversity as:

  • Aa weakness to be removed
  • Ba strength and a civilisational ideal of acceptance✓
  • Ca threat to be feared
  • Dunimportant

Working

✓

Vasudhaiva kutumbakam frames diversity as a strength and an ideal of acceptance. It is not a weakness, threat, or trivial matter.

Q6

The chapter says newcomers came to India for two main reasons:

  • Aonly ever for trade
  • Bseeking refuge from persecution, or seeking opportunity through trade✓
  • Conly ever to conquer
  • Donly to study

Working

✓

Newcomers came either for refuge (fleeing persecution) or opportunity (trade). It was not trade-only, conquest-only, or study-only.

Q7

The claim 'all the communities came to India to trade and prosper' is wrong because:

  • Aeveryone came freely to trade
  • Bno one ever came for trade
  • Call came to conquer
  • Dmany came fleeing persecution, and the Siddis were brought enslaved✓

Working

✓

Many (Jews, Syriac Christians, Parsis, Baha'is) fled persecution, and the Siddis were brought enslaved - not all came freely to trade.

Q8

A 'refugee' differs from an ordinary immigrant in that a refugee:

  • Aalways comes freely seeking trade
  • Bis never a newcomer
  • Cflees danger or persecution and has little choice✓
  • Dcan never keep any traditions

Working

✓

A refugee flees danger with little choice, unlike those who come freely seeking opportunity. Both are newcomers, and refugees can keep traditions.

Q9

The chapter calls the two paths by which people came:

  • A'conquest' and 'trade'
  • B'war' and 'peace'
  • C'refuge' and 'opportunity'✓
  • D'land' and 'sea'

Working

✓

The two paths are 'refuge' (fleeing persecution) and 'opportunity' (seeking trade). They are not conquest/trade, war/peace, or land/sea.

Q10

Arab merchants coming to trade and Parsis fleeing persecution show, respectively:

  • Arefuge and opportunity
  • Bconquest and refuge
  • Copportunity and conquest
  • Dopportunity and refuge✓

Working

✓

Arab merchants came for opportunity (trade); Parsis came for refuge (fleeing persecution). The order is opportunity then refuge, not the reverse, and neither came to conquer.

Keeping synagogues and faith while joining Indian society shows identity-preserving integration. It was not loss of traditions, separation, or newcomer-rule.

  • CTibet

The Parsis settled mainly in Gujarat. Kerala is linked to the Syriac Christians, Tibet to Tibetan refugees, and Armenia to the Armenians.
