Q1

How many zeroes follow the leading 1 in one crore?

  • A5
  • B6
  • C7✓
  • D8

Working

✓

One crore = 1,00,00,000 = a 1 followed by **7** zeroes (10⁷). (A lakh has 5 zeroes; an arab has 9 — both are tempting near-misses.)

Q2

Written with Indian commas, the number 50000000 is:

  • A5,00,00,000✓
  • B50,00,000
  • C5,0000,000
  • D50,000,000

Working

✓

50000000 has 8 digits. Indian grouping is 3-2-2-2 from the right: 000 | 00 | 00 | 5 → 5,00,00,000 (five crore). Option D (50,000,000) is the International 3-3-3 grouping, and B/C have the wrong digit count.

Q3

One million equals how many lakhs?

  • A1
  • B1000
  • C100
  • D10✓

Working

✓

One million = 1,000,000 = 10,00,000 = **10 lakh**. The classic trap is treating a million and a lakh as equal — a million is ten times a lakh.

  • D1,00,001

Working

✓

Adding 1 to the largest 5-digit number: 99,999 + 1 = 1,00,000, the smallest 6-digit number (one lakh). 10,000 is only the smallest 5-digit number.

Q9

  • D5,99,700

Q10

Reading right to left, the Indian comma convention groups the digits as:

  • A3, then 3, then 3, …
  • B2, then 2, then 2, …
  • C2, then 3, then 3, …
  • D3, then 2, then 2, then 2, …✓

Working

✓

The Indian convention puts the first comma after 3 digits from the right, then after every 2 digits: **3, then 2, then 2, then 2, …**. The 3-3-3 pattern (option A) is the International system.

  • Bnearest ten-thousand is 60,000; "about a lakh" understates
  • Cnearest ten-thousand is 50,000; "about a lakh" is exactly right
  • Dnearest ten-thousand is 50,000; "about a lakh" overstates by roughly double✓

Working

✓

49,873 to the nearest ten-thousand: the thousands portion 9,873 ≥ 5,000, so round up → 50,000. But "about a lakh" (1,00,000) is roughly double the true ~50,000, so **nearest ten-thousand is 50,000; "about a lakh" overstates by roughly double**. --- *Solutions complete: 60 / 60.*
