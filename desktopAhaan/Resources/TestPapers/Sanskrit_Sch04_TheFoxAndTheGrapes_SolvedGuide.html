Set 1 · Q1–Q10

Q1

This chapter is the Sanskrit retelling of which fable?

  • AThe Lion and the Mouse
  • BThe Fox and the Grapes✓
  • CThe Tortoise and the Hare
  • DThe Crow and the Pitcher

Working

✓

It is the Sanskrit retelling of 'The Fox and the Grapes'. The others are different fables.

Q2

शृगालः means:

  • Afox / jackal✓
  • Blion
  • Cgrape
  • Dvine

Working

✓

शृगालः is the fox/jackal. A lion is सिंहः, a grape द्राक्षा, and a vine लता.

Q3

The central character (protagonist) of the story is:

  • Athe grape-vine
  • Ba teacher
  • Cthe fox (शृगालः)✓
  • Dthe Sun

Working

✓

The protagonist is the fox (शृगालः). The grape-vine, a teacher and the Sun are not the central character.

Q4

Where does the fox wander in the story?

  • Ain the ocean
  • Bon a mountain peak
  • Cin a city market
  • Din the forest (वने)✓

Working

✓

The fox wanders in the forest (वने). Not the ocean, a peak, or a market.

Q5

शृगालः is glossed as लोमशा, referring to the animal's:

  • Athirst
  • Bfurry / hairy nature✓
  • Cwisdom
  • Dwealth

Working

✓

लोमशा ('hairy') refers to the fox's furry nature. It is not thirst, wisdom, or wealth.

Q6

At the start, the fox is described as श्रान्तः, meaning:

  • Atired / weary✓
  • Bhappy
  • Cwealthy
  • Dwise

Working

✓

श्रान्तः means 'tired / weary'. It does not mean happy, wealthy, or wise.

Q7

श्रान्तः is glossed as क्लान्तः, meaning:

  • Ajoyful
  • Bangry
  • Csour
  • Dweary / fatigued✓

Working

✓

श्रान्तः (glossed क्लान्तः) means weary/fatigued. Not joyful, angry, or sour.

Q8

खिन्नः, used for the fox, means:

  • Asad / dejected✓
  • Btired
  • Cthirsty
  • Dbrave

Working

✓

खिन्नः means 'sad / dejected'. Tired is श्रान्तः, thirsty पिपासायुक्तः, and brave वीरः.

Q9

The fox is also full of पिपासा / तृषा, meaning:

  • Athirst✓
  • Bhunger
  • Canger
  • Dsleep

Working

✓

पिपासा / तृषा means 'thirst'. Hunger is बुभुक्षा, anger क्रोधः, and sleep निद्रा.

Q10

पिपासा is glossed as पातुम् इच्छा, meaning the:

  • Awish to eat
  • Bwish to drink✓
  • Cwish to sleep
  • Dwish to run

Working

✓

पिपासा = पातुम् इच्छा = 'the wish to drink' (thirst). It is not the wish to eat, sleep, or run.

Set 2 · Q11–Q20

Q11

Putting it together, the opening shows a fox that is:

  • Arested, happy and full
  • Bwealthy and proud
  • Cangry and strong
  • Dtired, sad and thirsty✓

Working

✓

The opening shows a fox that is tired (श्रान्तः), sad (खिन्नः) and thirsty (पिपासायुक्तः). Not rested, wealthy, or strong.

Q12

द्राक्षालता means:

  • Athe grape-vine (the whole climbing plant)✓
  • Ba single grape
  • Ca fox
  • Dthe forest

Working

✓

द्राक्षालता is the grape-vine, the whole climbing plant. A single grape is द्राक्षा; a fox is शृगालः; a forest वनम्.

Q13

द्राक्षालता breaks into द्राक्षा + लता, meaning:

  • A'fox' + 'forest'
  • B'sour' + 'fruit'
  • C'left' + 'right'
  • D'grape' + 'creeper' = grape-vine✓

Working

✓

द्राक्षा ('grape') + लता ('creeper') = grape-vine. The other splits are wrong.

Q14

द्राक्षा by itself means:

  • Agrape (the fruit)✓
  • Bthe whole vine
  • Ca fox
  • Dsourness

Working

✓

द्राक्षा by itself is the grape (fruit). The whole vine is द्राक्षालता, a fox शृगालः, and sourness आम्ल.

Q15

द्राक्षाफलम् means:

  • Athe creeper plant
  • Bthe forest
  • Cthe grape-fruit (the grapes)✓
  • Dthe fox

Working

✓

द्राक्षाफलम् is the grape-fruit (the grapes). The plant is द्राक्षालता, the forest वनम्, and the fox शृगालः.

Q16

Sanskrit using द्राक्षालता where English says 'grapevine' shows that Sanskrit often:

  • Auses fewer words than English always
  • Bhas no word for grape
  • Ccannot form compounds
  • Dcompounds two words where one English word suffices✓

Working

✓

Sanskrit often compounds two words (द्राक्षा + लता) where English uses one. It does not always use fewer words, lacks no word for grape, and certainly forms compounds.

Q17

उत्पतति means:

  • Ahe jumps / leaps up✓
  • Bhe sat down
  • Che slept
  • Dhe ate

Working

✓

उत्पतति means 'he leaps up'. It does not mean sat down, slept, or ate.

Q18

In उत्पतति, उत् is a:

  • Apast-tense augment
  • Bprefix (उपसर्ग) meaning 'up'✓
  • Ccase ending
  • Dsuffix meaning 'down'

Working

✓

उत् is a prefix (उपसर्ग) meaning 'up'. It is not the past augment, a case ending, or a 'down' suffix.

Q19

उत्पतति is in which tense?

  • Apast
  • Bfuture
  • Cpresent ('he leaps up')✓
  • Dimperative

Working

✓

उत्पतति is present tense, 'he leaps up'. The past would be उदपतत्; it is not future or imperative.

Q20

The PAST-tense form of this verb is:

  • Aउत्पतति
  • Bउत्पतिष्यति
  • Cपततु
  • Dउदपतत् (with the augment अ-)✓

Working

✓

The past tense is उदपतत्, with the augment अ-. उत्पतति is present, उत्पतिष्यति future, and पततु imperative.

Set 3 · Q21–Q30

Q21

The difference between उत् in उत्पतति and the अ- in उदपतत् is that उत् is a ____ while अ- is the ____.

  • Aaugment ... prefix
  • Bprefix (उपसर्ग) ... past-tense augment✓
  • Ccase ending ... suffix
  • Dverb ... noun

Working

✓

उत् is a prefix (उपसर्ग, 'up'); अ- is the past-tense augment. The other pairings reverse or misname them.

Q22

A prefix (उपसर्ग) like उत्-:

  • Amarks past tense
  • Battaches to the root and can change its meaning ('up')✓
  • Cis a case ending
  • Dis always silent

Working

✓

A prefix attaches to the root and can change its meaning (उत् = 'up'). It does not mark past tense, act as a case ending, or stay silent.

Q23

How many times does the fox jump for the grapes?

  • Aexactly once
  • Bnever
  • Conly in his sleep
  • Dagain and again (पुनः पुनः)✓

Working

✓

The fox jumps again and again (पुनः पुनः). Not exactly once, never, or only in sleep.

Q24

The sequence एकवारम्, द्विवारम्, पुनः पुनः means:

  • Aleft, right, behind
  • Btired, sad, thirsty
  • Conce, twice, again and again✓
  • Dgrape, vine, fruit

Working

✓

एकवारम्, द्विवारम्, पुनः पुनः = 'once, twice, again and again'. The other options are directions, states, or plant-words.

Q25

The fox keeps jumping पुनः पुनः but:

  • Aeasily reaches them at once
  • Bstill cannot reach the grapes✓
  • Cdecides not to try
  • Dfalls asleep instead

Working

✓

Despite jumping again and again, the fox cannot reach the grapes. He does not reach them, refuse to try, or fall asleep.

Q26

Unable to reach the grapes, the fox declares them:

  • Asweet
  • Bripe
  • Csour (आम्लम्)✓
  • Dpoisonous

Working

✓

The fox declares the grapes sour (आम्लम्). Not sweet, ripe, or poisonous.

Q27

आम्लम् means:

  • Asweet
  • Btired
  • Csour✓
  • Dleft

Working

✓

आम्लम् means 'sour'. Sweet is मधुरम्, tired श्रान्तः, and left वामतः.

Q28

The chapter's title/punchline न लभ्यते चेत् आम्लं द्राक्षाफलम् means:

  • Athe grapes are always sweet
  • Bif it is not obtained, the grape-fruit is (called) sour✓
  • Cthe fox eats the grapes
  • Dthe vine is on the left

Working

✓

It means 'if the grape-fruit is not obtained, it is (called) sour'. The other readings are wrong.

Q29

न लभ्यते चेत् means:

  • Athe grapes are sweet
  • Bif (it) is not obtained✓
  • Cthe fox is tired
  • Don the left side

Working

✓

न लभ्यते चेत् means 'if it is not obtained'. The other options are unrelated phrases.

Q30

The fox's verdict 'आम्लम्' is his reaction to:

  • Afailing to reach the grapes✓
  • Beating too many grapes
  • Cfinding sweet grapes
  • Dreaching the grapes easily

Working

✓

He calls the grapes sour because he fails to reach them. Not from eating, finding sweet ones, or reaching them easily.

Q31

The moral of the fable is:

  • Aalways give up quickly
  • Bgrapes are bad for you
  • Cdo not belittle ('call sour') what you fail to get✓
  • Dnever enter a forest

Working

✓

The moral is not to belittle what you fail to get. It is not about giving up, grapes being bad, or avoiding forests.

Q32

The fox's real mistake, according to the chapter, is:

  • Athe dishonest excuse, not the failure to jump✓
  • Bjumping too high
  • Cbeing thirsty
  • Dwandering in the forest

Working

✓

The fox's real mistake is the dishonest excuse, not the failed jump. Being thirsty or wandering is not the mistake.

Q33

Calling something 'sour' just because you couldn't get it is an example of:

  • Ahonest effort
  • Bdishonest rationalisation (making an excuse)✓
  • Cnoble sacrifice
  • Dcareful planning

Working

✓

It is dishonest rationalisation - an excuse. It is not honest effort, sacrifice, or planning.

Q34

The chapter notes that working hard at something unreachable is:

  • Aexactly the fox's mistake
  • Balways foolish
  • Ca different, even noble, story - the fable targets the excuse, not the effort✓
  • Dthe same as calling it sour

Working

✓

Hard work at the unreachable is a different, even noble, story; the fable targets the dishonest excuse, not the effort.

Q35

If a student fails a test and says 'that subject is useless anyway', this is most like:

  • Ahonest study
  • Bthe Sun Salutation
  • Cgood company (सङ्गतिः)
  • Dthe fox calling the grapes sour✓

Working

✓

Dismissing a subject after failing is just like the fox calling the grapes sour. It is not honest study, the Sun Salutation, or good company.

Q36

The lesson 'do not belittle what you fail to get' warns against:

  • Atrying hard
  • Bhelping others
  • Cdrinking water when thirsty
  • Dmaking excuses to hide our own failure✓

Working

✓

The lesson warns against making excuses to hide failure. It does not warn against effort, helping others, or drinking water.

Q37

The direction words वामतः, दक्षिणतः, अग्रतः, पृष्ठतः are formed with the suffix:

  • A-आय (dative)
  • Bतसिल् (-तः)✓
  • C-एन (instrumental)
  • D-स्य (genitive)

Working

✓

They are formed with the तसिल् suffix (-तः). -आय is dative, -एन instrumental, and -स्य genitive.

Q38

Words made with the तसिल् (-तः) suffix are:

  • Adirection adverbs ('in/at/towards that direction')✓
  • Bverbs
  • Cpast-tense forms
  • Dplural nouns

Working

✓

तसिल् (-तः) words are direction adverbs. They are not verbs, past-tense forms, or plural nouns.

Q39

वामतः differs from the case forms of वाम because वामतः is:

  • Athe accusative of वाम
  • Ban adverb 'on the left side', not a noun case form✓
  • Ca verb
  • Da past tense

Working

✓

वामतः is an adverb 'on the left side', distinct from वाम's case forms. It is not an accusative, a verb, or a past tense.

Q40

Using the accusative of वाम instead of वामतः for 'on the left' is wrong because direction is shown by:

  • Athe तसिल् suffix -तः (वामतः)✓
  • Bthe dative ending
  • Cthe past-tense augment
  • Da compound

Working

✓

Direction is shown by the तसिल् suffix -तः (वामतः), not a case ending, augment, or compound.

Q50
