Worked Solutions

1. (D) Convert all: 7/3 × 9/5 × 8/7 ÷ 18/5. Multiply the first three: (7×9×8)/(3×5×7) = 504/105 = 24/5. Then divide by 18/5 means multiply by 5/18: 24/5 × 5/18 = 24/18 = 4/3. A common slip is to flip the whole product or to forget to convert 3 3/5, which inverts the answer to its reciprocal 3/4.

2. (A) Start at 5/6. After removing 2/5, remaining = 5/6 × 3/5 = 1/2. After removing 1/4, remaining = 1/2 × 3/4 = 3/8. After removing 1/3, remaining = 3/8 × 2/3 = 6/24 = 1/4. Each 'of what remains' multiplies the running leftover, not the original 5/6; subtracting the named fractions of the whole instead would mis-state the leftover.

3. (D) The target is 7/9 × 12/5 = 84/45 = 28/15. Need 4/9 × x = 28/15, so x = (28/15) ÷ (4/9) = 28/15 × 9/4 = 252/60 = 21/5 = 4 1/5. Recheck: 28/15 × 9/4 = (28×9)/(15×4) = 252/60 = 21/5 = 4 1/5. So x = 4 1/5; multiplying instead of dividing, or mis-evaluating 7/9 of 2 2/5, gives the trap values.

4. (A) After rent, 5/8 remains. After food, 3/5 of that remains: 5/8 × 3/5 = 3/8. After travel, 2/3 of that remains: 3/8 × 2/3 = 1/4 of salary = Rs 5000. So salary = 5000 × 4 = Rs 20000. Each later fraction acts on the running remainder; treating 2/5 and 1/3 as fractions of the whole salary mis-values the final leftover.

5. (D) Use the common denominator 90: 11/15 = 66/90, 13/18 = 65/90, 7/10 = 63/90, 4/5 = 72/90. The smallest numerator over 90 is 63/90 = 7/10. Comparing by numerator alone (11 vs 13 vs 7 vs 4) or by denominator alone misranks them; only a common base is reliable.

6. (C) Sum: 5/2 + 7/4 = 10/4 + 7/4 = 17/4. Difference: 10/3 − 11/6 = 20/6 − 11/6 = 9/6 = 3/2. Divide: 17/4 ÷ 3/2 = 17/4 × 2/3 = 34/12 = 17/6 = 2 5/6. Subtracting the mixed numbers without a common denominator, or flipping the dividend, breaks the result.

7. (B) Convert: 16/5 ÷ 4/3 × 9/4 ÷ 9/5. Left to right: 16/5 × 3/4 = 48/20 = 12/5; 12/5 × 9/4 = 108/20 = 27/5; 27/5 ÷ 9/5 = 27/5 × 5/9 = 27/9 = 3. Only the divisor is flipped at each division; mishandling the order or flipping a multiplier gives the wrong value.

8. (B) Distance AB = 5/2 − 5/6 = 15/6 − 5/6 = 10/6 = 5/3. AP = 3/5 × 5/3 = 1. So P = A + AP = 5/6 + 1 = 11/6 = 1 5/6. Taking 3/5 of B's value instead of of the gap, or measuring back from B, gives the wrong location.

9. (A) White hens: 5/9 × 3/4 = 15/36 = 5/12 of the flock. Non-hens = 4/9; their white share = 4/9 × 2/5 = 8/45 of the flock. Use the common denominator 180: 5/12 = 75/180 and 8/45 = 32/180, so the total white fraction = 75/180 + 32/180 = 107/180. Merely averaging 3/4 and 2/5 ignores the unequal group sizes and is the trap.

10. (B) 8 3/4 ÷ 2/3 = 35/4 × 3/2 = 105/8 = 13 1/8. Only complete jugs count, so the answer is 13 (the eighth-of-a-jug remainder cannot fill another full jug). Rounding 13 1/8 up to 14, or keeping the improper fraction 105/8 as the count, are the standard errors.

11. (D) Fraction left = 3/5 × 2/3 × 3/4 = (3×2×3)/(5×3×4) = 18/60 = 3/10. So 3/10 of the start = 27, giving start = 27 × 10/3 = 90. Subtracting 2/5, 1/3 and 1/4 of the whole separately instead of compounding the running remainders gives a wrong total.

12. (C) Convert to a common denominator 336: 9/14 = 216/336, 5/8 = 210/336, 11/16 = 231/336, 2/3 = 224/336. Ascending by numerator: 210 < 216 < 224 < 231, i.e. 5/8 < 9/14 < 2/3 < 11/16. Ranking by raw numerators or denominators alone reorders them wrongly.

13. (D) The 9 litres raise the level from 2/5 to 11/15: rise = 11/15 − 2/5 = 11/15 − 6/15 = 5/15 = 1/3 of capacity. So 1/3 of capacity = 9, capacity = 27. Using 11/15 alone, or adding the fractions instead of subtracting, gives the wrong base.

14. (C) Inside: 5 1/4 = 21/4. 21/4 ÷ 3/2 = 21/4 × 2/3 = 42/12 = 7/2. Then 7/2 × 2/3 = 14/6 = 7/3. Now 3/4 of 7/3 = 3/4 × 7/3 = 21/12 = 7/4 = 1 3/4. Doing the 'of' before the bracketed division, or flipping the wrong factor, changes the result.

15. (A) Division before subtraction: 7/8 − 1/3 = 21/24 − 8/24 = 13/24. Then 13/24 ÷ 13/24 = 1. Now 5/6 + 3/4 = 10/12 + 9/12 = 19/12. Finally 19/12 − 1 = 19/12 − 12/12 = 7/12. So the value is 7/12; treating the whole expression left-to-right (ignoring that ÷ binds before −) gives a different answer.

16. (D) Start 8/9. After tomatoes (1/2 used), remaining = 8/9 × 1/2 = 4/9. After beans (3/4 used), remaining = 4/9 × 1/4 = 1/9. Herbs use 2/5 of that: 1/9 × 2/5 = 2/45 of an acre. Each step's fraction acts on the running remainder; applying 3/4 or 2/5 to the original 8/9 misstates the herb area.

17. (B) Need 7/3 × x = 14/9, so x = (14/9) ÷ (7/3) = 14/9 × 3/7 = 42/63 = 2/3. Dividing 1 5/9 by 2 1/3 means flipping the divisor 7/3; multiplying instead gives 7/3 × 14/9 = 98/27, the trap.

18. (C) 6 1/4 ÷ 3/4 = 25/4 × 4/3 = 25/3 = 8 1/3. So 8 full pieces are cut, using 8 × 3/4 = 6 m, leaving 6 1/4 − 6 = 1/4 m. The leftover is the fraction of a piece (1/3 of a 3/4 m piece = 1/4 m), not 1/3 m; rounding the count up to 9 overcounts.

19. (D) Half of 2/7 of 105 = 1/2 × 2/7 × 105 = 1/7 × 105 = 15. This equals 3/5 of the second, so 3/5 × second = 15, second = 15 × 5/3 = 25. Forgetting to invert the 3/5 (multiplying 15 by 3/5 instead) gives 9; mishandling the 'half' gives 50.

20. (A) Reciprocal of 5/8 = 8/5 = 1.6. Reciprocal of 7/4 = 4/7 ≈ 0.57. 9/5 of 5/6 = 9/5 × 5/6 = 9/6 = 3/2 = 1.5. 7/4 ÷ 5/4 = 7/4 × 4/5 = 7/5 = 1.4. The largest is 8/5 = 1.6, the reciprocal of 5/8. The reciprocal of 7/4 is a proper-fraction value below 1 and is in fact the smallest of the four; assuming the largest fraction gives the largest reciprocal is exactly backwards.

21. (A) Together they fill 3/10 + 1/5 = 3/10 + 2/10 = 5/10 = 1/2 of the tank per hour. To fill 1 whole tank: 1 ÷ 1/2 = 2 hours. Adding the per-hour rates and then forgetting to invert (reporting 1/2 as the time) is the trap.

22. (B) Convert: 21/4 × 20/9 ÷ 10/3 ÷ 7/4. First 21/4 × 20/9 = 420/36 = 35/3. Then 35/3 ÷ 10/3 = 35/3 × 3/10 = 105/30 = 7/2. Then 7/2 ÷ 7/4 = 7/2 × 4/7 = 28/14 = 2. Flip only each divisor; one missed inversion derails the chain.

23. (A) After first: 2/3 left. After second: 2/3 × 3/5 = 2/5 left. After third: 2/5 × 1/2 = 1/5 left. The fourth child gets that 1/5 = 12 beads, so jar = 12 × 5 = 60. Each share acts on the running remainder; computing fractions of the original jar throughout misstates the final leftover.

24. (A) First bracket: 5/6 ÷ 2/3 = 5/6 × 3/2 = 15/12 = 5/4. Second: 3/4 ÷ 9/8 = 3/4 × 8/9 = 24/36 = 2/3. Third (the divisor): 5/9 × 3/4 = 15/36 = 5/12. Now 5/4 × 2/3 = 10/12 = 5/6; then 5/6 ÷ 5/12 = 5/6 × 12/5 = 60/30 = 2. Treating the last bracket as a multiplier rather than a divisor gives 5/6 × 5/12 = 25/72, the trap.

25. (C) Multiply across: 7/9 × 27/35 × 5/6 × 9/4 × 16. Step by step: 7/9 × 27/35 = 189/315 = 3/5; 3/5 × 5/6 = 15/30 = 1/2; 1/2 × 9/4 = 9/8; 9/8 × 16 = 144/8 = 18. Cancelling pairwise throughout, and multiplying the whole-number 16 only at the end, keeps the arithmetic clean; dropping any factor lowers the result.

26. (B) Both have denominator 14. The distance of 13/14 from 1 is 1 − 13/14 = 1/14; the distance of 15/14 from 1 is 15/14 − 1 = 1/14. The distances are equal, so the two fractions are equally close to 1. The reasoning that 'larger means closer' or 'smaller numerator means closer' both ignore that closeness is measured by distance, which is 1/14 on each side.

27. (D) Need (21/5) ÷ x = 7/10, so x = (21/5) ÷ (7/10) = 21/5 × 10/7 = 210/35 = 6. Multiplying 4 1/5 by 7/10 instead of dividing gives 21/5 × 7/10 = 147/50 = 2 47/50, the trap.

28. (C) Convert: 24/5 ÷ 4/3 ÷ 6/5 × 5/6. 24/5 ÷ 4/3 = 24/5 × 3/4 = 72/20 = 18/5; 18/5 ÷ 6/5 = 18/5 × 5/6 = 90/30 = 3; then 3 × 5/6 = 15/6 = 5/2 = 2 1/2. Carelessly treating the final × 5/6 as a division gives 3 ÷ 5/6 = 18/5 = 3 3/5, the trap value.

29. (B) Buried + under water = 2/5 + 1/4 = 8/20 + 5/20 = 13/20 of the pole. The part sticking out = 1 − 13/20 = 7/20 of the pole = 7 m. So pole = 7 ÷ (7/20) = 7 × 20/7 = 20 m. Adding 2/5 and 1/4 as 3/9 (adding denominators) is the classic error.

30. (B) Inside, left to right: 2/3 × 3/4 = 6/12 = 1/2; then 1/2 ÷ 5/8 = 1/2 × 8/5 = 8/10 = 4/5. Now 9/10 ÷ 4/5 = 9/10 × 5/4 = 45/40 = 9/8 = 1 1/8. Doing the inner operations out of order, or flipping the outer dividend, changes the result.

31. (C) Seniors = 5/8 × 720 = 450. Science seniors = 2/5 × 450 = 180. Both science and computing = 1/2 × 180 = 90. Equivalently the chained product 5/8 × 2/5 × 1/2 × 720 = 1/8 × 720 = 90. Each fraction must act on the running subgroup, not on the whole school of 720; doing so overcounts.

32. (B) Left to right: 12 ÷ 4/5 = 12 × 5/4 = 15; 15 ÷ 3 = 5; 5 ÷ 5/2 = 5 × 2/5 = 2. Only divisors are flipped; treating 3 as 1/3 or forgetting an inversion changes the value. The answer is the whole number 2.

33. (C) Each hour, 3/4 of the current water remains. After 3 hours: (3/4)^3 = 27/64. Subtracting 1/4 three times (giving 1/4 of the original) is wrong because each hour's loss is of the smaller current amount, not of the original.

34. (D) Compute each: 18 × 5/12 = 90/12 = 7.5; 18 ÷ 12/5 = 18 × 5/12 = 7.5; 18 ÷ 5/6 = 18 × 6/5 = 108/5 = 21.6; 18 × 9/8 = 162/8 = 20.25. The largest is 18 ÷ 5/6 = 21.6, because dividing by a proper fraction enlarges most here. Assuming the multiplication by 9/8 wins, or that 18 × 5/12 equals 18 ÷ 12/5 being largest, misses that division by 5/6 grows fastest.
