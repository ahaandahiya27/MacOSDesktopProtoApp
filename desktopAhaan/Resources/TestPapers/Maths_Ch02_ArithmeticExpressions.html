Worked Solutions

1. (A) ×/÷ before +/−: 5 × 4 = 20 first, then 30 + 20 = 50. Evaluating left-to-right (30 + 5 = 35, × 4) gives the trap 140.

2. (B) 24 ÷ 2 = 12. The others: 2 × 5 = 10, 20 − 6 = 14, 6 + 7 = 13. Only 24 ÷ 2 hits 12.

3. (C) Do the bracket first: 15 + 56 = 71, then 100 − 71 = 29. (Dropping the bracket as 100 − 15 + 56 = 141 is the classic minus-bracket trap.)

4. (D) Terms are the parts separated by + after turning subtraction into adding the inverse, and a product stays whole. So 23 − 2 × 4 + 16 has terms 23, −(2 × 4), 16. (Splitting 2 × 4 into separate terms is the trap.)

5. (A) Compare the differences instead of adding: 1022 + 128 starts 1 lower (1022 vs 1023) but gains 3 more (128 vs 125), a net +2, so 1022 + 128 is the larger sum. Check: 1023 + 125 = 1148 and 1022 + 128 = 1150. ✓

6. (B) Two terms: 6 × 3 = 18 and 4 × 8 × 5 = 160. The expression is 18 − 160 = −142. (Forgetting the sign gives 142.)

7. (C) 3 × 4 = 12, 15 − 3 = 12, 10 + 2 = 12, but 24 ÷ 3 = 8. The odd one out is 24 ÷ 3.

8. (D) Multiply first: 5 × 4 = 20. Then 30 − 20 + 2 = 10 + 2 = 12. (Left-to-right with 30 − 5 = 25 then × 4 gives traps like 102/100.)

9. (B) A minus before the bracket flips every inner sign: 500 − (250 − 100) = 500 − 250 + 100. The −100 inside becomes +100.

10. (A) Distribute the 2 over both terms: 2 × (5 + 3) = 2 × 5 + 2 × 3. Multiplying only the first term (2 × 5 + 3) is the common slip.

11. (D) 23 × 100 − 23 = 23 × (100 − 1) = 23 × 99 by the distributive property, so the two are exactly equal — no computation of the product needed.

12. (C) Total the purchases first, then subtract from 100: change = 100 − (15 + 56). The bracket forces the sum before subtraction.

13. (A) 7 × 103 = 7 × 100 + 7 × 3 = 700 + 21 = 721. (Forgetting the 7 × 3 term gives 700/730.)

14. (C) Bracket first: 18 − 6 + 4 = 16. Then 50 − 16 = 34. (50 − 18 − 6 + 4 = 30 mishandles the inner signs.)

15. (B) Values: 10 + 10 = 20 ✓, 4 × 5 = 20 ✓, 25 − 5 = 20 ✓, 60 ÷ 2 = 30 ✗. So 3 of the 4 equal 20.

16. (D) Up 6 then down 4 is 6 + (−4) = 2 m, and by commutativity (−4) + 6 = 2 m as well — same height. 6 − 4 and 4 − 6 are NOT equal (2 vs −2), so that pair fails.

17. (B) Innermost first: 3 × 4 = 12, so 15 − 12 = 3. Then 2 × 3 ÷ 2 = 3 (×/÷ left to right). Finally 6 + 3 = 9.

18. (A) By the associative/commutative properties she need not restart — just add the missing number to the running total: 11749 + 9055 = 20804.

19. (D) Regroup so 25 pairs with 4: (25 × 4) × 17 = 100 × 17 = 1700 — easy mental maths. (25 × 17) gives 425, not 1700, so option A is wrong; 17 × 4 = 68 makes B/C arithmetic wrong too.

20. (C) The bracket forces 30 + 5 = 35 first, then × 4 = 140. (Without the bracket, 30 + 5 × 4 would be 50 — option A is that trap.)

21. (C) 30 + (5 × 4) = 50 but (30 + 5) × 4 = 140, so they differ — brackets DO change the value here. (A) and (D) are false; (B) is false because brackets can change a value.

22. (A) Bracket first: 40 + 3 = 43, then 200 − 43 = 157.

23. (B) Convert the subtraction to adding the inverse: 83 − 14 + 5 = 83 + (−14) + 5, so the terms are 83, −14, 5. The +5 stays positive.

24. (D) Subtraction is not commutative: 6 − 4 = 2 but 4 − 6 = −2, so 6 − 4 = 4 − 6 is false. The other three (addition and multiplication) are genuinely commutative.

25. (A) Group: (−7 + 10) + (−11) = 3 + (−11) = −8. (Adding magnitudes as 7 + 10 + 11 = 28 ignores the signs.)

26. (B) 102 × 45 = (100 + 2) × 45 = 100 × 45 + 2 × 45. Distributing over BOTH parts. "100 × 45 + 2" forgets to multiply the 2 by 45.

27. (D) Working backwards, 100 − 15 − 56 = 100 − (15 + 56) (a minus in front of the bracket distributes the subtraction to both terms). 100 − (15 − 56) would be 100 − 15 + 56.

28. (C) ÷ before +/−: 12 ÷ 4 = 3. Then 8 + 3 − 2 = 9.

29. (A) Getting 13 means doing 2 × 5 = 10 then adding the bare 3: 10 + 3 = 13 — multiplying only the first term inside the bracket. The correct distribution is 2 × 5 + 2 × 3 = 16.

30. (C) Compare the differences: the right side is 1 smaller on top (112 vs 113) AND subtracts 1 less (24 vs 25), a net change of 0. So 113 − 25 = 112 − 24 = 88. They are equal.

31. (B) Terms: 12, −(2 × 3) = −6, +(4 × 5) = +20. Sum = 12 − 6 + 20 = 26.

32. (D) "The sum of 8 and 5" is (8 + 5); "subtract it from 40" is 40 − (8 + 5). Without the bracket, 40 − 8 + 5 = 37 only subtracts the 8.

33. (A) 6 × 98 = 6 × (100 − 2) = 6 × 100 − 6 × 2 = 600 − 12 = 588. Both terms get the 6.

34. (B) Same precedence, left to right: 500 − 250 = 250, then 250 + 100 = 350.

35. (C) 4 × (2 + 4) = 24, 30 − 6 = 24, 48 ÷ 2 = 24, but 4 × 2 + 4 = 8 + 4 = 12. So 4 × 2 + 4 is the one that is NOT 24.

36. (D) Rewrite as a sum of terms: 9 − 4 × 2 = 9 + (−(4 × 2)) = 9 + (−8). The terms are 9 and −8. (4 × 2 is one whole term.)

37. (A) Differences: the left's first number is 2 smaller (47 vs 49) but its second is 3 larger (38 vs 35) — net +1 — so 47 + 38 is greater. (47 + 38 = 85, 49 + 35 = 84.)

38. (C) Inner term: 2 × 3 = 6, so the bracket is 8 − 6 = 2. Then 3 × 2 = 6, and 6 + 5 = 11.

39. (B) Distribute: 7 × (10 − 3) = 7 × 10 − 7 × 3 = 70 − 21 = 49. "7 × 10 − 3" only multiplies the first term.

40. (D) Bracket first: 2 + 3 = 5. Then ÷ and × left to right: 60 ÷ 5 = 12, then 12 × 2 = 24. (Doing × before ÷ to get 60 ÷ 10 = 6 is the trap.)

41. (A) With only + and − (same precedence), work left to right: 18 − 5 = 13, then 13 + 3 = 16. So the brother's 16 is correct. Reema wrongly treated 5 + 3 as a bracketed sum to subtract (18 − 8 = 10).

42. (C) Factor out 25: 25 × 4 + 25 × 6 = 25 × (4 + 6) = 25 × 10 = 250.

43. (B) Values: 2 + 3 × 4 = 14, 2 × 3 × 4 = 24, 2 × 3 + 4 = 10, (2 + 3) × 4 = 20. The greatest is 2 × 3 × 4 = 24.

44. (D) Innermost: 2 × 5 = 10, so the bracket 12 − 10 = 2. Then 3 × 2 = 6, and 100 − 6 = 94. (Subtracting before multiplying, 97 × ... , or 100 − 36 = ... are sign/order traps.)

45. (A) Minus before the bracket flips both inner signs: 15 − (7 − 2) = 15 − 7 + 2 = 10. The −2 inside becomes +2.

46. (B) Pens cost 8 × 12 and notebooks cost 5 × 20; total bill = 8 × 12 + 5 × 20 = 96 + 100 = ₹196. The other forms mismatch the quantities and prices.

47. (C) 1000 × 8 − 8 = 8 × (1000 − 1) = 8 × 999 = 999 × 8 by distributivity. They are equal — no product needed.

48. (D) Bracket first: 6 + 1 = 7. Terms: 4 × 5 = 20 and 2 × 7 = 14. So 20 − 14 = 6.

49. (B) Removing a bracket after a minus flips every inner sign: 80 − (20 − 5) = 80 − 20 + 5 = 65. (80 − 20 − 5 = 55 forgets to flip the −5.)

50. (A) Five days at ₹25 each: 5 × 25 = ₹125.

51. (C) Innermost bracket: 2 + 1 = 3. Then 4 × 3 = 12, so 3 + 12 = 15. Finally 2 × 15 = 30.

52. (D) 45 × 99 = 45 × (100 − 1) = 45 × 100 − 45 = 4500 − 45 = 4455. "45 × 100 − 1" subtracts only 1, not 45.

53. (A) Same precedence, left to right: 36 ÷ 4 = 9, then 9 ÷ 3 = 3. (Division is not associative: 36 ÷ (4 ÷ 3) ≠ 3.)

54. (B) 12 − 4 − 2 = 6 and 12 − (4 + 2) = 12 − 6 = 6 — equal, since subtracting two numbers equals subtracting their sum. The others differ: (12 − 4) − 2 = 6 but 12 − (4 − 2) = 10; and 12 ÷ 4 ÷ 2 = 1.5 vs 12 ÷ (4 ÷ 2) = 6.

55. (D) Terms: 7, +(3 × 4) = +12, −(5 × 2) = −10. Sum = 7 + 12 − 10 = 9.

56. (C) Minus before the bracket flips both inner signs: 64 − (24 + 16) = 64 − 24 − 16 = 24. (64 − 24 + 16 = 56 fails to flip the +16.)

57. (A) Bracket first: 4 − 2 = 2. Terms: 2 × 5 = 10 and 3 × 2 = 6. So 10 + 10 − 6 = 14.

58. (B) Pair numbers that make round tens: 8 + 2 = 10 and 17 + 13 = 30, giving 10 + 30 = 40 — easiest mentally. The other pairings (25 & 15, or 21 & 19) are not round tens.

59. (C) First bracket: 3 × 4 = 12, so 15 − 12 = 3. Second: 6 ÷ 2 = 3, +1 = 4, then × 2 = 8. Total = 3 + 8 = 11.

60. (D) ÷ and × before − and +: 12 ÷ 4 = 3 and 2 × 5 = 10. Then 36 − 3 + 10 = 43. (Left-to-right gives 36, the trap in option A.)
