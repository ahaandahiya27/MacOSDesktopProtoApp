Worked Solutions

1. (B) Innermost first: 2 × 3 = 6, so 9 − 6 = 3. Next bracket: 2 × 3 = 6, so 24 − 6 = 18. Then 5 × 18 = 90. Finally 300 − 90 + 7 = 217. Adding 90 instead of subtracting it gives the too-large value; mishandling the innermost subtraction (9 − 6 = 15 by adding) leads to the other inflated results.

2. (A) The inner expression 40 − 15 + 8 − 6 + 3 carries signs +40, −15, +8, −6, +3. A minus before the bracket reverses each one to −40, +15, −8, +6, −3, giving 250 − 40 + 15 − 8 + 6 − 3. The wrong options keep one or more signs unflipped.

3. (B) By the distributive property, 84 × 100 − 84 × 4 = 84 × (100 − 4) = 84 × 96, the very same product. Picking one side as larger ignores that distribution over a subtraction keeps the value identical.

4. (C) Bracket first: 2 × 3 = 6. Then × and ÷ left to right: 720 ÷ 6 = 120, 120 ÷ 4 = 30, 30 × 5 = 150. Doing the ÷ 4 and × 5 as ÷ (4 × 5) = ÷20 wrongly gives 6; multiplying before dividing inflates the result.

5. (C) Each product or quotient collapses to one signed term: 6 × 4 = 24 carries its leading minus to −24; 72 ÷ 8 = 9 stays +9; 5 × 2 = 10 carries its minus to −10; plus the standalone +100. So 100, −24, +9, −10. The split-into-atoms option and the all-positive option both ignore that the × and ÷ bind their numbers together with the preceding sign.

6. (D) Factor 17 out of every term: 17 × (100 − 4 + 1) = 17 × 97. Forgetting the final +17 (the lone 17 = 17 × 1) gives 17 × 96; subtracting it instead gives 17 × 95.

7. (D) From the first to the second, the larger number rises by 2 but the amount taken away rises by 3, a net change of 2 − 3 = −1. So the second difference is 1 less, making 613 − 287 the greater. Assuming the bigger top means the bigger difference is the trap.

8. (C) Innermost: 7 − 4 = 3, then 2 × 3 = 6, then 12 + 6 = 18, then 40 − 18 = 22, then 5 × 22 = 110, finally 110 − 18 = 92. Stopping at 110 (forgetting the −18) or adding instead of subtracting the 18 gives the wrong choices.

9. (A) Three numbers all subtracted may be collected as one subtraction of their sum: 120 − (18 + 24 + 11) = 120 − 53 = 67, matching 120 − 18 − 24 − 11. Any inner minus leaves a sign unflipped on removal, and the last option leaves a stray +11.

10. (B) 49 × 51 = (50 − 1)(50 + 1) = 50 × 50 − 1, exactly one less than 2500. Splitting a square into two factors that move equally apart always loses, so 50 × 50 is greater. Believing the spread-out pair stays equal is the misconception.

11. (B) Innermost: 8 − 5 = 3. Then 36 ÷ 3 = 12. Then 144 ÷ 12 = 12. Then 6 × 2 = 12, and 12 + 12 = 24. Treating 36 ÷ (8 − 5) as 36 ÷ 3 is correct, but dividing 144 by 36 first (ignoring the inner division) gives the wrong small value.

12. (A) Net hourly pay is 95 − 15 = 80, for 8 hours: 8 × (95 − 15), and the once-only allowance adds 120 outside the hourly product: 8 × (95 − 15) + 120. Subtracting only one hour of canteen, or multiplying the daily allowance by 8, mis-models the situation.

13. (C) Remove the inner bracket first: 60 − (25 − 8) = 60 − 25 + 8. Now 200 − (60 − 25 + 8) flips each: 200 − 60 + 25 − 8 = 157. Two sign-flip stages are needed; flubbing either stage produces the other listed values.

14. (D) Left bracket: 2 + 6 = 8, so 96 ÷ 8 = 12, then 12 × 5 = 60. Right bracket: 2 × 6 = 12, 15 − 12 = 3, then 3 × 3 = 9. Finally 60 − 9 = 51. Treating ÷ 8 × 5 as ÷ (8 × 5) = ÷40 collapses the first part to 1.2 and derails the rest.

15. (B) 102 = 100 + 2, so 47 × 102 = 47 × 100 + 47 × 2 = 4700 + 94 = 4794. Multiplying only the first part and tacking on a bare +2 is half-distribution; subtracting the 47 × 2 treats 102 as 100 − 2.

16. (D) Pairing 23 + 77 = 100 and 58 + 42 = 100, plus the lone 100, gives three hundreds = 300 with only round numbers. The other pairings reach 300 too but never form clean round tens/hundreds, and addition certainly may be regrouped.

17. (C) Both pairs sum to 100. For a fixed sum, the product is larger when the factors are closer together: 38 and 62 differ by 24, while 37 and 63 differ by 26, so 38 × 62 wins. Assuming equal sums force equal products is the trap.

18. (B) Innermost: 6 + 4 = 10, then 2 × 10 = 20, then 50 − 20 = 30, then 30 ÷ 5 = 6, then 4 × 6 = 24, finally 24 + 7 = 31. Forgetting the +7 leaves 24; dividing before subtracting (50 − 2 × 10 ÷ 5) breaks the bracket structure.

19. (D) Two consecutive subtractions equal subtracting the sum: 60 − 18 − 7 = 60 − (18 + 7) = 35. The +7 version differs, division is not associative so 72 ÷ 6 ÷ 2 = 6 but 72 ÷ (6 ÷ 2) = 24, and 60 − (18 − 7) leaves a sign unflipped.

20. (A) Factor out 23: 23 × (8 + 5 − 3) = 23 × 10 = 230. Adding all three coefficients (8 + 5 + 3 = 16) gives 368; only adding the first two then ignoring −3 gives 23 × 13 = 299 territory; mis-grouping yields the other small values.

21. (A) Products: 9 × 7 = 63, 4 × 6 = 24, 5 × 8 = 40, 3 × 9 = 27. With their signs: 63 − 24 + 40 − 27 = 52. Adding every product as positive gives 154-style errors; flipping the wrong signs gives the negative or the smaller value.

22. (A) (36 − 12) ÷ 2 + 4 = 24 ÷ 2 + 4 = 12 + 4 = 16. Without brackets the expression is 36 − 6 + 4 = 34; the option 36 − (12 ÷ 2 + 4) = 26, and 36 − 12 ÷ (2 + 4) = 34, none of which equal 16.

23. (D) Take the first sum minus the second, term by term: (124 − 126) + (89 − 88) + (211 − 209) = −2 + 1 + 2 = +1. The total is positive, so the first sum is 1 greater. The trap is stopping after the first column, where the second sum looks ahead.

24. (C) Innermost: 10 − 4 = 6, then 2 × 6 = 12, then 18 + 12 = 30, then 50 − 30 = 20, then 3 × 20 = 60, finally 250 − 60 = 190. Mishandling the deepest bracket or the final subtraction gives the distractors.

25. (B) Inner first: 8 − 3 = 5, so 20 − 5 = 15, then 90 − 15 = 75. Equivalently remove inner: 20 − (8 − 3) = 20 − 8 + 3, then 90 − (20 − 8 + 3) = 90 − 20 + 8 − 3 = 75. Dropping all minus-flips gives 59; flipping inconsistently gives 81 or 65.

26. (A) 98 = 100 − 2, so 26 × 98 = 26 × 100 − 26 × 2 = 2600 − 52 = 2548. Subtracting a bare 2 is half-distribution; 90 + 8 is not a valid factor split that keeps 26 attached; adding 26 × 2 treats 98 as 102.

27. (C) A minus before a bracket flips both inner signs, a plus keeps them. So −(25 − 12) → −25 + 12, +(18 − 9) → +18 − 9, −(6 − 2) → −6 + 2. Chained: 80 − 25 + 12 + 18 − 9 − 6 + 2. Each wrong option leaves one sign unflipped.

28. (B) Collapse products and quotients (× and ÷ left to right): 60 ÷ 5 = 12; 7 × 3 = 21; 24 ÷ 8 = 3, then × 2 = 6. So 100 − 12 + 21 − 6 = 103. Doing 24 ÷ (8 × 2) = 1.5 or adding the 12 instead of subtracting gives the wrong totals.

29. (D) Both factor-pairs sum to 100. Closer factors give the larger product: 53 and 47 differ by 6, while 54 and 46 differ by 8, so 53 × 47 is greater. Equal sums do not force equal products.

30. (B) Each box yields 8 − 3 = 5 good ones, across 12 boxes: 12 × (8 − 3) = 12 × 5 = 60 (equivalently 12 × 8 − 12 × 3 = 96 − 36 = 60). Subtracting only 3 once, or reducing the box count, mis-models the per-box damage.

31. (C) Compute each: (3 + 5)(4 − 1) = 8 × 3 = 24; 3 + 5 × 4 − 1 = 3 + 20 − 1 = 22; 3 × 5 + 4 × 1 = 15 + 4 = 19; 3 × 5 − 4 + 1 = 12. The bracketed product 24 is largest; left-to-right slips would wrongly inflate the second option.

32. (A) Work from the deepest: 8 − 3 = 5; 20 − 5 = 15; 50 − 15 = 35; 60 − 35 = 25. Each nested subtraction must be finished before the layer outside it; skipping a layer yields 15, 35, or 45.

33. (A) Factor 34: 34 × (45 + 55 − 100) = 34 × 0 = 0. Since 45 + 55 = 100, the bracket vanishes. Forgetting to subtract the 100, or adding it, leaves a nonzero multiple of 34.

34. (A) Inner signs are +50, −20, +10; the leading minus flips each to −50, +20, −10, giving 180 − 50 + 20 − 10. Every wrong option leaves at least one inner sign unflipped.

35. (D) 7 × (30 − 5) = 7 × 30 − 7 × 5 takes away 35, while 7 × 30 − 5 takes away only 5, so 7 × 30 − 5 is larger. Forgetting that distribution multiplies the subtracted 5 by 7 leads to calling them equal.

36. (A) Left: 5 − 1 = 4, 2 × 4 = 8, 8 + 8 = 16, 3 × 16 = 48. Right: 4 − 2 = 2, 3 × 2 = 6, 10 − 6 = 4, 2 × 4 = 8. Then 48 − 8 = 40. Adding the two parts gives 56; mishandling a nested subtraction shifts the result to 24 or 16.

37. (D) Each team has 9 + 2 = 11 people, five teams: 5 × (9 + 2) = 55; only 3 players total leave, subtracted once: 5 × (9 + 2) − 3 = 52. Subtracting 3 inside the bracket removes 3 per team (15 total), which is wrong.

38. (D) Common factor 19: 19 × (23 + 17) = 19 × 40. The half-distributed 19 × 23 + 17 drops a factor of 19 from the second term; doubling 19 to 38 double-counts the multiplier; 19 × 391 multiplies the addends instead of adding them.

39. (B) Bracket 4 × 3 = 12, then 480 ÷ 12 = 40, then 40 ÷ 2 = 20. Right: 2 × 5 = 10, 12 − 10 = 2, 5 × 2 = 10. So 20 + 10 = 30. Doing ÷ 12 then × the right block before the second division, or 480 ÷ 12 ÷ 2 as 480 ÷ 6, throws off the total.

40. (C) Book cost 6 × p, magazine cost 4 × q add together; the voucher subtracts 50 once and the tax adds m once: 6 × p + 4 × q − 50 + m. Wrapping the magazine cost into the book bracket, multiplying the whole bill by m, or multiplying 50 by m all distort the model.

41. (D) First 7 × 9 = 63. Bracket: 6 − 1 = 5, 2 × 5 = 10, 4 + 10 = 14, 3 × 14 = 42. So 63 − 42 + 8 = 29. Adding 42 instead of subtracting gives 113; dropping the +8 gives 21-style errors near 47/5 from misordered steps.

42. (D) Rewrite each as 30 × something: 30 × 45 − 30 × 2 = 30 × 43; 30 × 42 + 30 = 30 × 43; 30 × 40 + 30 × 3 = 30 × 43; and the lone 30 × 44 = 30 × 44. Since 44 > 43, 30 × 44 is greatest. Each distractor was engineered to collapse to 30 × 43.

43. (C) Inner signs +80, −40, +20, −10, +5 each flip under the leading minus: −80, +40, −20, +10, −5, giving 300 − 80 + 40 − 20 + 10 − 5. The wrong options keep one or more original signs.

44. (C) 25 × 44 = 25 × (40 + 4) = 25 × 40 + 25 × 4 = 1000 + 100 = 1100. Adding a bare 4 is half-distribution; subtracting 25 × 4 treats 44 as 36; doubling 25 × 40 misreads the split.

45. (C) Innermost: 48 − 8 = 40, 40 ÷ 5 = 8, 8 + 2 = 10, 10 × 3 = 30. Right: 7 − 5 = 2, 4 × 2 = 8. Then 30 − 8 = 22. Forgetting the right block leaves 30; mishandling the division order gives the smaller values.

46. (D) Left to right: 96 ÷ 8 = 12, then ÷ 3 = 4. With the bracket: 8 ÷ 3 first, then 96 ÷ (8 ÷ 3) = 96 × 3 ÷ 8 = 36. Division is not associative, so the bracket genuinely changes the value; the two are not equal.

47. (B) Both pairs have the same sum 176 and centre 88. The square 88 × 88 has factors at zero spread; 86 × 90 spreads them by 4 and equals 88 × 88 − 4. For a fixed sum the product shrinks as the factors part, so 88 × 88 is greater.

48. (A) Innermost: 2 × 2 = 4, 5 − 4 = 1, 2 × 1 = 2, 3 + 2 = 5, 4 × 5 = 20, then 6 + 20 − 7 = 19. Reading 5 − 2 × 2 as (5 − 2) × 2 = 6 derails the nest into 43; dropping the −7 gives 26-ish errors.

49. (A) Each day yields 8 − 2 = 6 good machines, over 15 days: 15 × (8 − 2), and distribution gives 15 × 8 − 15 × 2 = 120 − 30 = 90. The other options either subtract only 2 total or distribute incompletely, so the stated equality fails for them.

50. (D) Inner left: 6 − 1 = 5, 2 × 5 = 10. Inner right: 2 × 5 = 10, 12 − 10 = 2. Bracket: 10 − 2 = 8, 3 × 8 = 24, then 5 + 24 = 29. Adding the inner pieces instead of subtracting, or dropping the +5, gives the wrong values.

51. (D) Three sold bags remove 3 × 8 = 24 marbles, and 2 are added back: 96 − 3 × 8 + 2 = 96 − 24 + 2 = 74. Bracketing 96 − 3 first, or counting bags instead of marbles, mis-models the loss.

52. (B) Collapse: 5 × 4 = 20 with its minus → −20; 60 ÷ 12 = 5 → +5; 2 × 3 = 6 with its minus → −6; plus +80. So 80, −20, +5, −6. The atomised list and the all-positive list both ignore that ×/÷ bind to their preceding sign.

53. (B) Brackets: 20 − 8 = 12, 2 + 1 = 3, 12 ÷ 3 = 4. Then 6 × 4 = 24, and 4 + 24 − 9 = 19. Reading (20 − 8) ÷ (2 + 1) as 20 − 8 ÷ 2 + 1 breaks the structure; dropping the −9 gives 28.

54. (A) 60 ÷ (12 ÷ 4) = 60 ÷ 3 = 20, while 60 ÷ 12 ÷ 4 = 5 ÷ 4 = 1.25. Dividing by a smaller number gives a larger result, so the bracketed form is far greater. Treating division as associative makes them look equal.

55. (C) Deepest: 60 − 25 = 35; 150 − 35 = 115; 400 − 115 = 285; 1000 − 285 = 715. Each layer must close before the one outside it; skipping or mis-signing a layer yields the other values.

56. (C) The amount by which 50 exceeds 12 + 9 is 50 − (12 + 9) = 29; tripling it needs the whole difference inside a bracket: 3 × (50 − (12 + 9)) = 3 × 29 = 87. Tripling only the 50 first, or losing the sum-bracket, changes the meaning.

57. (A) 99 × 99 = 99 × (100 − 1) = 99 × 100 − 99 × 1 = 9900 − 99 = 9801. Subtracting a bare 1 is half-distribution; 100 × 100 − 1 changes the factor entirely; adding 99 treats the multiplier as 101.

58. (B) 7 × 8 = 56. Inside: 3 × 4 = 12, 15 − 12 = 3, 2 × 3 = 6, 40 − 6 = 34. Then 56 − 34 + 6 = 28. Adding 34 instead of subtracting, or mishandling the bracket sign, produces 96 and the smaller wrong values.

59. (C) Brackets first: 72 + 18 = 90, so the expression is 90 ÷ 9 + 4 × 3 = 10 + 12 = 22, matching 90 ÷ 9 + 12. The first distractor divides only 18 by 9; the others fuse the addition into the divisor, changing the value.

60. (B) Both pairs sum to 100. The pair closer together has the larger product: 64 and 36 differ by 28, while 65 and 35 differ by 30, so 64 × 36 is greater. Equal sums do not make the products equal.
