1. A player closes a verse on the word धैर्यम्. To answer legally the next opener must echo the actual final akṣara of धैर्यम्, which is:
(A) धै — the first syllable, because the opener always copies how the closing word begins(B) म् — the bare final consonant alone, since word-final vowels are always dropped(C) धैर्य — the whole stem before the case-ending, since the ending म् is discarded(D) र्यम् (rya-m) — the syllable य bearing र् as a pre-vocalic conjunct plus म्, i.e. the next verse must open on the यर्/र्य-coloured sound, since म् is a bare nasal closing the last full syllable र्य
2. A verse ends on the word गुरुः. By the game's syllable-rule, which proposed opener is LEGAL, and why?
(A) रुचिरम्... — गुरुः ends in the full syllable रु (the visarga ः being only the nominative ending after it), so the opener must carry रु, which रुचिरम् supplies(B) गगनम्... — because the opener echoes the first syllable गु of गुरुः, here softened to ग(C) विसर्गः... — because the very last mark heard is the visarga ः, and a word beginning विसर्ग- formally names it(D) रुग्णः... is illegal while गृहम्... is legal, because only ग-initial words may answer a गुरुः-ending verse
3. Player P ends on the syllable तू (त् + long ऊ). Opponent offers a verse beginning तुरगः.... Is this legal?
(A) Legal — the consonant त matches, and the game never counts vowel length(B) Legal — a long vowel may always be answered by its short counterpart, since they share one varṇa(C) Illegal — तुरगः opens on तु (त् bearing SHORT उ), but the carried syllable तू holds a LONG ऊ; vowel-length is part of the akṣara and must be reproduced(D) Illegal — but only because तुरगः is the name of a horse-word and animal-words are barred
4. In a four-player round the carried syllable becomes the rare cluster स्त्री (a single akṣara: स्+त्+र्+ई). Why is this the HARDEST kind of hand-over to answer?
(A) Because cluster-syllables are forbidden as endings, so the player who produced स्त्री automatically forfeits(B) Because a verse must open on that exact triple-conjunct-plus-ई syllable, and very few known verses begin with so dense a cluster, so the supply of legal openers is tiny(C) Because the next player may now open with any vowel, the cluster being unplayable and therefore waived(D) Because the next player need only match the final vowel ई, making it in fact one of the easiest carries

Worked Solutions

1. (D) Antyakshari hands over the last PRONOUNCED syllable, not the first syllable of the closing word, so copying धै is wrong. A bare consonant म् is not itself a recitable verse-opener and cannot be matched alone, so taking only म् fails. Discarding the whole inflectional body to keep only the 'stem' is not a game rule. The operative final syllable here is the र्य-bearing unit that म् closes, and that conjunct-coloured syllable is what the continuation must reproduce.

2. (A) The carried unit is the last vowel-bearing syllable रु; the trailing visarga is merely the case-ending and cannot itself start a word, so naming the visarga is not a move. The opener must echo the END of the previous verse, not its opening गु, so a ग-initial reply is the wrong target. रुचिरम् opens on रु and is therefore the legal continuation; the claim that only ग-initial words may answer simply misreads which end is matched.

3. (C) A Sanskrit syllable bundles a consonant with a SPECIFIC vowel, and that vowel's length is intrinsic to the syllable; matching त alone while ignoring ऊ vs उ is exactly the precision the game enforces, so 'length never counts' is false. Long and short vowels are distinct units, so a short reply does not satisfy a long carry. There is no rule barring animal-words; the sole defect is the ऊ/उ mismatch.

4. (B) Difficulty in antyakshari is a supply problem: the rarer the required opening syllable, the fewer remembered verses begin with it. Ending on a cluster is not forbidden and triggers no automatic forfeit. The rule is never relaxed to a free vowel just because the carry is awkward. And the carry is the WHOLE akṣara स्त्री, not merely its final vowel ई, so reducing it to 'match ई' misstates the rule and would make it spuriously easy.

5. (B) Antyakshari is recited aloud, so it keys on the heard sound; the operative carry is the open syllable द्या. An anusvāra is a nasalisation mark, not a stand-alone opener, so no word 'begins with ं'. Spelling is not the operative authority over sound in an oral game. And nothing in the rule shifts the carry to the word's FIRST syllable वि — that confuses the start with the end.

6. (D) The game forces a player to isolate and reproduce the precise final syllable; धा and ते differ in both consonant and vowel, so they demand different openers — exactly the phonetic discrimination claimed. The game is not meaning-free recall, since the wrong opener is rejected on sound; it is not a translation contest; and it tests recitation, not botany.

7. (C) The rule only ever inspects the LAST syllable just heard; if it is again मा, the next player must again open on मा, and nothing caps repetition. There is no reset-after-N rule, no switch to a verse's first syllable, and a legal repeat is not a failed hand-over — it is a perfectly valid (if monotonous) continuation.

8. (C) The carried syllable is the last pronounceable one, ति with a SHORT i; the visarga is the nominative ending and does not lengthen the vowel, so a long-ī opener like तीर्थम् mismatches the vowel. The opener echoes the verse's END, not its first syllable श. The medial conjunct क्ति is internal, not the final syllable, so opening on it is wrong. Only a ति-opener faithfully carries the heard final syllable.

9. (A) Visarga sandhi is conditioned by the following sound: a voiced consonant such as ग yields र् (निर्गन्ध), whereas the voiceless फ yields the sibilant ष् (निष्फल). The outcome is therefore rule-governed, not random, and the two forms are not reversed. निष्फल is the correct output before फ, not a misprint for निर्फल — treating both as र् ignores the voiced/voiceless split.

10. (D) श्लोक ends in अ and अन्त्य begins in अ — two LIKE vowels merging to a long आ (savarṇa-dīrgha). महा ends in आ and ईश begins in ई — UNLIKE vowels merging to the guṇa vowel ए. So they are different mechanisms: one is vowel-lengthening, the other guṇa-formation. Neither involves a visarga, and not every junction yields ए — that overgeneralises guṇa to like-vowel cases where lengthening, not guṇa, applies.

11. (C) श्लोक is an अ-ending stem, not a visarga-ending one; its junction with अन्त्य is a like-vowel lengthening (savarṇa-dīrgha), giving श्लोकान्त्य. There is no visarga to delete here, so the 'hidden visarga' premise is false. Visarga-to-र् changes (as in निर्गन्ध) belong to genuinely visarga-ending stems, not to this अ-ending one, and a seam is governed by one operative rule, not a 'half-and-half' blend.

12. (A) Both seams place the visarga before a VOICED sound — व and the vowel अ — and voiced environments trigger the र् outcome; this is the shared condition. It is not a voiceless trigger (that branch behaves differently, e.g. ष्/sibilants). It is not attraction to प (which is voiceless and not even the seam-sound in पुनरपि). And it is not restricted to long vowels — अ in अपि is short.

13. (B) When a visarga meets a following र्, the visarga is dropped and the preceding short vowel lengthens to avoid a र्-र् cluster, giving नीरव. It does not double into र्र् (निर्रव), the visarga is not retained unchanged before र, and the ष्-outcome belongs to voiceless फ/प-type environments (निष्फल), not before र. The privative निः- still yields the sense 'without sound', i.e. silent.

14. (B) निर्गन्धाः carries the nominative-plural ending and qualifies किंशुकाः, the nominative-plural standard of comparison, so it is nom. pl. — 'scentless flowers'. It is not a genitive 'of', and the -आः is a real plural ending, not decoration on a singular. शोभन्ते is intransitive ('shine'), taking no object, so an accusative reading has nothing to govern the flowers as objects.

15. (B) हीन ('devoid of') governs its complement in the instrumental/separation sense, so धनहीन resolves to धनेन हीनः — 'lacking by want of wealth' — exactly parallel to विद्यया हीनः. It is a dependent तत्पुरुष, not a coordinate द्वन्द्व listing two items; it is not a comparative 'exceeding'; and हीन does not take an accusative object here.

16. (A) विवादाय is the dative of purpose: the rule तादर्थ्ये चतुर्थी puts the END/goal of an action in the fourth case, so knowledge exists 'for the sake of' quarrel. It is not a genitive of possession ('of quarrel'), not a locative of place ('in quarrel'), and not an ablative of source ('from quarrel') — each of those would misread a purpose-dative as something else.

17. (B) Each good use belongs to the SAME gift whose wicked use it opposes: knowledge → spreading-knowledge (vs quarrel), wealth → giving (vs pride), power → protection (vs oppression). The other arrangements cross the gifts — pairing wealth with protection or power with giving — which breaks the within-gift opposition the verse is built on. Only knowledge↔ज्ञान, wealth↔दान, power↔रक्षण keeps the triple aligned.

18. (B) शक्तिः is the nominative subject; परेषाम् is the genitive plural of पर ('of others'), telling whom the oppression targets; परिपीडनाय is the dative of purpose ('for oppression'). They are NOT all nominative (that would make three subjects), not the mixed instrumental/accusative/locative set, and not all datives — only परिपीडनाय expresses purpose; परेषाम् expresses the relation 'of others'.

19. (D) रक्षणाय and पीडनाय are datives of purpose, just like the verse's विवादाय/दानाय, so the line opposes two AIMS of power — protection versus oppression. They are not genitives ('of'), not locatives ('in'), and not accusative objects; reading them as anything but purpose-datives loses the 'for X, not for Y' contrast.

20. (A) Sanskrit verbs agree with their subject in number and person; विद्याहीनाः is 3rd-person nominative plural, so the verb must be 3rd plural शोभन्ते — a singular शोभते would not agree. Both शोभते and शोभन्ते are present, so the issue is number, not tense. शोभन्ते here is the intransitive 'they shine', not a passive 'are made to shine'. And it is 3rd person (about them), not 2nd person 'you all'.

21. (C) Just as क्रीडाम/वयम् are matched first-person PLURAL, a first-person SINGULAR exhortation needs BOTH the verb and pronoun to be singular: गायानि अहम्. Keeping a plural verb गायाम with a singular pronoun अहम् breaks agreement. A third-person गायतु सः means 'let HIM sing', not 'let me'. And a second-person present गायसि त्वम् is an ordinary 'you sing', not a self-exhortation.

22. (B) The contrast is between an exhortative MOOD ('let us play') and an indicative statement ('we play') — both first-person plural, both present-time in reference, so it is neither a tense change (not past), nor a person change (both are first-person 'we'), nor a voice change (neither is passive). The mood alone shifts a report into an invitation.

23. (D) The possessive suffix -विन्/-वत् attached to a quality-stem means 'endowed with', so मेधा → मेधावी, exactly parallel to यशस् → यशस्वी. अमेधस् is the OPPOSITE (lacking intellect). A dative मेधाय means 'for intellect', not 'possessing it'. And मेधाहीन means 'devoid of intellect', not 'full of' it — the हीन-compound is privative, not possessive.

24. (C) Both are dependent (तत्पुरुष) compounds whose first member विद्या is governed by the second: आलय gives 'abode OF learning' (a school), हीन gives 'devoid OF learning'. Neither is a द्वन्द्व listing two coordinate things. विद्यालय names a place, not a person, so it is not a बहुव्रीहि 'the learned one'. And neither is an अव्ययीभाव adverbial compound.

25. (B) Here भूत is the participle 'become' (भारभूत = 'having become a burden') and कर is the agentive 'causing' (भारकर = 'burden-causing'); both describe one who is, or imposes, a burden, so the gloss is exact. भूत is not 'ghost' and कर not 'hand' in this compound; भूत is not 'the earth' nor कर 'tax'; and the two are deliberately equivalent, not unrelated.

26. (D) इव is an indeclinable; it does not govern a case, so the compared standard takes whatever case its counterpart has — here किंशुकाः matches the nominative-plural विद्याहीनाः. It does not put the next word in the accusative, instrumental, or genitive; treating इव as a case-governing word is the misconception each wrong option encodes.

27. (D) The किंशुक is invoked precisely because it is bright BUT scentless; the साधारणधर्म (shared property) is 'glorious appearance, no inner essence'. Drop the scentlessness and the flower seems flawless, so the unlearned person it stands for would seem flawless too — the criticism collapses. The error is not about rarity, not a subject/object reversal, and not a literal identification of knowledge with fragrance; it is the loss of the 'something missing'.

28. (D) The simile's structure is 'attractive surface, absent inner essence': the coin's gleam = outward splendour = the किंशुक's COLOUR; the worthless metal = the missing inner worth = what the किंशुक's SCENTLESSNESS stands for. Pairing the gleam with scent inverts which feature is the deficiency. The coin lacks inner worth, so it cannot map to विद्या/शीलम् being PRESENT. And the दान/मद wealth-axis is a different verse, not the surface/substance simile.

29. (C) The verse says the knowledge-LESS do not truly shine despite looks, i.e. 'no विद्या ⇒ beauty alone fails'. The other conditionals invert or distort it: beauty does not cancel the need for knowledge; having knowledge does not guarantee beauty; and the verse never claims that lacking beauty nullifies the value of knowledge — it makes the OPPOSITE point, that knowledge is what truly adorns.

30. (B) An उपमा turns on the ABSTRACT property shared by both terms; here it is 'glorious outside, empty inside'. It is not literal redness (the person is not red), not a forest habitat (irrelevant to the unlearned), and certainly not fragrance — the किंशुक is scentLESS, so attributing rich fragrance to it inverts the very feature the simile exploits.

31. (D) He has abundant विद्या, so he is no मूर्ख (whose fault is ignorance, not malice); he chooses to harm, which is the खल's defect of WILL, and his learning serves wrongdoing, not knowledge-sharing. Learning does not auto-confer character, so he is not a साधु, and his use is the opposite of रक्षण. The किंशुक is a warning about empty beauty, not a label for corrupt eloquence, and his use is विवाद, not दान.

32. (B) A's shortfall is purely intellectual with no ill-will — a मूर्ख; B knowingly turns eloquence to harm — a खल; C uses learning well — a साधु. The learned CAN be wicked (that is exactly B), so swapping A and B's labels is wrong. Kindness/learning do not make C wicked, and not everyone imperfect is a खल — that erases the moral-vs-intellectual distinction the chapter draws.

33. (A) विद्या is studied, acquired learning; ज्ञान is direct, realised insight — distinct, so one can exist without the other, which the sage-vs-scholar case illustrates. Degrees do not guarantee insight, so 'much विद्या ⇒ equal ज्ञान' fails. ज्ञान is not confined to a school. And the two are NOT interchangeable, so asserting one without the other is coherent, not self-contradictory.

34. (A) Turning knowledge to quarrel or power to oppression is a deliberate moral choice, the signature of the खल; the मूर्ख's problem is lack of wisdom, not malice, so the verse rightly targets the खल. A मूर्ख may well possess gifts (he simply uses them unwisely, not wickedly). खल and मूर्ख are NOT synonyms, and the मूर्ख is certainly not the praised साधु.

35. (C) The elder's failing is intellectual — ignorance without ill-will — which is exactly the मूर्ख. The minister twisting the law, the cheating trader, and the riot-spreading scholar all act with deliberate ill-will, turning their position or learning to harm — that is the खल (a moral fault), not the मूर्ख (an intellectual one).

36. (A) Worth, for the chapter, rests on शीलम्; lacking it he is a भारभूतः regardless of wealth, looks, or birth. Outward goods do NOT outweigh character — that reverses the doctrine. High birth does not guarantee good character. And the किंशुक image marks empty beauty as a WARNING; handsomeness does not 'supply scent' but is exactly the colour-without-scent the verse criticises.

37. (C) When wealth and looks are EQUAL, they cannot distinguish the cousins; only the differing quality — शीलम् — can. An equal trait cannot 'tip' a balance, so wealth or beauty cannot be the decider. And birth is not stated to differ here, so it cannot be invoked as the settling factor — the chapter's answer is character.

38. (D) The chapter ranks inner goods — character and rightly-used knowledge — above outward ones (beauty, wealth, birth). It does not put wealth/looks on top, nor birth alone, nor beauty: the किंशुक's colour is CRITICISED, not celebrated, so beauty cannot head the list. Only character-and-knowledge-above-the-rest matches the doctrine.

39. (D) Giving wealth to help others is दान, wealth's good use, and quiet generosity evidences शीलम्. It is the OPPOSITE of मद (self-glorifying pride), so calling display-free giving 'pride' is wrong. The verse explicitly treats wealth on the धन→दान/मद axis, so it is not silent about wealth. And giving makes one worthy, not a burden — depleting resources is not the chapter's notion of a भारभूत.

40. (A) लक्ष्मी is an ī-stem feminine (a goddess-name) declined with feminine endings and concord, exactly like नदी or यशस्विनी; the visarga is only the nom.-sg. marker, not a gender signal. Visarga-final words are not all masculine (लक्ष्मीः itself is feminine). Abstractions are not all neuter. And gender follows the noun's stem-class, not a supposed verbal origin.

41. (A) विपरीतम् ('opposite') is precisely what makes the same gift serve harm in the wicked and good in the साधु — a true statement. The किंशुक is a WARNING and is scentLESS, so 'its fragrance proves inner worth' is doubly false. शीलम् is glossed as वृत्तम्/आचरणम् (conduct), not wealth. And खल (a moral fault) is distinct from मूर्ख (an intellectual fault), so they are not the same.

42. (D) विपरीतम् is the pivot that opposes the wicked's and the good's uses of each gift; replacing it with समानम् ('same') would collapse that contrast and falsely equate the two. समानम् does not intensify a contrast — it erases it; it is the OPPOSITE of विपरीतम्, not a synonym; and changing this one word does not turn a verse about people into one about flowers.

43. (C) वि- in विपरीत conveys turning-the-opposite-way (reversal); निर्- in निर्गन्ध conveys lack (privation) — two different prefix-senses. They are not both privative (विपरीत is not 'pathless'), not both intensive (निर्गन्ध is scentLESS, not 'very fragrant'), and the senses are as stated, not swapped between the two prefixes.

44. (A) Both attach a possessive suffix to a quality-stem to mean 'having' that quality — endowed with beauty / with fame. They are not privatives ('without'), which would be the opposite sense; not datives ('for'), which mark purpose; and not comparatives ('more than'), which would require a different formation. The shared idea is possession of the named quality.

45. (C) खलस्य and साधोः are genitives identifying whose use of the gifts is described — the wicked's versus the good's. They are not datives marking a recipient ('to'), not ablatives marking source ('from'), and not vocatives of address ('O...!'); each of those misreads the possessive/relational role of the genitive here.

46. (A) Worth needs शीलम् and right USE; a विद्वान् who lacks character, or who turns learning to quarrel, remains a burden despite his learning. So a learned person CAN be judged worthless. Acquiring विद्या does not destroy character — that is not the chapter's claim. And the chapter roots worth in character and well-used knowledge, not in wealth alone.

47. (A) Because identical gifts produce OPPOSITE outcomes depending on the holder, the gifts themselves are neutral and the user's character/intent decides their worth. No gift is intrinsically evil or good (power can be रक्षण or परिपीडन). The wicked really DO possess the gifts — they misuse them, so the comparison is real. And gifts do not automatically reform their holders — the खल proves that.

48. (D) Using power to protect is रक्षण — power's good use — which marks the साधु. Merely HAVING power is not oppression; only its misuse (परिपीडन) is, so he is no खल. Protection of the weak is substantive good, not a burden, so he is not a भारभूत. And protection is real worth, not the empty 'colour without scent' the किंशुक warns against.

49. (A) The verse is built on identical-source-but-opposite-use: दान and मद both spring from धन yet pull in contrary directions, which refutes 'treated alike'. They are not equivalent just for sharing a source. The premise that both stem from धन is actually CORRECT, so denying it is wrong. And neither belongs to विद्या or शक्ति — both are uses of wealth.

50. (B) Each link of the correct chain holds: having विद्या rules out मूर्ख, misusing it as विवाद marks the खल, and lacking शीलम् keeps his worth low. The other chains break: merely having विद्या does not make one a साधु if it is misused; wealth and looks do not produce शीलम्; and being scentless-like-a-किंशुक signals EMPTINESS, not wisdom or worth.

51. (C) The किंशुक verse condemns splendour-without-substance and the शीलम् verse roots worth in character; together they make inner quality and right use decisive. They do not exalt outward splendour as the highest good, do not restrict the lesson to the unattractive or poor, and neither verse praises wealth or beauty — the किंशुक's colour is a warning, and शीलम् is about conduct.

52. (B) Both halves of the assertion are wrong: किंशुक is the particular tree whose scentlessness is the whole point of the simile, and खल (wickedness of will) is NOT the same as मूर्ख (foolishness of intellect). A full correction must fix BOTH errors, so options that concede किंशुक is 'any flower' or that खल equals मूर्ख leave one error standing; agreeing with the classmate leaves both.

53. (A) The continuation must reproduce the carried syllable; a vowel-initial verse does not carry the required consonant onset, so it is illegal. A vowel cannot freely substitute for a consonant-syllable — that ignores the matching rule. A bare-consonant ending does not free the next player to start with 'anything'. And vowel-initial verses are NOT forbidden in general; they are simply ineligible here because they fail to match this particular carry.

54. (D) The carry is the last PRONOUNCED syllable of the recited word रक्षणाय — the final य/या-coloured unit; antyakshari does not strip away the inflected ending before matching, so dropping -य to keep णा is wrong. The opener echoes the END, not the first syllable र. And the medial conjunct क्ष is internal, not final. The whole recited word's last syllable is what passes on.

55. (C) An akṣara is a syllable (consonant + vowel), and verses open on syllables, so matching syllables ensures the next opener is a genuine, pronounceable verse-start. Individual varṇas CAN be articulated in principle, so 'cannot be pronounced' is false. The last sound is not always a vowel (verses can close on a consonant-bearing syllable), so the two are not identical. And varṇa and akṣara are RELATED concepts, not random alternatives.

56. (C) He has abundant विद्या (so not a मूर्ख) but chooses to harm (the खल's defect of will), and lacking शीलम् his learning serves विवाद, leaving his worth low. Bad conduct is not the same as ignorance, so 'must be a मूर्ख' confuses moral and intellectual faults. Learning does not auto-confer character, so he is not a साधु. And विद्या alone does not guarantee worth — the whole chapter denies it.

57. (B) Both verses converge on this: inner quality plus right use decides worth, not the bare gift. 'More gifts ⇒ more worth' is exactly what the भारभूत-doctrine denies. Beauty and wealth alone are insufficient — that is the किंशुक's lesson. And knowledge HOWEVER used does not suffice, since misused विद्या (विवाद) marks the worthless खल — use matters.

58. (B) Same gift (wealth), विपरीत use: दान is other-directed giving, मद is self-directed pride — the difference is intent and direction, not quantity. The AMOUNT is irrelevant to the moral point (a larger fortune is not more virtuous). The SOURCE is not what the verse contrasts. And 'colour or form' of wealth is a category error, importing the किंशुक's surface-beauty image where it does not apply.

59. (D) मुक्तये is the dative (चतुर्थी) of मुक्ति, marking PURPOSE under तादर्थ्ये चतुर्थी — 'knowledge for the sake of liberation' — just as विवादाय and दानाय mark purpose in the verse. It is not a genitive ('of liberation'), not a locative ('in liberation'), and not an instrumental ('by knowledge'); each misreads the purpose-dative as another relation.

60. (C) The chapter grounds worth in well-used knowledge plus character, expressly subordinating beauty, wealth, birth and fame. Wealth-and-beauty alone is what the किंशुक and भारभूत verses reject; high birth alone does not confer worth; and fame 'however gained' ignores that the chapter cares about character and right use, not mere renown. Only विद्या-rightly-used-plus-शीलम् matches the doctrine.
