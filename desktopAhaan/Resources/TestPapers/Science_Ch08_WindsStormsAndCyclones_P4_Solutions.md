## Worked Solutions

**1. (D)** Air pressure acts equally in every direction, so the large downward force on the roof is matched by an upward force from below and by the indoor air pushing out; the pushes nearly cancel, which is why the roof is not crushed even though the air column genuinely is heavy. The idea that the column is too light to matter is wrong because the load really is large; it is the balancing, not the smallness, that saves the roof. No material is immune to air pressure, and air presses on solids just as it does on liquids and bodies.

**2. (C)** The trapped air is the same throughout; heating expands it so some is pushed out as bubbles, and cooling contracts it, lowering the inside pressure so the surrounding pressure drives water up the tube. No air is created or destroyed by temperature. The bubbles are escaping air, not boiling water, and the slight change in the rigid glass cannot account for the effect.

**3. (A)** Fast-moving air exerts lower pressure, so whichever side the fast stream is on becomes the low-pressure side and the still higher-pressure air on the opposite side pushes the ball toward the stream; with the fan on the outer face, the ball is pushed outward. Moving air does not 'pull' by attraction, and a side stream certainly does affect a light hanging ball. The motion is caused by the pressure difference, not by the fan's air physically shoving the ball.

**4. (D)** Between adjacent equally spaced towns, the P-to-Q drop is 4 units while the Q-to-R drop is 12 units, so the steeper gradient Q-to-R drives the fastest wind, blowing from higher (Q) to lower (R). Merely having the highest pressure does not make P's gradient the steepest. Wind always moves from high to low pressure, so it cannot blow from R toward Q or from R toward P.

**5. (A)** Steam condensing to liquid removes nearly all the gas, producing a far larger inside-pressure drop than merely cooling air, so A crushes hard while B, whose air only contracts a little, barely dents. The metal is not softened by boiling; both cans are equally rigid. B does not start with extra air, nor does A start with a vacuum, and ice water cools air just as readily as steam.

**6. (B)** Both the planetary circulation and the local breeze arise because more-heated air expands, becomes less dense, rises, and leaves a low-pressure region that denser cooler air flows into. Warm air is lighter, not heavier, so it rises rather than sinks; sunlight warms air rather than shoving it sideways; and although the Earth turns, the question's scope attributes both flows to uneven heating, not to rotation alone.

**7. (B)** Just as deeper water carries a taller column of water pressing down, a low valley has more air above it than a peak does, so the valley feels a heavier air column and higher pressure while the peak feels less. Nearness to the Sun does not 'thin air away,' air does not lose its weight at altitude, and the effect is about the height of the air column, not the spin of the Earth.

**8. (B)** Cooling makes the air in the bag contract and become denser, so it is heavier than the surrounding air and the bag sinks, the opposite of the candle case where warmed, less-dense air made the bag rise. Cold air is denser and heavier, not lighter; temperature clearly does change the balance as the candle case proves; and ice does not emit a lifting vapour.

**9. (C)** Where air or water moves fast, its pressure drops, so the still higher-pressure fluid on the outer sides pushes the two bodies toward each other across the low-pressure gap. There is no total removal of air, the moving fluid's pressure is lower (not higher) in the gap so it cannot push them apart, and no magnetic attraction is involved.

**10. (C)** The sea breeze is driven by the land heating faster than the sea, which lowers the land's surface pressure; if the heating briefly stops, that pressure difference fades and the breeze weakens. Darkness does not by itself increase wind, the land's stored warmth means it cannot become colder than the sea within mere minutes to drive an immediate reversal, and the breeze certainly does depend on differential heating.

**11. (A)** The moisture-laden sea-to-land summer wind blows because the strongly heated land develops lower pressure than the sea; without that heating, the driving pressure difference is gone and the rains fail or weaken. Cool land does not attract more moist wind, the monsoon direction is not fixed but follows the heating, and land carries little moisture so a reversed land wind would be dry, not rainy.

**12. (A)** A thunderstorm needs warm air that rises rapidly carrying abundant vapour to condense into tall clouds; removing the warmth and moisture removes the engine and the storm cannot form. Adding more vapour, making the air warmer, or giving more room overhead all encourage the storm rather than preventing it.

**13. (B)** Light reaches the eye almost instantly while sound lags, so the time gap measures how far the sound travelled; a shorter gap means the storm is nearer. Closer storms give shorter gaps, not longer; the brightness or strength of the lightning does not set the delay; and the speed of sound does not change to close the gap, the distance does.

**14. (D)** Because the land air is rising it has left lower pressure at the land surface, and since wind blows from high to low pressure the surface wind must blow from the higher-pressure sea toward the lower-pressure land. Rising air cannot leave higher pressure behind, a sea-to-land breeze cannot come from a land-to-sea arrow, and equal pressures would produce no definite breeze at all.

**15. (C)** Fast wind across the chimney top lowers the pressure there, so the higher pressure below pushes the smoke up; remove the wind and that low-pressure draw disappears, so the upward pull weakens. Still air does not suck harder than moving air, the wind plainly does affect the smoke, and calm air does not actively push smoke back down the chimney.

**16. (B)** Wind speed depends on how steeply pressure changes with distance; the same pressure difference packed into a shorter gap gives a steeper gradient and faster wind, so Map 1's winds are stronger. More room does not let wind 'speed up' more; spacing matters as much as the values; and centres need not touch for wind to blow.

**17. (B)** The sea's slow heating and cooling, combined with the daily exchange of sea and land breezes, moderates the coast's temperatures while the inland desert, far from any such buffer, swings widely. Same-latitude places receive comparable sunlight, sea air does have weight and does store heat, and the sea's moderating influence is real, not coincidental.

**18. (B)** The cycle is fed by warm, moist air from the sea; over cooler water less warm vapour is supplied, less condensation heat is released, the low deepens more slowly and the storm weakens. Cool water gives the storm less, not more, energy; a cyclone keeps needing the warm sea to sustain itself; and the wind is not 'frozen,' it simply loses its energy source.

**19. (D)** A tornado is a narrow violent column, the eye is a small calm core perhaps tens of kilometres across, a thunderstorm cell is larger, and the whole cyclone spans hundreds of kilometres, so smallest to largest runs tornado, eye, thunderstorm cell, cyclone. The other orders wrongly make the cyclone smallest, place the tornado as larger than the eye is meant to be reversed, or scramble the relative sizes.

**20. (C)** With trapped air above, that air presses down on the water and adds to the downward load, so the upward air pressure on the card may be overcome and the card falls; in the full case the upward air pressure alone exceeds the water's weight. Less water is lighter, not heavier; air pressure acts regardless of how full the tumbler is; and the air does not adhere to and pull the card down.

**21. (D)** Modern satellite and radar warnings give enough advance notice for people to evacuate to shelters, which is the chief reason cyclone deaths have fallen even when storm strength is similar. The storms have not become gentler, storm surge still floods low coasts, and people still live along the coast, so those cannot be the main reason.

**22. (B)** Pressure is the true driver: a hot region's rising air leaves low pressure, so denser cooler high-pressure air flows in, which often coincides with the cool-to-hot direction the student noticed, but heat does not 'attract' air. The student's reason is wrong even when the direction happens to match; wind does not always blow hot-to-cold; and direction is fundamentally tied to pressure differences set up by temperature.

**23. (A)** Cooling contracts the trapped air, dropping its pressure below the outside pressure, so the higher outside pressure pushes the balloon down into the flask. Cold air contracts rather than expands, cooling certainly does reverse the swelling, and it is the air's lowered pressure, not the rubber acting on its own, that drives the balloon inward.

**24. (B)** A steadily falling barometer signals an approaching low-pressure system, and faster-spinning anemometer cups confirm strengthening wind, the two together pointing to an oncoming storm. Falling pressure with rising wind is the opposite of settled weather; the anemometer measures wind speed, not temperature; and the barometer measures pressure, not current rainfall.

**25. (B)** Whichever surface the fast air sweeps across becomes the low-pressure side; with the fast air below, the higher-pressure still air above pushes the strip down. Blowing does not always lift paper, the side you blow across does matter, and air rushing beneath lowers the pressure there rather than adding lift.

**26. (D)** A cyclone, typhoon and hurricane are the same type of large rotating tropical storm under different regional names, so an identical description simply uses a different label. They are not fixed different sizes, a typhoon is not a tornado, and they are not unrelated, they are one phenomenon named variously.

**27. (A)** A deeper central low draws surrounding higher-pressure air in more forcefully, giving faster winds, so the cyclone with the less-deep low has slower strongest winds. Higher central pressure does not fling air outward, cyclones do not all spin at one rate, and a shallower low still moves plenty of air.

**28. (C)** Passing through the centre, a ship meets the front eye wall (fierce wind), then the calm clear eye, then the rear eye wall whose winds blow from the opposite side, exactly matching the log. It is one storm, not two; the cyclone does not pause and restart; and the reversal is a consequence of the eye's structure, not random.

**29. (D)** The eye is a calm, clear, low-pressure core, and it is the surrounding eye wall that holds the fastest winds and heaviest rain, making the eye wall the most dangerous part. The eye is not the most violent place, it is not as violent as the eye wall, and the outer bands are milder than the eye wall, not fiercer.

**30. (B)** As the air is warmed it expands, becomes less dense and rises away, so less air remains pressing on the surface and the pressure there falls. Warm air is lighter, not heavier; heating clearly does change surface pressure; and the air rises away rather than vanishing, so the pressure drops but does not reach zero.

**31. (A)** The sealed air inside pushes out as strongly as in the valley, but on the peak the thinner outside air pushes in less, so the jar feels a net outward strain. Mountain air pressure is lower, not stronger, so there is no extra inward squeeze; the surroundings do change even though the trapped air does not; and air does not lose all pressure at altitude.

**32. (D)** The combination of very low central pressure and fierce winds heaps the sea into a great mound that washes far inland, flooding the coast much worse than an ordinary tide. The surge is sea water driven by the storm, not slow rainwater; the sea level rises, not drops; and it is sea water, not backward-flowing river water.

**33. (C)** A cyclone feeds on warm, moist sea air; once over land that supply is cut off, so it loses energy and weakens within a day. It does not freeze, buildings cannot wall off a storm hundreds of kilometres wide, and the eye does not snap shut on contact with land, the loss of the moist-air supply is the cause.

**34. (B)** The same trapped air expands on heating, pushing the water down, and contracts on cooling, letting the water rise, with no air created or destroyed. The student's claim is wrong, the effect is driven by the air rather than the water acting alone, and the slight glass change cannot explain the clear level shifts.

**35. (A)** While cyclone, hurricane and typhoon are indeed regional names for one type of storm, a tornado is a small violent whirling column and a thunderstorm is yet another phenomenon, so lumping all five together is wrong. The statement is not error-free, a thunderstorm is not a typhoon, and a cyclone is not a kind of tornado.

**36. (D)** The violent up-and-down motion of rapidly rising warm moist air and falling water and ice is the engine of a thunderstorm's energy and its electric activity. Quiet sinking dry air does the opposite of driving a storm, lightning is a result of the churning rather than its cause, and the ground does not heat the high cloud by touch.

**37. (C)** A sharply falling barometer together with rising wind points to an incoming low-pressure storm, so the prudent step is to warn of a possible cyclone and call the boats back. Such readings are the opposite of settled weather, they are far from meaningless, and they indicate a pressure-driven storm rather than a freeze.

**38. (D)** The fast stream is always a low-pressure region, so the surrounding higher-pressure still air pushes the ball back toward the stream no matter which way it points, keeping the ball cradled in the slanted flow. The ball is not weightless, the air is not sticky, and there is no magnetic pull involved.

**39. (A)** Storing clean drinking water is wise because a cyclone can contaminate or interrupt normal supplies. Opening all doors and windows lets damaging wind and rain in rather than helping, going to the shore exposes people to the surge and waves, and a lone tall tree is dangerous in high wind and lightning rather than protective.

**40. (A)** The chain starts with the Sun warming the sea so moist air rises (iii), then condensation releases heat that deepens the low (ii), which makes faster wind spiral in (i), and that feeds yet more moist air upward (iv), looping the cycle. Beginning with the inflowing wind, the heat release, or the feedback step puts an effect before its cause.

**41. (D)** The upward air pressure on the cover holds up the water plus the cover's own weight; a heavy plate adds enough downward weight that the air pressure may no longer balance it, so the plate can fall. Air pressure does not grip heavier covers harder, the cover's weight does matter, and air pressure does not lift heavy metal upward and away.

**42. (A)** Wind blows from high to low pressure, so surface air moves from the cool high-pressure side to the warm low-pressure side, where the warm, less-dense air rises. Air does not flow from low to high pressure, the difference does drive a flow, and warm air rises rather than sinking because it is lighter, not heavier.

**43. (B)** The sea breeze grows from the land heating faster than the sea; reflecting sunlight away heats the land less, shrinking the pressure difference and weakening the breeze. Mirrors do not generate wind, the breeze does depend on land heating, and reflecting sunlight cools the land rather than making it hotter than the sea.

**44. (A)** In winter the land cools more than the sea, so the cooler land becomes higher pressure and pushes air out to sea, and because that air comes off the land rather than over the ocean it carries little moisture and stays dry. The sea does not out-cool the land in winter, the winds are horizontal rather than vertical, and land winds are drier, not wetter, than the moist summer monsoon.

**45. (B)** Because light arrives almost instantly and sound lags, the time gap measures distance; a steady gap means steady distance and a sudden drop means the storm moved closer. The gap depends on distance, not brightness; a shrinking gap means approach, not retreat; and the speed of sound does not change, the distance does.

**46. (D)** Warm sea water is the source of the warm moist air whose rising and condensation power the storm; remove it and the cyclone is starved of energy and cannot intensify. Adding more vapour, deepening the low, or supplying more warm moist air all strengthen the storm rather than stopping it.

**47. (C)** Head-on sunlight makes the equator much warmer than the grazing-lit poles, so warm equatorial air rises and cold polar air sinks, driving the planet-scale circulation that moves heat around. The poles are colder, not warmer; the heating is uneven rather than equal; and the equatorial air does not lose its weight.

**48. (C)** Air rushes from high pressure toward low pressure, so a deeper central low means a larger pressure difference and faster inrushing wind. A deep low energises rather than stills the wind, it draws air inward rather than pushing it out, and the central pressure is directly linked to wind speed.

**49. (D)** Air genuinely has weight, so a balloon full of air is heavier than an empty one, and it is precisely this weight of the atmosphere that produces air pressure. Air is not weightless, its weight does not switch on only when it moves, and adding air makes a balloon heavier, not lighter.

**50. (C)** A narrow, very fast whirling column reaching from cloud to ground, only a few hundred metres wide, short-lived and cutting a narrow path of destruction, is a tornado. A cyclone is hundreds of kilometres across, a sea breeze is a gentle daytime coastal wind, and a high-pressure region is calm and broad, none matching the description.

**51. (D)** Cooling contracts the trapped air and lowers its pressure, so the higher-pressure surrounding room air pushes the balloon inward, the reverse of the heating case. Cooling contracts rather than expands the air, the surrounding higher-pressure air is exactly what pushes the balloon in, and the air does not freeze solid in this everyday demo.

**52. (C)** Fast wind over a roof lowers the pressure above it, creating an upward lift, so a heavy, well-anchored, low-profile roof best resists being torn off. A light loose roof is easiest to lift, a large unanchored roof offers more surface for lift, and the wind creates lift rather than pressing the roof down.

**53. (D)** The flame's draught works because warm air rises and leaves the top while fresh air is drawn in below; blocking the top halts that circulation, so the flame is starved of fresh air and dims or dies. Trapping warm air does not feed the flame more, the flame plainly depends on the through-flow of air, and blocking the top slows rather than speeds the draught.

**54. (C)** The eye sits at the centre yet is calm, while the eye wall around it is the most violent, showing that central position does not equal maximum violence. The centre is not automatically the most violent, the calm eye and the violent eye wall are distinct regions, and the outer edge is milder than the eye wall.

**55. (A)** With a large open hole, outside air rushes in to keep the inside and outside pressures equal, so no crushing pressure difference can develop and the can stays whole. An open hole prevents the crush rather than worsening it, the pressure difference cannot build on only one side, and cooling does not melt the metal.

**56. (A)** The sea breeze relies on the land being warmer and lower in pressure than the sea; cloud cover or a cold land removes or reverses that condition, so the breeze weakens or flips. Cloud and cold do not increase wind here, the breeze does depend on land temperature, and on the cold day air would tend to flow seaward as a land-type breeze, not a stronger sea breeze.

**57. (A)** Because land heats and cools faster than the sea, in summer the hot land becomes low pressure and draws in moist sea air, while in winter the cool land becomes high pressure and pushes dry air out to sea, so the same heating-rate difference underlies both seasons. The Moon does not drive the monsoon, it is the land (not the sea) that changes temperature faster, and the reversal follows heating rather than a fixed calendar alone.

**58. (B)** The hot day first warms the surface air (iv), the warm moist air rises and cools (ii), its vapour condenses into tall clouds (iii), and finally the drops grow heavy and fall as rain (i). The other orders put rain or cloud formation before the warming and rising that must come first.

**59. (C)** A deeper, still-deepening central low draws in faster winds, so X with the lower and falling pressure is the more dangerous storm. Higher central pressure means weaker, not stronger, winds; cyclones over one sea do not all behave identically; and central pressure is directly tied to wind strength.

**60. (C)** From the centre out, a cyclone has a calm low-pressure eye, then the violent eye wall with the fastest winds and heaviest rain, then outer spiralling cloud bands. The eye is low pressure and calm rather than violent and high pressure, the eye wall is the fiercest zone rather than the outer rim, and a cyclone certainly has a violent zone, namely the eye wall.
