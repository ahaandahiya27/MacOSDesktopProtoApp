Worked Solutions

1. (A) The rule is अः before a word-initial अ becomes ओ, after which that अ drops and the drop is recorded by the अवग्रह. So देवः + अपि gives देवोऽपि. No long आ forms (that needs आ/अ + अ in a single word), no nasal appears, and the avagraha is obligatory whenever the अ is elided.

2. (D) द्वीपोऽण्डमानः-type joins arise from विसर्ग → ओ plus elision of the next अ. Reversing them must give back the विसर्ग (नरः) and the full अयम्. नरो is not an independent word, the अ of अयम् genuinely existed (the avagraha records it), and ओ cannot drift to the second word.

3. (A) The विसर्ग of द्वीपः became ओ and the initial अ of अण्डमानः was then dropped (marked by ऽ). No consonant ever stood at that boundary, so nothing consonantal was deleted. The विसर्ग did not survive unchanged either.

4. (B) However it is realised in chanting, the written अवग्रह exists to flag the अ that the sandhi elided. It does not lengthen ओ, it is not mere punctuation between unrelated words, and it never brings back the विसर्ग.

5. (D) The avagraha appears only when विसर्ग→ओ is followed by an elided short अ. रामः + ईशः involves अः before ई (not अ), giving रामेशः by a different (गुण) process with no ओ and no elided अ, hence no avagraha. The other joins all have अः + अ → ओऽ.

6. (B) Sandhi merely smooths the sound at a word-boundary; both द्वीपः and अण्डमानः remain separate nominative words naming the same thing (apposition). A समास would fuse them into one word with a single ending — which has not happened here. The avagraha marks an elision, not compounding.

7. (A) द्वीपेषु (locative PLURAL) selects the Andaman out of the whole set of islands — the निर्धारण use. द्वीपे (singular) names just one island as a location, dropping the 'one out of many' nuance. The locative is never instrumental, and the singular does not mean 'two'.

8. (D) द्वीपेषु is the locative plural. The locative plural of the neuter अ-stem वन is वनेषु. वनानि is nominative/accusative plural, वनेन is instrumental singular, and वनयोः is the dual locative/genitive.

9. (A) When singling one out from a group, Sanskrit uses either the locative or the genitive of that group; so द्वीपानाम् रम्यः ('loveliest of the islands') matches द्वीपेषु रम्यः. The accusative (object), instrumental (means) and dative (purpose) express none of this selection sense.

10. (C) निर्धारण picks the superlative member out of a set, exactly as in 'among the islands, the Andaman is loveliest'. The locative does not mark a destination of motion, is not an instrument ('by'), and the plural never means precisely 'two'.

11. (B) In actual use द्वीप is one word meaning 'island' (its द्वि+अप् origin is only etymology, not the running sense), and -एषु is the regular locative plural. So द्वीपेषु is 'among the islands'. द्वीप never means 'prison', -एषु is not nominative, and the literal 'two waters' is the etymology, not the meaning in the sentence.

12. (D) Exactly two calls for the dual; the locative dual of द्वीप is द्वीपयोः. द्वीपेषु (plural) covers three or more, द्वीपे is singular, and द्वीपौ is the nominative dual (subject), not a locative.

13. (C) Tradition handed down द्वीप with a lengthened ई, not the regular यण्-outcome द्व्यप्. The word still transparently means 'two-watered' (island), so अप् is meaningful, the द्व+ईप story is fabricated, and अप् is not silent.

14. (B) अप्सरा ('moving in the waters') genuinely carries अप् 'water', just as द्वीप does. The प in रूप, दीप and पाप is incidental and unrelated to the word for water.

15. (B) 'Peninsula' literally means 'almost an island', sharing द्वीप's water-defined notion of land. paene is 'almost' (not 'two', 'inner' or 'great'), and insula is 'island' (not 'water' or 'sea').

16. (B) द्वीप names land in terms of the water encircling it; 'island', 'isle' and 'peninsula' (almost-island) all share that water-defined idea. A 'continent' is simply a large landmass, not framed by surrounding water — so it is the odd one out.

17. (C) क्तवतु (सोढवान्) is agent-oriented and active in sense; the क्त form सोढः is patient-oriented and passive. They differ in voice/orientation, not tense, and are not interchangeable; the option that swaps their orientations is the trap.

18. (A) Forming the past participle of सह् triggers a regular sound-change of the aspirate-plus-त cluster into ढ, giving सोढ. It is not a misprint, not caused by दश, and not random.

19. (A) क्तवतु participles agree with their subject in number; a plural subject takes सोढवन्तः. The singular सोढवान् would not agree, सोढवती is feminine, and सोढ्वा (gerund) cannot serve as the main verb.

20. (B) सोढवान् carries a completed (past) sense, the opposite of future, and is the क्तवतु past-active participle, not a finite future verb. The traps keep the false 'future' or treat it as a noun.

21. (C) सोढवान् (क्तवतु) is the past ACTIVE participle, सोढः (क्त) the past PASSIVE participle, and सोढ्वा the indeclinable gerund. The other rows swap voices, invent tenses, or mislabel the gerund.

22. (C) सावरकरः (masc. sg.) pairs only with सोढवान् (masc. sg.). A feminine subject needs सोढवती, a plural subject needs सोढवन्तः, and the gerund सोढ्वा cannot be the sentence's main verb.

23. (B) The क्तवतु participle marks a completed past action, so the ordeal reads as a finished historical fact — apt for a ten-year term recalled afterwards. The present, future and hypothetical readings all clash with that completed sense.

24. (B) A stretch of time over which an action runs stands in the accusative of duration: 'for ten years'. It is not the subject (Savarkar is), not an instrument, and not a single point 'in the tenth year'.

25. (D) A neuter अ-stem in the accusative takes -म् / -ए / -आणि, giving वर्षम्, वर्षे, वर्षाणि. The masculine endings (-ः/-औ/-अन्), the un-retroflexed -अनि, and the feminine series are all wrong.

26. (D) In this construction दश functions as a fixed cardinal qualifying वर्षाणि and does not inflect to दशानि; it is not a verb, and वर्षाणि (with दश) is the accusative of duration, not an object of a numeral-subject.

27. (B) Both express duration in the accusative; they differ only in number — एकं वर्षम् (singular, 'for one year') versus दश वर्षाणि (plural, 'for ten years'). Neither is nominative, locative or instrumental, and एकम् takes the singular, not the plural.

28. (D) रम्य concords with the head noun in gender, number and case. The head वनम् is neuter singular nominative, so the form is रम्यम्. रम्यः is masculine, रम्या feminine, and रम्याणि neuter plural — none match a neuter singular.

29. (A) रम्यः carries the masculine-singular-nominative ending, so by concord its head noun द्वीपः must share exactly those three features — which it does.

30. (D) कष्टम् is the direct object of सोढवान् ('he bore the suffering'), so it stands in the accusative singular neuter, and its adjective घोरम् concords. The bearer is Savarkar (nominative), so कष्टम् is not the subject, nor an instrument, nor a locative.

31. (A) Concord in number is obligatory, so both noun and adjective move to the dual together: द्वीपौ रम्यौ. द्वीप does inflect, the adjective cannot lag, and no gender change is involved.

32. (C) कारा means 'confinement/cell' and अगार 'house', so कारागार is a 'house of confinement' = prison. कारा is not 'work' (that misreads it as the Hindi कारखाना), nor 'coral', nor 'two waters'.

33. (C) मातृभूमि casts the homeland as a mother, so the sacrifice reads as patriotic love. It is not his literal mother, not service to the colonial crown, and not ownership of private farmland.

34. (D) समूह 'group' + द्वीप 'island' = a cluster of islands, i.e. an archipelago. A peninsula is one near-island, a continent a single landmass, and a reef is coral, not a group of islands.

35. (D) उप in this nominal compound carries the locational sense 'near/beside', naming the sea adjacent to Bengal — the Bay of Bengal. It does not double the word, सागर means 'sea' not 'mountain', and उप is not a negation.

36. (A) स्वातन्त्र्यवीर is a तत्पुरुष where वीर is the head noun standing in a genitive relation to स्वातन्त्र्य — 'hero of freedom'. It is not a दвандва of equals, not a self-referential बहुव्रीहि, and not a negation; the chapter's tale of suffering for national freedom fits only this.

37. (A) Here final आ meets initial अ, merging into a single long आ (दीर्घ-सन्धि), and अतीत ('gone beyond') yields 'beyond imagination'. There is no visarga, no consonant junction, and no गुण ए.

38. (D) रत्न (ending in अ) + आकर (beginning in आ) merge by दीर्घ-सन्धि to रत्नाकर, 'jewel-mine' — a poetic name for the sea. It is not कालापानी, not 'king-maker', and not 'three-storeyed'.

39. (D) त्रि 'three' + तल 'storey/level' + आत्मक 'having the nature of' = 'three-storeyed'. The coral-tile, three-prisoner and three-century readings each misread one of the parts.

40. (A) काल spans 'black' and 'time/death', so कालापानी resonates with darkness and doom — a deadly, one-way exile across the seas. The 'comfortable/short', 'sweet/cool' and 'white/sacred' readings invert the grim force the name actually carries.

41. (B) कालापानी names the prison and the dread of exile across the seas; reading it as literal black water loses that proper-name, symbolic force. There is no actual black sea, river or drinking-water at issue.

42. (C) कालापानी denotes a prison and the agony of sea-exile, so advertising it as a serene spa is wholly mistaken — there is no river or resort, not merely a spelling or colour slip.

43. (C) Literally काल 'black' + पानी 'water', yet the unit functions as a proper name for the Cellular Jail and the exile across the seas — not a real black river. The other splits and references are fabricated.

44. (A) The first element decides: कारा 'confinement' makes कारागार a prison, while 'work' makes कारखाना a factory. The other options swap the roots or invent a 'dining-hall' reading.

45. (C) मुक्तः with कारागारात् takes the ablative of separation, 'freed FROM the prison'. It is not locative ('in'), instrumental ('by'), nor genitive ('of').

46. (A) The locative of an अ-stem is -ए / -योः / -एषु, giving कारागारे, कारागारयोः, कारागारेषु. The other rows are the instrumental, the genitive, and the ablative respectively.

47. (C) The context of years of suffering endured for freedom can only describe a prison, not a factory, library or temple — so कारागार here is unambiguously 'prison'.

48. (C) A feminine इ-stem takes -ः / -ी / -अयः in the nominative singular/dual/plural, giving जनजातिः, जनजाती, जनजातयः. The other strings use impossible or neuter-style endings.

49. (B) जनजाति denotes a defined indigenous/Scheduled community; सामान्यजन is everyone in general. They are not synonyms, not swapped, and neither means prisoners or guards.

50. (A) Only the first sentence treats जनजाति as a human indigenous community that dwells on the islands. The others absurdly make tribes manufactured, swimming fish, or a single school-student.

51. (B) 'Uncontacted' means living in isolation, not extinct; the Sentinelese remain a living community, and the law is in force precisely to shield them. Both the 'extinct' premise and the 'repealed law' claim are false.

52. (C) The protection signals respect for indigenous autonomy — the opposite of forced tourism, assimilation, or forest-clearing.

53. (B) The juxtaposition holds together memory of sacrifice and present-day stewardship of indigenous peoples. It does not blame the tribes for the prison, equate the two, or deny present worth.

54. (C) प्रवाल is coral, the reef-builder of the warm seas. It is not a coconut palm, not a prison wall, and not a river inlet.

55. (D) Coral and coconut palms are markers of warm tropical-island, sea-fringed landscapes — not alpine, desert or urban settings.

56. (C) रत्नाकरः ('jewel-mine') is a poetic name for the sea, fitting सागर's sense. पर्वतराज is a mountain, कारागार a prison, and जनजाति a tribe — none names the sea.

57. (B) Only the first option names the visarga-sandhi correctly (with the avagraha marking an elided अ), treats द्वीपेषु as a locative plural of निर्धारण, and matches the chapter's praise. The rest misname the sandhi, the elided element, or the case.

58. (D) Only the first option is wholly accurate across location, beauty, the jail, Savarkar's ten years and the protected tribes. Each other option breaks on multiple clauses (wrong country, terrain, builder or duration).

59. (A) सोढवान् is the completed past-active क्तवतु of सह्, and कालापानी is the jail's dread name, not a river. The other claims falsify the participle's tense, the meaning of कारागार/जनजाति, or the meaning of द्वीप and the islands' location (they lie to the east/south-east, in the Bay of Bengal).

60. (D) Etymology is not sentence-meaning: द्वीप functions as 'island', and द्वीपेषु is 'among the islands' (locative plural), giving 'Among the islands, the Andaman is the lovely island'. The water-origin is real (so 'invented' is false), and रम्यः is the adjective, not the head noun.
