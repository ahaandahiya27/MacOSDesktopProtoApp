Set 1 · Q1–Q10

Q1

A forest is best described as:

  • Aa large number of tall trees growing close together
  • Ba place where only wild animals live, with no plants
  • Cany open area covered with grass and a few bushes
  • Da system of trees, shrubs, herbs, climbers, animals and micro-organisms living together✓

Working

✓

A forest is not just a crowd of tall trees — it is a whole system of trees, shrubs, herbs, climbers, animals, birds, insects and micro-organisms living together and depending on one another. The other options leave out most of the community.

Q2

The topmost, leafy "roof" of a forest, formed by the branches of the tallest trees, is called the:

  • Aunderstorey
  • Bforest floor
  • Chumus layer
  • Dcanopy✓

Working

✓

The canopy is the topmost branchy "roof" made by the crowns of the tallest trees. The understorey and forest floor are lower layers; humus is decomposed matter in the soil.

Q3

The branchy, leafy top part of a single tree is its:

  • Acrown✓
  • Bcanopy
  • Cunderstorey
  • Dtrunk

Working

✓

The crown is the branchy, leafy top of a single tree. The canopy is the combined roof formed by many crowns together — a property of the whole forest, not one tree.

Q4

Small trees and shrubs that grow in the shade below the tallest trees make up the:

  • Acanopy
  • Bunderstorey✓
  • Ccrown
  • Demergent layer

Working

✓

Smaller trees and shrubs growing in the shade beneath the tallest trees form the understorey. The canopy and emergent layer are at the top; the crown belongs to one tree.

Q5

Plants of the **forest floor**, such as herbs and ferns, usually receive:

  • Amore sunlight than the canopy trees
  • Bexactly the same sunlight as the canopy
  • Cno light at all, so they make food from soil
  • Donly the sunlight that filters down through the upper layers✓

Working

✓

Floor herbs and ferns get only the sunlight that filters down through the canopy and understorey above them. They still make their own food by photosynthesis (so "from soil" is wrong); they certainly do not get more light than the canopy.

Q6

The branches at the top of tall forest trees interlock to form a canopy. The main reason different plants grow at different heights in a forest is:

  • Athe soil is different at each height
  • Bcompetition for animals
  • Ccompetition for sunlight✓
  • Dtaller plants are always older

Working

✓

Plants grow at different heights chiefly because they compete for sunlight — the tallest grab it first, and shorter plants survive on what filters through. Soil and age are not the main driver.

Q7

A herb that needs full, direct sunlight is most likely to grow well:

  • Ain a sunny gap where a big tree has fallen✓
  • Bon the dark forest floor under a dense canopy
  • Conly in the topmost canopy
  • Dinside the trunk of a tree

Working

✓

A sun-loving herb does best in a bright gap where a big tree has fallen and light reaches the ground. Under a dense canopy it is too shady; it cannot live up in the canopy or inside a trunk.

Q8

Which set lists the forest layers in order from **top to bottom**?

  • Aforest floor → shrubs → understorey → canopy
  • Bcanopy → understorey → shrubs → forest floor✓
  • Cshrubs → canopy → forest floor → understorey
  • Dunderstorey → canopy → shrubs → forest floor

Working

✓

From top to bottom the layers run canopy → understorey → shrubs → forest floor. The other orders jumble these levels.

Q9

On the forest floor, dead leaves, twigs and animal droppings are broken down mainly by:

  • Atall canopy trees
  • Bsunlight alone
  • Cdecomposers such as fungi and micro-organisms✓
  • Dgrazing deer

Working

✓

Dead leaves, twigs and droppings on the floor are broken down by decomposers — fungi and micro-organisms. Canopy trees, sunlight alone and grazing deer do not do this recycling work.

Q10

The dark, soft, nutrient-rich material formed when decomposers break down dead leaves and droppings is called:

  • Asand
  • Bcanopy
  • Chumus✓
  • Dnectar

Working

✓

The dark, nutrient-rich material made when decomposers break down dead matter is humus. Canopy is a layer, sand is mineral grains, and nectar is a flower sugar.

Set 2 · Q11–Q20

Q11

A student says, "Decomposers are harmful because they make things rot and should be removed from the forest." The best correction is:

  • Acorrect — rotting spreads disease, so decomposers are harmful
  • Bdecomposers are essential; they recycle dead matter into humus that feeds new plants✓
  • Cdecomposers only live in ponds, not forests
  • Ddecomposers eat living trees, so they damage the forest

Working

✓

This is the decomposer trap. Decomposers are essential, not harmful: by rotting dead matter into humus they recycle nutrients that feed new plants. Without them the forest would choke on dead material.

Q12

Imagine every decomposer in a forest suddenly vanished. Over time the forest would most likely:

  • Astay exactly the same
  • Bbecome buried under dead matter and starved of recycled nutrients✓
  • Cgrow faster because nothing breaks down its leaves
  • Dimmediately turn into a desert overnight

Working

✓

With no decomposers, dead leaves and bodies would pile up and the locked-in nutrients would never return to the soil — the forest would be buried in dead matter and starved of recycled nutrients. (It would not improve, nor change overnight.)

Q13

Decomposers return the nutrients from dead plants and animals to the:

  • Aair, where plants cannot use them
  • Bcanopy, where animals eat them
  • Csoil, where plant roots can take them up again✓
  • Dsunlight, which carries them away

Working

✓

Decomposers return nutrients to the soil, where plant roots can absorb them again. This closes the loop; the air, canopy and sunlight do not store these nutrients for roots.

Q14

The continuous movement of nutrients from soil → plants → animals → dead matter → decomposers → soil is best called the:

  • Awater cycle only
  • Bnutrient (recycling) cycle of the forest✓
  • Cfood chain reversing itself
  • Dcarbon-only cycle

Working

✓

The repeating path soil → plants → animals → dead matter → decomposers → soil is the forest's nutrient (recycling) cycle. It involves more than water and more than carbon, and it is not a food chain run backwards.

Q15

In a forest food chain, the **producers** are the:

  • Atigers and other carnivores
  • Bdeer and other herbivores
  • Cgreen plants✓
  • Dfungi and bacteria

Working

✓

Producers are the green plants — they make food from sunlight. Herbivores (deer), carnivores (tiger) and decomposers (fungi) all depend on that food; they do not produce it.

Q16

In the food chain grass → deer → tiger, the deer is a:

  • Aproducer
  • Bdecomposer
  • Cherbivore (and the tiger's prey)✓
  • Dcarnivore that eats the tiger

Working

✓

In grass → deer → tiger, the deer eats the plant (so it is a herbivore) and is itself eaten by the tiger (so it is the tiger's prey). It is neither a producer nor a carnivore.

Q17

A **food web** differs from a single food chain because a food web:

  • Ahas no producers
  • Bleaves out all the animals
  • Calways has exactly three organisms
  • Dshows many interlinked food chains in the forest✓

Working

✓

A food web is many food chains interlinked, because most animals eat, and are eaten by, more than one kind of organism. It still has producers and animals; it is not fixed at three organisms.

Q18

In this web, the grasshopper is eaten by both:

  • Agrass and frogs
  • Btigers and hawks
  • Cdeer and snakes
  • Dfrogs and small birds✓

Working

✓

Trace Web 1: grasshoppers are eaten by frogs, and the small birds also eat grasshoppers. So the grasshopper is eaten by both frogs and small birds. Tigers eat deer (not grasshoppers); snakes eat frogs.

Q19

If a disease killed almost all the **frogs** in this web, the most direct effect would be:

  • Athe grass would disappear
  • Bgrasshopper numbers would tend to rise and snake food would fall✓
  • Ctigers would immediately die out
  • Dnothing would change anywhere

Working

✓

Frogs eat grasshoppers and are eaten by snakes. Remove most frogs and grasshoppers face one fewer predator (numbers tend to rise) while snakes lose a food source (their food falls). Grass would not vanish, and tigers (on the deer chain) are unaffected at first.

Q20

In this web, which animal eats prey from **two different** food chains?

  • Athe hawk (it eats both snakes and small birds)✓
  • Bthe grasshopper
  • Cthe deer
  • Dthe frog

Working

✓

The hawk eats snakes (grass → grasshopper → frog → snake → hawk) and also eats small birds (grass → grasshopper → small bird → hawk) — prey from two different chains. The deer, grasshopper and frog each sit on a single chain here.

Set 3 · Q21–Q30

Q21

The ultimate source of energy for **every** organism in this forest web is:

  • Athe tiger at the top
  • Bthe humus in the soil
  • Cthe Sun, captured by green plants✓
  • Dthe decomposers on the floor

Working

✓

Every chain in the web starts with green plants, which capture energy from the Sun. So the ultimate energy source for all organisms is the Sun. The tiger, humus and decomposers are not energy sources for the whole web.

Q22

If hunters removed all the **tigers** from this web, the most likely first effect is that:

  • Athe deer population would tend to increase✓
  • Bthe grass would vanish at once
  • Cthe hawks would stop eating snakes
  • Dthe frogs would start eating tigers

Working

✓

Tigers eat deer. Remove the tigers and the deer lose their main predator, so the deer population tends to increase first. Grass would not vanish at once, and the hawk-snake link is on another chain.

Q23

Forests are often called the "green lungs" of the Earth because their plants:

  • Atake in oxygen and release carbon dioxide
  • Brelease oxygen and absorb carbon dioxide✓
  • Cbreathe exactly like animal lungs
  • Dstore water in the air

Working

✓

Forests are the "green lungs" because their plants release oxygen and absorb carbon dioxide during photosynthesis. They do not breathe like animal lungs, and option A reverses the gases.

Q24

During the day, a forest helps keep the air's gases balanced mainly because its plants:

  • Aonly respire and give out CO₂
  • Bphotosynthesise, taking in CO₂ and giving out O₂✓
  • Cabsorb all oxygen from the air
  • Dmake no gases at all

Working

✓

By day, forest plants photosynthesise — taking in CO₂ and giving out O₂ — which helps keep the air's gases in balance. They do not only respire, nor absorb all the oxygen, nor make no gases.

Q25

Cutting down a large area of forest tends to **increase** the amount of which gas in the air?

  • Aoxygen
  • Bwater vapour only
  • Cnitrogen only
  • Dcarbon dioxide✓

Working

✓

Cutting a large forest removes plants that were absorbing CO₂, so the amount of carbon dioxide in the air tends to rise. Oxygen, nitrogen and water vapour are not the gas that increases here.

Q26

Forest roots help **prevent floods and soil erosion** mainly by:

  • Agripping the soil and slowing rainwater so it soaks in✓
  • Bdrinking up entire rivers
  • Cblocking sunlight from the ground
  • Dpushing the soil downhill faster

Working

✓

Roots grip the soil and the leaf litter slows the rain so water soaks in rather than rushing off — this is how forests curb erosion and floods. They do not drink rivers dry or push soil downhill.

Q27

After a hillside forest is cut down, heavy rain on the bare slope is most likely to cause:

  • Aless soil erosion than before
  • Bmore soil erosion and a greater chance of floods✓
  • Cthe soil to become richer in humus
  • Dno change to the slope at all

Working

✓

Strip a hillside of forest and there are no roots to hold the soil or slow the rain, so heavy rain causes more soil erosion and a greater flood risk — the opposite of "less erosion."

Q28

Forests help **recharge groundwater** because tree cover and leaf litter:

  • Aturn groundwater into oxygen
  • Bseal the soil so no water can enter
  • Csend all rainwater straight to the sea
  • Dlet rainwater soak slowly into the ground instead of running off✓

Working

✓

Forest cover and litter let rainwater soak slowly into the ground, recharging the groundwater, instead of running straight off. They do not seal the soil or rush water to the sea.

Q29

Through transpiration and shade, large forests tend to influence the local climate by:

  • Amaking the area hotter and drier
  • Bstopping all wind permanently
  • Cadding moisture to the air and helping bring rainfall✓
  • Dhaving no effect on rainfall at all

Working

✓

Through transpiration (releasing water vapour) and shade, large forests add moisture to the air and help bring rainfall, cooling and dampening the local climate — they do not dry the area or stop wind.

Q30

A forest is called a "lifeline" because it provides, all at once:

  • Aonly timber for furniture
  • Boxygen, clean water, soil protection, climate control and habitat for wildlife✓
  • Conly a home for tigers
  • Donly firewood for villages

Working

✓

A forest is a "lifeline" because it gives oxygen, clean water, soil protection, climate control and wildlife habitat — all at once. The other options each name just one narrow use.

Set 4 · Q31–Q40

Q31

Which of these is a **product** we get from forests?

  • Aplastic
  • Bpetrol
  • Cgum, honey, timber and medicinal herbs✓
  • Dglass

Working

✓

Gum, honey, timber and medicinal herbs are genuine forest products. Plastic, petrol and glass are manufactured from other raw materials, not harvested from a forest.

Q32

A single fallen forest tree slowly rots over years. The nutrients locked in its wood are:

  • Alost forever from the forest
  • Breturned to the soil by decomposers for new plants to use✓
  • Ccarried up into the canopy
  • Dturned directly into sunlight

Working

✓

As a fallen tree rots, decomposers return the nutrients in its wood to the soil for new plants to use — they are recycled, not lost, raised to the canopy, or turned into sunlight.

Q33

Which chain correctly shows the path of **energy** in a forest?

  • Atiger → deer → grass → Sun
  • Bdeer → tiger → grass → Sun
  • Cgrass → Sun → tiger → deer
  • DSun → grass → deer → tiger✓

Working

✓

Energy flows Sun → grass → deer → tiger: plants capture the Sun's energy, herbivores eat plants, carnivores eat herbivores. The other chains run the arrows backwards or jumble the order.

Q34

In a forest, the number of **carnivores** like tigers is usually much smaller than the number of deer because:

  • Amuch less energy is available higher up the food chain✓
  • Btigers cannot move
  • Cdeer never get eaten
  • Dcarnivores do not need food

Working

✓

Much less energy is available higher up a food chain (most is used up at each step), so the top carnivores like tigers can only be few, while the deer below them are many. The other options are false.

Q35

A wheat field has millions of plants of one kind growing together for one season. It is **not** called a forest because a forest:

  • Amust contain at least one tiger
  • Bis a stable, many-layered community of many different species lasting many years✓
  • Cis always smaller than a field
  • Dhas no animals in it

Working

✓

A wheat field is one species, one layer, lasting one season; a forest is a stable, many-layered community of many different species lasting many years — that is the real difference, not the presence of a tiger or its size.

Q36

Which statement about a forest is **correct**?

  • Aa forest is simply a collection of trees and nothing else
  • Bdecomposers play no role in a forest
  • Cforests have no effect on the air we breathe
  • Da forest is a community of plants, animals and micro-organisms that depend on one another✓

Working

✓

The correct statement is that a forest is a community of plants, animals and micro-organisms that depend on one another. "Just trees," "no effect on air," and "decomposers play no role" are all wrong.

Q37

After a forest fire clears an open patch, over the following years the area usually:

  • Astays bare rock forever
  • Bbecomes a desert that never recovers
  • Cturns instantly into a full grown forest the next morning
  • Dis recolonised by herbs, then shrubs, then trees — the forest regrows✓

Working

✓

After a clearing, the patch is recolonised step by step — first herbs, then shrubs, then trees — and the forest regrows. It neither stays bare forever nor springs up overnight nor becomes permanent desert.

Q38

The fact that a burnt or cleared forest can slowly grow back shows that a forest is:

  • Adynamic — it grows and regenerates naturally over time✓
  • Bcompletely unchanging and fixed
  • Cman-made and cannot recover on its own
  • Dthe same as a crop field

Working

✓

The fact that a burnt or cleared forest grows back shows a forest is dynamic — it grows and regenerates naturally over time. It is therefore not fixed, not man-made-only, and not the same as a crop field.

Q39

Of canopy, understorey and forest floor, the layer that receives the **least** sunlight is the:

  • Aforest floor✓
  • Bunderstorey
  • Ccanopy
  • Dall three receive equal sunlight

Working

✓

Sunlight is caught first by the canopy, less reaches the understorey, and least of all reaches the forest floor at the bottom. So the forest floor receives the least sunlight.

Q40

Which organism in a forest is a **decomposer**?

  • Aa deer
  • Ba mango tree
  • Ca mushroom (fungus)✓
  • Da hawk

Working

✓

A mushroom is a fungus — a decomposer that breaks down dead matter. A deer is a herbivore, a mango tree a producer, and a hawk a carnivore.

Set 5 · Q41–Q50

Q41

In this second web, the fox gets its food from:

  • Aplants directly
  • Btwo chains — it eats both rabbits and snakes✓
  • Cinsects only
  • Dthe fungi on the floor

Working

✓

In Web 2 the fox eats rabbits (trees → rabbit → fox) and also eats snakes (trees → insect → small bird → snake → fox) — food from two chains. It does not eat plants, insects directly, or the floor fungi.

Q42

If the **rabbits** in this web were wiped out, which animal would be most directly short of food?

  • Athe snake
  • Bthe small bird
  • Cthe insect
  • Dthe fox (it loses one of its prey)✓

Working

✓

Foxes eat both rabbits and snakes. Wipe out the rabbits and the fox loses one of its two prey, so it is the animal most directly short of food. Snakes, small birds and insects do not feed on rabbits.

Q43

In this second web, the dead bodies of foxes, snakes and trees are returned to the soil by the:

  • Afungi and bacteria (decomposers)✓
  • Bsmall birds
  • Crabbits
  • Dinsects

Working

✓

When foxes, snakes and trees die, the fungi and bacteria (decomposers) on the floor break them down and return their nutrients to the soil. Rabbits, small birds and insects are not decomposers.

Q44

If the number of **insects** in this web increased sharply, the most likely first effect is that:

  • Asmall birds would have more food and could increase✓
  • Bthe trees would grow taller
  • Cthe foxes would disappear at once
  • Dthe fungi would stop working

Working

✓

Small birds eat insects, so a sharp rise in insects means more food for small birds, whose numbers could then increase first. The trees, foxes and fungi are not the most direct first responders.

Q45

Counting the chains in this web, the rabbit and the insect both depend, in the end, on the:

  • Afoxes for energy
  • Bsnakes for energy
  • Ctrees and shrubs (the producers) for energy✓
  • Ddecomposers for energy

Working

✓

Follow every chain back to its start: both the rabbit (trees → rabbit) and the insect (trees → insect) feed on the trees and shrubs — the producers — for their energy. Foxes, snakes and decomposers are not the base.

Q46

Match the layer to its typical resident: the **canopy** is the most likely home of:

  • Abirds and monkeys feeding among the high branches✓
  • Bground beetles in the litter
  • Cearthworms and fungi
  • Dmoss growing in deep shade

Working

✓

The canopy, high among the branches, is the typical home of birds and monkeys. Earthworms, fungi, litter beetles and deep-shade moss all belong to the forest floor, not the canopy.

Q47

Earthworms, fungi and many beetles live mainly on or in the:

  • Acanopy
  • Bcrowns of the tallest trees
  • Cemergent layer
  • Dforest floor✓

Working

✓

Earthworms, fungi and many beetles live mainly on or in the forest floor, among the leaf litter — not in the canopy, crowns or emergent layer at the top.

Q48

Which is the best reason a forest's soil is usually **rich and fertile**?

  • Athe canopy blocks all the rain
  • Banimals plough the soil with their feet
  • Cdecomposers constantly turn fallen leaves and dead matter into humus✓
  • Dthere is no sunlight at the bottom

Working

✓

Forest soil is rich because decomposers constantly turn fallen leaves and dead matter into humus, recharging the soil with nutrients. Blocked rain, animal feet and lack of sunlight do not make soil fertile.

Q49

A keystone animal such as the tiger keeps deer numbers in check. If tigers vanish, deer may multiply and overgraze. This shows that in a forest:

  • Aremoving one species can disturb many others✓
  • Banimals have no effect on plants
  • Cplants and animals are completely independent
  • Dmore deer is always good for the forest

Working

✓

When losing the tiger lets the deer multiply and overgraze, it shows that removing one species can disturb many others in a forest. Animals clearly do affect plants, and more deer is not always good.

Q50

Which chain of events follows large-scale **deforestation**?

  • Afewer trees → more soil erosion, less rainfall, lost habitat and more CO₂✓
  • Bmore trees → more rain → richer soil
  • Cfewer trees → more oxygen in the air
  • Dfewer trees → groundwater rises everywhere

Working

✓

Large-scale deforestation sets off: fewer trees → more soil erosion, less rainfall, lost habitat and more CO₂. The other chains wrongly claim benefits from cutting trees.

Set 6 · Q51–Q60

Q51

A villager notices the stream near a freshly cleared forest now floods in the rains and dries up in summer. The best explanation is that, without the forest:

  • Athe trees were drinking all the floodwater
  • Bthe Sun stopped shining on the hill
  • Cthe stream moved to a new place
  • Drainwater now runs off fast instead of soaking in to feed the stream slowly✓

Working

✓

Without the forest, rainwater now runs off fast instead of soaking in, so the stream floods in the rains and dries up in summer — the slow, steady release is gone. The trees were not "drinking floodwater," and the Sun and stream did not move.

Q52

The plants of a forest are the **producers** because they:

  • Aeat other animals
  • Bbreak down dead leaves
  • Cmake their own food using sunlight, water and carbon dioxide✓
  • Dcannot make food and must be fed

Working

✓

Plants are producers because they make their own food using sunlight, water and carbon dioxide. They do not eat animals, break down dead leaves, or depend on being fed.

Q53

Which pairing of forest role and organism is **correct**?

  • Aproducer — tiger
  • Bherbivore — deer✓
  • Cdecomposer — deer
  • Dcarnivore — grass

Working

✓

The correct pairing is herbivore — deer. Tigers are carnivores (not producers), deer are not decomposers, and grass is a producer (not a carnivore).

Q54

Two students argue. Aman: "A forest is only useful for the wood we cut from it." Bina: "A forest gives oxygen, holds the soil, recharges water and shelters wildlife." Who is right?

  • AAman, because wood is the only product
  • Bboth are equally wrong
  • CBina, because a forest provides many life-supporting services✓
  • Dneither — forests do nothing useful

Working

✓

Bina is right: a forest provides many life-supporting services — oxygen, soil holding, water recharge and wildlife shelter — not just wood. Aman's "wood only" view misses almost everything a forest does.

Q55

In the food chain leaves → caterpillar → small bird → snake, the **snake** is a:

  • Acarnivore at the top of this chain✓
  • Bherbivore
  • Cproducer
  • Ddecomposer

Working

✓

In leaves → caterpillar → small bird → snake, the snake eats the bird and nothing in this chain eats the snake, so it is a carnivore at the top of this chain — not a producer, herbivore or decomposer.

Q56

Why does removing the **canopy** trees badly hurt the lower forest layers and animals?

  • Ait suddenly exposes shade plants and floor animals to harsh sun, heat and rain✓
  • Bit has no effect on them
  • Cit makes the floor colder than the canopy
  • Dit gives the floor plants too little carbon dioxide

Working

✓

Removing the canopy suddenly exposes shade-adapted plants and floor animals to harsh sun, heat and pounding rain that they are not built for, harming the lower layers. It does not leave them unaffected or make the floor colder.

Q57

Sunlight filtering through the canopy is **important** for the forest because it:

  • Ais harmful and should be blocked completely
  • Blets shade-loving understorey and floor plants make enough food to survive✓
  • Conly warms the animals, not the plants
  • Dhas no role once the canopy exists

Working

✓

Filtered sunlight matters because it lets shade-loving understorey and floor plants make enough food to survive. The light is helpful, not harmful, and it feeds plants, not just animals.

Q58

A forest captures and stores carbon mainly in its:

  • Athe empty air between trees
  • Banimals' fur
  • Cflowing streams
  • Dwood, leaves and soil (as plants take in CO₂)✓

Working

✓

A forest stores carbon mainly in its wood, leaves and soil as the plants take in CO₂ and build it into their tissues and humus. Animal fur, streams and the air between trees are not the main carbon stores.

Q59

Putting it together: which single statement best explains why a forest is called "our lifeline"?

  • Ait only gives us timber and firewood to sell
  • Bit matters only to the animals that live in it, not to people
  • Cit is simply a large group of trees with no other use
  • Dit supplies oxygen, recycles nutrients, holds soil and water, balances climate, and shelters countless species — all together✓

Working

✓

A forest is "our lifeline" because, all together, it supplies oxygen, recycles nutrients, holds soil and water, balances the climate and shelters countless species. The single-use options each miss the bigger picture.

Q60

Which action would best help **conserve** a forest and the services it provides?

  • Aclearing it for a single crop to use the land
  • Bremoving all the decomposers to stop rotting
  • Cprotecting it and planting trees where forest has been lost✓
  • Dcutting the canopy so more light reaches the floor

Working

✓

Conserving a forest means protecting it and planting trees where forest has been lost (afforestation). Clearing it for one crop, removing decomposers, or cutting the canopy would all damage the forest, not conserve it. --- *Solutions complete: 60 / 60.*
