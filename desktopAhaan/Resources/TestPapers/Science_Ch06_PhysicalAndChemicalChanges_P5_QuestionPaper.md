**Marking scheme:** **+4** for each correct answer · **−1** for each wrong answer · **0** if unattempted.

**Instructions:** Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. This hardest paper layers several traps at once: a single clue (heat, light, colour, gas, irreversibility, dissolving) is never decisive; mass of the solid product is not the same as conservation of total mass; one event can be physical and chemical together; controlled rust set-ups isolate exactly one factor, so read which factor each pair actually changes; galvanising's sacrificial action is chemical protection, not mere sealing; crystallisation, dissolving, magnetising and state-changes stay physical no matter how dramatic; and counting 'clues' or classifying lists requires checking every member, not the first. Do not write on this paper — mark answers on your answer sheet.

1. A change is observed in four stages: first a clear liquid warms slightly, then it turns from pale yellow to deep orange, then a fine white solid drops out, and finally the original yellow liquid cannot be got back by evaporating. A student asks which one of these four observations, taken entirely alone, would still leave room for doubt that a new substance had formed. Which is it?
   (A) the colour shifting from pale yellow to deep orange, because a colour change can only happen when a new substance forms
   (B) the slight warming, because both physical and chemical changes can release a little heat, so warmth on its own settles nothing
   (C) the fine white solid dropping out, because an insoluble solid appearing from a clear liquid can only be a new substance
   (D) the failure to recover the yellow liquid by evaporating, because that test can sometimes pass even when nothing new formed

2. A 4 g coil of magnesium ribbon is burnt completely; the white magnesium oxide collected weighs 6.6 g. In a separate tray the burning is repeated but this time the rising white smoke is trapped and weighed too, giving 0.4 g of escaped powder. Which single statement about masses is correct?
   (A) the powder gained 2.6 g because burning created new matter from the flame's energy, and the escaped smoke proves some of that new matter floated away
   (B) since the metal was only 4 g, any product over 4 g must include weighing errors, so the true oxide mass is really 4 g
   (C) the 0.4 g of escaped smoke shows magnesium was lost as smoke, so the reaction destroyed 0.4 g of magnesium permanently
   (D) the 2.6 g gain in the collected powder is oxygen that joined from the air, and the 0.4 g of escaped smoke means part of the oxide was simply carried off, not that matter was destroyed

3. Five changes are listed: (i) a wax candle melting, (ii) the same wax vapour burning at the wick, (iii) ice subliming inside a freezer, (iv) milk curdling, (v) a copper wire being drawn thinner. How many of these five are chemical changes?
   (A) three — the burning, the curdling, and the subliming, because subliming changes the solid straight into a different state
   (B) two — the wax vapour burning and the milk curdling, because only those two form new substances
   (C) one — only the burning, because curdling milk merely thickens the same milk without making anything new
   (D) four — every change except drawing the wire, because all of those involve heat or spoiling

4. A controlled rust study uses four sealed tubes, each with one identical nail: T1 = freshly boiled water sealed under oil; T2 = unboiled tap water open to air; T3 = a few drops of water in air sealed with a drying agent removed; T4 = dry air with a drying agent and no water. After two weeks only T2 shows heavy rust. To prove specifically that air (oxygen) is required while water is present, which single pair of tubes should be compared?
   (A) T2 versus T4, because one is wet and open while the other is dry and sealed, which directly shows what rusting needs
   (B) T1 versus T4, because neither rusts, and comparing two tubes that both stay clean confirms air is needed
   (C) T1 versus T2, because both contain liquid water yet only T2 lets air reach it, so they differ only in the presence of air
   (D) T3 versus T4, because both keep a drying agent and so isolate the effect of oxygen cleanly

6. Hot saturated potash-alum solution is split: portion A is cooled very slowly in a still beaker, portion B is cooled quickly by standing the beaker in iced water, and portion C is left in an open dish in the warm sun. All three yield solid alum. Which statement is fully correct?

   (D) only the slowly cooled portion is physical; the fast-cooled and sun-dried portions form new substances because the change was hurried

7. A teacher passes exhaled breath through fresh limewater until it is milky, then keeps blowing hard for several minutes until the milkiness clears. A student claims the clearing 'proves the whole thing was only physical bubbling.' The best verdict is:

9. Three claims are made about telling change types apart. (P) 'If a change is reversible it must be physical.' (Q) 'If a change is irreversible it must be chemical.'
