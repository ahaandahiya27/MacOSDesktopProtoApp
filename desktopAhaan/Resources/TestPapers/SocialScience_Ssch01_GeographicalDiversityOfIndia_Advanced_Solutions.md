# Social Science — Chapter 1: Geographical Diversity of India · Paper 2 (Advanced) Solutions

**1. (C)** The Himalayas rose where the northward-drifting **Indian plate** rammed into the **Eurasian plate**. The collision crumpled the rocks between them into the youngest, highest mountains on Earth — and the push still continues.

**2. (A)** Fold mountains form when horizontal rock layers are **squeezed and buckled** by compression — exactly what happened as India pressed into Asia. Lava layers make volcanic mountains; dropped blocks make block (fault) mountains.

**3. (D)** The Andaman arc sits above a **subduction zone**, where one oceanic plate dives beneath another and melts to feed volcanoes like Barren Island. The Himalayas formed by a continent–continent **collision** with no plate diving deep enough to melt, so no volcanoes — not a matter of cold or rock type.

**4. (B)** The Northern Plains are alluvium dropped by the **Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra** systems. The Narmada/Tapi (west-flowing) and the Godavari/Krishna/Kaveri (Peninsular) built other landscapes, not the great northern plain.

**5. (A)** Just below the foothills comes the pebbly, porous **Bhabar** belt where streams vanish underground; below it the water re-emerges to make the marshy **Terai**. So the order from the hills outward is Bhabar then Terai.

**6. (C)** The Deccan's black soil weathered from the **basalt of the Deccan Traps** — vast sheets of lava that erupted and cooled long ago. That iron-rich rock gives the dark, moisture-holding "regur" soil.

**7. (D)** The **Western Ghats are a continuous, higher** wall (broken only by a few passes); the **Eastern Ghats are lower and broken** into detached hills by the great east-flowing rivers. The Western, not Eastern, Ghats catch the heavy rain.

**8. (B)** The Narmada and Tapi race down **hard-rock rift valleys**, so they reach the sea fast and their silt is flushed straight out — forming estuaries, not the slow, silt-dropping deltas of the east coast.

**9. (A)** The **Narmada** flows *west* into the Arabian Sea (as an estuary) and builds no east-coast delta. The Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri all flow east and do build deltas.

**10. (C)** A desert is defined by **very low rainfall**, not by heat. Ladakh is freezing yet bone-dry — a *cold* desert — which is why "all deserts are hot" is a trap.

**11. (D)** Lakshadweep is built of **coral**; the Andaman & Nicobar Islands are **tectonic/volcanic** (part of a rising arc, with Barren Island active). So respectively: coral and tectonic/volcanic.

**12. (A)** **Kangchenjunga** (8,586 m, Sikkim) is the highest mountain peak in India. Everest lies in Nepal/Tibet and K2 in the Pakistan-administered north; Nanda Devi is high but lower than Kangchenjunga.

**13. (B)** The **Aravallis are among the world's oldest fold mountains**, worn down by aeons of erosion to low ridges — proof that mountains age and shrink. The still-rising Himalayas are the young contrast.

**14. (C)** The Western Ghats are locally the **Sahyadri**. The Vindhya and Satpura border the plateau to the north; the Nilgiris are where the two Ghats meet.

**15. (A)** Himalayan rivers are fed by **both monsoon rain and melting snow/glaciers**, so they flow even in the dry season (perennial). Many Peninsular rivers, fed by rain alone, shrink once the monsoon ends.

**16. (B)** The **Ganga–Brahmaputra** delta (the Sundarbans), shared with Bangladesh, is the world's largest delta and is famous for its mangrove forest.

**17. (D)** Land lying between two rivers is a **doab** (literally "two waters") — the Ganga–Yamuna doab is the classic example. A dun is a valley; a ghat is a mountain pass/step; bhabar is a foothill belt.

**18. (C)** The Northern Plains are densely peopled because **flat land, deep fertile alluvial soil and ample river water** make farming and settlement easy — not minerals, climate, or defence.

**19. (A)** Air that has dumped its rain on the windward Ghats descends dry over the Deccan, leaving a **rain-shadow area**. This is why the interior plateau is much drier than the coast.

**20. (B)** India spans about 30° of longitude. At 4 minutes of time per degree that is roughly **two hours** of real sun-time difference between the east and west — the reason a single Standard Time (82°30′E) is used.

**21. (D)** The Tropic of Cancer runs **across the middle of India, through about eight states**, neatly splitting the country into a tropical south and a sub-tropical north.

**22. (C)** Kerala's south-western coastal plain is the **Malabar Coast**. Coromandel and the Northern Circars are on the east coast; the Konkan lies further north (Maharashtra–Goa).

**23. (A)** Chilika Lake, India's largest coastal **lagoon**, lies on the **Odisha** coast on the Bay of Bengal.

**24. (B)** The moisture-retentive black (regur) soil of the Deccan is famous for growing **cotton** ("black cotton soil"). Tea, coffee and rubber prefer the well-drained hill slopes.

**25. (D)** By stopping the icy Central Asian winds and wringing rain from the monsoon, the Himalayas act as India's great **climatic barrier** — they shape the whole subcontinent's weather.

**26. (A)** The Ganga springs from the Gangotri glacier; the Yamuna rises from the **Yamunotri** glacier, close by in the Himalayas.

**27. (C)** Peninsular rivers are **older than Himalayan rivers**; over long ages they have settled into shallow, broad, stable valleys, whereas the young Himalayan rivers still cut deep gorges.

**28. (B)** The flat-floored longitudinal valleys of the lesser Himalayas, such as Dehra Dun, are called **duns**. Karewas are Kashmir lake deposits; doabs are river interfluves.

**29. (D)** The north-eastern frontier hills — Patkai, Naga, Mizo — are together the **Purvanchal** (the "eastern hills"), a southern continuation of the Himalayan arc.

**30. (A)** The claim is wrong: **Peninsular rivers are rain-fed and depend on the monsoon**, not snow. Only the Himalayan rivers draw on snow-melt, so the blanket statement fails.

**31. (C)** Passes like the Thal, Bhor and Pal Ghats are vital because they **let roads and railways cross the Western Ghats** — natural gateways between the coast and the interior.

**32. (A)** The Peninsular Plateau is a **stable, ancient block of hard rock** — one of Earth's oldest landmasses — in contrast to the young, restless, rising Himalayas.

**33. (B)** Mangroves thrive only in salty, tidal mud, so the Sundarbans show that vegetation is shaped by **the local landform and water conditions** — not by economics or tourism.

**34. (D)** The **Narmada and Tapi** are the two large rivers that flow *west* into the Arabian Sea through rift (fault) valleys, which is why they make estuaries.

**35. (C)** Tea cannot stand water around its roots, so it is grown on **sloping land where heavy rain drains away** — hence the hill gardens of Assam and Darjeeling rather than the flat, waterlogged plains.

**36. (A)** The southernmost point of the *mainland* is **Kanyakumari** (Cape Comorin), where three seas meet. Indira Point is further south but lies on an island.

**37. (B)** India's southernmost point of all is **Indira Point**, on **Great Nicobar Island** in the Bay of Bengal — well south of the mainland's Kanyakumari.

**38. (D)** The plateau is bounded on the north by the **Vindhya and Satpura** ranges (with the Narmada between them). The Himadri/Shivalik are Himalayan; the Ghats flank the plateau east and west.

**39. (C)** Over the Thar the monsoon winds blow **roughly parallel to the Aravallis**, so almost no air is forced up to cool and rain — leaving the region arid. (Had the range stood across the wind, it would have wrung out rain.)

**40. (A)** **Ladakh**, a high, cold, rain-shadow desert behind the Himalayas, is the "Roof of the World" region of India.

**41. (B)** At its mouth a river **slows down and drops its silt into a spreading fan of distributaries** — that built-up sediment is a delta. Speeding up would cut a gorge, the opposite process.

**42. (D)** From the plains upward the three ranges are **Shivalik (outermost/lowest), Himachal (middle), Himadri (innermost, highest)**. Climbing toward the snow line you meet them in that order.

**43. (C)** The terraced, saucer-shaped old lake deposits of the Kashmir Valley, prized for saffron and orchards, are the **karewas**.

**44. (A)** Coral islands such as Lakshadweep are built from **the limy skeletons of countless tiny coral polyps**, piled up over ages — not from lava, silt or sand.

**45. (B)** The new, fertile, frequently renewed flood-plain alluvium is **Khadar**; the older, higher, less fertile alluvium above the flood line is Bhangar.

**46. (D)** Because the Himalayas are shared with several neighbouring countries and are hard to cross, they serve as a **natural and political frontier** as well as a climatic wall.

**47. (C)** The plateau is largely **old igneous and metamorphic rock**, which is why it holds India's richest stores of minerals (iron, coal, manganese, etc.).

**48. (A)** On the broad, gentle eastern coastal plain the great east-flowing rivers slow right down and **build wide deltas** — the opposite of the swift west-coast estuaries.

**49. (B)** **Mahanadi → Bay of Bengal** is correct. The Godavari also flows to the Bay (not the Arabian Sea), while the Narmada and Tapi flow *west* to the Arabian Sea, not the Bay of Bengal.

**50. (D)** The Nilgiris, the meeting point of the two Ghats, carry **the highest peaks of the Peninsular south**, such as Doddabetta — not a desert, river or volcano.

**51. (C)** Crossing from the wet Malabar coast to the dry Deccan, rainfall drops because of the **rain-shadow effect of the Western Ghats**, which strip the moisture from the air on the way up.

**52. (A)** The Himalayas' youth shows in their **sharp, towering, still-rising peaks and frequent earthquakes**; the ancient Aravallis, by contrast, are low and rounded from long erosion.

**53. (B)** The Rann of Kachchh, a great seasonal salt marsh, lies in **Gujarat** — flooded in the monsoon, a glittering salt flat in summer.

**54. (D)** India's enormous variety of climates and crops springs from its **great range of landforms and latitudes** — snow mountains, plateaus, plains, deserts and tropical coasts all in one country.

**55. (C)** The Brahmaputra **carries an immense silt load across a wide, gently sloping plain**, so it splits and re-splits into shifting channels — a braided river — and floods Assam each year.

**56. (A)** The windward Western Ghats catch **very heavy monsoon rainfall**, nourishing dense evergreen forests — which is why the range is a global biodiversity hotspot.

**57. (B)** Kerala's backwaters (kayals) are **networks of brackish lagoons and lake channels running parallel to the coast**, linked to the sea — not mountain lakes or oases.

**58. (D)** Of coast, plateau, plain and mountains, the **Himalayan mountains are the youngest** (and still rising), while the Peninsular Plateau is among the oldest.

**59. (C)** Without the Himalayan wall, India's winters would be **much colder, as icy Central Asian winds swept down** unobstructed across the plains.

**60. (B)** All these contrasting landforms in one country are captured by **"unity in geographical diversity"** — varied land, one nation.
