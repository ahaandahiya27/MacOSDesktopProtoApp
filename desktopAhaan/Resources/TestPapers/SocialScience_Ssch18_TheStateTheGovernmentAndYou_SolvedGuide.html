Set 1 · Q1–Q10

Q1

A 'state' (or nation) is best described as:

  • Aonly the buildings where ministers work
  • Ba political organisation with authority to govern a defined territory and population✓
  • Cthe same thing as the current government
  • Da single elected leader

Working

✓

A state is a political organisation governing a defined territory and population, with four parts. It is not just buildings, the current government, or one leader.

Q2

The four parts of a state are:

  • Alegislature, executive, judiciary and police
  • Bunion, state, local and village
  • Cprotector, provider, regulator and voter
  • Dpopulation, territory, government and sovereignty✓

Working

✓

A state's four parts are population, territory, government and sovereignty. The others list organs, tiers, or government roles, not the parts of a state.

Q3

Within a state, the government is:

  • Athe whole state by itself
  • Bonly one of the four parts✓
  • Cthe same as the population
  • Dthe same as the territory

Working

✓

The government is just one of the state's four parts. It is not the whole state, the population, or the territory.

Q4

The claim 'the government and the state are the same thing' is wrong because:

  • Athere is no government in a state
  • Bthe state changes at every election but the government never does
  • Cthe state is only the police
  • Dgovernments change after elections, but the state (people, territory, sovereignty) remains✓

Working

✓

The government is one changing part; the state endures across elections - like teachers changing while the school stays. The reversal and 'state is only police' options are wrong.

Q5

The chapter's analogy 'teachers change while the school stays' compares the school to the:

  • Agovernment, and the teachers to the state
  • Bterritory, and the teachers to the population
  • Csovereignty, and the teachers to the police
  • Dstate, and the teachers to the government✓

Working

✓

The lasting school is the state; the changing teachers are the government. The other pairings reverse or confuse the analogy.

Q6

A 'government' is the group or system that runs the country by:

  • Amaking, enforcing and resolving disputes about laws✓
  • Bowning all the land in the territory
  • Cchoosing the population's religion
  • Dreplacing the Constitution each year

Working

✓

A government makes, enforces and interprets laws. It does not own all land, dictate religion, or rewrite the Constitution yearly.

Q7

Unlike the state, a government:

  • Anever changes at all
  • Bhas no role in running the country
  • Cchanges after elections✓
  • Dis the same as the territory

Working

✓

Governments change after elections, while the state endures. A government does run the country and is not the territory.

Q8

When a new party wins an election, what changes and what stays?

  • Athe state changes, but the government continues
  • Bboth vanish completely
  • Cneither changes ever
  • Dthe government changes, but the state continues✓

Working

✓

An election changes the government while the state continues. The reversal and the 'both vanish / neither changes' options are wrong.

Q9

Making, enforcing and interpreting laws are the work of the:

  • Apopulation alone
  • Bterritory itself
  • Csovereignty by itself
  • Dgovernment, through its three organs✓

Working

✓

The government, through its legislature, executive and judiciary, makes, enforces and interprets laws. The population, territory and sovereignty are other parts of the state.

Q10

The government runs the state, but the state is bigger because it also includes:

  • Aonly the ministers
  • Bthe people, the land, and sovereignty✓
  • Conly the courts
  • Dnothing beyond the government

Working

✓

Besides the government, the state includes its population, territory and sovereignty. It is not only ministers, only courts, or just the government.

Q11

  • Bthe full power of a state to make and enforce its own laws without outside control✓

  • Dthe land a state governs

Working

✓

Sovereignty is a state's full power to make and enforce its own laws free of outside control. It is not a minister's whim, the vote, or the territory.

Q12

A state with full sovereignty:

  • Ais not controlled by any outside power in making its laws✓
  • Bmust obey a foreign government's orders
  • Chas no laws of its own
  • Dcannot enforce any law

Working

✓

A sovereign state makes and enforces its own laws without outside control. It is not foreign-controlled, lawless, or unable to enforce law.

Q13

The claim 'the police and the army ARE the government' is wrong because they are:

  • Athe entire government by themselves
  • Bthe same as the population
  • Cpart of the state's machinery that the government directs✓
  • Dan outside foreign power

Working

✓

The police and army are part of the state's machinery directed by the government, not the government itself. They are not the whole government, the population, or foreign.

Q14

After an election changes the government, the police and the armed forces:

  • Aare all dismissed and replaced
  • Bbecome the new government
  • Ccontinue serving the state✓
  • Dstop existing

Working

✓

The police and armed forces continue serving the state across changes of government. They are not all dismissed, made the government, or ended.

Q15

The police and army differ from the elected government in that they:

  • Aare permanent parts of the state, not a team elected for a fixed term✓
  • Bare elected every five years
  • Cchange with every new party
  • Dmake the country's laws

Working

✓

The police and army are permanent state machinery, unlike the government elected for a fixed term. They are not elected, party-changing, or lawmakers.

Q16

India is described as a:

  • Ahereditary monarchy
  • Bdemocratic republic✓
  • Ctheocracy
  • Done-party dictatorship

Working

✓

India is a democratic republic. It is not a monarchy, theocracy, or dictatorship.

Q17

A 'democracy' is a system of:

  • Arule by a hereditary king
  • Brule by religious leaders
  • Crule by a single unelected officer
  • Drule by the people, who choose their government through free and fair elections✓

Working

✓

Democracy is rule by the people through free and fair elections. King-rule, religious rule and unelected-officer rule are other forms.

Q18

A 'republic' is a form of government in which:

  • Athe head of state inherits the throne
  • Bthere is no constitution at all
  • Cthe head of state is elected (not hereditary) and rulers' powers are limited by a constitution✓
  • Dthe people never vote

Working

✓

In a republic the head of state is elected, not hereditary, with powers limited by a constitution. Inheritance, no constitution, and no voting describe other systems.

Q19

The claim 'all democracies are republics' is wrong because:

  • Conly republics can be democracies
  • Devery monarchy is a republic

Working

✓

The UK, Canada and Sweden are democracies with monarchs, so not republics - a democracy need not be a republic. The other options are false.

Q20

The UK, with an elected parliament but a hereditary monarch, is:

  • Aa democracy but not a republic✓
  • Ba republic but not a democracy
  • Cboth a democracy and a republic
  • Dneither a democracy nor anything else

Working

✓

The UK is a democracy (elected parliament) but not a republic (it has a hereditary monarch). The other options misclassify it.

Set 3 · Q21–Q30

Q21

The key difference between 'democracy' and 'republic' is that democracy concerns ____ while republic concerns ____.

  • Awho rules (the people) ... whether the head of state is elected, not hereditary✓
  • Bwhether the head of state is elected ... who rules
  • Cthe size of the army ... the size of the population
  • Dthe territory ... the sovereignty

Working

✓

Democracy is about the people ruling; a republic is about an elected (non-hereditary) head of state. The first option captures this; the others reverse or confuse it.

Q22

The Constitution guarantees everyone's fundamental rights so that:

  • Athe majority can do whatever it likes
  • Bthe majority cannot trample a minority's rights✓
  • Conly the majority has rights
  • Donly the government has rights

Working

✓

Guaranteed rights mean the majority cannot trample minorities - the rule of law over mere numbers. It is not majority-can-do-anything, majority-only, or government-only rights.

Q23

The claim 'in a democracy the majority can do whatever it wants' is wrong because:

  • Athe rule of law and guaranteed rights protect minorities from the majority✓
  • Bthe majority has no power at all
  • Cminorities rule over the majority
  • Dthere are no rights in a democracy

Working

✓

The rule of law and constitutional rights protect minorities, so the majority cannot do anything it likes. The majority does have power, does not lose to minorities, and rights do exist.

Q24

Protecting minority rights against the 'dominance of the majority' shows that, in India, the:

  • Amajority's wishes always override the law
  • Bminorities have no protection
  • Crule of law is stronger than the mere wishes of the majority✓
  • DConstitution can be freely ignored

Working

✓

Guaranteed rights make the rule of law stronger than the majority's wishes. The majority does not always override the law, minorities are protected, and the Constitution is not ignored.

Q25

The 'rule of law' is the principle that:

  • Aeveryone, including the government, must follow the law✓
  • Bthe government is above all law
  • Conly ordinary citizens must obey laws
  • Dlaws apply only to minorities

Working

✓

The rule of law means everyone, even the government, must follow the law. It is not government-above-law, citizens-only, or minority-only.

Q26

'Matsya nyaya', the 'law of the fishes', is the idea that:

  • Afish make the best laws
  • Bthe weak always rule the strong
  • Cwithout law and order, the powerful prey on the weak✓
  • Deveryone is automatically equal without any law

Working

✓

Matsya nyaya ('law of the fishes') means that without law, the strong devour the weak - why we need a government. The other options are wrong.

Q27

Why do we need a government, according to the rule-of-law idea?

  • Aso the strongest can rule without limits
  • Bto remove all laws
  • Cso no one ever has to follow rules
  • Dto maintain order so the powerful cannot simply prey on the weak✓

Working

✓

Government and the rule of law maintain order, preventing matsya nyaya where the strong prey on the weak. It is not unlimited strong-rule, removing laws, or rule-free life.

Q28

A society with no law or order would slide toward:

  • Aperfect equality with no effort
  • Bthe rule of law
  • Cmatsya nyaya, where the strong dominate the weak✓
  • Da guaranteed constitution

Working

✓

Without law and order, society slides into matsya nyaya, the strong preying on the weak. It would not yield automatic equality, the rule of law, or a working constitution.

Q29

The rule of law is the opposite of matsya nyaya because it:

  • Aprotects the weak by binding even the powerful to the law✓
  • Blets the powerful ignore the law
  • Capplies only to the weak
  • Dabolishes all government

Working

✓

The rule of law binds even the powerful, protecting the weak - the opposite of matsya nyaya. It does not free the powerful, target only the weak, or abolish government.

Q30

The 'legislature' is the organ of government that:

  • Acarries out and enforces the laws
  • Bmakes the laws✓
  • Cinterprets the laws and settles disputes
  • Dcommands the army

Working

✓

The legislature makes laws (Latin lex 'law' + latio 'proposing'). Enforcing is the executive, interpreting is the judiciary, and commanding the army is not its defining role.

Set 4 · Q31–Q40

Q31

The word 'legislature' comes from Latin roots meaning:

  • A'to carry out'
  • B'judge' or 'to say the law'
  • C'law' and 'a bringing or proposing'✓
  • D'treaty or league'

Working

✓

Legislature is from lex/legis ('law') + latio ('proposing'). 'To carry out' gives executive, 'judge' gives judiciary, and 'treaty' gives federalism.

Q32

A body of elected representatives debating and passing a new law is acting as the:

  • Aexecutive
  • Bjudiciary
  • Cbureaucracy
  • Dlegislature✓

Working

✓

Passing laws is the legislature's work. The executive enforces, the judiciary interprets, and the bureaucracy is the permanent executive.

Q33

The 'executive' is the organ that:

  • Amakes the laws
  • Binterprets the laws and settles disputes
  • Celects the legislature
  • Dcarries out and enforces the laws✓

Working

✓

The executive carries out and enforces laws (Latin exsequi, 'to carry out'). Making laws is the legislature and interpreting them the judiciary.

Q34

The executive has two parts: the political executive and the:

  • Ahereditary executive
  • Bjudicial executive
  • Cpermanent executive (the civil-service bureaucracy)✓
  • Dlegislative executive

Working

✓

The executive's two parts are the political executive (ministers) and the permanent executive (bureaucracy). The other terms are invented.

Q35

The 'political executive' (ministers):

  • Astays on whichever party wins
  • Bis selected by competitive exams
  • Csets the direction and changes with elections✓
  • Dnever changes at all

Working

✓

The political executive - ministers - sets direction and changes with elections. Staying on regardless and exam-selection describe the permanent executive.

Q36

The 'permanent executive' (bureaucracy):

  • Achanges completely after every election
  • Bstays on and implements decisions whichever party wins, giving steady administration✓
  • Cmakes the country's laws
  • Dis the head of state

Working

✓

The permanent executive - civil servants - stays on and implements decisions across governments, giving stability. It does not change each election, make laws, or serve as head of state.

Q37

The claim 'only the ministers we elect run the country day to day' is wrong because:

  • Athe permanent bureaucracy actually implements decisions whichever party wins✓
  • Bministers do everything entirely alone
  • Cthere is no bureaucracy at all
  • Dthe courts run the daily administration

Working

✓

The day-to-day work is largely done by the permanent bureaucracy, not ministers alone. There is a bureaucracy, and the courts do not run daily administration.

Q38

'Bureaucracy' refers to the:

  • Aelected ministers who change with elections
  • Bpermanent, non-elected, exam-selected officers (civil servants) who run the government✓
  • Cjudges of the Supreme Court
  • Dvoters in an election

Working

✓

The bureaucracy is the permanent, exam-selected civil service. Elected ministers are the political executive, judges the judiciary, and voters the electorate.

Q39

The 'judiciary' is the organ that:

  • Amakes the laws
  • Benforces the laws
  • Ccollects the taxes
  • Dinterprets the laws and settles disputes✓

Working

✓

The judiciary interprets laws and settles disputes (Latin judex, 'judge'). Making laws is the legislature, enforcing the executive.

Q40

The judiciary is called the 'watchdog' because it:

  • Aobeys every order of the ruling party
  • Bmakes all the laws itself
  • Cindependently checks that laws and actions follow the Constitution✓
  • Dcommands the police

Working

✓

As an independent watchdog, the judiciary checks laws and actions against the Constitution. It does not obey the ruling party, make laws, or command the police.

  • Bunion, state and local
  • Cprotector, provider and regulator✓

  • Conly builds hospitals
  • Donly holds elections

As regulator (Latin regula, 'rule') the government sets and enforces fair rules. Fighting wars is protection and building hospitals is provision.

  • Bprotector defending the borders
  • Cprovider building roads
  • Dvoter in an election

Working

✓

Setting and enforcing safety rules is the regulator role. Border defence is protection, road-building is provision, and voting is a citizen's act.

Q49

Q50
