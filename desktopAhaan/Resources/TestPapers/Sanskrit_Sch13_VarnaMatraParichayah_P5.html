30. (B) लक्ष् short अ closed by the conjunct क्ष्म (गुरु by position). मी long ई (गुरु by nature). कान् long आ plus the closing conjunct न्त (गुरु by nature and position). तः short अ closed by विसर्ग (गुरु by position). All four गुरु.

31. (A) अ is short, so रश् cannot be heavy by nature. But a short vowel closed by a following consonant cluster becomes heavy by position; here श्म closes रश्. So it is गुरु by position. A conjunct most certainly can trigger position-weight; it is not limited to अनुस्वार/विसर्ग.

32. (D) प्र = प् + र + short अ; what follows this short अ is the conjunct ज्ञ, which closes it, so प्र is गुरु by position. The conjunct प्र at the start is merely the onset (before the vowel) and does not by itself decide weight; the long आ belongs to the next syllable, not to प्र.

33. (A) पु has short उ closed by only the single onset न, so it is लघु. नः has short अ closed by विसर्ग, which makes it गुरु by position even though विसर्ग adds no मात्रा. So exactly one syllable, नः, is heavy. Being short does not stop a vowel from becoming position-गुरु when something closes it.

34. (B) उ is short, so उत् is not heavy by nature; but the following conjunct त्स closes the short उ, making उत् गुरु by position. Word position (initial/medial/final) is irrelevant to the rule — only what closes the vowel matters, and उ is short, not long.

35. (D) Both vowels are short, so neither is heavy by nature. But अनुस्वार closes वं and विसर्ग closes शः, and each closure produces गुरु by position. Neither अनुस्वार nor विसर्ग adds मात्रा, yet both make the preceding short vowel heavy — so both syllables are गुरु.

36. (D) Position-weight depends on what FOLLOWS the short vowel, not the cluster that precedes it. The conjunct क्र is the onset of क्रि (before its इ); after the इ comes only the single consonant य starting या. So nothing closes the short इ, and क्रि is लघु. A preceding cluster does not by itself make a syllable heavy.

37. (C) Both vowels are short, so neither is heavy by nature. बिम् is closed by अनुस्वार (गुरु by position); बम् is closed by the word-final consonant म् (गुरु by position). A word-final consonant closes the vowel just as a conjunct or अनुस्वार does. Pattern गुरु-गुरु.

38. (B) By the traditional उच्चारण-स्थान scheme, अ and आ are कण्ठ्य — sounded from the throat. They are not lip-sounds (those are उ ऊ and प-वर्ग), not palatal (those are इ ई and च-वर्ग), and not retroflex. So the place of articulation for अ is the कण्ठ.

39. (D) इ and ई are तालव्य — sounded at the palate, the same place as the च-वर्ग consonants (च छ ज झ ञ) and the semivowel य. They are not throat-sounds (those are अ आ) nor lip-sounds (उ ऊ).

40. (C) उ and ऊ are ओष्ठ्य — sounded with the lips, the same place as the प-वर्ग consonants (प फ ब भ म). They are not palatal (इ ई) nor throat-sounds (अ आ).

41. (C) The ट-वर्ग is मूर्धन्य — the tongue curls back to touch the मूर्धा (the hard palate/roof). The त-वर्ग (त थ द ध न) is the दन्त्य (dental) set; the प-वर्ग is ओष्ठ्य; the क-वर्ग is कण्ठ्य.

42. (B) The त-वर्ग is दन्त्य — the tongue-tip touches the teeth. This is distinct from the retroflex ट-वर्ग (मूर्धन्य), the palatal च-वर्ग (तालव्य), and the guttural क-वर्ग (कण्ठ्य). A common slip is confusing the दन्त्य त-वर्ग with the मूर्धन्य ट-वर्ग.

43. (C) The क-वर्ग is कण्ठ्य — sounded from the throat, the same place as the vowels अ आ and the visarga/ह. The च-वर्ग is तालव्य, the प-वर्ग is ओष्ठ्य, and the त-वर्ग is दन्त्य.

44. (B) The च-वर्ग is तालव्य — correct. The प-वर्ग is ओष्ठ्य (lips), not दन्त्य; the ट-वर्ग is मूर्धन्य, not कण्ठ्य; the क-वर्ग is कण्ठ्य, not ओष्ठ्य. Each wrong option swaps a वर्ग to the wrong place of articulation.

45. (D) स्वर are the vowels — they can be sounded on their own and carry मात्रा: अ आ इ ई उ ऊ ए ऐ ओ औ. The वर्ग-initial consonants and the semivowels य र ल व are व्यञ्जन; अनुस्वार and विसर्ग are dependent signs (अयोगवाह), not स्वर at all.

46. (C) The short vowels are अ, इ, उ (three of them, 1 मात्रा each). Everything else in the list is long: आ, ई, ऊ are the long counterparts, and ए, ऐ, ओ, औ are all दीर्घ as well — seven long vowels in total. The frequent error is forgetting that ए ऐ ओ औ count as long.

47. (D) अनुस्वार and विसर्ग are अयोगवाह — they depend on a preceding vowel and are classed apart from the ten स्वर and the व्यञ्जन. They carry no मात्रा of their own, but they close a preceding short vowel and so can turn its syllable गुरु by position. They are not vowels worth 2, nor प्लुत, nor independent consonants.

48. (A) A स्वर (vowel) such as अ can be sounded on its own and is what carries मात्रा. A व्यञ्जन (consonant) such as क cannot be sounded without a supporting vowel and adds no मात्रा by itself. So the bare क-sound is a व्यञ्जन while the अ inside it is the स्वर supplying the duration.

49. (B) The अन्तःस्थ ('in-between') sounds are य र ल व — produced with a near-contact, between full stops and open vowels. क is a स्पर्श (full stop), not an अन्तःस्थ; none of these are स्वर; and श ष स (plus ह) are the ऊष्म (sibilant/spirant) class, the opposite assignment to what the wrong option states.

50. (D) इ/ई are sounded at the palate (तालव्य) and उ/ऊ are sounded with the lips (ओष्ठ्य) — genuinely different places of articulation, which is why they are distinct vowels rather than mere length-variants of one another. Neither is कण्ठ्य (that is अ आ) nor मूर्धन्य.

51. (D) वनम् has three short अ vowels = 3 मात्रा (the final म् adds no time). वीणा has long ई (2) and long आ (2) = 4 मात्रा. So वीणा exceeds वनम् by 4 − 3 = 1 मात्रा, even though वनम् has more syllables — duration follows vowel-length, not syllable-count.

52. (B) अर् has short अ closed by the conjunct र्ज, so गुरु by position. जु has short उ closed only by a single onset न, so लघु. नः has short अ closed by विसर्ग, गुरु by position. Pattern heavy-light-heavy. Calling अर् light ignores that the conjunct र्ज closes its short vowel.

53. (C) Add each vowel: long औ is 2, short अ is 1, long आ is 2 → 2+1+2 = 5. Two of the three vowels are long. The conjunct ल्य makes शल् गुरु by position but adds no मात्रा, so it cannot raise the total to 6; and ignoring the diphthong-length gives the wrong 3.

54. (D) A प्लुत is indeed 3 मात्रा, so the first half is right. But position-weight (here from अनुस्वार) changes syllable WEIGHT, not the vowel's DURATION: the short vowel is still 1 मात्रा. Confusing 'heavy' with 'longer' is the misconception — अनुस्वार adds zero मात्रा.

55. (C) Before junction the two long आ vowels total 2 + 2 = 4 मात्रा. They merge into a single long आ worth 2. So the duration falls from 4 to 2; sandhi does not always conserve total vowel-time. The merged vowel is दीर्घ (2), not प्लुत (3).

56. (A) The forms differ only in how long the final vowel is held: a short (1 मात्रा) form versus a प्लुत (3 मात्रा) drawn-out vocative used in calling. Because quantity is phonemic here, length alone signals the distinct vocative/chant function. वर्ण means sound (not caste), and no consonant is added; quantity certainly can change function.

57. (A) A short इ is 1 मात्रा; held as a प्लुत it becomes 3 मात्रा — an increase of 3 − 1 = 2. The result is प्लुत, the third quantity. Going only to 2 मात्रा would be दीर्घ; there is no 4-मात्रा fourth class in Sanskrit.

58. (A) राधा has two long आ vowels, so both रा and धा are गुरु by nature — गुरु-गुरु. हरिः is light-heavy (short ह, then short इ closed by विसर्ग). गुरुः is light-heavy likewise. नदी is light-heavy (short अ, then long ई). Only राधा is heavy-heavy.

59. (D) Sum every vowel across the line: short उ 1, short उ 1, short इ 1, short अ 1, long ओ 2, short अ 1, short अ 1, short इ 1 = 9. Only ओ is long (worth 2); the other seven vowels are short. The conjuncts ष्य and the final म् add no time, and the विसर्ग on रुः makes that syllable गुरु by position but adds no मात्रा.

60. (A) Claim 1 fails: ए is long (2 मात्रा), not short. Claim 2 fails: अनुस्वार contributes no मात्रा (it only forces position-weight). Claim 3 fails: a short उ closed by a following conjunct becomes गुरु by position, not लघु. All three are errors.
