Set 1 · Q1–Q10

Q1

Two distinct straight lines in a plane can intersect in at most how many points?

  • A1✓
  • B0
  • C2
  • Dinfinitely many

Working

✓

Two distinct straight lines, once they touch, immediately separate (a straight line can't bend back to meet again). So they share **at most one** point. (Zero if parallel, one if intersecting — but "at most" is 1.)

Q2

Two straight lines cross at a point, forming four angles. One of them is 110°. The angle vertically opposite to it measures:

  • A70°
  • B90°
  • C110°✓
  • D250°

Working

✓

Vertically opposite angles are always equal, regardless of how the lines cross. So the angle opposite 110° is **110°**. Trap: 70° is the *adjacent* (linear-pair) angle, not the opposite.

Q3

Two lines intersect and one of the four angles is 110°. The angle adjacent to it (its linear pair) is:

  • A110°
  • B90°
  • C80°
  • D70°✓

Working

✓

Adjacent angles on a straight line form a linear pair summing to 180°. So 180° − 110° = **70°**. Trap (A) 110° confuses adjacent with vertically opposite.

Q4

When two lines cross so that all four angles are equal, each angle is 360 ÷ 4. Such lines are called:

  • Aparallel
  • Bperpendicular✓
  • Ctransversal
  • Dco-interior

Working

✓

All four equal ⇒ each = 360 ÷ 4 = 90°. Lines crossing at right angles are **perpendicular**.

Q5

Two adjacent angles on a straight line form a linear pair. If one is 4 times the other, the larger angle is:

  • A36°
  • B120°
  • C144°✓
  • D150°

Working

✓

Let the smaller be x; the larger is 4x. Linear pair: x + 4x = 180 ⇒ 5x = 180 ⇒ x = 36. Larger = 4·36 = **144°**. Trap (A) 36° is the *smaller* angle.

Q6

Opposite edges of a rectangular sheet of paper are ____ and adjacent edges are ____.

  • Aperpendicular; parallel
  • Bparallel; perpendicular✓
  • Cparallel; parallel
  • Dperpendicular; perpendicular

Working

✓

Opposite edges of a sheet run the same way and never meet — **parallel**. Adjacent edges meet at a 90° corner — **perpendicular**.

Q7

A transversal crosses two lines at two distinct points. The total number of angles formed is:

  • A4
  • B6
  • C8✓
  • D12

Working

✓

Each crossing of the transversal makes 4 angles; two crossings ⇒ 4 × 2 = **8** angles.

Q8

A transversal cuts two parallel lines. A corresponding angle at the first line is 73°. The matching corresponding angle at the second line is:

  • A17°
  • B107°
  • C287°
  • D73°✓

Working

✓

Corresponding angles are equal when the lines are parallel, so the matching angle is **73°**. Traps: 107° is its co-interior/linear-pair partner; 17° is its complement (irrelevant here).

Q9

A transversal cuts two parallel lines. One co-interior (same-side interior) angle is 118°. The other co-interior angle is:

  • A118°
  • B62°✓
  • C72°
  • D242°

Working

✓

Co-interior (same-side interior) angles are supplementary on parallel lines: 180° − 118° = **62°**. Trap (A) 118° wrongly assumes they are equal.

Q10

Two lines cross. One angle is 35°. Its vertically opposite angle and its linear-pair neighbour are, respectively:

  • A35° and 145°✓
  • B35° and 35°
  • C145° and 145°
  • D55° and 145°

Working

✓

Vertically opposite to 35° is **35°**; the linear-pair neighbour is 180° − 35° = 145°. So **35° and 145°**.

Set 2 · Q11–Q20

Q11

Two intersecting lines form four angles a, b, c, d in order around the point. Which statement is ALWAYS true, whatever the lines?

  • Aa = b
  • Ba = c✓
  • Ca = b = c = d
  • Da + c = 180°

Working

✓

Whatever the lines, opposite angles are equal: a = c (and b = d). a = b only if perpendicular; a + c = 180° is false in general (a + b = 180° is the true relation). So **a = c**.

Q12

A transversal cuts two parallel lines, making just two distinct angle measures. If one measure is 72°, the two measures are:

  • A72° and 108°✓
  • B72° and 90°
  • C72° and 18°
  • D72° only

Working

✓

A transversal on parallel lines gives angles of just two sizes that are supplementary: 72° and 180° − 72° = 108°. So **72° and 108°**. Trap (B) 72°+90° and (C) 72°+18° misuse complement/right-angle ideas.

Q13

Line a ⊥ line b, and line b ∥ line c. The angle between line a and line c is:

  • A0°
  • B45°
  • C90°✓
  • D180°

Working

✓

a ⊥ b means 90° between them. b ∥ c means c has b's direction, so a meets c at the same 90°. Answer **90°**.

Q14

At an intersection of two lines, one angle is x° and an adjacent angle is (x + 40)°. The value of x is:

  • A80
  • B110
  • C90
  • D70✓

Working

✓

Adjacent angles on a line: x + (x + 40) = 180 ⇒ 2x = 140 ⇒ x = **70**. Trap (C) 110 is the value of the second angle, not x.

Q15

Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. A pair of alternate interior angles measure (2x + 10)° and (3x − 20)°. The value of x is:

  • A30✓
  • B10
  • C6
  • D38

Working

✓

Alternate interior angles are equal: 2x + 10 = 3x − 20 ⇒ 30 = x ⇒ x = **30**. (Then each angle = 70°.) Trap (B) 10 comes from wrongly setting the sum to 180.

Q16

A transversal cuts two parallel lines p and q. An angle of 65° is formed at p. Its corresponding angle, an alternate angle, and a co-interior angle to it measure, respectively:

  • A65°, 115°, 115°
  • B115°, 65°, 65°
  • C65°, 65°, 115°✓
  • D65°, 65°, 65°

Working

✓

Corresponding = 65° (equal), alternate = 65° (equal), co-interior = 180° − 65° = 115° (supplementary). So **65°, 65°, 115°**. Trap (D) wrongly makes the co-interior angle equal too.

Q17

Which of these conditions guarantees that two lines cut by a transversal are parallel?

  • Aa pair of co-interior angles add to 200°
  • Ba pair of alternate angles are equal✓
  • Ca pair of co-interior angles are equal
  • Dthe transversal is perpendicular to only one of the lines

Working

✓

Equal alternate angles force the lines parallel. (A) co-interior must sum to *180°*, not 200°; (C) equal co-interior would mean each 90° only — not a general parallel test; (D) is about one line only. Answer **alternate angles equal**.

Q18

Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. One co-interior angle is twice the other. The smaller co-interior angle is:

  • A30°
  • B90°
  • C120°
  • D60°✓

Working

✓

Co-interior angles are supplementary. Let the smaller be x, larger 2x: x + 2x = 180 ⇒ x = 60. Smaller = **60°**. Trap (A) 30 ignores that they must add to 180°.

Q19

Two lines intersect. The sum of the two angles that are vertically opposite to each other in ONE such pair (i.e. an angle plus its vertically-opposite partner) is 86°. Each of these two angles measures:

  • A43°✓
  • B86°
  • C94°
  • D172°

Working

✓

An angle and its vertically opposite partner are equal, so each = 86° ÷ 2 = **43°**. Trap (B) 86° forgets to halve.

Q20

Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. Two corresponding angles are (5x)° and (3x + 40)°. The common value of each corresponding angle is:

  • A20°
  • B100°✓
  • C60°
  • D140°

Working

✓

Corresponding angles equal: 5x = 3x + 40 ⇒ 2x = 40 ⇒ x = 20, so each = 5·20 = **100°**. Trap (A) 20° gives x, not the angle.

Set 3 · Q21–Q30

Q21

Which statement is FALSE?

  • AVertically opposite angles are always equal.
  • BCorresponding angles are equal only when the two lines are parallel.
  • CCo-interior angles between two parallel lines are equal.✓
  • DAdjacent angles on a straight line add to 180°.

Working

✓

Co-interior angles between parallel lines are **supplementary, not equal** — so "they are equal" is the FALSE statement. The other three are all true.

Q22

A transversal cuts two lines (NOT stated to be parallel). At the first line a pair of corresponding positions has 80°; at the second line the corresponding angle is measured to be 95°. From this you can conclude the two lines are:

  • Aparallel
  • Bperpendicular
  • Ccoincident
  • Dnot parallel✓

Working

✓

Corresponding angles are equal *only* when the lines are parallel. Here they differ (80° vs 95°), so the lines are **not parallel**. Trap (A) reverses the rule.

Q23

Three angles around the meeting point of two intersecting lines are marked 2y, 3y, and 2y reading around. The fourth angle equals:

  • A2y
  • B3y✓
  • C5y
  • Dy

Working

✓

Going round, the 1st and 3rd (both 2y) are a vertically opposite pair (equal ✓). The remaining pair is the 2nd and 4th, which are also vertically opposite, so the 4th = the 2nd = **3y**.

Q24

In the figure, lines AB and CD cross at O. ∠AOC = (3x − 10)° and ∠BOD = (2x + 25)° are vertically opposite. Then ∠AOC measures:

  • A35°
  • B60°
  • C95°✓
  • D105°

Working

✓

Vertically opposite ⇒ 3x − 10 = 2x + 25 ⇒ x = 35. ∠AOC = 3·35 − 10 = **95°**. Trap (A) 35 reports x instead of the angle.

Q25

A transversal t cuts parallel lines m and n. At m it makes an angle of 40° with the transversal on one side. The acute angle the transversal makes with n is:

  • A40°✓
  • B50°
  • C140°
  • D20°

Working

✓

The transversal cuts the parallels at the same inclination, so the acute angle at n equals the acute angle at m = **40°** (corresponding/alternate equality). Trap (B) 50° is the complement.

Q26

Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. The two co-interior (same-side interior) angles measure (4x)° and (5x − 18)°. Then x equals:

  • A18
  • B22✓
  • C20
  • D30

Working

✓

Co-interior angles are supplementary: 4x + (5x − 18) = 180 ⇒ 9x = 198 ⇒ x = **22**. Trap (B-vs-C): setting them *equal* gives x = 18 (option A), the classic co-interior slip.

Q27

Ray OP stands on a line, making two angles 3x and 2x on either side. Then x is:

  • A18°
  • B60°
  • C72°
  • D36°✓

Working

✓

Ray OP on a line ⇒ the two angles form a linear pair: 3x + 2x = 180 ⇒ 5x = 180 ⇒ x = **36°**. Trap (B) 60° comes from dividing 180 by 3.

Q28

A transversal cuts two parallel lines. One of the eight angles is 124°. How many of the eight angles equal 124°?

  • A1
  • B2
  • C4✓
  • D8

Working

✓

Eight angles take two supplementary sizes, four of each. So **4** of them equal 124° (its vertically opposite + the two corresponding-position equals). Trap (B) 2 forgets the corresponding pair at the other line.

Q29

Two lines are perpendicular. A third line is parallel to the first. The angle between this third line and the second line is:

  • A90°✓
  • B45°
  • C0°
  • Dcannot be found

Working

✓

Perpendicular lines: 90° between first and second. A line parallel to the first keeps its direction, so it meets the second at the same **90°**.

Q30

Lines l and m are parallel, cut by transversal t. ∠1 (above l, right of t) = 118°. Its alternate interior angle (below l) pairs with the interior angle at m. The alternate interior angle measure is:

  • A62°
  • B118°✓
  • C90°
  • D236°

Working

✓

Alternate interior angles are equal on parallel lines, so the alternate of 118° is **118°**. Trap (A) 62° wrongly treats them as supplementary (that's co-interior).

Set 4 · Q31–Q40

Q31

At a crossing of two lines, the four angles in order are p, q, p, q. Given p = 3q, then q equals:

  • A30°
  • B60°
  • C135°
  • D45°✓

Working

✓

Around the point the four angles are p, q, p, q with p + q = 180° (adjacent linear pair). With p = 3q: 3q + q = 180 ⇒ 4q = 180 ⇒ q = **45°**.

Q32

A transversal makes co-interior angles (x + 30)° and (2x)° with two parallel lines. The larger co-interior angle is:

  • A50°
  • B80°
  • C100°✓
  • D130°

Working

✓

Co-interior supplementary: (x + 30) + 2x = 180 ⇒ 3x = 150 ⇒ x = 50. The two angles are 80° and 100°; the larger is **100°**. Trap (B) 80° is the smaller.

Q33

Two angles are both supplementary AND equal to each other. Each angle is:

  • A90°✓
  • B60°
  • C45°
  • D180°

Working

✓

"Both supplementary AND equal": x + x = 180 ⇒ x = **90°**. Trap (D) 180° is the sum, not each angle.

Q34

A transversal cuts two parallel lines. The bisector of one of the co-interior angles and the bisector of the other co-interior angle meet at an angle of:

  • A45°
  • B60°
  • C90°✓
  • D120°

Working

✓

The two co-interior angles sum to 180°; their halves sum to 90°. In the triangle formed by the two bisectors and the transversal, the bisectors meet at 180° − 90° = **90°**. (A neat classic result.)

Q35

In a figure, line PQ ∥ line RS, and a transversal meets PQ at A and RS at B. ∠PAB = 5x° and ∠ABS = 4x° are co-interior angles. Then ∠PAB measures:

  • A20°
  • B80°
  • C120°
  • D100°✓

Working

✓

∠PAB and ∠ABS are co-interior ⇒ supplementary: 5x + 4x = 180 ⇒ 9x = 180 ⇒ x = 20. ∠PAB = 5·20 = **100°**. Trap (B) 80° is ∠ABS.

Q36

Which of the following pairs of angles need NOT be equal even when two lines are cut by a transversal?

  • Avertically opposite angles
  • Bcorresponding angles when the lines are not parallel✓
  • Cangles in a linear pair that are both right angles
  • Dtwo angles each equal to 90°

Working

✓

Vertically opposite angles, right-angle linear pairs, and two 90° angles are always equal. But **corresponding angles when the lines are NOT parallel** need not be equal — that's the answer.

Q37

Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal so that a pair of alternate angles are (60 − x)° and (2x)°. Each of these alternate angles measures:

  • A40°✓
  • B20°
  • C60°
  • D120°

Working

✓

Alternate angles equal: 60 − x = 2x ⇒ 60 = 3x ⇒ x = 20, so each = 2·20 = **40°**. Trap (B) 20° reports x, not the angle.

Q38

Lines a and b are parallel. A transversal makes an interior angle of 105° with a on one side. The interior angle with b on the SAME side of the transversal is:

  • A165°
  • B105°
  • C75°✓
  • D90°

Working

✓

The SAME-side interior angle pairs as co-interior with 105°, so it is supplementary: 180° − 105° = **75°**. Trap (B) 105° wrongly treats them as equal (that would be alternate, opposite side).

Q39

At an intersection, an angle and its vertically opposite angle are (4x + 5)° and (6x − 25)°. The value of x is:

  • A10
  • B15✓
  • C20
  • D30

Working

✓

Vertically opposite ⇒ equal: 4x + 5 = 6x − 25 ⇒ 30 = 2x ⇒ x = **15**. Trap (A) 10 comes from a sign slip.

Q40

A transversal cuts lines u and v. Corresponding angles are 88° (at u) and 88° (at v). What can you conclude?

  • Au and v are perpendicular
  • Bu and v meet at 88°
  • Cnothing at all
  • Du and v are parallel✓

Working

✓

Equal corresponding angles guarantee the lines are **parallel** (the converse rule). Trap (C) "meet at 88°" misreads the equality.

Q41

Lines l ∥ m, cut by transversal t. One interior angle on the left of t at l is 7x° and the co-interior angle at m is 2x°. Then the angle 7x° measures:

  • A20°
  • B40°
  • C140°✓
  • D120°

Working

✓

The given interior angle at l and the co-interior angle at m are supplementary: 7x + 2x = 180 ⇒ 9x = 180 ⇒ x = 20. So 7x = **140°**. Trap (D) 120° = 6x, an arithmetic slip.

Q42

Two lines intersect, and one of the four angles is a right angle. The number of the four angles that are right angles is:

  • A4✓
  • B2
  • C3
  • D1

Working

✓

If one of the four angles is 90°, its linear-pair neighbour is 90°, its vertically opposite is 90°, and the fourth is 90° too. So **4** of them are right angles.

Q43

A transversal cuts two parallel lines. An exterior angle on one line is 130°. The interior angle on the same side of the transversal, at that SAME crossing (its linear pair), is:

  • A130°
  • B50°✓
  • C65°
  • D230°

Working

✓

The exterior angle (130°) and the interior angle at the same crossing are a linear pair: 180° − 130° = **50°**. Trap (A) 130° forgets the straight-line sum.

Q44

Parallel lines l and m are cut by transversal t. ∠a and ∠b are alternate exterior angles, with ∠a = (3x + 15)° and ∠b = (4x − 5)°. Then ∠a measures:

  • A20°
  • B60°
  • C105°
  • D75°✓

Working

✓

Alternate exterior angles are equal on parallel lines: 3x + 15 = 4x − 5 ⇒ 20 = x. ∠a = 3·20 + 15 = **75°**. Trap (A) 20 reports x.

Q45

Two roads cross. The angle in the north-east "corner" between them is 65°. The angle in the south-west corner is:

  • A25°
  • B115°
  • C65°✓
  • D130°

Working

✓

The NE corner and the SW corner are vertically opposite, hence equal: **65°**. Trap (B/D) 115°/130° treat them as supplementary/double.

Q46

A transversal cuts two parallel lines, and one of the eight angles is a right angle. Then:

  • Aall eight angles are right angles✓
  • Bexactly two are right angles
  • Cexactly four of the eight angles are right angles
  • Donly that one angle is a right angle

Working

✓

With a right angle present, that whole crossing becomes four 90° angles, and corresponding angles at the parallel line are 90° too — so **all eight** angles are right angles. (This is why a perpendicular transversal makes a fully right-angled grid.)

Q47

Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. The difference between a pair of co-interior angles is 40°. The larger co-interior angle is:

  • A70°
  • B110°✓
  • C90°
  • D140°

Working

✓

Co-interior sum 180°, difference 40°. Larger = (180 + 40) ÷ 2 = **110°** (smaller 70°). Trap (A) 70° is the smaller angle.

Q48

Lines AB and CD intersect at O. ∠AOC = 50°. Ray OE is drawn so that ∠COE = 90° (OE between OC and OB region). Then ∠EOB equals:

  • A130°
  • B50°
  • C90°
  • D40°✓

Working

✓

A, O, B are collinear so ∠AOB = 180°. ∠AOC + ∠COE + ∠EOB = 180° ⇒ 50° + 90° + ∠EOB = 180° ⇒ ∠EOB = **40°**. Trap (B) 50° just echoes ∠AOC.

Q49

A transversal cuts parallel lines so that an alternate interior angle is 3 times its co-interior partner on the same line. (The alternate interior angle and the co-interior angle here form a linear pair on the cut line.) The alternate interior angle is:

  • A135°✓
  • B90°
  • C120°
  • D45°

Working

✓

The alternate-interior angle and the co-interior angle sharing that vertex form a linear pair (sum 180°). With alternate = 3 × co-interior: 3k + k = 180 ⇒ k = 45 ⇒ alternate = **135°**. Trap (D) 45° is the co-interior partner.

Q50

Which set of three angle measures could be the three angles among the four formed by two intersecting lines (the fourth being the remaining one)?

  • A60°, 60°, 60°
  • B100°, 100°, 100°
  • C50°, 130°, 50°✓
  • D40°, 40°, 140°

Working

✓

Four angles around a crossing come in two equal vertically-opposite pairs, with adjacent ones supplementary. **50°, 130°, 50°** works: fourth angle = 130°, pairs 50/50 and 130/130, and 50 + 130 = 180 ✓. (B) needs four equal 60°s impossible; (D) 100° three times can't sum correctly.

Q51

Lines p ∥ q. A transversal makes a 3:2 ratio between a pair of co-interior angles. The smaller of the two angles is:

  • A36°
  • B72°✓
  • C54°
  • D108°

Working

✓

Co-interior angles sum to 180° in ratio 3:2 ⇒ 5 parts = 180° ⇒ 1 part = 36°. Smaller = 2 parts = **72°**. Trap (A) 36° is one part, not the smaller angle.

Q52

A transversal crosses two parallel lines at exactly 90°. The number of distinct angle measures among the eight angles is:

  • A8
  • B2
  • C4
  • D1✓

Working

✓

At 90° every one of the eight angles is a right angle, so there is just **1** distinct measure (90°). Trap (B) 2 forgets the perpendicular case collapses both sizes to 90°.

Q53

∠1 and ∠2 are corresponding angles for two parallel lines, and ∠2 and ∠3 form a linear pair. If ∠1 = 116°, then ∠3 equals:

  • A64°✓
  • B116°
  • C90°
  • D26°

Working

✓

∠1 = ∠2 = 116° (corresponding, parallel). ∠2 and ∠3 are a linear pair ⇒ ∠3 = 180° − 116° = **64°**. Trap (B) 116° forgets the linear-pair step.

Q54

Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. An alternate-interior angle and a co-interior angle that share the same vertex on one of the lines satisfy: their measures are in ratio 2:1. Each unknown — the alternate-interior angle — measures:

  • A60°
  • B90°
  • C120°✓
  • D135°

Working

✓

The alternate-interior angle and the co-interior angle at that vertex form a linear pair (sum 180°) in ratio 2:1 ⇒ 2k + k = 180 ⇒ k = 60. Alternate-interior = 2k = **120°**. Trap (A) 60° is the co-interior partner.

Q55

Lines m and n are crossed by a transversal. Corresponding angles measure (x + 25)° at m and (2x − 15)° at n, and m ∥ n. Then the corresponding angle measure is:

  • A40°
  • B65°✓
  • C50°
  • D80°

Working

✓

Corresponding angles equal (m ∥ n): x + 25 = 2x − 15 ⇒ 40 = x. Angle = 40 + 25 = **65°**. Trap (A) 40 reports x.

Q56

A transversal cuts two parallel lines l and m. ∠A on l and ∠B on m are a pair of alternate angles, and ∠A is 30° less than twice ∠B. Then ∠A measures:

  • A15°
  • B45°
  • C60°
  • D30°✓

Working

✓

Alternate angles are equal: ∠A = ∠B. Given ∠A = 2∠B − 30 ⇒ ∠B = 2∠B − 30 ⇒ ∠B = 30, so ∠A = **30°**. Trap (C) 60° doubles incorrectly.

Q57

Two intersecting lines make four angles; three of them are (x)°, (x)°, and (2x)°. The value of x is:

  • A60✓
  • B45
  • C72
  • D90

Working

✓

Four angles around a crossing form two vertically-opposite pairs. Three given are x, x, 2x, so the pair x, x is one vertically-opposite pair and 2x belongs to the other (whose partner is also 2x). Sum = x + x + 2x + 2x = 6x = 360 ⇒ x = **60**. Check: 60° + 60° + 120° + 120° = 360° ✓. Trap (D) 90 would force all four equal, contradicting x ≠ 2x.

Q58

Lines a ∥ b, cut by a transversal. The bisector of one of the corresponding angles and the line b enclose what angle, if the corresponding angle is 80°? (The bisector makes 40° with the transversal; find the acute angle it makes with b.)

  • A20°
  • B80°
  • C40°✓
  • D60°

Working

✓

The corresponding angle is 80°, so its bisector makes 40° with the transversal. Because the angle's other arm lies along line b's direction... measuring the acute angle the bisector makes with b: 80° − 40° = **40°**. Trap (D) 80° forgets the bisecting.

Q59

A transversal cuts two lines. If a pair of co-interior angles measure 100° and 90°, the two lines are:

  • Aparallel
  • Bnot parallel✓
  • Cperpendicular
  • Dcoincident

Working

✓

For parallel lines co-interior angles must sum to 180°. Here 100° + 90° = 190° ≠ 180°, so the lines are **not parallel**. Trap (A) assumes parallel without checking the sum.

Q60

Parallel lines l and m are cut by a transversal. ∠P is an interior angle at l and ∠Q is the alternate interior angle at m. A second transversal cuts the same lines making ∠Q's alternate angle equal to ∠P. If ∠P = 67°, then the alternate interior angle ∠Q equals:

  • A23°
  • B113°
  • C134°
  • D67°✓
