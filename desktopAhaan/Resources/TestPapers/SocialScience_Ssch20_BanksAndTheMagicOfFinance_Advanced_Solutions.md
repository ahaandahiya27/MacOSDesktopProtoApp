# Solutions — Social Science (Class 7) · Paper 2 (Advanced)
## Chapter 20: Banks and the Magic of Finance

Each answer is keyed, then worked. The recurring move is to follow money as it
flows: a saver deposits, the bank keeps a reserve and lends the rest to a
borrower at a higher rate (the *spread*), the RBI sets the benchmark rate that
guides all the others, and payments move by cheque, UPI or share. Three traps
recur and are worth pinning down once: the RBI is the **banker to banks**, not a
bank where people open accounts; a **share can fall as well as rise**, unlike a
fixed deposit; and **no genuine bank ever asks for your PIN, password or OTP**.

---

**1. (C)** Financial infrastructure is the network of banks, payment systems and stock markets that *moves and manages money* — distinct from physical infrastructure (roads, power) and social infrastructure (schools, hospitals).

**2. (A)** It *complements* physical infrastructure because building and running a road or factory needs loans and a way to pay — money the financial system supplies. The two work together; neither alone is enough.

**3. (D)** A business making many payments with no transaction limit and no need for interest uses a **current account** — designed for frequent transactions rather than for earning interest.

**4. (B)** Money locked in for a fixed period in exchange for a higher rate is a **fixed deposit**. The lock-in is the trade for the better rate.

**5. (B)** The higher rate rewards the *commitment*: because the money is parked for a set term, the bank can lend it out for longer without fearing a sudden withdrawal, so it pays the saver more.

**6. (D)** Doubling grains on each chess square explodes to an astronomical total — the chapter's image for **compounding**, where interest earns interest and small amounts grow huge.

**7. (A)** Under compounding you earn interest on the principal *plus the interest already added*; under simple interest you earn only on the original principal each year.

**8. (C)** Rule of 72: doubling time ≈ 72 ÷ rate. Here 72 ÷ 8 = **9 years**.

**9. (A)** Same money, same rate — but Asha's deposit compounds for ten extra years. Compounding rewards *time*, so the earlier start wins.

**10. (C)** The bank earns the gap on the principal: (7% − 3%) × ₹2,000 = 4% × 2,000 = **₹80**.

**11. (D)** The gap between the rate charged to borrowers and the rate paid to savers is the **spread** — the bank's main source of income.

**12. (B)** A bank keeps part of its deposits as **reserves** so depositors can withdraw on demand; lending out *every* rupee would leave it unable to pay people back.

**13. (D)** A bank is a **middleman** for money: it pools savers' deposits (paying them interest) and lends to borrowers (charging more), connecting the two.

**14. (B)** The bank pays Navdeep interest on his deposit and lends part of the pooled deposits to Rima at a higher rate — serving the saver and the borrower at once.

**15. (C)** *Credere* is Latin for **"to believe, to trust"** — credit is money lent in the trust that it will be repaid.

**16. (A)** The Jan Dhan Yojana (2014) gave crores of Indians — many of them women — their first no-minimum-balance account, pulling them into the formal financial system.

**17. (C)** True financial inclusion means **wages, pensions and benefits reach people directly and safely**, and savers can earn interest and access loans — far more than just storing cash.

**18. (A)** When a deposit is partly lent, spent and re-deposited to be lent again, the same original deposit supports a *chain* of new deposits and loans, so total money in accounts grows — banks "create" money by lending.

**19. (B)** **NABARD** funds rural and agricultural development specifically.

**20. (D)** For a borrower, compounding is a *disadvantage*: unpaid interest itself starts earning interest, so a delayed debt can balloon.

**21. (A)** A home loan and an education loan are both the bank extending **credit to households** for long-term needs.

**22. (D)** Keeping no reserves means the bank could not return money to depositors who wish to withdraw — and could collapse. That is exactly why reserves exist.

**23. (B)** The original sum, before any interest, is the **principal**.

**24. (C)** Inclusive banking succeeds when **people across incomes and regions** — including the poor and women — can open and use accounts, not just the wealthy.

**25. (B)** The "banker to banks" that keeps order among India's banks is the **Reserve Bank of India**.

**26. (C)** The RBI was established in **1935**.

**27. (A)** It is likened to Kubera, god of wealth, because it **holds banks' accounts, lends to banks and the government, and guards the nation's money**.

**28. (D)** The RBI is a **central bank for the banking system, not a retail bank** — ordinary people bank at commercial banks, which in turn keep accounts at the RBI.

**29. (D)** The power to print and distribute India's currency belongs to the **RBI alone**.

**30. (A)** The benchmark rate is the **base rate the RBI fixes for lending to commercial banks**, which then guides the rates banks charge their own customers.

**31. (C)** To cool rising prices, the RBI **raises the benchmark rate**: borrowing and spending fall, demand eases, and prices rise more slowly. Printing more cash would worsen inflation.

**32. (B)** A rate *cut* lets banks borrow cheaply and lower their lending rates, so households and firms **borrow and spend more**, speeding the economy up.

**33. (C)** Inflation is a **general rise in prices over time**, so each rupee buys less.

**34. (B)** Cash under a mattress **earns no interest, and inflation steadily erodes what it can buy** — the saver loses purchasing power.

**35. (D)** The RBI took over full central-bank duties — becoming the "banker to banks" for independent India — in **1949**. (It was *established* in 1935.)

**36. (A)** A commercial bank's core job is to **take deposits from and lend to the public, earning on the spread** — printing currency and setting the benchmark rate are the RBI's.

**37. (A)** A cheque is a **paper order instructing your bank to pay a named person** from your account.

**38. (B)** UPI is **instant and works from a smartphone, without paper** — far faster than a cheque, which is slow and may need a bank visit.

**39. (D)** UPI is "India's gift to the world" because it powered India's cashless boom and has been **adopted abroad — by Nepal, the UAE, France, Sri Lanka, Bhutan and Mauritius**.

**40. (C)** **Nepal** was the first country to adopt UPI, in 2022.

**41. (D)** A share is a **unit of ownership** — buying one makes you a part-owner of the company.

**42. (C)** The slice of company profit paid to each shareholder is a **dividend** (Latin *dividendum*, "a thing to be divided").

**43. (A)** The stock market **channels savings into companies** (which raise money by issuing shares), so idle money is put to productive use.

**44. (B)** Asia's oldest stock exchange, founded in 1875 and said to have begun under a banyan tree in Mumbai, is the **Bombay Stock Exchange**.

**45. (B)** A deposit earns a **fixed, safe** return; a **share can rise *or fall*** with the company and the market — that risk is the key difference.

**46. (D)** Issuing shares brings in money the company **need not repay like a loan**, in exchange for sharing future profits as dividends — useful for raising capital.

**47. (C)** "Higher possible return, higher risk" describes **buying shares** — a deposit and cash carry far less risk and lower expected return.

**48. (A)** Someone who cannot bear *any* loss of principal should choose a **bank deposit**, where the principal is safe and the return fixed — not shares.

**49. (A)** "Bank" comes from Italian *banca*, **a bench**, where medieval moneychangers sat to do business.

**50. (D)** A lender out of money had his *banca rotta* — "broken bench" — the origin of **"bankrupt."**

**51. (C)** "Finance," from Latin *finis* ("end"), once meant **the settling, or ending, of a debt**.

**52. (B)** **Refuse** — a genuine bank never asks for your OTP, PIN or password; naming your bank is exactly the trick a fraudster uses. Report it (helpline 1930).

**53. (C)** The one rule that defeats most scams: **never share your PIN, password or OTP** with anyone.

**54. (D)** The "locker" image is wrong because a bank **lends most deposits out, keeps some as reserves, and earns from the spread** — money does not sit untouched.

**55. (B)** Savings are not idle: the bank **lends most of the deposit out** to fund homes, studies and businesses, keeping the economy moving while the saver earns interest.

**56. (A)** India's record digital-payment volumes flow directly from the **spread of UPI and smartphones, especially after COVID-19**.

**57. (B)** Costlier loans → less borrowing → prices rise more slowly. This whole chain is the RBI's tool against **inflation**.

**58. (A)** RBI → **central bank**; Jan Dhan Yojana → **accounts for all (2014)**; BSE → **a stock exchange**.

**59. (C)** The conclusion is **sound**: compounding rewards *time*, so starting early with small amounts can beat a larger but later sum.

**60. (D)** Finance is the economy's "plumbing": it **moves money from those who have it to those who can use it** — via banks, the RBI, UPI and the stock market — turning idle savings into growth.
