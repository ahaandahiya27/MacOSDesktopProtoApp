Set 1 · Q1–Q10

Q1

Two figures are said to be congruent when:

  • Athey have the same shape and the same size✓
  • Bthey have the same area only
  • Cthey have the same shape only
  • Done is an enlargement of the other

Working

✓

Congruent means one figure can be placed exactly on the other — that requires the **same shape AND the same size**. "Same area only" (a 2×6 and a 3×4 rectangle) or "same shape only" (an enlargement) fail this.

Q2

The symbol used to denote "is congruent to" is:

  • A≈
  • B≅✓
  • C=
  • D∼

Working

✓

≅ is the congruence symbol. ∼ means "similar", ≈ means "approximately", = is ordinary equality.

Q3

A drawing is photocopied at 150% (enlarged). The copy and the original are:

  • Acongruent
  • Bneither similar nor congruent
  • Csimilar but not congruent✓
  • Dcongruent only if the original was a triangle

Working

✓

A 150% copy keeps the shape but multiplies every length by 1.5, so it is bigger. Same shape, different size = **similar but not congruent**. (Congruent needs equal size.)

Q4

Which set of equal parts does NOT, by itself, guarantee that two triangles are congruent?

  • Athree pairs of equal sides
  • Btwo sides and the included angle
  • Ctwo angles and the included side
  • Dthree pairs of equal angles✓

Working

✓

SSS, SAS and ASA each pin a triangle down completely. **AAA (three equal angles)** fixes only the shape — the triangles can be different sizes (similar), so it does not guarantee congruence.

Q5

Two triangles have all three pairs of corresponding sides equal. The criterion that proves them congruent is:

  • ASAS
  • BSSS✓
  • CASA
  • DRHS

Working

✓

Three pairs of equal corresponding sides is exactly the **SSS** criterion.

Q6

In an isosceles triangle the two equal sides each measure x cm. Which other measurement is always equal to its partner across the axis of symmetry?

  • Athe base only
  • Bthe apex angle's two halves only
  • Cthe two base angles✓
  • Dnothing — only the equal sides match

Working

✓

Fold an isosceles triangle along its axis of symmetry (the altitude from the apex): the two halves coincide, so the **two base angles** are forced equal. The equal sides face equal angles.

Q7

Why are two side-lengths NOT enough to copy a triangle exactly, while three are?

  • Atwo sides give a triangle that is too small
  • Btwo sides leave the perimeter unknown
  • Ctwo sides always force a right angle
  • Dthe two given arcs meet at two points, so two different triangles fit✓

Working

✓

Drawing a base and swinging two arcs from its ends, **the arcs meet at two points** (one above, one below the base), giving two different triangles. A third side removes the ambiguity, so SSS fixes the triangle.

Q8

In ∆ABC and ∆DEF, AB = DE, BC = EF and ∠B = ∠E. By which criterion are they congruent?

  • ASAS✓
  • BASA
  • CSSS
  • DRHS

Working

✓

AB = DE and BC = EF are two sides, and ∠B is the angle **between** them (the included angle). Two sides + included angle = **SAS**.

Q9

The "included angle" between two named sides of a triangle is:

  • Athe angle opposite the longer side
  • Bthe largest angle of the triangle
  • Cthe angle formed where those two sides meet✓
  • Dany angle not touching either side

Working

✓

The included angle of two named sides is **the angle where those two sides meet** (their common vertex). This is the key word in SAS.

Q10

In ∆PQR and ∆XYZ, ∠P = ∠X, ∠Q = ∠Y and PQ = XY. The correct congruence statement and criterion is:

  • A∆PQR ≅ ∆YXZ by ASA
  • B∆PQR ≅ ∆XYZ by SAS
  • Cthey need not be congruent (AA only)
  • D∆PQR ≅ ∆XYZ by ASA✓

Working

✓

Two angles (∠P = ∠X, ∠Q = ∠Y) with the **included** side PQ = XY give **ASA**, and the vertices already correspond in order P↔X, Q↔Y, R↔Z, so ∆PQR ≅ ∆XYZ. (AA alone would only give similarity, but here a side is also equal.)

Set 2 · Q11–Q20

Q11

Each angle of an equilateral triangle measures:

  • A60°✓
  • B90°
  • C45°
  • D120°

Working

✓

All three sides equal ⇒ all three angles equal; they sum to 180°, so each is 180 ÷ 3 = **60°**. (Not 90° and not 360 ÷ 3.)

Q12

An isosceles triangle has apex (vertex) angle 40°. Each base angle measures:

  • A40°
  • B70°✓
  • C100°
  • D140°

Working

✓

Apex 40°, so the two equal base angles share 180 − 40 = 140°, giving 140 ÷ 2 = **70°** each. (40° is the apex itself — the trap.)

Q13

In ∆ABC, ∠B = ∠C = 50°. Which two sides must be equal?

  • AAB = BC
  • BBC = AC
  • Call three are equal
  • DAB = AC✓

Working

✓

Equal angles are faced by equal sides. ∠B and ∠C are equal, so the sides opposite them — AC (opposite B) and AB (opposite C) — are equal: **AB = AC**.

Q14

Two right-angled triangles have equal hypotenuses and one pair of equal legs. They are congruent by:

  • ARHS✓
  • BSSS
  • CSAS
  • DASA

Working

✓

A right angle, equal hypotenuses and one equal leg is precisely the **RHS** criterion. (SSS would need the third side proved equal first; RHS gives it directly.)

Q15

A triangle has angles 50° and 60°. A second triangle also has angles 50° and 60° but is drawn twice as large. The two triangles are:

  • Acongruent by ASA
  • Bsimilar but not congruent✓
  • Ccongruent by AAA
  • Dcongruent by SAS

Working

✓

Equal angles fix only the shape; "twice as large" means a different size. So the triangles are **similar but not congruent** — AAA is not a congruence rule.

Q16

In isosceles ∆ABC, AB = AC and ∠A = 80°. The value of ∠B is:

  • A80°
  • B40°
  • C50°✓
  • D100°

Working

✓

AB = AC ⇒ ∠B = ∠C. With ∠A = 80°, 2∠B = 180 − 80 = 100, so ∠B = **50°**. (80° is ∠A; 100° is the sum of the base angles.)

Q17

To prove the base angles of an isosceles triangle equal, we drop the altitude from the apex and show the two halves are congruent. The criterion used for the two halves is:

  • ARHS✓
  • BSAS
  • CSSS
  • DASA

Working

✓

The altitude makes ∠ADB = ∠ADC = 90° (right angle), AB = AC (hypotenuses), AD common (a leg) → **RHS**. The matching base angles then equal by CPCT.

Q18

Two triangles meet the AAA condition (all three angles equal). We can be sure they are:

  • Acongruent
  • Bthe same shape but possibly different size✓
  • Cthe same size but possibly different shape
  • Dright-angled

Working

✓

AAA guarantees only **the same shape but possibly different size** (similar). It is not a congruence criterion.

Q19

In ∆ABC, AB = 6 cm, AC = 8 cm, ∠A = 55°; in ∆DEF, DE = 6 cm, DF = 8 cm, ∠D = 55°. The triangles are congruent because the equal angle is:

  • Aopposite a side (SSA)
  • Bthe largest angle (AAS)
  • Cincluded between the two equal sides (SAS)✓
  • Da right angle (RHS)

Working

✓

The equal angle ∠A sits **between the two equal sides** AB and AC, so this is the included angle — **SAS**. (If the angle were opposite a side it would be the unreliable SSA case.)

Q20

∆ABC has AB = 5 cm, BC = 7 cm and ∠A = 40° (the angle at A, NOT between AB and BC). A second triangle has the same three measurements in the same role. We can conclude the two triangles are:

  • Acertainly congruent by SAS
  • Bcertainly congruent by ASA
  • Ccertainly congruent by SSS
  • Dnot necessarily congruent — this is the SSA (ambiguous) case✓

Working

✓

Here ∠A is at vertex A while the given sides are AB and BC; ∠A is NOT between them — it is a **non-included angle (SSA)**, which can fit two different triangles. So congruence is **not guaranteed**.

Set 3 · Q21–Q30

Q21

If ∆ABC ≅ ∆DEF, ∠A = 50° and ∠B = 65°, then ∠F equals:

  • A50°
  • B65°✓
  • C115°
  • D130°

Working

✓

In ∆ABC, ∠C = 180 − 50 − 65 = 65°. Congruence ∆ABC ≅ ∆DEF makes ∠F correspond to ∠C, so ∠F = **65°**.

Q22

If ∆ABC ≅ ∆PQR, which side of ∆PQR equals BC?

  • APQ
  • BPR
  • CQR✓
  • Dcannot be decided

Working

✓

Under ∆ABC ≅ ∆PQR the correspondence is A↔P, B↔Q, C↔R. Side BC (between B and C) corresponds to **QR** (between Q and R).

Q23

CPCT stands for "Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles (are equal)." It may be used:

  • Abefore proving the triangles congruent, to set up the proof
  • Bonly for the sides, never for the angles
  • Conly when the triangles are equilateral
  • Donly after the triangles have been proved congruent✓

Working

✓

CPCT lets you conclude corresponding parts are equal — but **only after** the two triangles have been shown congruent. It is a consequence of congruence, never a step to prove it.

Q24

A triangle has two equal sides of 5 cm and an included angle of 60° between them. This triangle is:

  • Aequilateral✓
  • Bright-angled
  • Cisosceles but never equilateral
  • Dscalene

Working

✓

Two equal sides with a 60° included angle: the third side, by the isosceles property the base angles are equal, and equal base angles with a 60° apex force all angles to 60°, so all three sides become equal — the triangle is **equilateral**. (A 5-5 triangle with a 60° apex is the classic equilateral.)

Q25

The minimum number of independent measurements needed to fix a triangle's shape and size is:

  • A2
  • B5
  • C3✓
  • D6

Working

✓

A triangle has six measurements, but only **3** well-chosen independent ones (e.g. SSS, SAS or ASA) are needed to fix it completely. Two are too few; six is unnecessary.

Q26

In ∆ABC, ∠A = 70° and ∠B = 70°. The triangle is:

  • Aequilateral
  • Bisosceles with AB = AC
  • Cright-angled
  • Disosceles with AC = BC✓

Working

✓

∠A = ∠B = 70°, so the sides facing them are equal: side opposite A is BC, side opposite B is AC, hence **AC = BC** (isosceles). (Watch which sides face the equal angles.)

Q27

Two triangles have sides 4 cm, 6 cm, 9 cm and 4 cm, 6 cm, 9 cm respectively. They are:

  • Acongruent by SSS✓
  • Bsimilar but not congruent
  • Ccongruent by SAS
  • Dnot necessarily congruent

Working

✓

All three pairs of sides equal (4, 6, 9 each) ⇒ congruent by **SSS**. Equal corresponding sides means same size too, not merely similar.

Q28

In ∆ABC, AB = AC. If ∠B = (2x + 10)° and ∠C = (3x − 20)°, then x equals:

  • A10
  • B30✓
  • C6
  • D50

Working

✓

AB = AC ⇒ ∠B = ∠C: 2x + 10 = 3x − 20 → 30 = x, so x = **30**. (Check: ∠B = 70° = ∠C.)

Q29

A square is folded along one of its diagonals. The two triangular halves are:

  • Asimilar but not congruent
  • Bcongruent only if the square is also a rectangle
  • Cnever congruent
  • Dcongruent✓

Working

✓

A diagonal splits a square into two triangles with the two sides of the square equal, the included right angle equal, and the diagonal shared — by SAS (or SSS) they are **congruent**. Folding maps one exactly onto the other.

Q30

In ∆ABC ≅ ∆DEF, AB = 7 cm and DF = 9 cm. Given the order of letters, the length of AC is:

  • A9 cm✓
  • B7 cm
  • Ccannot be found
  • D16 cm

Working

✓

∆ABC ≅ ∆DEF gives A↔D, B↔E, C↔F. Side AC corresponds to DF, so AC = DF = **9 cm**. (AB = DE = 7 cm is a different pair — the trap.)

Set 4 · Q31–Q40

Q31

Which statement is FALSE?

  • Aevery equilateral triangle is isosceles
  • Bsimilar triangles are always congruent✓
  • Ccongruent triangles are always similar
  • DAAA does not prove congruence

Working

✓

Congruent ⇒ similar (true), every equilateral triangle is isosceles (true), AAA fails to prove congruence (true). The **false** statement is "**similar triangles are always congruent**" — similar triangles can differ in size.

Q32

In ∆PQR, PQ = PR and ∠Q = 65°. Then ∠P equals:

  • A65°
  • B25°
  • C50°✓
  • D130°

Working

✓

PQ = PR ⇒ ∠Q = ∠R = 65°. ∠P = 180 − 65 − 65 = **50°**. (65° is a base angle, not the apex.)

Q33

Two triangles are drawn: the first with base 5 cm and base angles 50° and 60°; the second with base 5 cm and base angles 50° and 60° in the same positions. They are congruent by:

  • AASA✓
  • BSAS
  • CSSS
  • DRHS

Working

✓

Base 5 cm with the two base angles (50° and 60°) equal in the same positions: two angles + the included side ⇒ **ASA**.

Q34

A right triangle has legs 6 cm and 8 cm. A second right triangle has hypotenuse 10 cm and one leg 6 cm. These two triangles are:

  • Anot congruent
  • Bcongruent (the second's other leg is also 8 cm)✓
  • Csimilar only
  • Dcongruent only if both are isosceles

Working

✓

First triangle: legs 6, 8 ⇒ hypotenuse 10. Second: hypotenuse 10, one leg 6 ⇒ other leg √(100 − 36) = 8. Right angle + equal hypotenuse + equal leg (RHS) makes them **congruent**, with the missing leg also 8 cm.

Q35

∆ABC ≅ ∆DEF is written with that exact letter order. This tells us ∠A corresponds to:

  • A∠E
  • B∠F
  • C∠D✓
  • Dany of the three

Working

✓

The letter order in ∆ABC ≅ ∆DEF pairs the vertices in place: A↔D. So ∠A corresponds to **∠D**.

Q36

In ∆ABC, AD is the median to BC and AB = AC. Comparing ∆ABD and ∆ACD, they are congruent by:

  • AASA
  • BRHS
  • CAAA
  • DSSS✓

Working

✓

AD is a median ⇒ BD = DC; AB = AC (given); AD common. Three pairs of equal sides ⇒ **SSS**.

Q37

The exterior angle at the apex of an isosceles triangle is 100°. Each base angle is:

  • A100°
  • B50°✓
  • C80°
  • D40°

Working

✓

Exterior angle 100° at the apex ⇒ interior apex angle = 180 − 100 = 80°. Base angles share 180 − 80 = 100°, so each = **50°**. (Using 100° directly as the interior apex is the trap.)

Q38

Two triangles cannot be congruent if they have:

  • Aequal perimeters and an SSS match
  • Bthe same three angles
  • Cdifferent areas✓
  • Dtwo equal sides and the included angle equal

Working

✓

Congruent triangles must be identical in size, hence equal area — so two triangles with **different areas** can never be congruent. (Equal perimeter + SSS, equal angles, or SAS are all consistent with congruence.)

Q39

In ∆LMN and ∆RST, LM = RS, ∠L = ∠R and ∠M = ∠S. The congruence criterion is:

  • ASAS
  • BSSS
  • CSSA
  • DASA✓

Working

✓

∠L = ∠R, ∠M = ∠S are two angles, and the side LM = RS lies **between** ∠L and ∠M — included side. Two angles + included side ⇒ **ASA**.

Q40

An isosceles triangle has a base angle of 75°. Its apex angle is:

  • A30°✓
  • B75°
  • C105°
  • D15°

Working

✓

Base angle 75° ⇒ both base angles 75°, summing to 150°. Apex = 180 − 150 = **30°**.

Set 5 · Q41–Q50

Q41

In ∆ABC, ∠A = 90°, AB = AC. Then ∠B equals:

  • A90°
  • B60°
  • C45°✓
  • D30°

Working

✓

∠A = 90°, AB = AC ⇒ ∠B = ∠C; they share 180 − 90 = 90°, so each = **45°**.

Q42

Which of these guarantees congruence of two RIGHT triangles using exactly three matching parts that include the right angle?

  • Aall three angles equal
  • Bone acute angle equal (and the right angle), but no side given
  • Cthe perimeters equal
  • Dthe two pairs of legs equal (and the right angle between them)✓

Working

✓

With the right angle counted as one of the parts, **two pairs of equal legs (with the included right angle)** gives SAS congruence using exactly the right angle + two sides. ("All three angles" is mere AAA — shape only; a right angle + one acute angle with no side given is still only angles (AAA), fixing shape not size; and equal perimeters alone prove nothing.)

Q43

A triangle is fixed by its three sides 3 cm, 4 cm, 5 cm. How many genuinely different (non-congruent) triangles have exactly these three side-lengths?

  • Aexactly one✓
  • Bnone
  • Cexactly two
  • Dinfinitely many

Working

✓

Three fixed side-lengths leave **exactly one** triangle up to congruence (SSS pins it completely). Any two such triangles can be laid exactly on top of each other.

Q44

In ∆ABC, AB = AC and ∠A = 100°. The largest angle of the triangle is:

  • Aeach base angle at 40°
  • B∠A at 100°✓
  • C∠B at 80°
  • Dall equal

Working

✓

AB = AC ⇒ ∠B = ∠C = (180 − 100)/2 = 40°. The biggest angle is the apex, **∠A at 100°** (each base angle is only 40°).

Q45

∆ABC has AB = AC. The bisector of ∠A meets BC at D. Then BD compared with DC is:

  • ABD > DC
  • BBD < DC
  • Ccannot be determined
  • DBD = DC✓

Working

✓

In an isosceles triangle the bisector of the apex angle is also the median and altitude to the base, so it bisects BC: **BD = DC**. (∆ABD ≅ ∆ACD by SAS, then CPCT.)

Q46

Two triangles have two pairs of equal sides and a pair of equal angles, but the equal angle is NOT between the equal sides. This information:

  • Adoes not guarantee congruence (SSA, ambiguous)✓
  • Balways proves congruence by SAS
  • Calways proves congruence by SSS
  • Dproves the triangles are equilateral

Working

✓

Two equal sides and an equal angle that is **not** between them is the SSA pattern, which can fit two different triangles. So it **does not guarantee congruence**.

Q47

In ∆ABC, the perpendicular bisector of side BC passes through A. This means:

  • ABC is the longest side
  • BAB = AC, so the triangle is isosceles✓
  • C∠A = 90°
  • Dthe triangle is scalene

Working

✓

Every point on the perpendicular bisector of BC is equidistant from B and C; since A lies on it, AB = AC, so the triangle is **isosceles**. (It says nothing about a right angle or the longest side.)

Q48

∆ABC ≅ ∆PQR. The perimeter of ∆ABC is 24 cm. The perimeter of ∆PQR is:

  • A12 cm
  • B48 cm
  • C24 cm✓
  • Dcannot be found

Working

✓

Congruent triangles are identical copies, so all corresponding sides are equal and the perimeters match: **24 cm**.

Q49

In ∆ABC, ∠A = 40° and AB = AC. Then ∠C of ∆ABC is:

  • A70°✓
  • B40°
  • C100°
  • D50°

Working

✓

AB = AC ⇒ ∠B = ∠C, and ∠B + ∠C = 180 − 40 = 140°, so ∠C = **70°**. (40° is ∠A, not a base angle.)

Q50

Which pair of conditions is enough to construct a UNIQUE triangle?

  • Athree angles
  • Btwo angles and the included side✓
  • Ctwo sides only
  • Done side and one angle

Working

✓

**Two angles and the included side (ASA)** fix a unique triangle. Three angles give only shape (AAA), two sides alone are ambiguous, and a single side-and-angle is far too little.

Q51

The angles of a triangle are in the ratio 1 : 1 : 2. Such a triangle is:

  • Aequilateral
  • Bscalene
  • Cisosceles and right-angled✓
  • Dimpossible

Working

✓

1 : 1 : 2 over 180° gives angles 45°, 45°, 90°: two equal angles (isosceles) and a right angle — **isosceles and right-angled**.

Q52

In ∆DEF, DE = DF and ∠D = 2∠E. Then ∠E equals:

  • A36°
  • B60°
  • C90°
  • D45°✓

Working

✓

DE = DF ⇒ ∠E = ∠F. With ∠D = 2∠E: ∠D + ∠E + ∠F = 2∠E + ∠E + ∠E = 4∠E = 180 → ∠E = **45°**.

Q53

A triangle has sides 7 cm, 7 cm, 7 cm. Each of its angles is:

  • A45°
  • B60°✓
  • C50°
  • D70°

Working

✓

All sides equal (7, 7, 7) ⇒ equilateral ⇒ every angle = **60°**.

Q54

∆ABC ≅ ∆DEF by SAS, using AB = DE, ∠A = ∠D and AC = DF. The angle ∠A is the included angle between sides:

  • AAB and BC
  • BBC and AC
  • CAB and AC✓
  • Dthe hypotenuse and a leg

Working

✓

SAS uses two sides and the angle between them. The named sides are AB and AC, both meeting at A, so ∠A is included between **AB and AC**.

Q55

Two triangles ∆ABC and ∆ABD share the common base AB, lie on opposite sides of it, with AC = AD and BC = BD. The criterion proving ∆ABC ≅ ∆ABD is:

  • ASAS
  • BASA
  • CRHS
  • DSSS✓

Working

✓

AC = AD, BC = BD (given) and AB is the common base — three pairs of equal sides ⇒ **SSS**.

Q56

In isosceles ∆ABC, AB = AC, so ∠B = ∠C. If ∠B = (x + 20)° and the apex ∠A = (2x)°, then ∠A equals:

  • A70°✓
  • B35°
  • C50°
  • D130°

Working

✓

AB = AC ⇒ ∠B = ∠C = (x + 20)°; with apex ∠A = (2x)°: 2x + (x + 20) + (x + 20) = 180 → 4x + 40 = 180 → x = 35. So ∠A = 2x = **70°**. (Check: base angles 55° each, 70 + 55 + 55 = 180. ✓)

Q57

A child claims: "If two triangles have the same area, they are congruent." This claim is:

  • Aalways true
  • Btrue only for right triangles
  • Cfalse — equal area does not force equal shape and size✓
  • Dtrue only for equilateral triangles

Working

✓

Equal area does NOT force equal shape and size — a tall thin triangle and a short wide one can share an area. So the claim is **false**.

Q58

In ∆ABC, AB = AC = 13 cm and BC = 10 cm. The altitude from A to BC has foot M. The length BM is:

  • A6.5 cm
  • B12 cm
  • C10 cm
  • D5 cm✓

Working

✓

AB = AC, so the altitude from apex A to base BC also bisects BC (isosceles property): M is the midpoint, giving BM = BC/2 = 10/2 = **5 cm**. (6.5 wrongly halves a slant side; 12 is the altitude length √(13² − 5²).)

Q59

∆ABC ≅ ∆ACB (note the letter order) for a single triangle ABC. This self-congruence forces:

  • AAB = AC, i.e. the triangle is isosceles✓
  • B∠A = 90°
  • Cthe triangle is scalene
  • Dthe triangle is equilateral

Working

✓

∆ABC ≅ ∆ACB pairs A↔A, B↔C, C↔B, so AB corresponds to AC ⇒ **AB = AC**: the triangle is isosceles. (This need not make it equilateral or right-angled.)

Q60

A triangle has angles in the ratio 2 : 2 : 5. The triangle is:

  • Aequilateral
  • Bisosceles✓
  • Cright-angled
  • Dscalene

Working

✓

2 : 2 : 5 over 180° (9 parts of 20°) gives 40°, 40°, 100°: two equal angles ⇒ **isosceles** (and obtuse, but isosceles is the classification asked). --- *Solutions complete: 60 / 60.*
