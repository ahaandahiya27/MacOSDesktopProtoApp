# Science — Chapter 19: Earth, Moon and the Sun · Paper 2 (Advanced) Solutions

**1. (C)** The spinning of the Earth on its own axis is **rotation**.

**2. (A)** Earth completes one rotation in about **24 hours**, giving one day-night cycle.

**3. (D)** Day and night arise mainly from Earth's **rotation on its axis** — the lit half faces the Sun.

**4. (B)** Earth's journey around the Sun is its **revolution**.

**5. (B)** One revolution around the Sun takes about **365.25 days** — one year.

**6. (D)** The leftover quarter-day each year is collected into an extra day every four years in a **leap year**.

**7. (A)** Earth's axis is tilted at about **23.5°** from the upright.

**8. (C)** The seasons are caused by **the tilt of Earth's axis as it revolves around the Sun**, not by changing distance.

**9. (A)** It is summer where the hemisphere is **tilted toward the Sun, receiving more direct sunlight**.

**10. (C)** When the Northern Hemisphere has summer, the Southern Hemisphere has **winter** — they are opposite.

**11. (D)** The Moon is Earth's **only natural satellite**.

**12. (B)** The Moon takes about 27.3 days to **orbit once around the Earth**.

**13. (D)** The Moon is not a light source; it shines because it **reflects sunlight**.

**14. (B)** The changing shapes of the lit Moon through the month are its **phases**.

**15. (C)** When the Moon's lit side fully faces Earth as a complete disc, it is the **full moon**.

**16. (A)** When the Earth-facing side is unlit and the Moon is nearly invisible, it is the **new moon**.

**17. (C)** A solar eclipse occurs when **the Moon comes between the Sun and the Earth, casting its shadow on Earth**.

**18. (A)** Because the Moon must sit between Sun and Earth, a solar eclipse happens only at a **new moon**.

**19. (B)** A lunar eclipse occurs when **the Earth comes between the Sun and the Moon, casting its shadow on the Moon**.

**20. (D)** Because Earth must sit between Sun and Moon, a lunar eclipse happens only at a **full moon**.

**21. (A)** The regular rise and fall of sea level is a **tide**.

**22. (D)** Tides are caused mainly by the gravitational pull of the **Moon (with a smaller effect from the Sun)**.

**23. (B)** The Sun is **a star — a huge ball of gas releasing energy by nuclear fusion**.

**24. (C)** A recognisable star pattern is a **constellation**.

**25. (B)** The Saptarishi, part of the Great Bear, is a **constellation**.

**26. (C)** The Sun plus all the bodies orbiting it form the **Solar System**.

**27. (A)** Rocky bodies smaller than planets, mostly orbiting between Mars and Jupiter, are **asteroids**.

**28. (D)** A space rock burning up in the atmosphere as a bright streak is a **meteor (shooting star)**.

**29. (D)** Stars twinkle while planets shine steadily because **starlight, coming from far points, is bent more by Earth's moving air**.

**30. (A)** We see the same face of the Moon because it **takes the same time to rotate once as it takes to orbit Earth once**.

**31. (C)** With no rotation, **one side facing the Sun would have permanent day, the other permanent night**.

**32. (B)** Summer days are longer because **the hemisphere tilted toward the Sun spends longer in sunlight as Earth rotates**.

**33. (C)** Daytime darkens in a total solar eclipse because **the Moon's shadow falls on a part of the Earth, blocking the Sun there**.

**34. (B)** From one new moon to the next is about **one month (about 29–30 days)**.

**35. (D)** Moving from new moon toward full moon, the lit portion is **growing (waxing)**.

**36. (A)** The Pole Star seems fixed because it lies nearly above **Earth's north axis (the North Pole)**.

**37. (A)** Travellers used the Pole Star to **find the direction north at night**.

**38. (B)** Correctly: **the Sun is a star, the Earth is a planet, the Moon is a satellite**.

**39. (D)** The Sun looks largest and brightest because it is **by far the closest star to Earth**.

**40. (C)** A natural satellite **formed in space and orbits naturally, like the Moon**, unlike a human-launched one.

**41. (D)** The seasons would disappear if Earth's axis were **not tilted at all (upright)**.

**42. (C)** High tide faces the Moon because there the Moon's pull on the water is **strongest, lifting the water into a bulge**.

**43. (A)** The Solar System has eight recognised **planets**.

**44. (B)** A comet of ice and dust grows, near the Sun, a glowing **tail pointing away from the Sun**.

**45. (B)** The hidden side we never see directly is loosely called the **"dark side" (far side) of the Moon**.

**46. (D)** If the Moon's orbit lay exactly in Earth's orbital plane, eclipses would happen **at every new moon and full moon**.

**47. (C)** Correctly: **rotation causes day and night; revolution (with tilt) causes the seasons**.

**48. (A)** The eclipsed Moon glows dim red because **some sunlight bends through Earth's atmosphere onto the shadowed Moon**.

**49. (A)** An astronaut weighs less on the Moon because the Moon has **weaker gravity than Earth**.

**50. (D)** The phase order is **new → crescent → first quarter → gibbous → full**.

**51. (C)** Summers are hotter because the Sun's rays **strike that hemisphere more directly and for longer hours**.

**52. (B)** A shooting star is really **a small meteor burning up in the atmosphere**.

**53. (C)** The band of countless stars across a dark sky is part of our galaxy, the **Milky Way**.

**54. (D)** The Moon shows phases because **as it orbits Earth, we see differing amounts of its sunlit half**.

**55. (B)** A solar eclipse is seen from only a narrow strip because **the Moon's small shadow touches only a narrow path, but Earth's large shadow covers the whole Moon for everyone facing it**.

**56. (A)** In increasing size: **Moon < Earth < Sun**.

**57. (B)** Understanding tides matters chiefly for **navigation, fishing and coastal activities that depend on sea level**.

**58. (A)** The bodies link up as **the Earth orbits the Sun while the Moon orbits the Earth**.

**59. (C)** Eclipses do not happen monthly because **the Moon's orbit is slightly tilted, so its shadow usually misses Earth (and vice versa)**.

**60. (D)** Day and night, the seasons, the Moon's phases and the tides all arise from **the motions of the Earth and Moon and the pull of gravity**.
