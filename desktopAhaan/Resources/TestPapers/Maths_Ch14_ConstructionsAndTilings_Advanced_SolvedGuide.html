Set 1 · Q1–Q10

Q1

The standard tools for a perpendicular-bisector construction are:

  • Aa protractor and ruler
  • Ba set square only
  • Ca compass and a straightedge✓
  • Da calculator

Working

✓

Classical constructions use only a compass and a straightedge.

Q2

A perpendicular bisector of a segment always passes through its:

  • Amidpoint✓
  • Bendpoint
  • Clongest part
  • Dreflection

Working

✓

The perpendicular bisector cuts the segment exactly at its midpoint.

Q3

Any point on the perpendicular bisector of AB is equidistant from:

  • Athe midpoint only
  • Bneither A nor B
  • Cthe line itself
  • DA and B✓

Working

✓

Every point on the perpendicular bisector of AB is the same distance from A and from B.

Q4

To bisect a given angle you use:

  • Aa protractor's scale
  • Ba compass and a straightedge✓
  • Ca ruler only
  • Dtwo protractors

Working

✓

An angle is bisected with a compass and a straightedge (equal arcs).

Q5

Constructing a 60° angle relies on the fact that an equilateral triangle has angles of:

  • A90°
  • B45°
  • C60°✓
  • D30°

Working

✓

The 60° construction copies an angle of an equilateral triangle.

To construct a 30° angle, first construct 60° and then:

  • Dreflect it

Bisecting the 60° angle gives 30°.

✓

90° is half of a straight angle, 180°.

Set 2 · Q11–Q20

Q11

A triangle can be constructed from three given side lengths exactly when they satisfy:

  • Athe angle sum
  • Bthe exterior-angle rule
  • CPythagoras' theorem
  • Dthe triangle inequality✓

Working

✓

Three lengths form a triangle exactly when each pair sums to more than the third — the triangle inequality.

Q12

Can a triangle be drawn with sides 3, 4 and 8?

  • Ayes
  • Bno, since 3 + 4 < 8✓
  • Conly if right-angled
  • Donly if isosceles

Working

✓

3 + 4 = 7 < 8, so no such triangle exists.

Q13

The SAS construction of a triangle needs two sides and the:

  • Alargest angle
  • Bopposite angle
  • Cincluded angle✓
  • Dhypotenuse

Working

✓

SAS fixes the triangle using two sides and the angle between them.

Q14

The ASA construction needs two angles and the:

  • Aincluded side✓
  • Blongest side
  • Chypotenuse
  • Dopposite side

Working

✓

ASA uses two angles and the side between them.

Q15

If two angles to be constructed are 70° and 80°, the third angle of the triangle is fixed at:

  • A150°
  • B110°
  • C90°
  • D30°✓

Working

✓

The third angle is 180° − 70° − 80° = 30°.

Q16

Can 100° and 90° be two of the angles of a triangle?

  • Ayes
  • Bno, their sum already exceeds 180°✓
  • Conly if the third is 0°
  • Donly in a right triangle

Working

✓

100° + 90° = 190° already exceeds 180°, so they can't both be angles of one triangle.

Q17

For a unique ASA construction the two given angles must add to less than:

  • A90°
  • B360°
  • C180°✓
  • D270°

Working

✓

The two angles must leave room for a positive third angle, so their sum is below 180°.

Q18

Constructing a right triangle from its hypotenuse and one leg uses the rule:

  • ARHS✓
  • BAAA
  • CSSA
  • DASA

Working

✓

Hypotenuse-and-leg is the RHS case.

Q19

The least number of independent measurements needed to fix a triangle's shape and size is:

  • A1
  • B2
  • C5
  • D3✓

Working

✓

Three independent measurements (e.g. SSS, SAS or ASA) fix a triangle.

Q20

A triangle constructed with sides 5, 5 and 5 is:

  • Ascalene
  • Bequilateral✓
  • Cright-angled
  • Dobtuse

Working

✓

Three equal sides give an equilateral triangle.

Set 3 · Q21–Q30

Q21

Which regular polygon CANNOT tile the plane on its own?

  • Aequilateral triangle
  • Bsquare
  • Cregular pentagon✓
  • Dregular hexagon

Working

✓

A regular pentagon's 108° interior angle does not divide 360°, so it cannot tile alone.

Q22

The number of equilateral triangles that meet at a single vertex in a regular tiling is:

  • A6✓
  • B4
  • C3
  • D8

Working

✓

360° ÷ 60° = 6 equilateral triangles meet at a vertex.

Q23

The number of squares meeting at a vertex in a square tiling is:

  • A6
  • B3
  • C8
  • D4✓

Working

✓

360° ÷ 90° = 4 squares.

Q24

The number of regular hexagons meeting at a vertex in a hexagonal tiling is:

  • A6
  • B3✓
  • C4
  • D2

Working

✓

360° ÷ 120° = 3 hexagons.

Q25

Regular pentagons cannot tile the plane because their interior angle (108°):

  • Ais too small
  • Bis more than 180°
  • Cdoes not divide 360° exactly✓
  • Dequals 90°

Working

✓

108° does not divide 360° exactly, so pentagons leave gaps.

Q26

The interior angle of a regular hexagon is:

  • A120°✓
  • B60°
  • C90°
  • D108°

Working

✓

(6 − 2) × 180° ÷ 6 = 720° ÷ 6 = 120°.

Q27

The interior angle of a regular octagon is:

  • A45°
  • B120°
  • C108°
  • D135°✓

Working

✓

(8 − 2) × 180° ÷ 8 = 1080° ÷ 8 = 135°.

Q28

The number of distinct regular polygons that tile the plane by themselves is:

  • A1
  • B3✓
  • C5
  • D4

Working

✓

Exactly three regular polygons tile alone: the triangle, square and hexagon.

Q29

A semi-regular tiling is one that uses:

  • Aonly triangles
  • Bcurved tiles
  • Cmore than one type of regular polygon✓
  • Dno polygons

Working

✓

A semi-regular tiling mixes more than one kind of regular polygon.

Q30

At any vertex of a tiling, the angles meet to total:

  • A360°✓
  • B180°
  • C90°
  • D270°

Working

✓

The angles around any tiling vertex add up to a full turn, 360°.

  • D108°✓

Six equilateral triangles around a point work because 6 × 60° equals:

  • A180°
  • B360°✓
  • C300°
  • D240°

Working

✓

6 × 60° = 360°, a full turn, so six triangles fit exactly.

Q33

Regular octagons together with squares can tile the plane because at a vertex 135° + 135° + 90° equals:

  • A270°
  • B300°
  • C360°✓
  • D405°

Working

✓

135° + 135° + 90° = 360°, so octagons and squares tile together.

Q34

The interior angle of a regular 12-sided polygon (dodecagon) is:

  • A150°✓
  • B120°
  • C144°
  • D160°

Working

✓

(12 − 2) × 180° ÷ 12 = 1800° ÷ 12 = 150°.

Q35

A regular dodecagon (interior angle 150°) cannot tile the plane alone because 150° does NOT:

  • Aexceed 90°
  • Bequal an exterior angle
  • Cmeasure a reflex angle
  • Ddivide 360° exactly✓

Working

✓

150° does not divide 360° exactly (360 ÷ 150 is not whole), so dodecagons can't tile alone.

Q36

  • D45°

Working

✓

Q37

The sum of the exterior angles of any polygon is:

✓

The exterior angles of any polygon always total 360°.

Q38

The sum of the interior angles of a pentagon is:

  • D450°

Working

✓

(5 − 2) × 180° = 540°.

Q39

The sum of the interior angles of a hexagon is:

  • A540°
  • B900°
  • C1080°
  • D720°✓

Working

✓

(6 − 2) × 180° = 720°.

Q40

A regular polygon whose exterior angle is 40° has each interior angle equal to:

  • A40°
  • B140°✓

  • B12

  • D9

✓

  • A12✓

  • C30
  • D10

Working

✓

n = 360° ÷ 30° = 12 sides.

  • D1080°✓

✓

Q44

A polygon whose interior angles sum to 900° has how many sides?

  • A5
  • B7✓
  • C9
  • D6

Working

✓

Number of sides = (900° ÷ 180°) + 2 = 5 + 2 = 7.

  • Dpair of angle bisectors✓
