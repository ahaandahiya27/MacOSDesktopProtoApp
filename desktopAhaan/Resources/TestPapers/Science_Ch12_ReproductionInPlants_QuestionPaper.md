**Marking scheme:** **+4** for each correct answer · **−1** for each wrong answer · **0** if unattempted.

**Instructions:** Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. All content stays within the NCERT Class 7 chapter on Reproduction in Plants — modes of reproduction, parts of a flower, pollination, fertilisation, fruit and seed formation, and seed dispersal. Many questions need two to four reasoning steps; watch for traps — especially the difference between *pollination* (transfer of pollen) and *fertilisation* (fusion of gametes). Do not write on this paper — mark answers on your answer sheet.

1. Which of the following is a feature of **asexual** reproduction?
   (A) only one parent is involved and no gametes fuse
   (B) gametes fuse to form a zygote
   (C) two parents are always needed
   (D) seeds are always produced

2. A gardener cuts a piece of a rose stem and plants it; it grows into a new rose plant. This is an example of:
   (A) vegetative propagation
   (B) sexual reproduction
   (C) spore formation
   (D) fertilisation

3. New Bryophyllum plants grow from buds present on the:
   (A) tips of its roots
   (B) margins of its leaves
   (C) inside of its flowers
   (D) surface of its seeds

4. The "eyes" of a potato are actually:
   (A) seeds formed after fertilisation
   (B) ovules waiting to be dispersed
   (C) buds that can grow into new plants
   (D) tiny roots

5. Yeast reproduces by a process in which a small outgrowth forms on the parent and detaches. This is called:
   (A) budding
   (B) spore formation
   (C) fragmentation
   (D) pollination

6. Spirogyra, a green alga, multiplies mainly by breaking into pieces, each piece growing into a new individual. This is:
   (A) fragmentation
   (B) budding
   (C) vegetative propagation by leaf
   (D) cross-pollination

7. Bread mould (Rhizopus) and ferns both reproduce by:
   (A) budding
   (B) seed dispersal by wind
   (C) stem cuttings
   (D) spore formation

8. Spores are especially useful to fungi because they:
   (A) can survive unfavourable conditions and spread easily by wind
   (B) need two parents to form
   (C) are heavy and stay near the parent
   (D) are produced only inside flowers

9. Which list contains **only** plants grown by vegetative propagation in everyday gardening?
   (A) potato, ginger, sugarcane, money plant
   (B) mango, coconut, balsam, maple
   (C) yeast, Hydra, Spirogyra, Rhizopus
   (D) drumstick, madar, castor, lotus

10. A student says, "Plants grown from a cutting of one parent are exact copies of that parent." This statement is:
    (A) wrong, because cuttings always mix two parents' traits
    (B) wrong, because cuttings produce seeds first
    (C) correct, because asexual offspring are clones of the single parent
    (D) correct only if the plant also flowers

11. The **male** reproductive part of a flower is the:
    (A) pistil
    (B) ovary
    (C) stamen
    (D) stigma

12. A stamen is made up of:
    (A) stigma and style
    (B) petal and sepal
    (C) ovary and ovule
    (D) anther and filament

13. Inside the flower, pollen grains are produced by the:
    (A) stigma
    (B) anther
    (C) ovary
    (D) ovule

14. The pistil (carpel) of a flower consists of, from top to bottom:
    (A) stigma, style, ovary
    (B) anther, filament, ovary
    (C) petal, sepal, ovule
    (D) ovary, style, anther

15. The female gametes of a flowering plant are found inside the:
    (A) anther
    (B) ovules
    (C) filament
    (D) stigma

16. A flower that has **both** stamens and pistil is described as:
    (A) bisexual
    (B) unisexual
    (C) asexual
    (D) seedless

17. A papaya tree bears separate male flowers and female flowers. These flowers are best described as:
    (A) bisexual
    (B) spore-bearing
    (C) unisexual
    (D) self-pollinating

18. Pollination is correctly defined as the transfer of pollen from the:
    (A) ovary to the ovule
    (B) seed to the soil
    (C) stigma to the anther
    (D) anther to the stigma

19. The sticky, often feathery part of the flower on which pollen lands is the:
    (A) filament
    (B) stigma
    (C) anther
    (D) sepal

20. When pollen from a flower is carried to the stigma of **another flower on the same plant**, the pollination is:
    (A) self-pollination
    (B) cross-pollination
    (C) fertilisation
    (D) fragmentation

21. Cross-pollination occurs when pollen reaches the stigma of:
    (A) the same flower
    (B) a flower on a different plant of the same kind
    (C) another flower on the same plant
    (D) any plant of any kind

22. Which set of flower features would most suit pollination by **insects**?
    (A) dull colour, no scent, no nectar
    (B) bright colour, sweet scent, and nectar
    (C) very light, dry, powdery pollen and no petals
    (D) flowers hidden underwater

23. Wind-pollinated flowers typically have pollen that is:
    (A) sticky and heavy
    (B) wrapped in a hard fruit
    (C) brightly coloured and scented
    (D) light and produced in large amounts

24. A flower is small, green, scentless and produces huge amounts of light, dry pollen. Its likely pollinating agent is:
    (A) insects
    (B) water only
    (C) wind
    (D) birds attracted by colour

25. Fertilisation in a flowering plant is the:
    (A) transfer of pollen to the stigma
    (B) scattering of seeds by wind
    (C) ripening of the ovary into a fruit
    (D) fusion of the male gamete with the egg inside the ovule

26. Which statement correctly separates pollination from fertilisation?
    (A) they are two names for the same event
    (B) pollination needs water but fertilisation does not
    (C) fertilisation happens first, then pollination
    (D) pollination is the transfer of pollen; fertilisation is the fusion of gametes

27. After a pollen grain lands on the stigma, the male gamete reaches the ovule by travelling through a:
    (A) pollen tube growing down the style
    (B) hollow filament
    (C) hole in the petal
    (D) root hair

28. Pollination must happen **before** fertilisation can occur because:
    (A) the ovary ripens into the fruit first
    (B) pollen has to reach the stigma before the male gamete can fuse with the egg
    (C) seeds are dispersed before the gametes meet

29. The immediate product formed when the male gamete fuses with the female gamete is the:
    (A) fruit
    (B) seed coat
    (C) stigma
    (D) zygote

30. After fertilisation, the **ovary** of the flower develops into the:
    (A) seed
    (B) pollen
    (C) fruit
    (D) new flower

31. After fertilisation, each **ovule** develops into a:
    (A) fruit
    (B) seed
    (C) stamen

32. A fruit is found to contain many seeds inside it. This tells us the flower's ovary had:
    (A) only one ovule
    (B) many anthers

33. After fertilisation, what usually happens to the petals, stamens and stigma of the flower?
    (A) they grow into the fruit
    (B) they turn into seeds
    (C) they become the ovary
    (D) they wither and fall off

34. Which sequence of events is in the correct order?

38. Coconut seeds are well suited to dispersal by **water** because they are:
    (A) tiny and dry
    (B) covered with hooks
    (C) light, with a spongy, buoyant husk that floats

    (A) water
    (B) wind
    (C) animals that eat them

40. Seeds of plants such as Xanthium that bear tiny hooks or spines are usually dispersed by:
    (A) bursting open suddenly
    (B) floating on water
    (C) being blown far by wind

    (A) wind
    (B) water
    (C) insects
    (D) explosion (sudden bursting)
