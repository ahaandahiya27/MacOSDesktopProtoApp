**Instructions:** Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. Traps target the classic confusions: equating breathing with respiration, thinking plants don't respire by day, believing anaerobic respiration releases as much energy as aerobic, assuming exhaled air has zero oxygen, confusing the organs of different animals (gills/spiracles/tracheae/skin/stomata/lenticels), reversing the diaphragm/rib movements, mixing up the pressure logic of inhalation/exhalation, and reading breathing-rate data backwards. 1. A teacher writes 'glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy' on the board and asks which item on the right-hand side is NOT a substance you could collect in a jar. The best answer is:
   (A) energy, because it is released, not a material substance
   (B) water, because water is not produced in respiration
   (C) carbon dioxide, because it is used up rather than made
   (D) oxygen, because oxygen appears on the right side too

2. A student claims, 'When I run, I breathe faster, so I respire faster.' The most accurate response is:
   (A) breathing and respiring are unrelated, so faster breathing changes nothing inside the cells
   (B) breathing faster slows respiration because the lungs cannot keep up
   (C) respiration only happens in the lungs, so cell respiration does not change
   (D) faster breathing supplies the extra oxygen the cells need to respire faster, so both speed up together

3. Per molecule of glucose, aerobic respiration releases far more energy than anaerobic respiration. The best reason is that:

4. Yeast added to warm sugar dough makes the dough rise. Connecting cause to effect, the rising is caused by:

   (B) oxygen given off by the yeast as it photosynthesises the sugar

   (D) lactic acid produced by the yeast making the dough swell

5. Two glasses of grape juice are left out: one with baker's yeast and one without. After a day the yeast glass smells of alcohol and bubbles, while the plain glass does not. This shows that:
