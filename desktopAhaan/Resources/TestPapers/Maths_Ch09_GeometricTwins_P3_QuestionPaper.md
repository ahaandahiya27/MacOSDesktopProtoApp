**Instructions:** Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. Beware of treating SSA or AAA as a congruence rule, confusing congruence with mere similarity, miscounting the order of rotational symmetry, and forgetting that the equal angle in SAS must be the included one. 1. In two triangles ABC and PQR you are told AB = PQ, BC = QR and angle C = angle R. A student declares them congruent by SAS. Is the student right, and why or why not?
   (A) Yes — two sides and an angle always give SAS, so the triangles are congruent.
   (B) No — the equal angle (at C and R) is NOT the angle included between the two pairs of equal sides, so this is the ambiguous SSA case and congruence is not guaranteed.
   (C) No, but they are congruent by ASA instead, using the two sides as if they were angles.
   (D) Yes — the triangles are congruent by SSS because two sides already match and the third is then forced.

2. Triangle DEF is congruent to triangle XYZ written in that exact letter order. If DE = 5 cm, EF = 7 cm, FD = 9 cm, which side of triangle XYZ measures 7 cm?
   (A) XY
   (B) YZ
   (C) ZX
   (D) It cannot be decided from the letter order.

3. Two triangles satisfy: angle A = angle P = 50°, angle B = angle Q = 60°, and AB = PQ = 6 cm. By which criterion are triangles ABC and PQR congruent, and is the side AB correctly positioned for it?
   (A) AAA — three angles match once you find the third, so they are congruent by AAA.
   (B) ASA — AB lies between the two equal angles A and B, so it is the included side, making the triangles congruent.
   (C) SAS — two angles and a side count as two sides and an angle.
   (D) They are only similar, not congruent, because the third angle is the same in both.

4. How many lines of symmetry does a non-square rhombus have?
   (A) 2 — its two diagonals.
   (B) 4 — the same as a square.
   (C) 1 — only the longer diagonal.
   (D) 0 — a rhombus has no line of symmetry.

5. What is the order of rotational symmetry of a regular pentagon?
   (A) 10
   (B) 1
   (C) 2
   (D) 5

6. In triangle ABC, AB = AC and AD is the bisector of angle A meeting BC at D. Using the congruence of the two halves, which statement about BD, DC and angle ADB is fully correct?
   (A) BD = DC only; angle ADB need not be 90°.
   (B) angle ADB = 90° only; BD and DC need not be equal.
   (C) Neither follows, because the two halves are merely similar, not congruent.
   (D) BD = DC and angle ADB = 90°, because triangles ABD and ACD are congruent by SAS (AB = AC, angle BAD = angle CAD, AD common).

7. Two arcs of fixed radii are used to construct a triangle on a given base. Why do exactly the side-lengths 3 cm, 4 cm, 5 cm produce only ONE triangle (up to congruence), whereas being told only two of the three sides would not?
   (A) With all three sides fixed, SSS forces a unique triangle; with only two sides the two arcs can meet at more than one point or at none of fixed shape, leaving the triangle undetermined.
   (B) Because 3-4-5 is a right triangle, and only right triangles are uniquely fixed by their sides.
   (C) Because 3 cm, 4 cm, 5 cm have no common factor, and coprime sides always give a unique triangle.
   (D) Two sides actually do fix a unique triangle; the third side adds no new information.

8. A figure looks the same after being reflected in a vertical line through its centre AND after a 180° rotation about its centre. Which capital letter (standard block form) fits BOTH descriptions?
   (A) A
   (B) S
   (C) H
   (D) Z

9. A scalene triangle (all three sides of different lengths) has how many lines of symmetry?
   (A) 1
   (B) 3
   (C) 2
   (D) 0

10. A regular hexagon is rotated about its centre. Through what is the SMALLEST positive angle of rotation that maps it onto itself, and what is its order of rotational symmetry?
   (A) 60°, order 6.
   (B) 120°, order 3.
   (C) 60°, order 12.
   (D) 30°, order 12.

11. Triangles ABC and DEF have AB = DE = 6 cm, AC = DF = 6 cm, and BC = EF = 6 cm. A student says 'they are congruent by SAS.' What is the most precise correct statement?
   (A) They are congruent by SAS, since equal sides also force equal included angles.
   (B) They are congruent, but by SSS (three equal sides), not SAS — no angle was given.
   (C) They are only similar, because equal sides give equal shape but the size could differ.
   (D) Congruence cannot be claimed because no angle is given at all.

12. In triangle PQR, PQ = PR and the bisector of angle P meets QR at S. Combining symmetry and congruence, which set of conclusions is entirely correct?
   (A) QS = SR, but PS need not be perpendicular to QR.
   (B) angle PSQ = 90°, but QS and SR need not be equal.
   (C) PS bisects QR only if angle P happens to be 90°.
   (D) PS is the perpendicular bisector of QR, QS = SR, and angle PSQ = 90°.

13. Which of these has EXACTLY one line of symmetry?
   (A) A rectangle (non-square).
   (B) An equilateral triangle.
   (C) An isosceles (non-equilateral) triangle.
   (D) A circle.

14. If triangle ABC is congruent to triangle DEF and the perimeter of ABC is 30 cm with AB = 8 cm and BC = 11 cm, what is the length of side DF?
   (A) 8 cm
   (B) 30 cm
   (C) 11 cm
   (D) Cannot be found without the angles.

15. Two right triangles have a right angle each. In the first, the legs are 9 cm and 12 cm. In the second, the hypotenuse is 15 cm and one leg is 9 cm. Are they congruent, and by what reasoning?
   (A) No — the second triangle's other leg is unknown, so congruence cannot be settled.
   (B) Yes, congruent by SSA, since two sides and a non-included angle match.
   (C) Yes, congruent by RHS — the first has hypotenuse √(9²+12²)=15 cm, so both share a 15 cm hypotenuse and a 9 cm leg with the right angle.
   (D) Only similar, because one is described by legs and the other by hypotenuse.

16. A square is rotated about its centre. How many times in one full 360° turn (counting the final return to start) does it look exactly the same as its starting position?
   (A) 8
   (B) 4
   (C) 2
   (D) 1

17. When a figure is reflected across a line, which of the following is ALWAYS true of the image compared with the original?
   (A) It is similar but larger than the original.
   (B) It is congruent to the original (same size and shape) but its orientation (left/right sense) is reversed.
   (C) It is congruent and keeps the same orientation as the original.
   (D) Its area is doubled, since two copies now exist.

18. Two triangles ABC and DEF have angle A = angle D = 90°. Also AB = DE = 5 cm and AC = DF = 12 cm (the two legs). Which criterion most directly proves congruence, and is RHS appropriate here?
   (A) RHS, because both triangles contain a right angle.
   (B) ASA, since the right angle counts as one of two equal angles.
   (C) SAS is the most direct (two legs with the included right angle equal); RHS is not needed because we have both legs, not the hypotenuse.
   (D) AAA, because both have a 90° angle and the shapes match.

19. A figure has rotational symmetry of order 1. What does this tell you?
   (A) It has NO rotational symmetry beyond the trivial full turn — only a 360° rotation brings it back, so effectively it is not rotationally symmetric.
   (B) It looks the same after a 90° rotation.
   (C) It has exactly one line of symmetry.
   (D) It can be turned through 180° and look unchanged.

20. In triangles ABC and DEF, AB = DE, angle A = angle D, and angle B = angle E. A student concludes congruence by ASA. A second student says it is actually AAS for some pairing. Which is the safest correct verdict?
   (A) Not congruent — two angles and a side never fix a triangle.
   (B) Only similar, since two equal angles give the same shape but the size is free.
   (C) Congruent by SAS, treating the two angles as the 'two sides'.
   (D) Congruent by ASA — AB is the side included between the two equal angles A and B.

21. Point P is reflected across a line m to give image P'. Which property of the segment PP' relative to line m is correct?
   (A) Line m passes through P' but not through the midpoint of PP'.
   (B) Line m is parallel to PP'.
   (C) P and P' are the same distance from m only if PP' is horizontal.
   (D) Line m is the perpendicular bisector of PP' — it crosses PP' at its midpoint at a right angle.

22. If triangle ABC ≅ triangle DEF, angle A = 48° and angle E = 67°, what is the measure of angle C?
   (A) 65°
   (B) 67°
   (C) 48°
   (D) Cannot be found.

23. Which English capital letter (block form) has rotational symmetry of order 2 but NO line of symmetry?
   (A) O
   (B) T
   (C) S
   (D) E

24. A triangle has two sides 6 cm and 8 cm with a 5 cm side, matched against another triangle with sides 6 cm, 8 cm and 5 cm. A child argues 'they need not be congruent because no angle is given.' Evaluate this.
   (A) The child is wrong — all three pairs of sides are equal, so SSS proves them congruent without any angle.
   (B) The child is right — at least one angle is always required to prove congruence.
   (C) They are only similar, because three sides fix shape but not size.
   (D) They are congruent, but only by SAS using the 6 cm and 8 cm sides.

25. An equilateral triangle is reflected in one of its three lines of symmetry. The image is then rotated 120° about the triangle's centre. The final figure compared with the original equilateral triangle is:
   (A) Larger than the original because two transformations were applied.
   (B) A mirror image that can never be made to coincide with the original.
   (C) Rotated 240° from the original and so out of place.
   (D) Congruent and exactly coincident with the original (occupies the same region), because both a reflection in an axis and a 120° rotation map an equilateral triangle onto itself.

26. Triangles ABC and ABD share side AB, with C and D on opposite sides of line AB. Given AC = AD and BC = BD, by which criterion is triangle ABC ≅ triangle ABD?
   (A) SSS — AC = AD, BC = BD, and AB is common to both.
   (B) SAS, using AB and the angle at A.
   (C) RHS, because the two triangles together look like a kite with a right angle.
   (D) ASA, using the two base angles.

27. How many lines of symmetry and what order of rotational symmetry does a regular octagon have?
   (A) 8 lines of symmetry but rotational symmetry of order 4.
   (B) 8 lines of symmetry and rotational symmetry of order 8.
   (C) 4 lines of symmetry and rotational symmetry of order 8.
   (D) 16 lines of symmetry and rotational symmetry of order 8.

28. A rectangle that is NOT a square has how many lines of symmetry, and what is its order of rotational symmetry?
   (A) 2 lines of symmetry and rotational symmetry of order 2.
   (B) 4 lines of symmetry and rotational symmetry of order 4.
   (C) 2 lines of symmetry and rotational symmetry of order 1.
   (D) 0 lines of symmetry and rotational symmetry of order 2.

29. In triangle ABC, AB = AC and angle A = 36°. The angle bisector from B meets AC at D. Using base-angle and isosceles reasoning, what is angle DBC?
   (A) 72°
   (B) 36°

30. Two congruent figures are placed so that one is the mirror image of the other across a line. Which statement about their areas and perimeters is correct?
   (A) Perimeters are equal but areas may differ.
   (B) Areas are equal but perimeters may differ.
   (C) Both their areas and their perimeters are equal, because reflection preserves all lengths and the enclosed region's size.
   (D) Both area and perimeter are halved in the mirror image.

31. If triangle ABC ≅ triangle PQR, which of the following equalities is guaranteed by CPCT (corresponding parts)?
   (A) angle B = angle Q and AC = PR.
   (B) angle B = angle R and AC = PQ.
   (C) AB = QR and angle A = angle Q.
   (D) angle C = angle P and BC = PR.

32. An equilateral triangle has rotational symmetry of order 3. After how many degrees of the SMALLEST positive rotation does it first coincide with itself, and how does this relate to its three lines of symmetry?
   (A) 60°, with 3 lines of symmetry — half the rotation gives the same look.
   (B) 120°; it has order 3 rotation AND 3 lines of symmetry, the counts matching because it is a regular polygon.
   (C) 120°, but only 1 line of symmetry through the top vertex.
   (D) 90°, with 4 lines of symmetry like a square.

33. Which statement is FALSE?
   (A) If two triangles have equal areas, they must be congruent.
   (B) If two triangles are congruent, they are also similar.
   (C) AAA proves similarity but not congruence.
   (D) Reflection produces a figure congruent to the original.

34. A capital letter 'N' is reflected in a vertical mirror line. The result looks identical to which letter?
   (A) A reversed N, which looks like the letter 'И' / a back-to-front N — it does NOT match a normal N (because N has no vertical line of symmetry).
   (B) A normal N, because N is symmetric about a vertical line.
   (C) The letter Z, because reflection turns N into Z.
   (D) The letter M, because N and M are reflections of each other.

35. In triangle ABC, the perpendicular from A to BC has foot M, and you observe BM = MC. What can you conclude, and by which congruence does it follow?
   (A) Nothing can be concluded; the foot being the midpoint is a coincidence.
   (B) angle A = 90°, by RHS.
   (C) AB = AC (the triangle is isosceles), since triangles ABM and ACM are congruent by SAS (BM = MC, the right angles at M equal, AM common).
   (D) The triangle is equilateral, by SSS.

36. Two triangles satisfy angle B = angle Q = 90°, hypotenuse AC = PR = 13 cm, and one leg AB = PQ = 5 cm. Which criterion proves congruence, and why is this NOT the ambiguous SSA case?
   (A) SSA — and so the triangles need not be congruent.
   (B) SAS, with the right angle included between the hypotenuse and the leg.
   (C) RHS proves congruence; the right angle fixes the configuration so the third side is uniquely 12 cm, removing the SSA ambiguity.
   (D) ASA, using the right angle and one other angle.

37. Which figure has rotational symmetry of order MORE than 1 but ZERO lines of symmetry?
   (A) A non-square rectangle.
   (B) An isosceles trapezium.
   (C) A regular pentagon.
   (D) A parallelogram that is neither a rectangle nor a rhombus.

38. A figure is mapped onto itself by a rotation of 90° about a point. What is the LEAST possible order of its rotational symmetry?
   (A) 2

39. In triangle ABC, AB = AC = 10 cm and BC = 12 cm. The altitude from A meets BC at M. Using the isosceles symmetry, what is the length AM?
   (A) 6 cm

   (D) 2 cm

40. Two triangles have all three angles respectively equal (AAA), and additionally one pair of corresponding sides are equal. What can be concluded?
   (A) Only similar, because AAA can never give congruence even with a side.
   (B) They are congruent — once one pair of corresponding sides is equal, equal angles (ASA/AAS) fix the size as well.
   (C) Congruent by AAA, because all three angles match.
   (D) Nothing, because mixing angle and side conditions is not allowed.

41. The diagonal of a kite ABCD (with AB = AD and CB = CD) is AC. Reflecting the kite across line AC maps B onto:
   (A) C, the far vertex.
   (B) D — line AC is the axis of symmetry of the kite, swapping B and D.
   (C) A, the near vertex.
   (D) the midpoint of BD.

42. Triangle ABC is isosceles with AB = AC. We write the self-correspondence ABC ↔ ACB. This 'maps the triangle to itself' and shows angle B = angle C. Which congruence criterion justifies this self-congruence?
   (A) SAS — AB = AC, angle A = angle A (common), AC = AB, mapping the triangle onto its mirror image, giving angle B = angle C by CPCT.
   (B) SSS, because all three sides obviously match a triangle with itself.
   (C) ASA, using angle B and angle C which we are trying to prove equal.
   (D) RHS, because the triangle must contain a right angle.

43. A figure looks the same after rotations of 90°, 180° and 270°. How many lines of symmetry MUST it have?
   (A) Exactly 4.
   (B) Exactly 2.
   (C) It need not have any — order-4 rotational symmetry does not by itself guarantee a line of symmetry.
   (D) At least 1.

44. In triangle ABC and triangle DEF, AB = DE = 7 cm, angle B = angle E = 40°, and BC = EF = 9 cm. The student must pick the criterion. The correct one is:
   (A) SSA — the angle is not between the two sides, so congruence is uncertain.
   (B) SAS — the equal angle B (= E) is included between the equal sides AB and BC.
   (C) ASA — two equal angles and a side.
   (D) RHS — the 40° angle plays the role of a right angle.

45. Which of these regular figures has the GREATEST order of rotational symmetry: equilateral triangle, square, regular hexagon, regular dodecagon (12 sides)?
   (A) The regular hexagon, with order 6.
   (B) The square, because four right angles maximise symmetry.
   (C) All four are equal, since every regular polygon has the same rotational order.
   (D) The regular dodecagon, with order 12.

46. Triangle ABC has angle A = angle B. A line is drawn so that triangle ABC reflects onto itself. Which line is the axis of symmetry?
   (A) The perpendicular bisector of AB (equivalently, the line through C and the midpoint of AB), because angle A = angle B makes CA = CB, so the triangle is isosceles with axis through C.
   (B) The line AB itself.
   (C) The perpendicular bisector of CA.
   (D) There is no axis of symmetry.

47. Two triangles ABC and DEF have AB = DE, BC = EF and a pair of equal angles. For congruence to be GUARANTEED, the equal angle must be:
   (A) the angle at A and D (opposite sides BC and EF) -- a side-side-angle match still pins the triangle down.
   (B) the angle at C and F -- then SSA still guarantees congruence.
   (C) the angle at B and E (between AB and BC, and between DE and EF) — the included angle, giving SAS.
   (D) any one of the three pairs of angles; the position does not matter.

52. Two triangles are reflections of each other across a line (mirror images). A student insists 'a triangle and its mirror image can never be congruent because one is left-handed and one is right-handed.' Evaluate this for triangles.
   (A) The student is right — mirror images are only similar, never congruent.
   (B) The student is right for scalene triangles but wrong for isosceles ones.
   (C) Mirror images are congruent only if the triangle is equilateral.
   (D) The student is wrong — a triangle and its reflection are always congruent (all corresponding sides and angles are equal); congruence allows flips, so 'handedness' does not block it.

53. Triangle ABC ≅ triangle DEF, and you know angle A = 2·angle B, angle C = 80°. What is angle E (in triangle DEF)?
   (A) 66.7°, the value of angle A.
   (B) About 33.3° (one-third of 100°), since angle A + angle B = 100°, angle A = 2·angle B gives angle B = 100/3 ≈ 33.3°, and E corresponds to B.
   (C) 80°, the value of angle C.
   (D) 50°, half of 100°.

54. Which statement about lines of symmetry is TRUE?
   (A) Every quadrilateral has at least one line of symmetry.
   (B) A scalene triangle has exactly one line of symmetry.
   (C) A circle has infinitely many lines of symmetry, since any diameter is an axis.
   (D) An angle (two rays from a vertex) has no line of symmetry.

55. In triangle ABC, AD is the median to BC and it is also perpendicular to BC. What does this combination force, and by which congruence?
   (A) angle A = 90°, by RHS.
   (B) AB = AC, the triangle is isosceles, by SAS on triangles ABD and ACD (BD = DC since AD is a median, equal right angles at D, AD common).
   (C) Nothing extra — a median can always be perpendicular without consequence.
   (D) The triangle is equilateral, by SSS.

56. An equilateral triangle and a regular hexagon are compared. Which has more lines of symmetry, and by how many more?
   (A) They have the same number of lines of symmetry.

   (C) The regular hexagon, with 6 lines, has 3 more than the equilateral triangle's 3 lines.
   (D) The hexagon has 6 more lines than the triangle.

57. Two triangles ABC and PQR have angle A = angle P, AB = PQ, and AC = PR. A student writes 'congruent by ASA.' What is the correct criterion, and is the student right?
   (A) ASA is correct, treating the two sides as the two angles.
   (B) SSA — and so congruence is not guaranteed.
   (C) AAA, because the third angle will also match.
   (D) SAS — angle A is included between sides AB and AC; the student is wrong to call it ASA because only one angle is given.

58. A figure has both a horizontal and a vertical line of symmetry. What is the MINIMUM order of rotational symmetry it must have?
   (A) 1 — two mirror lines do not force any rotational symmetry.
   (B) 4 — two mirror lines always give order-4 rotation.
   (C) 0 — rotational order is unrelated to mirror lines.
   (D) 2 — having two perpendicular mirror lines forces at least order-2 (180°) rotational symmetry.

59. Triangle ABC ≅ triangle BAC for the SAME triangle ABC (note the swapped first two letters). What does this self-congruence force?
   (A) AB = BC, making it isosceles with apex A.

   (C) The triangle is equilateral.
   (D) angle C = 90°.

   (A) Both (i) and (ii) are false.
