Instructions: Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. Classic traps at this level: charging an 'included first item' twice, distributing a coefficient (or a leading minus) over only the first term of a bracket, merging unlike terms or terms of different powers, dropping or sign-flipping a constant, squaring a sum as if it were the sum of squares, applying a negative sign before versus after squaring, mistaking 2n for n+2 or 3a for a-cubed, and reading a pattern's first value as its common difference. Keep every sign exactly as given; only LIKE terms combine; this chapter builds and simplifies expressions and reads off rules — it never solves equations. Worked Solutions

1. (C) First courier: 18(d - 3) + 70 = 18d - 54 + 70 = 18d + 16. Second courier: 25d. Extra cost of the second over the first = 25d - (18d + 16) = 25d - 18d - 16 = 7d - 16. Writing 7d + 16 fails to carry the minus onto the +16 constant. Adding the two per-km rates as 43d ignores that one whole cost is being subtracted from the other. Keeping -70 forgets that the 70 was already reduced by the 18*3 covered kilometres. Seven d minus sixteen is the simplified difference.

2. (B) a^2 = 4 so 2a^2 = 8. ab = (-2)(5) = -10 so -3ab = -3*(-10) = +30. b^2 = 25. Total 8 + 30 + 25 = 63. Treating -3ab as -3*(+10) = -30 (dropping the negative sign on a) gives 8 - 30 + 25 = 3, and a further slip lands on -17. Using 2a^2 = (2*-2)^2 = 16 instead of 2*(a^2) gives 16 + 30 - ... an inflated total. The value sixty-three is the only one that keeps every sign on the substitution.

3. (C) Inner bracket first: 3x - (x - 4y) = 3x - x + 4y = 2x + 4y. Then -2[2x + 4y] = -4x - 8y. The first product 5(2x - 3y) = 10x - 15y. Sum: 10x - 15y - 4x - 8y + 7y. x-terms: 10x - 4x = 6x. y-terms: -15y - 8y + 7y = -16y. Result 6x - 16y. Distributing the -2 over only the 3x leaves the y-count short, giving -2y. Forgetting to combine the 10x and -4x gives 2x. Mis-signing the -8y gives +16y. Six x minus sixteen y is correct.

4. (A) Factor out -12: -12(2x) + (-12)(-3) = -24x + 36 = 36 - 24x. Check the choice: -12(2x - 3) = -24x + 36, correct. 12(3 - 24x) = 36 - 288x, which over-shrinks the x-term. -12(2x + 3) = -24x - 36, wrong constant sign. 6(6 - 24x) = 36 - 144x, again the wrong coefficient on x. Only minus-twelve times the quantity two x minus three reproduces the original.

5. (C) Stage n has (n + 1)^2 tiles, since stage 1 = 4 = 2^2, stage 2 = 9 = 3^2. Going to stage n+1 gives (n + 2)^2. The increase is (n + 2)^2 - (n + 1)^2 = (n^2 + 4n + 4) - (n^2 + 2n + 1) = 2n + 3. Check directly: 9 - 4 = 5 = 2(1)+3, 16 - 9 = 7 = 2(2)+3. The plain square-difference 2n + 1 fits a pattern starting at 1^2, not this one shifted up by one. n + 1 mistakes the side-length growth for the tile growth. Two n plus three is the actual difference.

6. (C) The term -3x has coefficient -3 (the sign stays with the number). The term 5x^2 has coefficient 5. The constant term is the number with no letter, 8. The order asked is coefficient of x, then x^2, then constant: -3, 5, 8. Reporting +3 drops the minus that belongs to the x-term. Reporting -8 sign-flips a constant that is genuinely positive. Reporting -1 for x confuses the coefficient of x with that of x^3. Minus three, five, eight is correct.

7. (D) Increase by 5: x + 5. Double it: 2(x + 5) = 2x + 10. Subtract 3 times the original: 2x + 10 - 3x = -x + 10. Forgetting to distribute the doubling over the 5 (writing 2x + 5) gives -x + 5. Doubling only x (and adding 5 separately as 5x) misreads the wording. Minus x plus ten keeps both the distributed 10 and the single leftover x.

8. (D) (a - b)^2 = (7 - 3)^2 = 4^2 = 16. a^2 - b^2 = 49 - 9 = 40. So 16 - 40 = -24. Treating (a - b)^2 as a^2 - b^2 (the classic squaring-a-difference error) would make the whole thing 0, and a partial slip lands on -8. Forgetting the leading minus on the bracket gives +24. Stopping at a^2 - b^2 reports 40. Minus twenty-four keeps the true square 16 minus 40.

9. (C) Innermost: 3a - (a - 5) = 3a - a + 5 = 2a + 5. Next level: [2a + 5] so 2a - (2a + 5) - 4 = 2a - 2a - 5 - 4 = -9. Now 4a - {-9} = 4a + 9. Mishandling the double minus as -9 kept negative gives 4a - 9 then a slip to 4a - 1. Dropping the -4 inside gives 4a + ... a different constant. Four a plus nine results from turning the subtracted brace, which evaluates to negative nine, into a plus nine.

10. (B) Test each: 15(2x - 3) = 30x - 45, equivalent. -15(3 - 2x) = -45 + 30x = 30x - 45, equivalent. 5(6x - 9) = 30x - 45, equivalent. But 5(6x - 8) = 30x - 40, which has the wrong constant (-40 not -45), so it is NOT equivalent. The trap rewards checking the constant term after distributing, not just the x-coefficient.

11. (D) 10th term: 3(10) + 2 = 32. 20th term: 3(20) + 2 = 62. Sum 32 + 62 = 94. Using only 3n and forgetting the +2 on each term gives 30 + 60 = 90. Reporting just the 20th term (62) ignores the word SUM. Treating the sum's index as 30 and computing one term 3(30)+2 = 92 is close but wrong logic; ninety-four is the genuine total of the two named terms.

12. (A) At x = 4: P = 16 - 3 = 13; Q = 12 + 1 = 13; R = 8 + 6 = 14; S = 16 - 5 = 11. The only equal pair is P and Q, both 13. R = 14 and S = 11 are the distractor values of the unmatched expressions. Computing x^2 as (4)^2 correctly is essential; a slip to 16 for P would wrongly pair it elsewhere. Thirteen is the shared value of P and Q.

13. (B) Add the two fixed charges: 2000 + 500 = 2500. The only per-guest charge is 60 each, giving 60g. Total 60g + 2500. Forgetting the 500 cleaning fee leaves 60g + 2000. Merging the fixed 2500 into the rate as 2560g wrongly multiplies a one-time fee by g. Dropping the 2000 base leaves 60g + 500. Sixty g plus twenty-five hundred combines both flat fees correctly.

14. (B) 2x - y = 6 - (-1) = 7, so (2x - y)^2 = 49. x + y = 3 + (-1) = 2. So 49 / 2 = 24.5. Computing 2x - y as 6 - 1 = 5 (sign slip) gives 25/2 = 12.5, near a wrong choice. Forgetting to square before dividing or halving twice yields 12.25. Twenty-four point five is the squared numerator over the correct denominator two.

15. (B) Distribute the middle minus over every term: -(x^2 - 4x - 3) = -x^2 + 4x + 3. Combine: x^2 terms 3 - 1 = 2 giving 2x^2; x terms -2 + 4 + 2 = 4 giving 4x; constants 5 + 3 - 1 = 7. Result 2x^2 + 4x + 7. Distributing the minus over only the first term leaves a +1 constant. Keeping -4x as +(-4x)... mishandling the +4x flips the x-sign. Two x-squared plus four x plus seven is the full distribution.

16. (A) (1/3)(9x - 6) = 3x - 2. (1/2)(4x - 10) = 2x - 5. Subtract: (3x - 2) - (2x - 5) = 3x - 2 - 2x + 5 = x + 3. Failing to flip the sign of the -5 when subtracting the bracket gives x - 7. Adding the two leading coefficients as 5x ignores that one bracket is subtracted. Mis-signing the constants gives x - 3. x plus three is the careful difference.

17. (D) Layer 6: 6*7 = 42. Layer 7: 7*8 = 56. Added = 56 - 42 = 14. The differences run 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14 — the jump into layer 7 is the sixth difference, 14. Reading the difference pattern off by one gives 12. Reporting layer 6's total (42) or layer 7's total (56) answers a different question. Fourteen is the genuine increment.

18. (C) The sign always travels with the coefficient: -2x^2 gives coefficient -2; +7x gives coefficient 7; the lone number -9 is the constant term. The option claiming +2 drops the minus on the squared term. The option with -7 sign-flips the x-term, which is genuinely positive. The option with +9 sign-flips the constant. Only minus two, seven, minus nine reads every sign faithfully.

19. (C) Mango cost: 240n. Orange crates number 2n at 180 each: 180*2n = 360n. Subtotal 240n + 360n = 600n, plus the one-time 150 transport = 600n + 150. Forgetting to double the orange crates gives 240n + 180n = 420n. Multiplying the flat charge by something doubles the constant to 300. Treating 'twice as many' as a power inflates to n-squared. Six hundred n plus one fifty is correct.

20. (B) Numerator a^2 - b^2 = 81 - 16 = 65. Denominator a - b = 9 - 4 = 5. 65 / 5 = 13. (This equals a + b = 13, a useful check.) Reporting 5 gives only the denominator. Reporting 65 stops at the numerator. Dividing the wrong way (5/65... or mis-squaring) yields 3.25. Thirteen is the quotient and matches a + b.

21. (C) First brace: 2a - (a - b) = 2a - a + b = a + b; times 3 gives 3a + 3b. Second brace: 3b - (a + b) = 3b - a - b = -a + 2b; times -2 gives 2a - 4b. Add the trailing a. Total: (3a + 2a + a) + (3b - 4b) = 6a - b. Forgetting the minus on the second bracket flips to +b. Mis-distributing the leading 3 over only the first term loses 2a. Six a minus b is the careful sum.

22. (D) 2(3x - 4) = 6x - 8. -3(2x - 5) = -6x + 15. 4(x - 2) = 4x - 8. x-terms: 6x - 6x + 4x = 4x. Constants: -8 + 15 - 8 = -1. Result 4x - 1. Distributing the minus over only the 2x (leaving +5 unchanged sign) gives constant +15. Forgetting one -8 lands on -21. Adding all coefficients as 12x ignores the cancellation. Four x minus one is correct.

23. (D) Row 1 = 14 means at r = 1 the rule must give 14. With common difference 3 the rule is 3r + c where 3(1) + c = 14, so c = 11: rule 3r + 11. Check row 2: 3(2) + 11 = 17 = 14 + 3, correct. Row 8: 3(8) + 11 = 35. Writing 3r + 14 over-counts because it gives 17 at row 1. Swapping rate and start gives 14r + 3. Three r plus eleven, value thirty-five, fits the first row exactly.
