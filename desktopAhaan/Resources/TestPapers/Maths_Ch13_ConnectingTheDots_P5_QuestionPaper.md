**Marking scheme:** **+4** for each correct answer · **−1** for each wrong answer · **0** if unattempted.

**Instructions:** Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. These problems stack constraints: solve for a missing value only after fixing the total, re-sort before reading any median, weight every combined mean by its group size, never average two means naively, treat the change in a mean as distinct from the new data point, read a bar's true value from its scale and axis-start (not its apparent height), and for probability count favourable outcomes over equally likely total outcomes. Do not write on this paper — mark answers on your answer sheet.

1. The mean of seven numbers is 30. The mean of the smallest four of them is 24 and the mean of the largest four is 39. What is the value of the fourth number (which belongs to BOTH groups, since the two groups of four overlap in exactly one number)?
   (A) 30
   (B) 63
   (C) 15
   (D) 42

2. The mean of 9 numbers is 14. A 10th number is added and the mean rises to 16; then an 11th number is added and the mean drops back to 15. What is the 11th number?

20. In a data set every value appears exactly once except one value v that appears FOUR times. Three copies of a new value w (w ≠ v, previously absent) are added. After this, the mode is:
