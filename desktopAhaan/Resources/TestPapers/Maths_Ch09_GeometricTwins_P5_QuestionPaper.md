**Marking scheme:** **+4** for each correct answer · **−1** for each wrong answer · **0** if unattempted.

**Instructions:** Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. This is the hardest tier: expect multi-step chains that combine isosceles base-angle reasoning with Pythagoras, congruence criteria layered on top of CPCT, self-correspondence arguments, and symmetry counts woven into angle work. The recurring traps are SSA disguised as SAS, AAA or equal-area/equal-perimeter mistaken for congruence, the wrong base angle in nested isosceles figures, mixing up the order of rotational symmetry with the count of mirror lines, and reading a correspondence's letter order incorrectly. Do not write on this paper — mark answers on your answer sheet.

1. In triangle ABC, AB = AC = 17 cm and BC = 30 cm. A point P lies on the base BC with BP = 9 cm. The length of the segment AP is:
   (A) 8 cm
   (B) 10 cm
   (C) √145 cm
   (D) 12 cm

2. Triangles ABC and DEF have AB = DE = 9 cm, AC = DF = 12 cm and angle B = angle E = 90°. A student writes 'RHS'. What is the most precise correct verdict?
   (A) Congruent, but the right angle is at B/E with hypotenuse AC/DF = 12 cm and leg AB/DE = 9 cm, so RHS does apply (equal right angle, equal hypotenuse, equal leg)
   (B) Not congruent, because angle B is between the two given sides making this the ambiguous SSA case
   (C) Congruent by SAS, since the right angle is included between AB and AC
   (D) Only similar, because one piece given is a hypotenuse and the other a leg

3. In triangle ABC, AB = AC. The bisector of angle B meets AC at P, and BP = BC. If angle A = 36°, find angle ABP.
   (A) 36°

   (D) 24°

4. A figure is mapped onto itself by reflection in a horizontal mirror line and by reflection in a vertical mirror line through the same centre, but by NO other mirror line. What is its order of rotational symmetry, and could it be a regular polygon?
   (A) Order 4, because two perpendicular mirror lines always force 90° rotational symmetry
   (B) Order 2 (the two perpendicular reflections compose to a 180° rotation), and it cannot be a regular polygon with more than 4 sides since those have more than 2 mirror lines
   (C) Order 1, since reflections never create rotational symmetry
   (D) Order 2, and it must be a square

8. Two triangles ABC and DEF satisfy AB = DE = 6 cm, BC = EF = 8 cm and angle A = angle D = 90°. A student says 'SAS using the right angle.' The correct verdict is:
   (A) SAS, because the right angle is the included angle between AB and BC
   (B) SSA, so congruence is not guaranteed
   (C) RHS: angle A = 90° is opposite BC, so BC (8 cm) is the hypotenuse and AB (6 cm) a leg; equal right angle, equal hypotenuse, equal leg gives congruence
   (D) Only similar, since the second triangle's other leg is unknown

10. Triangle ABC ≅ triangle CBA for the SAME triangle (note A and C swapped, B fixed). What is forced, and what is the strongest classification?

   (C) angle B = 90°

12. Triangle ABC has AB = AC. D is on AB and E is on AC with AD = AE. Lines BD... rather, segments BE and CD intersect at O. By which congruence is triangle ABE ≅ triangle ACD, and what does it give about OB and OC?
   (A) SSS, and it gives OB = OD
   (B) ASA, and it gives nothing about O
   (C) AAA, so only similarity follows and OB, OC are unrelated
   (D) SAS (AB = AC, angle A common, AE = AD) gives BE = CD; and by further congruence OB = OC, so triangle OBC is isosceles

13. The angles of a triangle are in the ratio 2 : 3 : 4, and the triangle is then reflected in a line. How many lines of symmetry does the reflected triangle have, and is it congruent to the original?

   (B) 0 lines of symmetry, and it is only similar because reflection reverses orientation
   (C) 0 lines of symmetry, and it is congruent to the original (reflection preserves all lengths and angles)
   (D) 3 lines of symmetry, because the angles are all different

16. Two triangles have all three pairs of angles equal, and the perimeter of the first is exactly equal to the perimeter of the second. Can we conclude they are congruent?

   (C) Yes — equal corresponding angles make them similar, and equal perimeters then force the scale factor to be 1, so corresponding sides are equal and they are congruent

20. Triangles ABC and PQR satisfy BC = QR, angle B = angle Q and angle C = angle R. A student writes 'congruent by SAS.' The correct criterion is:

22. Triangle ABC has vertices arranged so that AB = AC = 5 cm and BC = 6 cm. A second triangle DEF has DE = DF = 5 cm and an area equal to triangle ABC's area but EF ≠ 6 cm. Which is true?
