**Instructions:** Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. Watch for SSA dressed as SAS, AAA/AAS misread as congruence, the wrong base angle in algebra problems, conflating line symmetry with rotational symmetry, and assuming equal area or equal perimeter forces congruence. 1. In triangles ABC and PQR, AB = PQ = 7 cm, AC = PR = 7 cm and BC = QR = 7 cm. A student writes "congruent by SAS, since the equal sides force a 60° included angle." Choose the most precise correct verdict.
   (A) Congruent by SAS, because each triangle is equilateral so the 60° included angle is automatically equal.
   (B) Only similar, because three equal sides fix the shape but the size could still differ.
   (C) Congruence cannot be claimed, since at least one angle measurement is required by every criterion.
   (D) They are congruent, but the criterion is SSS — no angle was given, even though all angles do happen to be 60°.

2. Triangle ABC ≅ triangle QRP in that exact letter order. If ∠A = 35° and ∠B = 85°, then ∠P equals:
   (A) 35°
   (B) 60°
   (C) 85°
   (D) 120°

3. In triangle ABC, AB = AC and ∠A = (2x)°, while each base angle is (x + 30)°. The apex angle ∠A measures:
   (A) 60°
   (B) 30°
   (C) 75°
   (D) 45°

4. A figure has rotational symmetry of order 4 about its centre but you are asked the smallest positive angle that maps it onto itself, AND how many lines of symmetry this alone guarantees. The correct pair is:
   (A) 90°, and exactly 4 lines of symmetry are guaranteed.
   (B) 45°, and 4 lines of symmetry are guaranteed.
   (C) 90°, and 0 lines of symmetry are guaranteed by order-4 rotation alone.
   (D) 120°, and 2 lines of symmetry are guaranteed.

5. Triangles ABC and DEF satisfy AB = DE, ∠A = ∠D and ∠C = ∠F. A student claims congruence is impossible because the equal angle is not 'between' the side and another angle. The correct verdict is:
   (A) Not congruent, because two angles and a side never determine a triangle.
   (B) Only similar, since two equal angles fix the shape but leave the size free.
   (C) Congruent by SAS, treating the two equal angles as the two equal sides.
   (D) Congruent (AAS): with ∠A=∠D and ∠C=∠F the third angles match, so ∠B=∠E, and AB lies between A and B, giving ASA.

6. In isosceles triangle ABC, AB = AC. The altitude from A meets BC at M, with AB = AC = 25 cm and BC = 14 cm. The length AM is:
   (A) 24 cm
   (B) 20 cm
   (C) 7 cm
   (D) 18 cm

7. Triangle ABC ≅ triangle ACB for the SAME triangle (note B and C swapped). Combined with the fact that this self-correspondence maps the triangle onto itself, the strongest forced conclusion is:
   (A) AB = BC only, with no angle equality forced.
   (B) ∠A = 90°, making it a right triangle.
   (C) AB = AC and ∠B = ∠C, so the triangle is isosceles with apex A.
   (D) The triangle must be equilateral.

8. Two triangles have sides {5, 12, 13} cm and {5, 12, 13} cm respectively. Which statement is the most complete true description?
   (A) They are similar but not congruent, because no angle was stated.
   (B) They are congruent by SSS and both are right-angled, since 5² + 12² = 13².
   (C) They are congruent by RHS, using the 13 cm hypotenuse and the 5 cm leg.
   (D) They cannot be compared without knowing which angle is the right angle.

9. A regular dodecagon (12 sides) is rotated about its centre. The number of distinct positive angles less than 360° (including those that are multiples of the smallest) at which it maps onto itself is:
   (A) 12
   (B) 11
   (C) 6
   (D) 1

10. In triangle ABC, AD is drawn so that D is on BC, BD = DC, and ∠ADB = ∠ADC. Which conclusion is forced, and by which congruence?
   (A) ∠BAC = 90°, by RHS on the two halves.
   (B) Nothing is forced, because two angles at D being equal is automatic for any point on BC.
   (C) AB = AC: triangles ABD and ACD are congruent by SAS (BD = DC, the equal adjacent angles at D, AD common), so the triangle is isosceles.
   (D) The triangle is equilateral, by SSS.

11. Triangle PQR has PQ = PR and ∠P = 20°. The bisector of ∠Q meets PR at S. Using isosceles base-angle reasoning, ∠SQR equals:
   (A) 80°
   (B) 20°
   (C) 40°
   (D) 70°

12. Two triangles ABC and DEF have AB = DE = 8 cm, BC = EF = 6 cm and ∠C = ∠F = 50°. The safest correct verdict about congruence is:
   (A) Congruent by SAS, since two sides and an angle are equal.
   (B) Not guaranteed — the equal angle (at C, F) is opposite AB/DE, not included between the two equal sides, so this is the ambiguous SSA case.
   (C) Congruent by ASA, treating ∠C and the side BC together.
   (D) Congruent by RHS, because 50° behaves like a fixed right angle.

13. A figure is mapped onto itself by reflection in a horizontal line and also by reflection in a vertical line through the same centre. The minimum order of rotational symmetry it must have, and the smallest forced rotation angle, are:
   (A) Order 2 and 180°.
   (B) Order 1 and 360° (no rotational symmetry forced).
   (C) Order 4 and 90°.
   (D) Order 2 and 90°.

14. In triangle DEF, DE = DF and ∠D = ∠E + 30°. Using isosceles symmetry, ∠F equals:
   (A) 50°

   (D) 65°

15. Triangle ABC ≅ triangle DEF. It is given that the perimeter is 36 cm, DE = 13 cm and EF = 11 cm. The length of side CA is:
   (A) 13 cm
   (B) 11 cm
   (C) 12 cm

16. Which capital letter (standard block form) has a horizontal line of symmetry but NO vertical line of symmetry and NO rotational symmetry of order > 1?
   (A) H

   (D) S

17. Two right triangles each have a right angle. The first has legs 7 cm and 24 cm; the second has hypotenuse 25 cm and one leg 7 cm. The correct verdict is:
   (A) Congruent by RHS — the first's hypotenuse is √(7²+24²) = 25 cm, so both share hypotenuse 25 cm and a 7 cm leg with the right angle.
   (B) Not congruent, since the second triangle's other leg is unknown.
   (C) Congruent by SSA, two sides and a non-included angle matching.
   (D) Only similar, because one is given by legs and the other by a hypotenuse.

18. The angles of a triangle are in the ratio 3 : 4 : 5. Such a triangle is:
   (A) right-angled, because one ratio part is the largest.
   (B) isosceles, because 3 and 4 are close.
   (C) scalene and acute (all angles different and each below 90°).
   (D) obtuse, because the largest angle exceeds 90°.

19. Triangle ABC has AB = AC and the interior apex angle ∠A = 140°. Each base angle of the triangle is:
   (A) 70°

   (D) 20°

20. Two triangles ABC and DEF satisfy ∠A = ∠D = 90°, AB = DE = 9 cm and BC = EF = 15 cm (BC and EF are the hypotenuses). The most direct correct criterion is:
   (A) SAS, using the right angle as the included angle between the two given sides.
   (B) RHS — equal right angle, equal hypotenuse (15 cm) and an equal leg (9 cm).
   (C) SSA, so congruence is not guaranteed.
   (D) ASA, since the right angle is one of two equal angles.

21. A regular pentagon and a regular hexagon are drawn. The difference (hexagon minus pentagon) in their number of lines of symmetry, and in their orders of rotational symmetry, are respectively:
   (A) 2 more lines of symmetry and 2 more in rotational order.
   (B) 0 (equal lines) and 1 more in rotational order.
   (C) 1 more line of symmetry but equal rotational order.
   (D) 1 more line of symmetry and 1 more in rotational order.

22. In triangle ABC, the perpendicular bisector of BC passes through A, and separately the perpendicular bisector of AB passes through C. What must the triangle be?
   (A) Equilateral.
   (B) Isosceles but not equilateral.
   (C) Right-angled.
   (D) Scalene.

23. Triangle ABC ≅ triangle PQR. Which single statement of corresponding parts is guaranteed by CPCT?

26. Triangles ABC and DEF have AB = DE = 10 cm, ∠B = ∠E = 35° and BC = EF = 14 cm. The correct criterion is:
   (A) SSA, so congruence is not guaranteed.
   (B) SAS — the equal angle (B = E) is included between the equal sides AB and BC.
   (C) ASA, since one angle and adjacent sides are given.
   (D) AAS, treating BC as the angle's opposite.

28. Two triangles are reflections of one another across a line. Compared with the original, the reflected triangle has:

   (C) the same area and the same perimeter, but reversed orientation (a left/right flip).

32. Consider: (i) a figure with a line of symmetry must have rotational symmetry of order > 1; (ii) a figure with rotational symmetry of order > 1 must have a line of symmetry. Which is/are FALSE?
   (A) Only (i) is false.
   (B) Both (i) and (ii) are false.

33. Triangles ABC and DEF have ∠A = ∠D, ∠B = ∠E and the side BC = EF. A student says ASA. The most accurate verdict is:
   (A) Congruent by ASA, since two angles and a side are given.
   (B) Not congruent, because the side is not the included side.
   (C) Congruent by AAS — the equal side BC is opposite the equal angle A, not between the two equal angles, so it is AAS rather than ASA, but congruence still holds.
   (D) Only similar, since two angles fix only the shape.

34. A figure looks identical after a 60° rotation about its centre. The smallest order of rotational symmetry it can have is:
   (A) 60
   (B) 6
   (C) 3
   (D) 1

35. Triangle ABC is isosceles with AB = AC. D is the midpoint of BC. A student wants to prove ∠ADB = 90°. The cleanest congruence to use on triangles ABD and ACD is:
   (A) RHS, assuming ∠ADB = 90° from the start.
   (B) SSS — AB = AC, BD = DC (D is the midpoint) and AD common — then ∠ADB = ∠ADC by CPCT, and since they are supplementary each is 90°.
   (C) ASA, using the two base angles and BC.
   (D) SAS, using ∠BAD = ∠CAD which is not yet known.

36. Two triangles satisfy: all three pairs of corresponding angles equal, and one pair of corresponding sides in the ratio 1 : 1. What can be concluded?
   (A) Only similar, because AAA never gives congruence even with one equal side.
   (B) Congruent by AAA, since all angles match.
   (C) They are congruent — equal angles plus one pair of equal corresponding sides fixes the size (AAS/ASA).
   (D) Nothing, because angle and side data cannot be combined.

42. Triangle ABC has AB = AC. The bisector of ∠A meets BC at D and is extended to meet the circumcircle — but ignoring the circle, what can be said about AD relative to BC, using only congruence?
   (A) AD bisects BC but need not be perpendicular to it.
   (B) AD is the perpendicular bisector of BC: BD = DC and AD ⟂ BC, since triangles ABD and ACD are congruent by SAS (AB=AC, ∠BAD=∠CAD, AD common).

43. A triangle and its mirror image across a line are placed side by side. A student claims they cannot be congruent because one is 'left-handed' and one 'right-handed.' The correct evaluation is:
   (A) The student is right; mirror images are only similar, never congruent.
   (B) The student is wrong — a triangle and its reflection are always congruent; congruence permits flips, so reversed handedness does not block it.
   (C) Right for scalene triangles, wrong for isosceles ones.
   (D) Mirror images are congruent only if the triangle is equilateral.

45. Two triangles ABC and PQR have AB = PQ, ∠A = ∠P and AC = PR. A student writes 'congruent by ASA.' The correct criterion and verdict is:
   (A) ASA is correct, treating the two sides as the two angles.
   (B) SAS — ∠A is included between AB and AC; only one angle is given, so calling it ASA is wrong, but the triangles are congruent.
   (C) SSA, so congruence is not guaranteed.
   (D) AAA, because the remaining angles will match.
