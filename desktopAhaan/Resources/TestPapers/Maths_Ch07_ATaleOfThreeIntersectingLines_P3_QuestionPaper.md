**Marking scheme:** **+4** for each correct answer · **−1** for each wrong answer · **0** if unattempted.

**Instructions:** Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. Classic triangle traps: equal-sum sides fail the inequality (a sum must EXCEED, not merely match, the third side), an exterior angle equals the SUM of the two remote interior angles (not the adjacent one), 'acute' demands ALL three angles below 90°, an altitude is a perpendicular (not a side or median), and the largest angle always sits opposite the longest side. 1. In triangle ABC the three angles are in the ratio 3 : 5 : 7. Side BC is extended at the vertex with the LARGEST interior angle to form an exterior angle there. What is that exterior angle?
   (A) 84°
   (B) 96°
   (C) 108°
   (D) 72°

2. In triangle PQR, side QR is extended beyond R to S so that the exterior angle ∠PRS measures 124°. Inside the triangle ∠Q is 26° smaller than ∠P. Find ∠P.
   (A) 49°
   (B) 62°
   (C) 75°
   (D) 98°
