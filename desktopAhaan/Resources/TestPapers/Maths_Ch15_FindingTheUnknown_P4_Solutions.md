## Worked Solutions

**1. (A)** Inner bracket: 2x − (x − 5) = 2x − x + 5 = x + 5. Then 3(x + 5) − 4 = 3x + 15 − 4 = 3x + 11. The equation 3x + 11 = 2x + 12 gives 3x − 2x = 12 − 11, so x = 1. Check: 3[2 − (−4)] − 4 = 3(6) − 4 = 14 and 2(7) = 14. Forgetting to flip the sign inside the bracket (using x − 5, so 3(x − 5) − 4 = 2x + 12) gives −1; leaving the bracket un-tripled (x + 11 = 2x + 12) gives 11; mis-combining 15 + 4 instead of 15 − 4 in the constant gives 19.

**2. (C)** Inner: 5 − (x − 3) = 5 − x + 3 = 8 − x. Then −2(8 − x) = −16 + 2x, so the left side is 4x − 16 + 2x = 6x − 16. The equation 6x − 16 = x − 6 gives 5x = 10, so x = 2. Check: 4(2) − 2[5 − (−1)] = 8 − 2(6) = 8 − 12 = −4 and 2 − 6 = −4. Failing to flip the inner sign (using 5 − x − 3 = 2 − x) corrupts the constant toward 4; distributing −2 as −2x gives a sign trap −2; reading 5x = 10 as 5x = 50 gives 10.

**3. (A)** Build outward. Innermost: x − 4. Next: 2 − (x − 4) = 2 − x + 4 = 6 − x. Next: 3 − [6 − x] = 3 − 6 + x = x − 3. So the left side is 7x − (x − 3) = 7x − x + 3 = 6x + 3. The equation 6x + 3 = 5x + 1 gives x = −2. Check: 7(−2) − {3 − [2 − (−6)]} = −14 − {3 − 8} = −14 + 5 = −9 and 5(−2) + 1 = −9. Mishandling one nested sign gives +2 or 6; dropping the outer brace minus gives −6.

**4. (B)** Simplify the bracket: x − (2 − x) = x − 2 + x = 2x − 2, so 3(2x − 2) = 6x − 6. The left side is 8x − 4 − (6x − 6) = 8x − 4 − 6x + 6 = 2x + 2. The equation becomes 2x + 2 = 2x + 5 → 2 = 5, which is impossible, so there is NO solution. Finding one root and stopping suggests 'exactly one'; reading the two constants as equal suggests 'every number'; a linear equation can never have 'exactly two' roots.

**5. (D)** Innermost: x − 1. Next: 4 − (x − 1) = 4 − x + 1 = 5 − x. Next: 2x − [5 − x] = 2x − 5 + x = 3x − 5. Then 6 − (3x − 5) = 6 − 3x + 5 = 11 − 3x. The equation 11 − 3x = 3 − x gives 11 − 3 = 3x − x → 8 = 2x → x = 4. Check: 6 − {8 − [4 − 3]} = 6 − {8 − 1} = 6 − 7 = −1 and 3 − 4 = −1. A sign slip on the inner bracket gives −4 or 8; mis-transposing gives 2.

**6. (D)** Innermost: 4 − x. Next: 2x − (4 − x) = 2x − 4 + x = 3x − 4. Next: 3 − [3x − 4] = 3 − 3x + 4 = 7 − 3x. Then 5 − (7 − 3x) = 5 − 7 + 3x = 3x − 2. The equation 3x − 2 = 2x + 1 gives x = 3. Check: 5 − {3 − [6 − 1]} = 5 − {3 − 5} = 5 + 2 = 7 and 2(3) + 1 = 7. Not flipping a nested sign gives −3 or 9; a constant slip gives 1.

**7. (C)** Left side: x − 2(x − 3) = x − 2x + 6 = −x + 6, so 3(−x + 6) = −3x + 18. Right side innermost: x − 1; then 2 − (x − 1) = 3 − x; then x − [3 − x] = x − 3 + x = 2x − 3; then 4 − [2x − 3] = 4 − 2x + 3 = 7 − 2x. The equation −3x + 18 = 7 − 2x gives 18 − 7 = 3x − 2x → x = 11. Check: 3[11 − 2(8)] = 3[11 − 16] = −15 and 4 − {11 − [2 − 10]} = 4 − {11 + 8} = 4 − 19 = −15. Sign mishandling of the nested terms gives −11, 5, or 7.

**8. (C)** Simplify: 4 − (3 − x) = 4 − 3 + x = 1 + x, so 2(1 + x) = 2 + 2x. The left side is 6x − 3 − (2 + 2x) = 6x − 3 − 2 − 2x = 4x − 5, which is identical to the right side 4x − 5. The statement 4x − 5 = 4x − 5 holds for every value of x, so there are infinitely many solutions. Solving and reading off a single root suggests 'exactly one'; treating the equal constants as unequal suggests 'none'; a linear identity is never 'exactly two'.

**9. (C)** Multiply every term by the LCD 10: 2(2x + 3) − 5(x − 1) = (x + 7). Expand: 4x + 6 − 5x + 5 = x + 7 → −x + 11 = x + 7 → 11 − 7 = x + x → 4 = 2x → x = 2. Verify: (7)/5 − (1)/2 = 1.4 − 0.5 = 0.9 and (9)/10 = 0.9. Distributing −5(x − 1) as −5x − 5 corrupts the constant toward −2; a transposition slip gives 3; clearing only one denominator gives 12.

**10. (B)** Cross-multiply: 5(x − 1) = 2(2x + 1) → 5x − 5 = 4x + 2 → 5x − 4x = 2 + 5 → x = 7. Check: (6)/(15) = 2/5. Cross-multiplying the wrong way, 2(x − 1) = 5(2x + 1), gives a negative trap −7; transposition slips give 3 or 9.

**11. (C)** Multiply every term by the LCD 6: 3(x − 1) + 2(2x + 1) = (5x − 1) + 12. Expand: 3x − 3 + 4x + 2 = 5x + 11 → 7x − 1 = 5x + 11 → 2x = 12 → x = 6. Verify: (5)/2 + (13)/3 = 2.5 + 4.333… = 6.833… and (29)/6 + 2 = 4.833… + 2 = 6.833…. A sign error on (x − 1) gives −6; mis-clearing the LCD gives 2 or 4.

**12. (A)** Cross-multiply: 3(3x + 1) = 4(2x − 1) → 9x + 3 = 8x − 4 → 9x − 8x = −4 − 3 → x = −7. Check: 3/(−15) = −1/5 and 4/(−20) = −1/5. A dropped minus gives 7; transposition slips give 5 or 1.

**13. (A)** Let the number be n. The condition is (1/2)n = (2/5)n + 6, i.e. half exceeds two-fifths by 6: (1/2)n − (2/5)n = 6. The LCD is 10: (5n − 4n)/10 = 6 → n/10 = 6 → n = 60. Check: half of 60 is 30, two-fifths is 24, and 30 − 24 = 6. Equating (2/5)n − (1/2)n = 6 reverses the gap and gives a negative; using a difference of one-tenth as one-fifth gives 30; reading 'two-fifths' as the whole gap gives 20 or 12.

**14. (C)** Let the numerator be n, so the denominator is n + 3 and the fraction is n/(n + 3). Subtracting 1 from each: (n − 1)/(n + 2) = 3/4. Cross-multiply: 4(n − 1) = 3(n + 2) → 4n − 4 = 3n + 6 → n = 10. The denominator is 13, so the fraction is 10/13. Check: (9)/(12) = 3/4. Mis-placing the gap of 3 gives 7/10; an off-by-one in n gives 9/12 or 11/14.

**15. (A)** Multiply every term by the LCD 12: 6x + 4x − 3x = 84 → 7x = 84 → x = 12. Check: 6 + 4 − 3 = 7. Adding all three coefficients as 6 + 4 + 3 = 13 mis-signs the −x/4 and shifts the root; halving 12 gives 6; multiplying 7 by 3 or 4 instead of dividing gives 21 or 28.

**16. (D)** Let the son be s, so the father is 5s. In 6 years: son s + 6, father 5s + 6, with 5s + 6 = 3(s + 6). Expand: 5s + 6 = 3s + 18 → 5s − 3s = 18 − 6 → 2s = 12 → s = 6. Check: son 6, father 30; in 6 years 12 and 36, and 36 = 3 × 12. Comparing present ages 5s = 3s gives a nonsense; forgetting to add 6 to the son gives 5s + 6 = 3s → negative, mis-corrected to 12 or 18; halving the wrong gap gives 9.

**17. (D)** Let the daughter be d, so the mother is 50 − d. Five years ago: daughter d − 5, mother 45 − d, with 45 − d = 7(d − 5). Expand: 45 − d = 7d − 35 → 45 + 35 = 7d + d → 80 = 8d → d = 10. Check: daughter 10, mother 40; five years ago 5 and 35, and 35 = 7 × 5. Forgetting to subtract 5 from the daughter gives a smaller 5; mis-summing gives 15; a sign slip gives 8.

**18. (C)** Let B be b, so A is 60 − b. Ten years ago: A = 50 − b, B = b − 10, with 50 − b = 3(b − 10). Expand: 50 − b = 3b − 30 → 50 + 30 = 3b + b → 80 = 4b → b = 20, so A = 40. Check: ten years ago A = 40, B = 10, and 40 = 3 × 10. Reporting B's age gives 20; forgetting to subtract 10 from B gives 45; halving the sum gives 30.

**19. (B)** Let the ages be 5k and 2k. After 6 years: (5k + 6) : (2k + 6) = 2 : 1, so 5k + 6 = 2(2k + 6) → 5k + 6 = 4k + 12 → k = 6. The younger is 2k = 12 (and the elder 30). Check: in 6 years 36 and 18, and 36 : 18 = 2 : 1. Reporting k gives 6; reporting the elder gives 30; mis-scaling gives 18.

**20. (A)** Let the son be s, so the man is 2s + 3. In 4 years the sum is (2s + 3 + 4) + (s + 4) = 3s + 11 = 56 → 3s = 45 → s = 15, so the man is 2(15) + 3 = 33. Check: man 33, son 15; in 4 years 37 and 19, sum 56. Reporting the son's age gives 15; forgetting the +3 gives 30; mis-handling the +4's gives 36.

**21. (B)** Let the daughter be d, so the man is d + 24. In 6 years: daughter d + 6, man d + 30, with d + 30 = 3(d + 6). Expand: d + 30 = 3d + 18 → 30 − 18 = 3d − d → 12 = 2d → d = 6. Check: daughter 6, man 30; in 6 years 12 and 36, and 36 = 3 × 12. Comparing present ages d + 24 = 3d gives 12; forgetting to add 6 to the daughter gives 18; a halving slip gives 8.

**22. (D)** Let the tens digit be t and units u, number 10t + u. The condition 10t + u = 7u gives 10t = 6u → 5t = 3u. With t = u − 2: 5(u − 2) = 3u → 5u − 10 = 3u → 2u = 10 → u = 5, t = 3, number 35. Check: 7 × 5 = 35 and 3 = 5 − 2. Reversing the relation gives 53; ignoring 5t = 3u gives 57 or 46.

**23. (D)** Let the integers be n, n + 1, n + 2, n + 3. Their sum is 4n + 6, and 3 times the largest minus 2 is 3(n + 3) − 2 = 3n + 7. Set 4n + 6 = 3n + 7 → n = 1, giving 1, 2, 3, 4. Check: sum = 10 and 3 × 4 − 2 = 10. Reporting the second term gives 2; a sign slip on the constants gives −1; using the largest gives 3.

**24. (B)** Let tens t, units u, number 10t + u. Reversed 10u + t = (10t + u) + 36 → 9u − 9t = 36 → u − t = 4. With u + t = 10: adding gives 2u = 14 → u = 7, t = 3, number 37. Check: reversed 73 = 37 + 36 and 3 + 7 = 10. The reversed number 73 is a trap; mis-solving the pair gives 28 or 46.

**25. (D)** Let the multiples be 5n, 5n + 5, 5n + 10. Their sum is 15n + 15, and 5 times the smallest plus 5 is 5(5n) + 5 = 25n + 5. Set 15n + 15 = 25n + 5 → 15 − 5 = 25n − 15n → 10 = 10n → n = 1, giving 5, 10, 15; the largest is 15. Check: sum 30 = 25(1) + 5. Reporting the smallest gives 5, the middle 10; adding 5 again gives 20.

**26. (A)** Since the fraction equals 3/5, write it as 3k/5k. After the changes: (3k + 2)/(5k − 2) = 1, so 3k + 2 = 5k − 2 → 4 = 2k → k = 2, giving 6/10. Check: (6 + 2)/(10 − 2) = 8/8 = 1. The lowest-terms form 3/5 fails the test (5/3 ≠ 1); k = 3 gives 9/15 which fails ((11)/(13) ≠ 1); 4/8 is not equal to 3/5.

**27. (D)** Let them be n, n + 2, n + 4. The condition is n + 4 = 2n − 6 → 4 + 6 = 2n − n → n = 10, giving 10, 12, 14; the middle is 12. Check: 14 = 2(10) − 6. Reporting the smallest gives 10, the largest gives 14; mis-solving gives 8.

**28. (A)** Angles sum to 180°: 2x + (3x − 10) + (x + 10) = 180 → 6x = 180 → x = 30. The angles are 60°, 80°, 40°, so the largest is 80°. Check: 60 + 80 + 40 = 180. Reporting (2x) gives 60° and (x + 10) gives 40°; mis-summing the constants to make 6x = 180 ± 20 gives 90°.

**29. (B)** The length exceeds the breadth by 7: (3x − 2) − (x + 1) = 7 → 2x − 3 = 7 → 2x = 10 → x = 5. Then length = 13 cm, breadth = 6 cm, perimeter = 2(13 + 6) = 38 cm. Check: 13 − 6 = 7. Using one length-plus-breadth without doubling gives 19; mis-solving x gives 42 or 26.

**30. (B)** Each exterior angle = 180° − 150° = 30°, and exterior angles sum to 360°, so the number of sides = 360 ÷ 30 = 12. Misreading 180 − 150 as 36 gives 10; dividing 360 by 24 gives 15; by 45 gives 8.

**31. (B)** Let each equal side be s, so the base is s + 5. Perimeter: 2s + (s + 5) = 50 → 3s + 5 = 50 → 3s = 45 → s = 15. The base is 20. Check: 15 + 15 + 20 = 50. Reporting the base gives 20; treating the base as 5 less than the side gives 12; splitting 45 by halves instead of thirds gives 10.

**32. (D)** The four parts sum to 3 + 4 + 5 + 6 = 18, and a quadrilateral's angles total 360°, so one part = 360 ÷ 18 = 20°. The largest angle is 6 × 20 = 120°. Check: (3 + 4 + 5 + 6) × 20 = 360. Using 350 ÷ 18 mis-rounds toward 100°; dividing by a triangle's 540 ÷ 18 × 6 idea gives 108° or 144°.

**33. (A)** Let the third angle be t, so each equal angle is t + 15. Then t + 2(t + 15) = 180 → 3t + 30 = 180 → 3t = 150 → t = 50. Check: 50 + 65 + 65 = 180. Reporting an equal angle gives 65°; subtracting 15 from the third gives 40° or 45°.

**34. (D)** Let the counts be 4k, 3k, 2k. The value is 1(4k) + 2(3k) + 5(2k) = 4k + 6k + 10k = 20k = 120 → k = 6. The ₹5 coins number 2k = 12. Check: 24 + 36 + 60 = ₹120 with 24, 18, 12 coins. Reading the ratio number for ₹1 coins gives 4k = 24; for ₹2 coins 3k = 18; halving k gives 6.

**35. (C)** Let a be adult tickets, so child tickets number 50 − a. Value: 60a + 35(50 − a) = 2350 → 60a + 1750 − 35a = 2350 → 25a = 600 → a = 24. Check: 24 adults = ₹1440, 26 children = ₹910, total ₹2350. The complement 50 − 24 = 26 is the child count, a swap trap; splitting evenly gives 25 toward 20 or 30.

**36. (C)** In 80 L the milk is (5/8) × 80 = 50 L and water (3/8) × 80 = 30 L. Adding w litres of water keeps milk at 50 L; for 1 : 1 we need water = milk, so 30 + w = 50 → w = 20. Check: 50 L milk and 50 L water is 1 : 1. Halving the gap gives 10 L; using the original 16-L difference idea gives 16 L; matching the milk total itself gives 30 L.

**37. (C)** Let B = b. Then A = b + 40 and C = 2b, summing to 560: (b + 40) + b + 2b = 560 → 4b + 40 = 560 → 4b = 520 → b = 130. So C = 2b = 260. Check: A = 170, B = 130, C = 260, total 560. Reporting B gives 130; reporting A gives 170; doubling the total share idea gives 320.

**38. (D)** Let h be the ₹40 articles, so ₹25 articles number 36 − h. Value: 40h + 25(36 − h) = 1140 → 40h + 900 − 25h = 1140 → 15h = 240 → h = 16. Check: 16 × 40 = 640 and 20 × 25 = 500, total 1140. The complement 36 − 16 = 20 is the ₹25 count, a swap trap; splitting evenly gives 18; a sign slip gives 24.

**39. (D)** Let the ₹5 coins number f, so the ₹2 coins number 2f + 4. Value: 5f + 2(2f + 4) = 98 → 5f + 4f + 8 = 98 → 9f = 90 → f = 10, so ₹2 coins = 2(10) + 4 = 24. Check: 10 × 5 = ₹50 and 24 × 2 = ₹48, total ₹98. Reporting the ₹5 count gives 10; forgetting the +4 gives 20; using 'twice f' without the +4 then mis-adding gives 14.

**40. (A)** Average speed is total distance over total time, never the mean of the speeds. First leg time = 120/40 = 3 h; second leg = 120/60 = 2 h; total 240 km in 5 h, so average = 240/5 = 48 km/h. The naive mean (40 + 60)/2 = 50 is the trap; adding the speeds gives 100; a time miscount gives 52.

**41. (A)** Rates add, not times. In one hour A fills 1/4 and B fills 1/6, together 1/4 + 1/6 = 3/12 + 2/12 = 5/12 of the tank, so the whole tank takes 12/5 = 2.4 hours. Averaging the times (4 + 6)/2 = 5 is the trap; adding the times gives 10; a rate slip gives 3.

**42. (B)** Downstream speed = 30/2 = 15 km/h = boat + stream; upstream speed = 30/5 = 6 km/h = boat − stream. Subtracting, 2 × stream = 15 − 6 = 9, so stream = 4.5 km/h (and boat = 10.5). The boat speed 10.5 is a trap; the downstream speed 15 → boat 9 is another; using only one leg gives 6.

**43. (A)** Past the pole the train covers its own length L in 12 s, so speed = L/12. Past the platform it covers L + 120 in 24 s, so speed = (L + 120)/24. Equate: L/12 = (L + 120)/24 → 24L = 12(L + 120) → 24L = 12L + 1440 → 12L = 1440 → L = 120. Check: speed 10 m/s; pole 120/10 = 12 s, platform 240/10 = 24 s. Doubling gives 240; halving gives 60; misadding gives 180.

**44. (B)** Moving toward each other, the closing speed is the sum 45 + 35 = 80 km/h, so the time to close 240 km is 240/80 = 3 hours. Using only one speed gives 240/35 ≈ 6.857; using the difference 10 km/h gives 24; using 240/45 gives about 5.333.

**45. (A)** Time is distance over speed for each leg. Downstream: 18/9 = 2 h; upstream: 18/6 = 3 h; total = 2 + 3 = 5 h. Using the average speed (9 + 6)/2 = 7.5 km/h on 36 km gives 36/7.5 = 4.8 h, the classic averaging trap; using one leg's speed on 36 km gives values like 4 or, via 36/15, 2.4; taking only the upstream leg gives 3.

**46. (B)** Solve: 5x − 4 = 3x + 8 → 5x − 3x = 8 + 4 → 2x = 12 → x = 6. Then x² + x = 36 + 6 = 42. Stopping at x = 6 reports 6; computing only x² gives 36; using 5x without context gives 30.

**47. (C)** Expand: 4x − 4 = 2x + 6 → 4x − 2x = 6 + 4 → 2x = 10 → x = 5. Then 3x² − 5 = 3(25) − 5 = 70. Reporting x itself gives 5; computing (3x)² loosely or 5² gives 25; using 3x − 5 = 10 gives 20.

**48. (A)** Substitute x = 5: 2(5 + k) = 3(5) − 7 → 10 + 2k = 8 → 2k = −2 → k = −1. Verify: 2(5 − 1) = 8 and 15 − 7 = 8, equal. A halving slip gives 1; forgetting to halve gives ±6.

**49. (C)** Solve: 7x − 3 = 4x + 9 → 3x = 12 → x = 4. Then 2x + 5 = 8 + 5 = 13. Stopping at x = 4 reports 4; computing only 2x gives 8; using 3x + 5 gives 17.

**50. (D)** Solve: 6x − 5 = 4x + 7 → 2x = 12 → x = 6. Then (x² − 4)/2 = (36 − 4)/2 = 32/2 = 16. Reporting x itself reports 6; forgetting to divide by 2 gives 32; dropping the −4 and computing x²/2 = 36/2 gives 18.

**51. (C)** The right side factors as 2x − 2 = 2(x − 1). The equation m(x − 1) = 2(x − 1) is true for every x exactly when the coefficients match, i.e. m = 2 (then both sides are identical). Check: 2(x − 1) = 2x − 2 for all x. Taking m = 1 leaves x − 1 = 2x − 2, which holds only at x = 1, not everywhere; m = −2 or m = 0 makes the two sides differ for almost all x.

**52. (C)** 'The sum of n and 3' is (n + 3); 'twice the sum' multiplies the whole bracket: 2(n + 3). '4 less than five times the number' is 5n − 4. So 2(n + 3) = 5n − 4. Multiplying only n by 2 drops the bracket; '4 less' is −4, not +4; '4 less than 5n' is 5n − 4, not 4 − 5n.

**53. (A)** Let a pen cost p and a pencil q. Then 3p + 2q = 72 and 2p + 3q = 68. Multiply the first by 3 and the second by 2: 9p + 6q = 216 and 4p + 6q = 136. Subtract: 5p = 80 → p = 16 (and q = 12). Check: 3(16) + 2(12) = 72 and 2(16) + 3(12) = 68. The pencil price 12 is a swap trap; arithmetic slips give 14 or 20.

**54. (D)** Let apples cost a and grapes g per kg. Then 5a + 2g = 430 and 3a + 4g = 440. Multiply the first by 2: 10a + 4g = 860. Subtract the second: 7a = 420 → a = 60 (and g = 65). Check: 5(60) + 2(65) = 430 and 3(60) + 4(65) = 440. The grape price 65 is a swap trap; mis-elimination gives 70 or 55.

**55. (B)** Let the number be n. The condition is (3/4)n − (2/3)n = 5. The LCD is 12: (9n − 8n)/12 = 5 → n/12 = 5 → n = 60. Check: three-fourths of 60 is 45, two-thirds is 40, and 45 − 40 = 5. Treating the difference (1/12) as (1/5) gives 25 toward 20; misreading the gap gives 12 or 30.

**56. (B)** Let correct = c, so wrong = 40 − c. Score: 5c − 2(40 − c) = 102 → 5c − 80 + 2c = 102 → 7c = 182 → c = 26. Check: 26 correct gives +130, 14 wrong gives −28, net 102. Forgetting the penalty (5c = 102) drifts toward 20; a sign error on the −2 gives 22 or 28.

**57. (B)** From x − y = 1, x = y + 1. Substitute: 2(y + 1) + 3y = 12 → 2y + 2 + 3y = 12 → 5y = 10 → y = 2 (and x = 3). Check: 2(3) + 3(2) = 12 and 3 − 2 = 1. The value of x, 3, is a swap trap; a mis-substitution gives 4 or 1.

**58. (C)** Let the numbers be 5k and 7k. Then (5k + 8) : (7k + 8) = 7 : 9, so 9(5k + 8) = 7(7k + 8) → 45k + 72 = 49k + 56 → 72 − 56 = 49k − 45k → 16 = 4k → k = 4. The smaller number is 5k = 20 (and the larger 28). Check: 28 : 36 = 7 : 9. Reporting the larger gives 28; using k itself gives 4 toward 12 or 16.

**59. (D)** Add the two equations to eliminate b: (3a − 2b) + (a + 2b) = 5 + 11 → 4a = 16 → a = 4 (and b = 3.5). Check: 3(4) − 2(3.5) = 12 − 7 = 5 and 4 + 7 = 11. The value of b's neighbour 3 is a trap; forgetting to divide by 4 gives 16; subtracting instead of adding the equations gives 7.

**60. (B)** The fraction left is 1 − 1/3 − 1/4 = (12 − 4 − 3)/12 = 5/12 of the total. So (5/12) × total = 250 → total = 250 × 12/5 = 600. Check: rent 200, food 150, left 250, sum 600. Mis-adding the spent fractions to 1/2 leaves 1/2 → total 500; arithmetic slips give 750 or 450.
