**Instructions:** Each question has four options (A), (B), (C), (D); exactly one is correct. This paper digs into the soil profile and its horizons, the composition of soil, the three soil types and their particle sizes, percolation (including a calculation), soil erosion and conservation, and the crops suited to each soil. Reason from the property to the consequence. This paper does not repeat any question from Paper 1.

1. Soil is best described as a mixture of:
   (A) only broken rock
   (B) broken rock, decayed organic matter, water, air and microbes
   (C) pure sand only
   (D) only water and air

2. The vertical section through the soil from the surface down to the bedrock is called the:
   (A) percolation rate
   (B) soil profile
   (C) humus layer
   (D) water table

3. The dark, fertile organic matter formed by the decay of plant and animal remains is:
   (A) clay
   (B) humus
   (C) gravel
   (D) bedrock

4. In a soil profile, the topmost surface layer rich in dead leaves and organic matter is the:
   (A) C horizon
   (B) B horizon
   (C) O horizon
   (D) bedrock

5. The layer where most plant roots grow, dark with mixed humus and minerals, is the:
   (A) A horizon (topsoil)
   (B) B horizon (subsoil)
   (C) C horizon
   (D) parent rock

6. The B horizon (subsoil) is generally:
   (A) the richest in humus
   (B) lighter in colour, containing minerals washed down from above
   (C) made of pure bedrock
   (D) where most roots grow

7. The lowest layer of weathered parent rock in the soil profile is the:
   (A) O horizon
   (B) A horizon
   (C) C horizon
   (D) humus

8. By volume, a fertile soil is roughly:
   (A) 100% mineral particles
   (B) about 45% minerals, 25% air, 25% water, 5% humus

9. The mineral particles in soil are formed when larger rocks are broken down by:
   (A) photosynthesis
   (B) weathering by water, wind, ice and living things

10. Soils are classified into their three main types mainly by the:
    (A) colour of the soil
    (B) size of their mineral particles

11. Sandy soil is made mostly of:
    (A) very tiny particles packed tightly
    (B) large particles with big gaps between them

12. Because of its large particles and big air gaps, sandy soil:
    (A) holds water for a long time
    (B) drains water quickly and holds little water

    (D) becomes waterlogged easily

13. Clayey soil is made mostly of:
    (A) very small particles with tiny gaps
    (B) large particles
    (C) gravel
    (D) pure sand

14. Compared with sandy soil, clayey soil:
    (A) drains faster
    (B) holds water well and can become waterlogged
    (C) holds no water

15. The soil that is a balanced mixture of sand, silt and clay with plenty of humus is:
    (A) sandy soil
    (B) clayey soil
    (C) loamy soil
    (D) gravel

16. The best soil for growing most crops is generally:
    (A) pure sand
    (B) pure clay
    (C) loam
    (D) gravel

17. Percolation is the:
    (A) upward rise of water in soil
    (B) downward movement of water through soil
    (C) evaporation of water from leaves
    (D) freezing of soil water

18. The percolation rate is calculated as:
    (A) percolation time ÷ volume of water
    (B) volume of water ÷ percolation time

    (D) volume + time

    (B) 10 mL/min

    (B) clayey soil
