Set 1 · Q1–Q10

Q1

Shabnam is always 3 years older than Aftab. Using a for Aftab's age, Shabnam's age is written as:

  • A3a
  • Ba + 3✓
  • C3 − a
  • Da/3

Working

✓

Shabnam's age is always Aftab's age + 3. With Aftab's age = a, Shabnam's age = a + 3. (3a would mean three times Aftab's age; 3 − a reverses the relation.)

Q2

The expression 4n means:

  • A4 + n
  • B4 − n
  • C4 × n✓
  • Dn ÷ 4

Working

✓

By convention 4n is shorthand for 4 × n — the multiplication sign is simply dropped. It does NOT mean 4 + n; with n = 5, 4n = 20, not 9.

Q3

The value of 5m + 3 when m = 2 is:

  • A13✓
  • B10
  • C25
  • D16

Working

✓

Substitute m = 2: 5m + 3 = 5 × 2 + 3 = 10 + 3 = 13. (Reading 5m as 5 + m gives 10 — the trap; 5(m+3) gives 25.)

Q4

Writing 7 × k without the multiplication sign gives:

  • A7 + k
  • Bk7
  • C7k✓
  • Dk/7

Working

✓

Dropping the × sign and writing the number before the letter gives 7k. (k7 violates the number-first convention; 7 + k changes the operation.)

Q5

If a = −4, then the value of 10 − a is:

  • A6
  • B40
  • C−14
  • D14✓

Working

✓

The letter carries its whole sign: 10 − a = 10 − (−4) = 10 + 4 = 14. (Ignoring the negative sign gives the trap 6; 10 × (−4) would give −40.)

Q6

The perimeter of a square of side q is given by the formula:

  • Aq + 4
  • Bq⁴
  • Cq/4
  • D4q✓

Working

✓

Perimeter of a square = 4 × side = 4q. (q + 4 adds instead of multiplies; q⁴ raises to a power.)

Q7

The expression l + b + l + b simplifies to:

  • A2lb
  • Bl²b²
  • C4lb
  • D2l + 2b✓

Working

✓

l + b + l + b = (l + l) + (b + b) = 2l + 2b. The like terms l + l make 2l, not l²; and l and b are unlike, so they stay separate (not 2lb).

Q8

The sequence 4, 8, 12, 16, … has nth term 4n. Its 29th term is:

  • A116✓
  • B33
  • C120
  • D429

Working

✓

The nth term is 4n, so the 29th term is 4 × 29 = 116. (33 comes from 29 + 4; "429" mistakenly writes the digits side by side.)

Q9

Simplify: 5a + 3b − 2a + 7b − b.

  • A12ab
  • B3a + 9
  • C3a + 11b
  • D3a + 9b✓

Working

✓

Collect like terms: a-terms 5a − 2a = 3a; b-terms 3b + 7b − b = 9b. Total 3a + 9b. (3a + 11b forgets to subtract the −b; 12ab wrongly merges unlike terms.)

Q10

A matchstick pattern uses 3n + 1 sticks for the nth figure. The 10th figure needs:

  • A30
  • B34
  • C31✓
  • D301

Working

✓

Substitute n = 10 into 3n + 1: 3 × 10 + 1 = 30 + 1 = 31. (Stopping at 3n = 30 gives the trap 30; "301" writes the digits together.)

Set 2 · Q11–Q20

Q11

If p = 3 and q = 5, the value of 2pq − p² is:

  • A39
  • B24
  • C21✓
  • D51

Working

✓

2pq − p² with p = 3, q = 5: 2 × 3 × 5 − 3² = 30 − 9 = 21. (Forgetting to square, 30 − 3 = 27 isn't offered; 2 × 3 × 5 + 3² = 39 adds wrongly; 30 + ... gives 51.)

Q12

A rectangle has length (x + 5) and breadth x. Its perimeter, simplified, is:

  • A4x + 10✓
  • B2x + 5
  • C4x + 5
  • Dx² + 5x

Working

✓

Perimeter = 2(length + breadth) = 2((x + 5) + x) = 2(2x + 5) = 4x + 10. (2x + 5 forgets to double; x² + 5x multiplies instead of adding.)

Q13

The coefficient of the letter-number in the term 8y is:

  • Ay
  • B8✓
  • C8y
  • D1

Working

✓

In 8y the number multiplying the letter-number is the coefficient, so it is 8. (y is the letter-number itself; 1 would be the coefficient of a bare y.)

Q14

Parthiv makes L-shapes from matchsticks using 2 sticks per L. The number of sticks for n L-shapes is:

  • An + 2
  • Bn²
  • C2n✓
  • D2 + n²

Working

✓

Each L uses 2 sticks, so n L-shapes use 2 × n = 2n sticks. (n + 2 adds rather than multiplies; n² squares.)

Q15

Which of these pairs are LIKE terms?

  • A3x and 3y
  • B5a and 5a²
  • C7m and 2m✓
  • D4p and 4q

Working

✓

Like terms have the same letter part. 7m and 2m share the letter m, so they are like. (3x & 3y, 4p & 4q have different letters; 5a & 5a² have different powers.)

Q16

The perimeter of a regular pentagon with side q is 5q. When q = 6 cm, the perimeter is:

  • A11 cm
  • B56 cm
  • C25 cm
  • D30 cm✓

Working

✓

Perimeter = 5q; with q = 6, 5 × 6 = 30 cm. (5 + 6 = 11 adds; "56" writes digits together; 25 = 5².)

Q17

Simplify: 6x + 9 − 4x − 2.

  • A9x
  • B11x
  • C2x + 11
  • D2x + 7✓

Working

✓

Collect like terms: x-terms 6x − 4x = 2x; numbers 9 − 2 = 7. Result 2x + 7. (Merging everything into 9x or 11x wrongly adds unlike terms; 2x + 11 adds the numbers wrongly.)

Q18

The sum of three consecutive numbers can be written, using n for the middle number, as:

  • A3n + 1
  • Bn³
  • C3n✓
  • D3n − 1

Working

✓

Three consecutive numbers about a middle n are (n − 1), n, (n + 1); their sum is (n − 1) + n + (n + 1) = 3n. (3n + 1 / 3n − 1 keep a leftover that actually cancels.)

Q19

If t = 6, the value of the expression 4t − 9 is:

  • A15✓
  • B1
  • C33
  • D24

Working

✓

Substitute t = 6: 4t − 9 = 4 × 6 − 9 = 24 − 9 = 15. (24 forgets to subtract; reading 4t as 4 + t gives 1.)

Q20

"5 more than twice a number x" is written as:

  • A2(x + 5)
  • B2x + 5✓
  • C5x + 2
  • D2x − 5

Working

✓

"Twice a number" = 2x; "5 more than" it = 2x + 5. (2(x + 5) doubles after adding 5; 5x + 2 swaps the roles.)

Set 3 · Q21–Q30

Q21

Simplify: 3a + 4a + 5a.

  • A12a✓
  • B12a³
  • C60a
  • D12 + a

Working

✓

All three terms are like (letter a), so 3a + 4a + 5a = (3 + 4 + 5)a = 12a. (12a³ wrongly multiplies the powers; 60a multiplies the coefficients.)

Q22

If a = 2 and b = 5, the value of 3a + 2b is:

  • A16✓
  • B10
  • C25
  • D30

Working

✓

3a + 2b with a = 2, b = 5: 3 × 2 + 2 × 5 = 6 + 10 = 16. (Reading 3a as 3 + a etc. gives 10; "25" comes from 5².)

Q23

The term that is UNLIKE the others in {7x, −3x, x, 7} is:

  • A7x
  • B−3x
  • Cx
  • D7✓

Working

✓

7x, −3x and x are all like terms (letter x); 7 has no letter, so it is the odd one out — a constant, unlike the others.

Q24

A taxi-style rule charges a fixed 50 plus 12 for each unit n. The expression for the total is:

  • A50n + 12
  • B62n
  • C600n
  • D12n + 50✓

Working

✓

Fixed 50 plus 12 for each of n units = 12 × n + 50 = 12n + 50. (50n + 12 swaps which quantity is multiplied; 62n adds the two numbers first.)

Q25

The expression 9 − 2k, when k = −3, equals:

  • A3
  • B6
  • C−15
  • D15✓

Working

✓

9 − 2k with k = −3: 9 − 2 × (−3) = 9 − (−6) = 9 + 6 = 15. (Dropping the sign of k gives 9 − 6 = 3; mis-signing gives −15.)

Q26

Simplify: 10m − 3m + m.

  • A8m✓
  • B7m
  • C6m
  • D14m

Working

✓

All like terms (m): 10m − 3m + m = (10 − 3 + 1)m = 8m. (7m forgets the final + m; 6m subtracts it.)

Q27

A shop sells pencils at price c and erasers at price d. Buying 5 pencils and 4 erasers costs:

  • A9cd
  • B5c + 4d✓
  • C20cd
  • D4c + 5d

Working

✓

5 pencils at c plus 4 erasers at d cost 5c + 4d; c and d are unlike, so the terms stay separate (not 9cd or 20cd). (4c + 5d swaps the counts.)

Q28

The value of x² + 3x when x = 4 is:

  • A19
  • B28✓
  • C14
  • D44

Working

✓

x² + 3x with x = 4: 4² + 3 × 4 = 16 + 12 = 28. (Forgetting to square, 4 + 12 = 16 gives the trap 16; 14 = ... mis-reads 3x.)

Q29

Which expression equals l + l + l?

  • Al³
  • B3l✓
  • Cl + 3
  • D3 + l

Working

✓

l + l + l is three l's added = 3l. (l³ would be l × l × l; l + 3 / 3 + l add a number instead of counting the l's.)

Q30

The next two terms after 5x in the pattern x, 2x, 3x, 4x, 5x, … are:

  • A6x and 7x✓
  • B6 and 7
  • Cx⁶ and x⁷
  • D25x and 36x

Working

✓

The pattern's nth term is nx, so after 5x come 6x and 7x. (Dropping the letter gives 6 and 7; squaring the position gives 36x for the 6th.)

Set 4 · Q31–Q40

Q31

Across three days a stall earns: Day1 5c + 4d, Day2 3c + 6d, Day3 10c + d. The simplified total is:

  • A28cd
  • B18c + 11d✓
  • C18cd + 11
  • D11c + 18d

Working

✓

Add like terms across the three days. c-terms: 5c + 3c + 10c = 18c; d-terms: 4d + 6d + d = 11d. Total 18c + 11d. (28cd / 18cd + 11 wrongly merge unlike terms; 11c + 18d swaps the totals.)

Q32

If a = 3, b = 4 and c = 0, the value of a + bc is:

  • A7
  • B0
  • C12
  • D3✓

Working

✓

a + bc with a = 3, b = 4, c = 0 means a + (b × c) = 3 + 4 × 0 = 3 + 0 = 3. (Adding all three, 3 + 4 + 0 = 7, is the trap; (a + b)c = 0.)

Q33

Simplify: 4(2x).

  • A8x✓
  • B6x
  • C2x + 4
  • D8 + x

Working

✓

4(2x) = 4 × 2 × x = 8x. (6x adds the coefficients 4 + 2; 2x + 4 / 8 + x treat the product as a sum.)

Q34

The expression "the cost of n books at 15 each, then 20 taken off" is:

  • A15(n − 20)
  • B20 − 15n
  • C15n + 20
  • D15n − 20✓

Working

✓

Cost of n books at 15 each = 15n; "20 taken off" = 15n − 20. (15(n − 20) subtracts inside; 20 − 15n reverses the order; 15n + 20 adds.)

Q35

In the expression 6x − 7, the number 6 is called the:

  • Aconstant term
  • Bcoefficient of x✓
  • Clike term
  • Dvariable

Working

✓

In 6x − 7 the number 6 multiplies the letter x, so it is the coefficient of x. (−7 is the constant term; x is the variable.)

Q36

Two expressions are EQUAL when they:

  • Alook the same on paper
  • Bhave the same first letter
  • Cgive the same value for every substitution✓
  • Dboth contain a coefficient

Working

✓

Two expressions are equal when they give the same value for every substitution (e.g. l + b + l + b and 2l + 2b). Looking alike or sharing a first letter is irrelevant.

Q37

Simplify: 2l + 3b + 4l − b.

  • A6l + 2b✓
  • B8lb
  • C6l + 4b
  • D9lb

Working

✓

Collect like terms: l-terms 2l + 4l = 6l; b-terms 3b − b = 2b. Result 6l + 2b. (8lb / 9lb wrongly merge unlike terms.)

Q38

The perimeter of an equilateral triangle of side s is 3s. If the perimeter is to be written for side s = 12 cm, it is:

  • A15 cm
  • B36 cm✓
  • C27 cm
  • D312 cm

Working

✓

Perimeter of an equilateral triangle = 3s; with s = 12, 3 × 12 = 36 cm. (3 + 12 = 15 adds; "312" writes digits together; 27 = 3³.)

Q39

If x = 5, the value of 2(x + 3) is:

  • A13
  • B11
  • C16✓
  • D25

Working

✓

2(x + 3) with x = 5: 2 × (5 + 3) = 2 × 8 = 16. (2x + 3 = 13 forgets to distribute the 2 over the 3; 2 × 5 + ... mis-steps to 11; 5² = 25.)

Q40

The sum of any two even numbers, written with letters as 2a and 2b, is:

  • A2ab
  • B4ab
  • C2(a + b)✓
  • Da + b

Working

✓

2a + 2b = 2(a + b), which is 2 times a whole number, hence even. The factored form shows divisibility by 2. (2ab / 4ab wrongly multiply; a + b drops the factor 2.)

Q41

Simplify: 7p − p.

  • A7
  • B0
  • C7p
  • D6p✓

Working

✓

7p − p means 7p − 1p = (7 − 1)p = 6p. (Treating −p as −1 of nothing gives 7 or 0; 7p ignores the subtraction.)

Q42

The value of 100 ÷ n when n = 4 is:

  • A25✓
  • B400
  • C96
  • D104

Working

✓

100 ÷ n with n = 4: 100 ÷ 4 = 25. (400 multiplies; 96 subtracts; 104 adds.)

Q43

"Twice the sum of a number and 5" is written as:

  • A2x + 5
  • B2(x + 5)✓
  • C2x − 5
  • Dx + 10

Working

✓

"The sum of a number and 5" = (x + 5); "twice" it = 2(x + 5). (2x + 5 doubles only the number; x + 10 mis-distributes.)

Q44

Simplify: 3x + 5 + 2x + 7.

  • A5x + 12✓
  • B17x
  • C5x + 35
  • D6x + 12

Working

✓

Group like terms: x-terms 3x + 2x = 5x; numbers 5 + 7 = 12. Result 5x + 12. (17x / 6x + 12 wrongly merge x's and numbers; 5x + 35 multiplies the numbers.)

Q45

If m = −2, the value of m² + 3 is:

  • A1
  • B7✓
  • C−1
  • D−7

Working

✓

m² + 3 with m = −2: (−2)² + 3 = 4 + 3 = 7. (Squaring a negative gives +4; mistakenly writing −4 gives −1 or 1.)

Q46

The number of months in y years is given by:

  • Ay + 12
  • By/12
  • C12y✓
  • Dy − 12

Working

✓

1 year = 12 months, so y years = 12 × y = 12y months. (y + 12 adds; y/12 divides; y − 12 subtracts.)

Q47

Simplify: 9a − 4b − 3a + 4b.

  • A6a✓
  • B6a + 8b
  • C6ab
  • D6a − 8b

Working

✓

Collect like terms: a-terms 9a − 3a = 6a; b-terms −4b + 4b = 0. Result 6a. (6a + 8b / 6a − 8b mishandle the b-terms that actually cancel; 6ab merges unlike terms.)

Q48

The term 3a² and the term 5a are:

  • Alike terms
  • Bequal terms
  • Cunlike terms✓
  • Dboth constants

Working

✓

3a² has the letter part a² while 5a has the letter part a; different powers make them unlike terms, so they cannot be combined.

Q49

If the perimeter of a square is 4s, then for a square whose side is doubled to 2s the perimeter is:

  • A4s
  • B6s
  • C16s
  • D8s✓

Working

✓

Perimeter = 4 × side. With side 2s, perimeter = 4 × (2s) = 8s. (4s keeps the old side; 6s adds 4s + 2s; 16s squares the doubling.)

Q50

The value of (a + b)² when a = 1 and b = 2 is:

  • A5
  • B3
  • C6
  • D9✓

Working

✓

(a + b)² with a = 1, b = 2: (1 + 2)² = 3² = 9. (Computing a² + b² = 1 + 4 = 5 is the classic trap; 1 + 2 = 3 forgets to square; 6 = 2 × 3.)

Set 6 · Q51–Q60

Q51

A pattern uses 2n + 3 tiles for the nth shape. The number of EXTRA tiles added going from the 4th shape to the 5th shape is:

  • A3
  • B5
  • C2✓
  • D13

Working

✓

Tiles for the nth shape = 2n + 3. 5th shape: 2 × 5 + 3 = 13; 4th shape: 2 × 4 + 3 = 11. Extra added = 13 − 11 = 2 (the coefficient of n). (3 is the constant; 5 / 13 misread the question.)

Q52

Simplify: 5(x + 2) + 3x. (Treat 5(x + 2) as 5x + 10.)

  • A8x + 2
  • B8x + 10✓
  • C5x + 12
  • D8x + 12

Working

✓

5(x + 2) + 3x = (5x + 10) + 3x = (5x + 3x) + 10 = 8x + 10. (8x + 2 mishandles the 10; 5x + 12 forgets to distribute the 5; 8x + 12 adds wrongly.)

Q53

The total length of a caterpillar made of n square segments, each of side 1 unit, placed in a row so the whole is n units long, plus a 2-unit head, is:

  • A2n
  • Bn + 2✓
  • C3n
  • D2n + 2

Working

✓

The n body-segments give a length of n units, and the head adds 2 units, so the total length is n + 2. (2n doubles the body; 3n / 2n + 2 over-count.)

Q54

If x = 10, which expression has the GREATEST value?

  • Ax + 9
  • B2x − 5
  • Cx²÷ 10
  • D3x − 8✓

Working

✓

With x = 10: (A) 10 + 9 = 19; (B) 2 × 10 − 5 = 15; (C) 10² ÷ 10 = 100 ÷ 10 = 10; (D) 3 × 10 − 8 = 30 − 8 = 22. The greatest is (D) 22.

Q55

Using n for the middle number, the three consecutive numbers (n − 1), n, (n + 1) add up to:

  • A3n + 3
  • B3n✓
  • Cn³
  • D3n − 3

Working

✓

(n − 1) + n + (n + 1) = 3n: the −1 and +1 cancel, leaving 3n — exactly 3 times the middle number. (3n + 3 / 3n − 3 keep a leftover that actually cancels.)

Q56

Simplify: 4a + 7 − a + 3 − 2a.

  • Aa + 10✓
  • Ba + 4
  • C7a + 10
  • Da − 10

Working

✓

Collect like terms: a-terms 4a − a − 2a = a; numbers 7 + 3 = 10. Result a + 10. (a + 4 mishandles the numbers; 7a + 10 wrongly adds a coefficient.)

Q57

A square has side (n + 1). Its perimeter, simplified, is:

  • An + 4
  • B4n + 1
  • C4n + 4✓
  • Dn² + 1

Working

✓

Perimeter of a square = 4 × side = 4(n + 1) = 4n + 4. (n + 4 forgets to multiply the n; 4n + 1 distributes only over the n; n² + 1 squares.)

Q58

If x = 2, the values of 3x and 3 + x are, respectively:

  • A6 and 5✓
  • B5 and 6
  • C6 and 6
  • D5 and 5

Working

✓

3x with x = 2 is 3 × 2 = 6; 3 + x is 3 + 2 = 5. So the values are 6 and 5 respectively. (Swapping them gives 5 and 6 — the 3x = 3 + x confusion.)

Q59

A rule says: output = 4 × (input) − 1. When the input is 7, the output is:

  • A28
  • B27✓
  • C24
  • D11

Working

✓

Output = 4 × input − 1. With input 7: 4 × 7 − 1 = 28 − 1 = 27. (28 forgets the −1; 4 × (7 − 1) = 24 subtracts before multiplying; 4 + 7 = 11 adds.)

Q60

  • A5bp
  • B6bp
  • C3b + 2p✓
  • D2b + 3p

Working

✓
