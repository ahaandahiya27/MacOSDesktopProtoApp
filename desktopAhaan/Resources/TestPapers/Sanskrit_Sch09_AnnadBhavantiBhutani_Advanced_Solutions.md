# Sanskrit — Chapter 9: अन्नाद् भवन्ति भूतानि · Paper 2 (Advanced) Solutions

**1. (C)** The wheel runs ब्रह्म → कर्म → यज्ञ → **पर्जन्य (rain)** → अन्न → भूतानि.

**2. (A)** Reversed, the chain ends with यज्ञ arising from **कर्म (action)**.

**3. (D)** कर्म itself is said to arise from **ब्रह्म (the Absolute)** — कर्म ब्रह्मोद्भवम्.

**4. (B)** पर्जन्य (rain) produces **अन्न (food)** next in the chain.

**5. (B)** अन्नात् is the ablative; -आत् means **'from food'** (the source).

**6. (D)** The ablative marks food as the **source** of beings — they arise from it — not their maker.

**7. (A)** 'The fruit falls from the tree' puts वृक्ष in the ablative: **वृक्षात्**.

**8. (C)** 'He comes from the village' puts ग्राम in the ablative: **ग्रामात्**.

**9. (A)** The -द् of अन्नाद् comes from **sandhi** (अन्नात् + भवन्ति: त् → द् before भ्).

**10. (C)** The त् → द् change happens because the following sound भ् is **voiced**.

**11. (D)** सत् + भावना → **सद्भावना** (त् → द् before the voiced भ्).

**12. (B)** जगत् + ईशः → **जगदीशः** (त् → द् before the voiced vowel ई).

**13. (D)** After the sandhi, अन्नाद् is still the **ablative** ('from food').

**14. (B)** भूतानि (root भू 'to be') means **beings / things that have come to be**, not ghosts.

**15. (C)** The present 'they come to be' (from भू) is **भवन्ति**.

**16. (A)** भूत and भवति both come from the root **भू**.

**17. (C)** The future 'it will be' (from भू) is **भविष्यति**.

**18. (A)** जानीयात् ('one should know') is in the **optative (विधिलिङ्)**.

**19. (B)** The optative expresses **'should / ought to / may'** — advice or a rule.

**20. (D)** कुर्यात् is the optative of कृ, meaning **'one should do'**.

**21. (A)** एवं प्रवर्तितं चक्रम् ('the wheel thus set in motion') refers to **the cycle of action–rain–food–beings**.

**22. (D)** One who does not keep the wheel turning lives **in vain (a wasteful life)**.

**23. (B)** ब्रह्म (neuter) means **the all-pervading impersonal Absolute (सर्वव्याप्तः परमेश्वरः)**.

**24. (C)** ब्रह्म (the Absolute) is grammatically **neuter**.

**25. (B)** ब्रह्मा (long ā, masculine) means **the creator-god** (of the trimurti).

**26. (C)** ब्रह्मन् (masculine) here means **a priest / brahmin**.

**27. (A)** ब्रह्म, ब्रह्मा and ब्रह्मन् are told apart by their **gender** and meaning.

**28. (D)** In कर्म ब्रह्मोद्भवम्, ब्रह्म is the **neuter Absolute** — the ultimate source.

**29. (D)** यज्ञ here is best understood as **selfless, cooperative action / sacrifice that sustains the world**.

**30. (A)** पर्जन्य means **rain / rain-cloud**.

**31. (C)** ओषधि means **a herb / plant** (that yields food).

**32. (B)** The cycle's lesson is the world's **interdependence — each link sustains the next**.

**33. (C)** रसायनशास्त्रम् means **chemistry**.

**34. (B)** शास्त्रम् means **a science / treatise**.

**35. (D)** Reusing रसायन for chemistry shows Sanskrit's ability to **coin terms for new subjects from its own roots**.

**36. (A)** The ecological reading links यज्ञ to modern **sustainability / care for nature**.

**37. (A)** The two phrasings differ only in **word order and the sandhi at the join** — the meaning is the same.

**38. (B)** भवन्ति agrees with the plural भूतानि, so it is **third-person plural** ('they come to be').

**39. (D)** पर्जन्यात् अन्नम् means **'food from rain'** (ablative पर्जन्यात्).

**40. (C)** अन्नात्, पर्जन्यात्, यज्ञात् all mean **'from / arising out of'** (ablative).

**41. (D)** सर्वव्याप्त means **all-pervading**.

**42. (C)** The food-cycle is treated as an expression of the omnipresent **ब्रह्म (Cosmic Power)**.

**43. (A)** तत् + रूपम् → **तद्रूपम्** (त् → द् before the voiced र).

**44. (B)** सः धर्मं जानीयात् means 'he **should know** dharma' (optative).

**45. (B)** The optative is right for stating a **rule or moral 'ought' (one should ...)**.

**46. (D)** The correct pair is **अन्नात् = from food**. भूतानि = beings, पर्जन्य = rain, यज्ञ = sacrifice.

**47. (C)** The correct grammar: **अन्नात् = ablative ('from'); जानीयात् = optative ('should know')**.

**48. (A)** The correct sandhi: **अन्नात् + भवन्ति = अन्नाद्भवन्ति**. सत्+भावना = सद्भावना, जगत्+ईश = जगदीश.

**49. (A)** The correct pair: **ब्रह्म (neuter) = the impersonal Absolute**. ब्रह्मा = creator-god, ब्रह्मन् = priest.

**50. (D)** The correct link is **food → beings** (beings arise from food); beings do not make food.

**51. (C)** The repeated ablative teaches a relation of **mutual dependence** — each thing arising from another.

**52. (B)** भूत as 'a being' (not 'ghost') is settled by **the Vedanta/Gita context**.

**53. (C)** उद्भव (in ब्रह्मोद्भवम्) means **'origin / arising'**.

**54. (D)** यज्ञ matters because it **keeps the life-supporting wheel turning**.

**55. (B)** One keeps the wheel turning by **performing one's duty / selfless action (कर्म/यज्ञ)**.

**56. (A)** पर्जन्यात् + भवति → **पर्जन्याद्भवति** (त् → द् before the voiced भ्).

**57. (B)** The central theme is **the Gita's cycle of creation and the interdependence of all life**.

**58. (A)** अन्नाद् भवन्ति भूतानि means **'from food, all living beings come to be'**.

**59. (C)** The chapter links the verse with lessons on **the ablative, consonant sandhi, the optative, and the root भू**.

**60. (D)** भूतानि means 'beings' (root भू), and जानीयात् is the **optative ('one should know')** — the student is wrong on both.
