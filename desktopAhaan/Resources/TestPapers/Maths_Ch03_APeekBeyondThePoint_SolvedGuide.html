Q1

A screw reaches the 7th small mark past 2 on a ruler whose centimetres are each split into 10 equal parts. Its length is:

  • A2.07 cm
  • B2.7 cm✓
  • C2.17 cm
  • D0.27 cm

Working

✓

The space from 2 to 3 is split into 10 tenths; the 7th mark past 2 is 2 and 7 tenths = 2.7 cm. (2.07 would be 7 hundredths — the "put the digit in the wrong place" trap.)

Q2

How many wholes and tenths make up 34 tenths (34/10)?

  • A34 wholes and 0 tenths
  • B3 wholes and 4 tenths✓
  • C4 wholes and 3 tenths
  • D30 wholes and 4 tenths

Working

✓

34 tenths = 30 tenths + 4 tenths. Since 10 tenths = 1 whole, 30 tenths = 3 wholes. So 34/10 = 3 wholes and 4 tenths. (Reading the digits straight off as "4 wholes 3 tenths" reverses them — trap C.)

Q3

The first place to the right of the decimal point is called the:

  • Aoneths place
  • Bhundredths place
  • Ctenths place✓
  • Dunits place

Working

✓

There is no "oneths" place. Immediately right of the point is the TENTHS place, then hundredths, then thousandths.

Q4

How many hundredths are there in one whole unit?

  • A10
  • B1000
  • C50
  • D100✓

Working

✓

A unit holds 10 tenths and each tenth holds 10 hundredths, so a whole holds 10 × 10 = 100 hundredths. (10 is hundredths-in-a-tenth — the trap.)

Q5

An 8 9/10-unit sheet is folded exactly in half along its length. Its new length is:

  • A4.95 units

  • C4.4 units
  • D4.45 units✓

Working

✓

Half of 8 9/10 = 8.9 ÷ 2 = 4.45 units. It falls between 4.4 and 4.5, exactly at 4 and 45 hundredths. (4.5 is the careless "round up" answer.)

Q7

Which of these decimals is the largest?

  • A0.4✓
  • B0.04
  • C0.39
  • D0.198

Working

✓

Compare from the left. Tenths digits: 0.4 → 4, 0.39 → 3, 0.198 → 1, 0.04 → 0. The largest tenths digit wins: 0.4. (Picking 0.39 because "more digits" is the classic length trap.)

✓

7/100 means 7 hundredths = 0.07. (0.7 = 7 tenths = 70/100; 0.007 = 7 thousandths.)

Q9

Arrange in increasing order: 0.5, 0.45, 0.405, 0.054.

  • A0.5, 0.45, 0.405, 0.054
  • B0.054, 0.45, 0.405, 0.5
  • C0.054, 0.405, 0.45, 0.5✓
  • D0.405, 0.45, 0.5, 0.054

Working

✓

Pad to thousandths: 0.500, 0.450, 0.405, 0.054. Increasing: 0.054 < 0.405 < 0.45 < 0.5. So (C).

Q10

Which list contains numbers that are ALL equal to one another?

  • A4.5, 4.50, 4.500✓
  • B4.5, 4.05, 4.005
  • C0.5, 0.50, 0.05
  • D4.5, 45.0, 0.45

Working

✓

Trailing zeros never change a value: 4.5 = 4.50 = 4.500 (all four and five tenths). In (B) the digits sit in different places (4.05, 4.005 differ); (C) has 0.05 ≠ 0.5; (D) mixes 0.45 in. So (A).

Q17

Which decimal is exactly halfway between 0.3 and 0.4?

  • A0.7
  • B0.34

Working

✓

Halfway between 0.30 and 0.40 is their average: (0.30 + 0.40) ÷ 2 = 0.70 ÷ 2 = 0.35. (0.34 is not the midpoint; 0.7 is the sum, not the midpoint.)

Q19

Q51

Which fraction does NOT give a terminating (ending) decimal?

  • A1/2
  • B1/4
  • C1/5
  • D1/3✓

Working

✓

A fraction terminates only if its denominator (in lowest terms) uses just the primes 2 and 5. 1/2 = 0.5, 1/4 = 0.25, 1/5 = 0.2 all terminate; but 1/3 = 0.333… never ends (3 is neither 2 nor 5). So (D).
