# Science — Chapter 13: Motion and Time · Paper 2 (Advanced) Solutions

**1. (D)** Each blade-tip traces a circle, so the motion is circular. It also returns to the same orientation every 0.4 s, so it's periodic. It is NOT oscillatory (oscillation needs back-and-forth across an equilibrium point), and clearly not rectilinear or random.

**2. (C)** Oscillation requires back-and-forth across an equilibrium; the swing did that once. Periodicity requires the cycle to REPEAT — a single excursion doesn't qualify. So it's oscillatory but not periodic. Option A confuses crossing with repeating; B and D are unrelated.

**3. (C)** Equal time-intervals (1 hour each) but unequal distances (50 → 70 → 90 km) → non-uniform. The distance per hour is GROWING, so the car is speeding up. Slowing down would mean shrinking distances per equal time.

**4. (D)** The train's nose enters the platform and the train's tail leaves the other end — total distance covered = train length + platform length = 200 + 300 = 500 m. Speed = 500 m ÷ 25 s = 20 m/s.

**5. (C)** Equal distances → use harmonic mean: avg = 2·v₁·v₂/(v₁+v₂) = 2·4·6/(4+6) = 48/10 = 4.8 km/h. The trap is option A — averaging speeds arithmetically only works for equal TIMES, not equal distances.

**6. (C)** Downstream the current helps: 12 + 4 = 16 km/h. Upstream the current opposes: 12 − 4 = 8 km/h. Many kids reverse this — remember "down" means with the flow, which adds.

**7. (D)** T ∝ √L, so doubling L scales T by √2 (about 1.414×). Option A is the common mistake — period does NOT scale linearly with length.

**8. (B)** T ∝ √L, and √(1/4) = 1/2. So the period halves. Option C is the natural trap — but length-quartered does NOT quarter the period; the square root softens it.

**9. (A)** Moving in OPPOSITE directions adds their speeds for the rate of separation: 5 + 3 = 8 m/s. They draw apart at 8 m every second.

**10. (D)** In the SAME direction, the relative speed is the difference: 20 − 15 = 5 m/s. From the truck driver's frame, the car only seems to approach at 5 m/s.

**11. (C)** 7.5 km/s × 3600 s/h = 27 000 km/h. Many ISS-style satellites really do orbit at roughly this speed — about 27 000 km/h or Mach 25.

**12. (C)** Target avg of 60 km/h × 4 h trip would give 240 km total — so total time must be 4 hours. First 120 km at 40 km/h takes 3 hours. The remaining 120 km must therefore be covered in 1 hour → 120 km/h. Note this is faster than the target average because the slow first half ate up most of the time budget.

**13. (D)** T = 2π√(L/g). If g shrinks by a factor of 6, then T grows by √6 ≈ 2.45×. So a 1-second Earth pendulum becomes about 2.45 s on the Moon.

**14. (A)** 144 km/h × 5/18 = 40 m/s. Time = distance ÷ speed = 18 m ÷ 40 m/s = 0.45 s. That's why batters have only about half a second to react — they're really good.

**15. (A)** A day is 1440 minutes; losing 6 min is 6/1440 = 1/240 ≈ 0.4%. "Losing time" means the clock's seconds are LONGER than true seconds — its period is too LONG by ~0.4%.

**16. (A)** Equal TIME segments → arithmetic mean: (30+50+70)/3 = 150/3 = 50 km/h. This case is straightforward because the same hours weight each speed equally.

**17. (A)** Equal DISTANCE segments → harmonic mean. avg = 3/(1/20+1/30+1/60) = 3/((3+2+1)/60) = 3/(6/60) = 30 km/h. The slow legs eat up so much time that they dominate.

**18. (C)** From t=15 s to t=25 s the line falls from 60 m to 0 m, slope = −6 m/s. At t = 20 s, the object has descended for 5 s at 6 m/s → distance from start = 60 − 30 = 30 m, moving back toward the start.

**19. (C)** Up: 60 m (during 0→10 s). At rest: 0 m (during 10→15 s, no distance covered). Back: 60 m (during 15→25 s). Total path length = 60 + 0 + 60 = 120 m.

**20. (A)** 18 km/h = 5 m/s. Each revolution covers one circumference = 2 m. Revolutions per second = 5 ÷ 2 = 2.5.

**21. (C)** Slope of distance ÷ time = speed at that instant. The bigger the slope, the faster the motion at that moment. Slope is NOT mass, distance, or acceleration.

**22. (C)** A parabola that bends UPWARD means distance grows faster and faster as time passes — the object is speeding up. A straight line would be constant speed; a curve flattening out would be slowing.

**23. (A)** T ∝ √L. T_a : T_b = √1 : √4 = 1 : 2. So pendulum B (4 m) has TWICE the period of pendulum A (1 m), even though its length is 4× longer.

**24. (A)** T ∝ √L, so to halve T you need to QUARTER L. Many kids halve the length and get a √2 factor wrong; the correct answer is one-fourth.

**25. (A)** Sound travels 330 m/s × 6 s = 1980 m = 1.98 km. The flash arrived essentially the instant the lightning struck (light is much faster than sound), so the 6-second gap is the sound's travel time.

**26. (A)** Trains approach each other; their CLOSING speed is the sum: 60 + 40 = 100 km/h. The gap shrinks at 100 km every hour.

**27. (D)** Walking IN THE SAME DIRECTION as the walkway, ground speed adds: 3 + 5 = 8 km/h. Compare to standing still on the walkway (5 km/h) — walking adds your foot-speed.

**28. (C)** Line A: y = 5x. Line B: y = 20 + x. They meet when 5x = 20 + x → 4x = 20 → x = 5 s. At that moment y = 25 m. So they first cross at (5 s, 25 m).

**29. (D)** At t = 2 s: A is at 5×2 = 10 m, B is at 20 + 2 = 22 m. Gap = 22 − 10 = 12 m. B is still ahead (it had a 20 m head start; A has only gained 2 m so far).

**30. (C)** Same direction (north). The man's ground speed = bus speed + his speed relative to bus = 30 + 5 = 35 km/h north.

**31. (D)** Frequency = oscillations ÷ time = 24 ÷ 30 s = 0.8 Hz. So the pendulum completes 0.8 oscillations every second on average.

**32. (A)** f = 1/T = 1/0.5 = 2 Hz. A half-second period means twice-per-second oscillation.

**33. (A)** Total distance in 3 seconds: 2 + 6 + 10 = 18 m. Total time = 3 s. Average speed = 18 ÷ 3 = 6 m/s. The motion itself is non-uniform (it's accelerating), but the AVERAGE is still well-defined.

**34. (A)** 36 km/h = 10 m/s. To pass an observer completely, the train's whole length must cross — so distance = train length = 100 m. Time = 100 ÷ 10 = 10 s.

**35. (B)** They will be back in phase when both have completed an integer number of periods. The smallest common multiple of 2 s and 3 s is 6 s — after 6 s, A has done 3 full swings and B has done 2 full swings, both back at the same position.

**36. (C)** T = 1/f = 1/32768 ≈ 3.05 × 10⁻⁵ s ≈ 30 μs (microseconds). Quartz vibrates incredibly fast, which is why digital watches keep time so accurately.

**37. (B)** During the 0.6 s reaction time the car travels at 20 m/s × 0.6 s = 12 m — completely before the brake even starts working. That's why following distance matters so much at speed.

**38. (D)** 300 000 km/s × 480 s (8 minutes) = 1.44 × 10⁸ km. That same number written in plain words is 144 million km. So options B and C are two formats of the SAME distance — D acknowledges both are correct.

**39. (A)** Distance: 72 km/h × 0.5 h = 36 km; 90 km/h × (1/3) h = 30 km. Total 66 km in 50 min (5/6 h). Avg = 66 ÷ (5/6) = 66 × 6/5 = 79.2 km/h ≈ 79 km/h.

**40. (B)** A steeper d-t line = larger distance covered per unit time = higher speed. Steepness has nothing to do with mass, elapsed time, or axis choice (axes scale, not slope-meaning).

**41. (C)** Initially the steady straight-line car (A) is ahead because B starts slower. But B's speed is GROWING (the curve bends upward). If the curve bends enough, B's accumulated distance can overtake A's by t = 10 s. So C is correct: B may catch up and even surpass.

**42. (A)** Total trip target: 90 km ÷ 60 km/h = 1.5 hours. First 45 km already took 1 hour. So 30 minutes remain for the second 45 km → 45 km / 0.5 h = 90 km/h needed. So drive for 30 minutes.

**43. (B)** L doubles → T scales by √2 ≈ 1.414. The clock's seconds are now 1.414× too long, so it counts only 1/1.414 ≈ 0.707 of the true elapsed time. In 1 true hour (60 min) it shows 60 × 0.707 ≈ 42.4 min — about 17.6 min behind. Closest: 17.5 min behind.

**44. (A)** Equal TIMES (1 hour each) → arithmetic mean of speeds: (20+40+60)/3 = 120/3 = 40 km/h. Same problem shape as Q16.

**45. (B)** Equal DISTANCES (60 km each) → harmonic mean. avg = 3 ÷ (1/20 + 1/40 + 1/60) = 3 ÷ (11/120) = 360/11 ≈ 32.7 km/h. Notice it's slower than the time-weighted version because the slow legs hog more time.

**46. (B)** Sand falls under gravity. As long as gravity is roughly constant (it is, anywhere on Earth's surface), the sand drops at a roughly constant rate, so it takes the same time to flow through. A sand clock would NOT work in zero gravity (e.g. on the ISS).

**47. (B)** Horizontal line on a distance–time graph = no change in distance over time = zero speed. The object is at rest at the 20 m mark from t = 5 s to t = 12 s.

**48. (B)** Relative speed of the overtaking train with respect to the slower train = 20 − 10 = 10 m/s. Total distance to clear = 200 + 100 = 300 m (one train's nose must reach the tail of the other, then its own tail must clear). Time = 300 ÷ 10 = 30 s.

**49. (B)** Same distance, so t = d/v → t ∝ 1/v. t_A : t_B = (1/60) : (1/40) = 40 : 60 = 2 : 3. Car A is faster, so its time is shorter — the smaller number in the ratio.

**50. (B)** Diameter 0.5 m → circumference = π × 0.5 ≈ 1.57 m per revolution. At 5 m/s, revolutions per second = 5 ÷ 1.57 ≈ 3.18 ≈ 3.2.

**51. (D)** Statement D is FALSE — Galileo's discovery was that pendulum period depends ONLY on length (and g), NOT on bob mass. Two pendulums of the same length swing in identical times regardless of how heavy the bobs are. The other three statements are all true.

**52. (B)** A horizontal segment means distance is not changing as time passes → the object is stationary → speed = 0. The slope of a horizontal line is exactly zero.

**53. (B)** Speed (path-length basis) = total path ÷ total time = (600+600) m ÷ (40+20) s = 1200 ÷ 60 = 20 m/s. Note that DISPLACEMENT-based average velocity would be 0 (drone returns to start), but the question asks for path-based speed.

**54. (B)** Walker: 2 km / 4 km/h = 0.5 h = 30 min, finishes at t = 30 min. Cyclist: 2 km / 12 km/h = 10 min, but starts at t = 15 min, so finishes at t = 25 min. Cyclist beats walker by 30 − 25 = 5 min.

**55. (B)** T ∝ 1/√g. Smaller g (mountaintop) → larger T → seconds are too long → clock runs SLOWER than at sea level. This is why precision pendulum clocks must be re-calibrated when moved.

**56. (C)** Total distance = 30 + 30 = 60 km. Total time = 1 h (first leg) + 0.5 h (idle) + 0.5 h (second leg) = 2 h. Avg = 60 ÷ 2 = 30 km/h. The idle time counts even though no distance was covered.

**57. (B)** Cumulative distance only increases (or stays the same) — you can't "untravel" distance you've already covered. So the slope cannot be negative. All other options (zero slope = at rest, very large slope = high speed, same slope at two points = same speed twice) are possible.

**58. (A)** Frequency = oscillations ÷ time = 20 ÷ 5 = 4 Hz. Higher frequency = more vibrations per second.

**59. (D)** Check each conversion using 1 km/h = 5/18 m/s. A: 36 × 5/18 = 10 ✓. B: 54 × 5/18 = 15 ✓. C: 72 × 5/18 = 20 ✓. D: 90 × 5/18 = 25, but the option says 30 — that's wrong by 5 m/s. So D is the incorrect conversion.

**60. (B)** To cross a bridge, the train must travel one bridge-length PLUS one train-length: 240 + 60 = 300 m. Speed = 300 m ÷ 15 s = 20 m/s. Like the platform problem in Q4.
