The 10 terms to know

  • Prashasti · प्रशस्ति — A formal inscription composed in praise of a king, recording his achievements; the Prayaga Prashasti praises Samudragupta.
    Example: Harisena wrote the Prayaga Prashasti to celebrate Samudragupta's conquests.
  • Maharajadhiraja · महाराजाधिराज — A grand royal title meaning 'great king of kings', adopted by Gupta rulers to claim supreme authority.
    Example: Gupta emperors used 'maharajadhiraja' instead of the simpler 'maharaja'.
  • Chakravartin · चक्रवर्तिन् — The title of a universal or supreme ruler, claiming dominion over all lesser kings.
    Example: The Guptas' use of 'chakravartin' proclaimed their paramount status.
  • Regent · संरक्षक शासक — A person who governs a kingdom temporarily on behalf of a monarch who is unable to rule, often a child.
    Example: Prabhavati Gupta became the regent ruler of the Vakataka kingdom for her young sons.
  • Ashvamedha yajna · अश्वमेध यज्ञ — A grand horse-sacrifice ritual performed by ambitious kings to proclaim their power and build mighty empires.
    Example: Special coins commemorated the ashvamedha yajna of Gupta rulers.
  • Outcaste · बहिष्कृत — Someone rejected from a social or cultural group; in this period, a category of people considered socially too low to be part of the varna system.
    Example: Faxian wrote that the chandalas were treated as outcastes and lived outside the city limits.
  • Codified · संहिताबद्ध — Arranged or written down in an organised and systematic way.
    Example: Ayurveda was codified during the Gupta period in texts like the Charaka Samhita.
  • Vahana · वाहन — The animal mount associated with a deity or a deity-like ruler; for example, Garuda is the mount of Vishnu.
    Example: Chandragupta II's inscriptions often show Garuda, the vahana of Vishnu.
  • Vihara · विहार — A Buddhist monastery, a place where monks lived, studied and taught.
    Example: The Guptas patronised Nalanda university and several Buddhist viharas.
  • Aryabhatiya · आर्यभटीय — A short Sanskrit treatise on mathematics and astronomy written by Aryabhata around 500 CE.
    Example: In the Aryabhatiya, Aryabhata proposed that the Earth spins on its axis.
