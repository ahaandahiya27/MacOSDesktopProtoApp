Q1. Which Gupta ruler was also known as the 'Vikramaditya'? (NCERT page 166)

Chandragupta II was also known as 'Vikramaditya'. He was one of the most renowned rulers of the Gupta dynasty, a devotee of Vishnu whose mount Garuda appears on his inscriptions, and it was during his reign that art, architecture, literature and science particularly flourished. He is also identified with the king 'Chandra' named in the inscription on the rust-resistant Iron Pillar of Delhi.

Q2. 'Periods of peace support the development of various aspects of sociocultural life, literature, and the development of science and technology.' Examine this statement in the light of the Gupta empire. (NCERT page 166)

The Gupta empire strongly supports this statement. Its prolonged peace and stability strengthened the economy, which allowed the state to support scholars, artists and scientists. As a result, Sanskrit literature flourished (Kalidasa's works and the Puranas), astronomy and mathematics advanced (Aryabhata and Varahamihira), medicine was codified (the Charaka and Sushruta Samhitas), metallurgy progressed (the rust-resistant Iron Pillar), and art reached great heights (Sarnath, Ajanta, Udayagiri). This is exactly why historians call the Gupta period the 'classical age' of India — it shows that peace and prosperity allow culture, learning and technology to flourish.

Q3. Who were the Pallavas and where did they rule? (NCERT page 166)

The Pallavas were a powerful dynasty that rose in the south, gradually gaining power as the Satavahana dynasty (under whom they had been a tributary power) declined. They ruled over parts of present-day Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Telangana and Andhra Pradesh until the latter half of the 9th century CE. Their capital was Kanchipuram (in present-day Tamil Nadu), known as the 'city of a thousand temples' and a major centre of learning. The Pallavas were great patrons of art and architecture, mostly devotees of Shiva, and are credited with building magnificent temples and rock-cut caves.

Q4. Match the following: (1) Kanchipuram, (2) Ujjayini, (3) Udayagiri, (4) Ajanta, (5) Pataliputra with their descriptions. (NCERT page 166)

(1) Kanchipuram → known as 'a city of a thousand temples'. (2) Ujjayini → a prominent centre of learning in ancient India (home of Varahamihira). (3) Udayagiri → famous for rock-cut caves featuring intricate carvings of Hindu deities, especially Vishnu. (4) Ajanta → known for vibrant cave paintings that depict the Jataka tales. (5) Pataliputra → capital of the Guptas.
