The Gupta era at a glance (3rd–6th century CE)

A chronological walk through the rise, golden age and decline of the Gupta Empire and the great figures of the classical age.

  • Kushanas weaken (3rd century CE)
    The Kushana Empire, which ruled the north and northwest, begins to weaken, and new kingdoms emerge. The Gupta dynasty rises near present-day Uttar Pradesh.
  • Chandragupta I (early Guptas)
    Chandragupta I lays the foundation of the empire through coins and a marriage alliance with the Lichchhavi princess Kumaradevi, taking the grand title 'maharajadhiraja'.
  • Samudragupta, the warrior king
    Samudragupta expands the empire through many wars; his court poet Harisena records his conquests in the Prayaga Prashasti at Prayagraj.
  • Chandragupta II 'Vikramaditya' (c. 400 CE)
    Under Chandragupta II, art, literature and science flourish; the rust-resistant Iron Pillar is raised, and his daughter Prabhavati Gupta becomes regent of the Vakataka kingdom.
  • Faxian's visit (early 5th century CE)
    The Chinese pilgrim Faxian travels across India, describing a prosperous, charitable society in his travelogue.
  • The classical age (around 500 CE)
    Aryabhata (the Aryabhatiya) and Varahamihira (the Brihat Samhita) advance astronomy and mathematics; Kalidasa perfects Sanskrit poetry; Ayurveda is codified; Gupta art flourishes at Sarnath, Ajanta and Udayagiri.
  • Decline (6th century CE)
    Repeated Huna invasions from Central Asia and the rise of powerful regional rulers weaken the empire, while the Pallavas (south) and Kamarupa (northeast) flourish.
