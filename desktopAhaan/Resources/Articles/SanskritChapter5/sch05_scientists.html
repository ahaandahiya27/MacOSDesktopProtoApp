Minds Behind सेवा हि परमो धर्मः

Sanskrit's living tradition was built by real people. Here is one of them — whose work the chapter quietly stands on.

≈ 7 min read · for curious 12-year-olds

Every Sanskrit chapter you read is the visible tip of a much longer history. Behind each verse, each word-table, each story is at least one person who made the next step possible. This article names one of them.

Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi (1869 – 1948)

Indian. Made सेवा (selfless service) the foundation of his life and India's freedom struggle.

Gandhi is best known for leading India's non-violent independence movement. Less well-known is that the philosophical heart of his life was सेवा — service. He said: 'The best way to find yourself is to lose yourself in the service of others.' He built ashrams where every member, including himself, did manual work — cleaning toilets, weaving cloth, cooking — as part of daily practice. He refused to treat any service as menial. His writings include 'Sevā-dharma' as a central category. The chapter's claim — सेवा हि परमो धर्मः — was not a slogan for Gandhi; it was an organising principle.

💡 What they gave us

Service is not what powerful people do FOR less-powerful people. Service is the practice that makes anyone fully human. Gandhi lived that distinction every day.

📝 Stretch your thinking

The figures behind Sanskrit's tradition rarely worked alone — Pāṇini built on Yāska, Patañjali built on Pāṇini, Śaṅkara built on the Upaniṣads, Bhartṛhari built on centuries of prior verse. Every great mind sits on top of another. Whose 'shoulders' would you most want to stand on?
