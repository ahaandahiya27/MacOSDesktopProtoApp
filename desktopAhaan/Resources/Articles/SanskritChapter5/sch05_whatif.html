"What If?" — सेवा हि परमो धर्मः

Hypothetical questions with no single right answer. Think hard before you read the discussion.

≈ 7 min read · brain-stretcher level

The textbook teaches what IS. Real understanding comes from asking what IF? Pick a question below, pause and think for a minute, then read one possible discussion.

What if 1 — no one in society did सेवा?

The question: Imagine a society where every transaction was paid, every help was contractual.

🧠 Think first

What would be lost — practically, emotionally, civically?

One discussion path: Practical losses: hospitals, schools, voluntary fire-services would collapse — most of them run on partial-or-full सेवा. Emotional loss: there would be no one to trust beyond the price tag. Civic loss: nothing would hold a community together except commerce. The chapter is implicitly defending the social fabric, not just the individual choice.

What if 2 — the doctor had taken the prestigious position instead of serving the sick?

The question: The chapter's hero chose service over a high पदवी. Suppose he had chosen the other way.

🧠 Think first

Would his life have been WORSE — measurably?

One discussion path: By worldly measures, almost certainly better — money, title, comfort. By the chapter's measure (परमो धर्मः), much worse. The chapter's claim is that there is a measure beyond worldly comfort. It is not a claim everyone shares, but it is one the kid is being invited to consider.

What if 3 — all सेवा had to be paid for, as a wage?

The question: Suppose laws required every act of service to come with payment.

🧠 Think first

Would there still be सेवा?

One discussion path: Definitionally, no — a paid act becomes work, not service. Sanskrit's distinction between सेवा (selfless) and दानम् (giving) and वेतनम् (wage) is precise. The category 'unpaid help' would disappear. The Indian language for that category would survive only in poetry.

📝 Stretch your thinking

None of these has one 'correct' answer — they are for arguing about. Try one on a friend or a parent and see if their discussion path differs from yours.
