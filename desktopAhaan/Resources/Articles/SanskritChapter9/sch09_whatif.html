"What If?" — अन्नाद् भवन्ति भूतानि

Hypothetical questions with no single right answer. Think hard before you read the discussion.

≈ 7 min read · brain-stretcher level

The textbook teaches what IS. Real understanding comes from asking what IF? Pick a question below, pause and think for a minute, then read one possible discussion.

What if 1 — the cycle broke — beings consumed food but no rain came?

The question: Modern climate change makes this scenario very real.

🧠 Think first

Where in the Gītā's chain would the break be — and what would it cost?

One discussion path: The chain food→beings would continue for a while on stored grain, but rain→food fails (drought), then beings starve. The Gītā does not say WHO breaks the chain, but the verse implies that the break starts from human कर्म (action). The 21st century has been demonstrating exactly this dynamic — human carbon, less rain, less food, more suffering. Old verse, current science.

What if 2 — humans stopped doing कर्म altogether?

The question: Krishna's verse says action → yajña → rain → food → beings. Imagine humans STOPPED doing anything.

🧠 Think first

Does the verse predict universal famine?

One discussion path: Literally read, yes. The verse claims all of creation depends on humans participating in the cycle. Modern ecology would say the rain doesn't actually need human action — the water cycle is fine without us. But the verse's spiritual point is different: humans who do not ACT do not deserve to consume. It is an ethic about the right to eat.

What if 3 — there were no यज्ञ link in the chain?

The question: Imagine the verse said simply action → rain → food → beings, with no यज्ञ step.

🧠 Think first

Does removing 'sacrifice' change the meaning?

One discussion path: Yes, profoundly. यज्ञ in this verse means selfless, well-directed action. Removing it would make the chain mechanical — any action produces rain. The verse's claim is that only SELFLESS action (yajña) keeps the cycle whole. Greed-action breaks it. The middle link is the moral heart.

📝 Stretch your thinking

None of these has one 'correct' answer — they are for arguing about. Try one on a friend or a parent and see if their discussion path differs from yours.
