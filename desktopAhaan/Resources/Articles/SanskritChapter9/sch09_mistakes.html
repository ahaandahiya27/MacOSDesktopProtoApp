Common Mistakes

Five errors students often make in this chapter — and the gentle corrections that fix them.

≈ 5 min · honest-with-yourself level

Sanskrit makes precise distinctions that English glosses over — case endings, gender, tense, prefix vs augment. The mistakes below are not personal failings — they are signals that a specific detail of the language needs another look.

Mistake 1 — Treating 'भूतानि' as 'ghosts'

What students often write/say: Modern Hindi भूत often means 'ghost'.

The correct form: Classical Sanskrit भूतानि = 'beings, creatures' (every living thing).

💡 Why

Same root (भू, 'to be') but two different uses. In Vedānta/Gītā context भूतानि = beings; in folk-Hindi भूत = ghost (a specific kind of being). Context dictates.

Mistake 2 — Saying the verse claims 'food makes beings'

What students often write/say: Loose translation of अन्नात् भवन्ति.

The correct form: More precisely: 'FROM food, beings COME-TO-BE'. The food is the source, not a maker.

💡 Why

Sanskrit ablatives express 'arise from', not 'are made by'. The chain (food → beings) is dependence, not creation.

Mistake 3 — Confusing 'यज्ञ' with 'पूजा'

What students often write/say: Both involve ritual.

The correct form: यज्ञ = Vedic fire-ritual (often with offerings). पूजा = devotional worship (often image-based).

💡 Why

Two different ritual traditions. In the Gītā verse, यज्ञ is the broader concept of selfless action including ritual. पूजा is the personal/temple worship form.

Mistake 4 — Translating 'ज्ञेयः' as 'known'

What students often write/say: Looks like a past participle.

The correct form: ज्ञेयः is a GERUNDIVE: 'should be known' / 'is to be known'.

💡 Why

Sanskrit -य suffix on past-participial bases makes 'to-be-X-ed'. कृत = done; कार्य = to be done. ज्ञात = known; ज्ञेय = to be known. Big shift in meaning.

Mistake 5 — Reading 'अन्नाद्' as a noun ('food-er')

What students often write/say: The -द् ending might look like an agent.

The correct form: अन्नाद् is ablative of अन्न: 'FROM food'. Final -द is the ablative ending of अन्न after sandhi.

💡 Why

Sanskrit sandhi: अन्नात् + भवन्ति → अन्नाद्भवन्ति (the त् of -आत् becomes voiced -द् before the voiced भ्). Recognise sandhi rules and the case stays visible.

📝 Self-check

Re-read the chapter once with these five mistakes in mind. Notice the places where the textbook has DELIBERATELY chosen one form over another — that's often where the trap lies.
