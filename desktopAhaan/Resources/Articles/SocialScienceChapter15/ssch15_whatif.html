What If?

From the fading of the Gupta Empire (c.600 CE) to about 1200 CE, India had no single empire but many strong regional kingdoms - a creative, not declining, age.

≈ 6 min · read-mode · 🌏 Social Science

History, geography and society are full of 'what if' turning points. Think about each question yourself first — then open the panel to compare your reasoning.

What if one of the three Tripartite powers had won Kannauj for good?

Think first, then open to compare

A single decisive winner might have rebuilt a large north Indian empire, like the Guptas before. North India might have been more unified and perhaps better able to resist later invaders. Instead, the endless three-way struggle exhausted all three powers and kept the north fragmented, which (the next chapter suggests) left it more open to the Ghaznavid and Ghurid invasions.

What if Brahmagupta had never written down the rules of zero?

Think first, then open to compare

Mathematics everywhere would have developed more slowly. The Indian decimal system with zero, carried through Arabic and Latin translations, became the foundation of modern arithmetic and algebra. Without Brahmagupta's clear rules for zero and negative numbers, the maths used today in science, computing and money might have taken centuries longer to mature.
