Vocabulary Deck

From the fading of the Gupta Empire (c.600 CE) to about 1200 CE, India had no single empire but many strong regional kingdoms - a creative, not declining, age.

These are the chapter's key words, drawn from its own concept list. Knowing the words is what makes you confident in the questions and the exam.

The 11 terms to know

  • Tripartite Struggle · त्रिपक्षीय संघर्ष — The long contest among three powers (Palas, Gurjara-Pratiharas, Rashtrakutas) for control of Kannauj in the 8th-9th centuries.
    Example: No one won the Tripartite Struggle outright.
  • Samanta · सामंत — A subordinate ruler (vassal or tributary) through whom a king governed outer regions.
    Example: The Rashtrakutas were once samantas of the Chalukyas.
  • Monolithic · एकाश्म — Made from a single block of stone or rock.
    Example: The Kailasanatha temple at Ellora is a monolithic temple.
  • Bhakti · भक्ति — Personal, loving devotion to a deity, open to all, rather than only Vedic sacrifice.
    Example: The Alvars expressed bhakti towards Vishnu.
  • Alvars · आळवार — The twelve Tamil poet-saints devoted to Vishnu.
    Example: Andal was the only woman among the Alvars.
  • Nayanars · नायनार — The sixty-three Tamil poet-saints devoted to Shiva.
    Example: Karaikkal Ammaiyar was a woman Nayanar saint.
  • Sabha · सभा — A village assembly; the Chola Uttaramerur inscriptions describe how its members were selected.
    Example: The sabha managed local village affairs.
  • Land grant · भूमि अनुदान — Land given by a king or chief to a person, community or temple, creating a class of landholders.
    Example: Many land grants brought wild land under cultivation.
  • Dvarapandita · द्वारपंडित — A 'scholar gatekeeper' at Vikramashila who tested students before admission.
    Example: Each college had its own dvarapandita.
  • Prashasti · प्रशस्ति — A eulogistic inscription praising a ruler, which historians use carefully because it can exaggerate.
    Example: The Aihole inscription is a prashasti praising Pulakeshin II.
  • Rajatarangini · राजतरंगिणी — Kalhana's 12th-century epic poem recording the history of Kashmir's dynasties - a rare ancient Indian work of formal history.
    Example: The Rajatarangini tells us about King Lalitaditya.
