Common Mistakes

Five errors students often make in this chapter — and the gentle corrections that fix them.

≈ 5 min · honest-with-yourself level

Sanskrit makes precise distinctions that English glosses over — case endings, gender, tense, prefix vs augment. The mistakes below are not personal failings — they are signals that a specific detail of the language needs another look.

Mistake 1 — Saying 'वामम्' for 'on the left' instead of 'वामतः'

What students often write/say: वाम is the adjective 'left'. Students use the accusative.

The correct form: Correct: वामतः ('on the left side', with the तसिल् suffix -तः).

💡 Why

The -तः suffix makes direction adverbs (वामतः, दक्षिणतः, अग्रतः, पृष्ठतः) — distinct from the case forms of वाम. The -तः family means 'in/at/towards that direction'.

Mistake 2 — Confusing पिपासा (thirst) with बुभुक्षा (hunger)

What students often write/say: Both are desiderative nouns; easy to swap.

The correct form: पिपासा is from पा (drink) — wish to drink = thirst. बुभुक्षा from भुज् (eat) — wish to eat = hunger.

💡 Why

The root tells you which: पा-pā in पिपासा means drink; भुज् in बुभुक्षा means eat. Sanskrit's desiderative pattern (X-want-to-do) is one of its great compressions.

Mistake 3 — Saying 'द्राक्षा' (vine) when the chapter's word is 'द्राक्षालता' (vine-creeper)

What students often write/say: द्राक्षा alone is 'grape'.

The correct form: द्राक्षालता = 'grape + creeper' = the climbing vine plant.

💡 Why

Sanskrit often compounds two words where one English word suffices. द्राक्षा (grape, the fruit) + लता (creeper, the plant) = grape-vine — the whole plant, not just the fruit.

Mistake 4 — Treating 'उत्पतति' as past tense

What students often write/say: The उ- might look like an augment.

The correct form: उत्पतति is PRESENT tense ('he leaps up'). Past would be उदपतत् (with the अ- augment).

💡 Why

उत् here is a PREFIX (उपसर्ग) meaning 'up' — not the past-tense augment अ-. Watch for prefixes vs augments: prefixes attach to the root; the augment अ- attaches before the entire stem in past tense.

Mistake 5 — Calling the moral 'work hard at things you cannot have'

What students often write/say: Students may invert the moral.

The correct form: The moral is: do not belittle (call 'sour') what you have failed to get.

💡 Why

The fox's mistake is the EXCUSE, not the failure to jump. Working hard at the unreachable is a different (potentially even noble) story. This fable is specifically about dishonest rationalisation.

📝 Self-check

Re-read the chapter once with these five mistakes in mind. Notice the places where the textbook has DELIBERATELY chosen one form over another — that's often where the trap lies.
