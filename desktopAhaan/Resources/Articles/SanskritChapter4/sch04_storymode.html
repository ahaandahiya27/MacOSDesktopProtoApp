Story Mode — Where the Chapter Shows Up in Real Life

Three short moments from an ordinary kid's life where the chapter's idea suddenly appears.

≈ 6 min · see-it-everywhere level

The chapter lives in the textbook for an hour a week. The IDEA inside the chapter shows up everywhere — at breakfast, on the playground, in a song, in a hospital, on a news bulletin. Here are three small scenes from Aria's life where the chapter walked off the page.

The selection list

Aria's friend was not picked for the school play. Walking home, the friend says: 'The play was going to be boring anyway.' Aria recognises the move — straight from the chapter. She doesn't correct her friend. But she remembers the fox, and she resolves to be honest with herself when SHE doesn't get picked.

On the playground

A younger kid jumps three times for a ball stuck in the tree. Each time, slightly closer. After the third try, he walks away saying 'don't even want that ball.' Aria smiles. The Fox and the Grapes is real. It is happening on her playground.

A bedtime fable

Aria's little brother asks for a story. She tells him the Sanskrit Fox and the Grapes. He likes it. She tells him Aesop also told this story in Greek. He doesn't understand the geography but understands the fox. The same lesson lands in three minutes across two thousand years.
