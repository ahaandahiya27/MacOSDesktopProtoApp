Three "What Ifs?" About Respiration in Organisms

Hypothetical questions with no single right answer. Think hard before you read the discussion.

≈ 7 min read · brain-stretcher level

The textbook tells you what is. The best science teachers also ask what if? Pick any one of the questions below. Pause. Think for at least one minute before scrolling. Then read the discussion.

What if 1 — What if you held your breath as long as you could?

🧠 Think first

Pause. Write down your one-sentence guess before reading.

One discussion path: Most people can hold their breath 30-60 seconds; trained free-divers reach 5+ minutes. As CO₂ builds up in your blood, your brain stem (which doesn't need conscious permission) sends emergency 'breathe NOW' signals. If you ignore them, you eventually pass out — at which point your body breathes automatically. You can't kill yourself by breath-holding alone — your unconscious nervous system overrides any voluntary refusal. Your conscious self has limited authority over your respiratory system.

What if 2 — What if there were no plants on Earth (just animals)?

🧠 Think first

Pause. Write down your one-sentence guess before reading.

One discussion path: Atmospheric oxygen would slowly decline (currently ~21%, would drop within centuries to mostly CO₂). Within a few generations after that, animal respiration would fail — not enough O₂. Even more immediately, without plants, there's no FOOD for herbivores, who feed carnivores. The food chain collapses. Even with all the oxygen in the world, you can't eat air. Plants are doubly essential: they made the oxygen AND the food. Earth without them is dead within decades.

What if 3 — What if you could respire underwater like a fish?

🧠 Think first

Pause. Write down your one-sentence guess before reading.

One discussion path: You'd need gills with enough surface area to extract dissolved oxygen — water has only ~5% the oxygen of air per volume. So your gills would need to be ~10x larger surface area than your current lungs. You'd also need to expel CO₂ through them, which fish do automatically. The energy cost of pumping water through giant gills 24/7 would mean a much higher metabolic rate. Practical for a fish; awkward for a human-sized creature. Aquatic mammals (dolphins, whales) solved this differently — they kept lungs but learned to hold breath long.

💬 How to use this

These are not exam questions. The answer marked ‘one discussion path’ is one valid line of reasoning — your own may be different and still good. Practising this kind of thinking is how scientists become scientists.
