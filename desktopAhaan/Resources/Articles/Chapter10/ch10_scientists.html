One Curious Person Behind Chapter 10

The scientist most tied to respiration in organisms — their question, their experiment, and what changed because of them.

≈ 4 min read · biography

Every chapter has a person behind it — someone who first asked the right question, ran the right experiment, or wrote down the answer. This is a one-page portrait of the scientist most tied to this chapter's content.

Yellapragada Subbarao

1895–1948 · Indian (worked in USA)

🎧 In one line

Discovered ATP as the energy molecule of all life — the chemical respiration produces in every cell.

Yellapragada Subbarao was born in Bhimavaram, Andhra Pradesh, in 1895. He almost didn't make it through medical school due to malnutrition and disease. In 1923 he sailed to Boston for graduate study at Harvard Medical School. There, in his 30s, he made discoveries that would feed the entire 20th-century revolution in biochemistry. In 1929, he and Cyrus Fiske isolated ADENOSINE TRIPHOSPHATE (ATP) — the small molecule that respiration produces and that all life uses as energy currency. Every contraction of your heart, every spark of your neuron, every step of muscle — runs on ATP. Without Subbarao's discovery, modern biology would be silent on the fundamental question: what IS energy in a cell? Yet because he wasn't an American citizen, Subbarao was denied tenure at Harvard. He moved to industry, where he then helped develop methotrexate (the first effective cancer chemotherapy), tetracycline (the antibiotic that saved millions), and aureomycin. He died at 53, largely unknown. Today the cellular respiration chapter you'll meet in Class 10 and 11 — its central character is ATP, and behind ATP stands a Telugu boy who made it visible.

Knowing the human behind a discovery makes it easier to remember the discovery itself. Try to summarise this scientist's contribution in one sentence, in your own words.
