Question 2 — from Chapter 10

Why do we breathe faster when we run hard?

🧠 Answer in your head first

Don't peek. Speak the answer out loud, then check.

Answer: Hard exercise needs more energy, so cells need more oxygen and produce more CO₂; we breathe faster to bring in more O₂ and clear out CO₂.

Why: Muscles burn through ATP fast during exercise.

Question 4 — from Chapter 10

A pearl diver in Tamil Nadu can free-dive to 20m for 90 seconds. Compare what happens to their lungs at depth vs. surface, and why coming up too fast can cause 'the bends'.

🧠 Answer in your head first

Don't peek. Speak the answer out loud, then check.

Answer: At depth: water pressure squeezes the diver's lungs to ~1/3 their surface size (Boyle's law: pressure × volume = constant; pressure at 20m ≈ 3 atm). Gas in lungs is now compressed; oxygen is more concentrated, easier to absorb. Body absorbs extra nitrogen too. Coming up TOO FAST: the dissolved nitrogen comes out of solution as bubbles in the blood (like opening a soda bottle) — bubbles block blood vessels, cause joint pain, paralysis, death. Solution: ascend slowly (30 ft/min) to let nitrogen diffuse out gradually. Free divers don't usually get the bends because they're at depth too briefly to absorb much nitrogen. Scuba divers must follow ascent tables.

Why: At depth: pressure compresses lungs (Boyle's law).
