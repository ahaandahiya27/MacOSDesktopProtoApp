Respiration in Organisms

Chapter 10 · Class 7 Science

Why every living thing needs to breathe

Food is fuel, but fuel needs oxygen to release its energy. Respiration is the chemical process inside every living cell that combines glucose and oxygen to produce energy, water, and carbon dioxide.

Word equation: Glucose + Oxygen → Energy + Carbon dioxide + Water

Aerobic vs anaerobic respiration

Most respiration uses oxygen — that's aerobic. But some cells can release energy without oxygen — that's anaerobic.

  • Yeast ferments sugar without oxygen → alcohol + CO₂ (used in bread and brewing).
  • Tired muscles during a sprint switch briefly to anaerobic → lactic acid → cramps.

How humans breathe

Your diaphragm is a sheet of muscle below your lungs. When it contracts, your chest expands and air rushes in. When it relaxes, air pushes out. Each breath delivers oxygen to your blood and removes CO₂.

Inhaled air: ~21% O₂, 0.04% CO₂.
Exhaled air: ~16% O₂, ~4% CO₂. That's why breathing into limewater turns it milky — calcium carbonate forms.

Other species, other tricks

  • Fish — gills extract dissolved oxygen from water.
  • Cockroaches and other insects — tiny tubes (tracheae) carry air directly to every cell through holes called spiracles.
  • Earthworms — oxygen dissolves through their moist skin.
  • Frogs — lungs on land, moist skin in water.
  • Plants — leaves breathe through tiny pores called stomata.

Breathing rate

At rest, a child takes ~16 breaths per minute. While sprinting, it can climb past 50. After exercise, you keep breathing deeply for a while to repay an oxygen debt built up during anaerobic effort.
