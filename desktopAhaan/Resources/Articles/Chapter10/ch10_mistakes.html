Five Wrong Answers Class 7 Students Give

The five places almost every Class 7 student trips up on Chapter 10 — and the one-line fix for each.

≈ 5 min read · revision-tier

Examiners say the same wrong answers come back year after year. None of these mistakes are silly — they are almost right. That is exactly what makes them dangerous. Read each one, cover the answer, and try to spot what is off before peeking.

1. “Breathing and respiration are the same.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Breathing is the mechanical act of moving air in and out of lungs. Respiration is the chemical process happening in every cell where glucose is burned with oxygen to release energy. You breathe in your lungs; you respire in every cell. A plant that doesn't 'breathe' (in our sense) still respires — every cell uses oxygen to release energy.

2. “Plants only photosynthesise, not respire.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Plants do BOTH. They photosynthesise during the day (sunlight + CO₂ + water → glucose + O₂). They respire 24/7 (glucose + O₂ → CO₂ + water + energy). In the day, photosynthesis greatly exceeds respiration, so plants are net oxygen producers. At night, only respiration happens — they are net CO₂ producers. This is why sleeping under a tree was traditionally discouraged in Indian villages.

3. “We only inhale oxygen.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: We inhale air, which is ~78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen, 1% argon + traces. Of the 21% oxygen, our lungs extract only about 5%; we exhale air with ~16% oxygen still in it. That's why mouth-to-mouth resuscitation works — exhaled air still has plenty of oxygen for a non-breathing person.

4. “Lungs pump air in and out by themselves.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Lungs are passive — they don't pump. The diaphragm (a sheet of muscle below the lungs) and rib muscles do all the work. Diaphragm contracts → lungs expand → air flows in (inhalation). Diaphragm relaxes → lungs shrink → air flows out (exhalation). It's mechanical — like an accordion that breathes for you. Yogic pranayama practises conscious control of this mechanism.

5. “Fish breathe water.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Fish don't breathe water — they extract dissolved oxygen from water using gills. Water passes over thin gill filaments rich in blood. Oxygen diffuses out of water, into the blood. The fish then exhales water out the back of the gills. Gills are like upside-down lungs — for water instead of air. Why don't gills work on land? Without water support, gill filaments collapse on each other, losing all the surface area that made them effective.

📝 Stretch your thinking

Pick any two of these and explain them to a younger sibling using a one-line analogy each. If you can compress a mistake into one sentence with one image, you have understood it.
