Respiration Across Species

Topic t03 of Chapter 10 · Respiration in Organisms · Class 7 Science

The big idea

Fish use gills — feathery folds extract oxygen dissolved in water. Insects bypass blood entirely: tiny tubes (tracheae) carry air through holes (spiracles) straight to every cell. Earthworms breathe through moist skin. Frogs use lungs on land and skin underwater. Plants use stomata — tiny mouths on leaf undersides.

Different Solutions to the Same Problem. Every animal you see has solved the 'get oxygen in, get CO₂ out' problem differently — a fantastic case of evolution converging on the same biological need.
