How Humans Breathe

Topic t01 of Chapter 10 · Respiration in Organisms · Class 7 Science

The big idea

Air enters the nose, travels down the trachea, branches into bronchi, and ends in millions of tiny air sacs (alveoli) in the lungs. The diaphragm — a sheet of muscle below the lungs — contracts to pull air in and relaxes to push it out. At the alveoli, oxygen passes into the blood and carbon dioxide passes out.

Lungs, Diaphragm, Gas Exchange. Inhaled air is about 21% O₂ / 0.04% CO₂; exhaled is about 16% O₂ / 4% CO₂. That's why breathing into limewater turns it milky — calcium carbonate forms.
