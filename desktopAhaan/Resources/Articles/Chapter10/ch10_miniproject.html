Two Mini Projects for Chapter 10

Hands-on activities you can do today — with materials from the kitchen, not the lab. Each project takes about an hour and turns chapter ideas into something you can see and touch.

≈ 60 min total · hands-on

Reading is not the same as doing. These mini projects ask you to use your hands — predict, observe, and write down what surprises you. Try at least one before the next chapter test.

🍞 Yeast balloon — see anaerobic respiration

~60 min · one-sitting project

What you'll need

  • A packet of dry yeast (5 g)
  • A glass bottle with narrow neck
  • A balloon
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 1 cup warm (not hot) water

Steps

  1. Pour warm water into the bottle.
  2. Add the yeast and sugar. Shake gently.
  3. Stretch the balloon over the bottle's mouth.
  4. Place in a warm spot.
  5. Wait 30 minutes — observe.

👀 What you should see

The balloon slowly inflates over 30-60 minutes. The bottle smells faintly of bread / beer.

Why it works: Yeast is a single-celled fungus that performs anaerobic respiration. It eats sugar, producing CO₂ + alcohol + a little energy. The CO₂ gas can't escape through the balloon, so it inflates the balloon. The alcohol creates the smell. This is exactly what happens in idli batter, dosa fermentation, beer brewing, and bread rising. Anaerobic respiration in a balloon.

🌱 Germinating seed lime-water test

~240 min · one-sitting project

What you'll need

  • A glass jar with lid
  • A handful of moong / chana seeds soaked overnight
  • Lime water (calcium hydroxide solution from the chemist) OR fresh limewater (mix slaked lime with water, let settle, decant clear liquid)
  • An identical empty jar as control

Steps

  1. Place soaked seeds at the bottom of jar 1. Leave jar 2 empty.
  2. Close lids on both jars tightly. Place in a warm dark spot.
  3. After 4 hours, pour a small amount of lime water into each jar.
  4. Shake gently and observe.

👀 What you should see

Lime water in jar 1 (seeds) turns milky white. Lime water in jar 2 (empty) stays clear.

Why it works: Germinating seeds respire — using O₂ and releasing CO₂. The CO₂ accumulates in the closed jar. Lime water + CO₂ → calcium carbonate (CaCO₃) — chalky white precipitate. This is the classic 'plants respire' demonstration. Beauty of it: the seeds don't have green leaves yet (so not photosynthesising), so the CO₂ is unambiguously from respiration.

📝 How to use this

Before you start, predict what will happen. Write your prediction down. Then do the project. Compare. If your prediction was wrong, write down why — that's where real learning happens.
