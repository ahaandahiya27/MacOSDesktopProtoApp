Glucose to ATP — The Energy Coin of Life

How food becomes usable energy in every cell · Class 7 Science

Why ATP?

You can't burn food directly in your cells — the energy released would scorch the cell. Instead, your cells slowly break glucose down and pack the energy into a tiny molecule called ATP (adenosine triphosphate). ATP is the 'energy coin' that every chemical reaction in your body spends.

Aerobic respiration — the efficient route

With enough oxygen: Glucose + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O + 38 ATP. This happens inside the mitochondria. Each glucose molecule generates ~38 ATP coins. Sufficient for everything from running to thinking.

Anaerobic respiration — the emergency route

When muscles run out of oxygen during a sprint, they switch to anaerobic respiration: Glucose → Lactic acid + 2 ATP. Only 2 ATP per glucose — far less efficient — but it works without oxygen for a short burst. Lactic acid build-up causes muscle cramps.

Yeast and brewing

Yeast does anaerobic respiration differently: Glucose → Ethanol + CO₂ + 2 ATP. The CO₂ raises bread dough; the ethanol flavours beer and wine. Same biochemistry, different end product.

An adult body produces and uses about 50 kg of ATP every day. Each ATP molecule is used and remade about 1000 times per day — so the body only holds about 50 g of ATP at any moment.

Try this at home

Hold your breath and squeeze a tennis ball repeatedly. Within 30 seconds your forearm burns — that's lactic acid from anaerobic respiration. Breathe out and rest 30 seconds; the burn fades as oxygen returns.

Looking ahead

Class 11 Biology has a whole chapter on cellular respiration, glycolysis, Krebs cycle, and electron transport chain. NEET asks ~3 questions on respiration every year. Class 12 Biotechnology applies these pathways to ethanol production and biofuels.
