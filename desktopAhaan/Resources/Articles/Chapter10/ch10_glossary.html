The 10 terms to know

  • Respiration · श्वसन — The chemical process inside cells where glucose is broken down to release energy.
    Example: Every cell of your body respires every second to generate ATP — the energy currency.
  • Breathing · साँस लेना — The mechanical act of moving air in and out of the lungs.
    Example: An adult breathes ~16 times per minute at rest.
  • Aerobic respiration · वायवीय श्वसन — Respiration that uses oxygen; produces CO₂, water, and lots of energy.
    Example: Your normal sitting / walking energy comes from aerobic respiration.
  • Anaerobic respiration · अवायवीय श्वसन — Respiration without oxygen; produces lactic acid (in animals) or alcohol (in yeast) and a little energy.
    Example: Sprinting muscles when oxygen runs out; yeast in idli batter.
  • Alveoli · वायुकोष्ठिका — Microscopic balloon-like sacs at the end of bronchioles where gas exchange happens.
    Example: Your two lungs contain ~600 million alveoli with total surface area of a tennis court.
  • Diaphragm · मध्यपट — A sheet of muscle below the lungs that contracts to expand the chest cavity and pull air in.
    Example: Hiccups are spasms of the diaphragm.
  • Gills · गलफड़े — Feathery structures that extract dissolved oxygen from water; the equivalent of lungs in fish.
    Example: A fish gulps water; its gills filter oxygen and release CO₂ back into the water.
  • Spiracles · श्वासरंध्र — Small openings on the sides of insect bodies through which air enters their trachea network.
    Example: A cockroach has 10 pairs of spiracles along its body.
  • Oxygen debt · ऑक्सीजन ऋण — The extra oxygen needed after exercise to clear lactic acid and restore normal cell chemistry.
    Example: A sprinter pants for 2 minutes after running 100m — that's paying back oxygen debt.
  • Stomata · रंध्र — Tiny pores on the underside of leaves through which plants exchange gases — the plant equivalent of mouth and lungs combined.
    Example: Plant stomata open during the day for photosynthesis; partially close at night.
