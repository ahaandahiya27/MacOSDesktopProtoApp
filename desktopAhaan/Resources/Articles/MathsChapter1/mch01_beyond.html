Beyond the Book — How Big Is Big?

Four ideas about large numbers that don't fit in the textbook but make the lakhs and crores feel real.

≈ 6 min read · curious-kid level

The chapter teaches you to write and read large numbers. This article asks a different question: how big are they really? Read one idea at a time and try the questions out loud.

1. The ladder of Indian number names

After the thousand, the Indian system adds a new name every time the number grows 100 times: thousand → lakh (1,00,000) → crore (1,00,00,000) → arab → kharab. That is why the commas fall in a 3–2–2–2 rhythm — each comma marks the next name up the ladder.

So one lakh is 105 (a 1 with five zeros) and one crore is 107 (a 1 with seven zeros). A crore is one hundred lakhs.

💡 Try this

Write your age in seconds. A year is about 3.15 crore seconds — so a 12-year-old has lived roughly 38 crore seconds. Bigger than it feels, isn't it?

2. How long is a crore of seconds?

Numbers are easier to feel as time. One lakh of seconds is just over a day (about 27 hours). One crore of seconds — a hundred times more — is about 115 days, nearly four months. And a crore of days would be over 27,000 years.

That is the power of place value: each step left multiplies by ten, and ten steps turns a heartbeat into a lifetime.

3. The number named by a nine-year-old

In 1920 the mathematician Edward Kasner asked his young nephew, Milton Sirotta, to invent a name for a 1 followed by a hundred zeros. The boy said “googol.” That is 10100 — far more than the number of atoms in the entire observable universe.

Years later, the founders of a search engine wanted to name it after that huge number — but spelled it wrong. That typo is why we type into Google, not Googol.

4. Same number, two sets of commas

The Indian and the international systems describe the exact same numbers — they just group the digits differently. India groups 3–2–2 (thousand, lakh, crore); the international system groups in threes (thousand, million, billion).

So 1,00,00,000 (one crore) and 10,000,000 (ten million) are the very same quantity. One billion equals one hundred crore. The digits never change — only where you pause and what you call it.

💡 Try this

Take any 8-digit number — say the population of a big city. Write it with Indian commas, then with international commas. Read each one aloud. Same number, two rhythms.
