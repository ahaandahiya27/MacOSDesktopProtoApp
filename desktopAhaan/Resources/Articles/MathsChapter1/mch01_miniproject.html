🧪 Mini Project — Lakh and Crore Around Me

A hands-on activity that turns this chapter into something you actually do, not just read.

≈ 60 min · do-it-yourself level

Reading about maths and doing maths are two different things. This short project lets you see the ideas of "Large Numbers Around Us" at work with your own hands.

What you'll need

  • A notebook
  • Internet or local newspapers
  • A family member to discuss with

Steps

  1. List five categories where lakh/crore numbers naturally appear (population of cities, property prices, stadium capacities, government budgets, large-business turnovers).
  2. Find ONE concrete number for each category — from a newspaper, a Wikipedia article, or your family’s own knowledge.
  3. Write each number twice: once in the Indian system with commas (3-2-2-2), and once in the American system with commas (3-3-3).
  4. Read each number aloud in both systems.
  5. Discuss with a family member: which system feels more natural for that quantity?

🔍 What you should notice

After doing this, you’ll notice that money and population usually feel natural in lakh/crore (Indian convention), while scientific and international quantities often feel natural in million/billion.

Why it works

Conventions exist because they match how people in a culture USE numbers. The closer a unit is to the typical scale of conversation, the easier it is to speak about. Both systems are mathematically equivalent — the choice is about expressive convenience.
