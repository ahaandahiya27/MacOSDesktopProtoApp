Common Mistakes

Five errors students often make in this chapter — and the gentle corrections that fix them.

≈ 5 min · honest-with-yourself level

Sanskrit makes precise distinctions that English glosses over — case endings, gender, tense, prefix vs augment. The mistakes below are not personal failings — they are signals that a specific detail of the language needs another look.

Mistake 1 — Reading 'द्वीप' as 'two-island'

What students often write/say: द्वि = two; द्वीप looks composite.

The correct form: द्वीप = द्वि (two) + अप् (water) = land surrounded by water on BOTH sides → island.

💡 Why

Sanskrit द्वीप literally means 'two-watered', but it names an ISLAND (one piece of land, water around). Same logic as Hindi द्वीप and English peninsula (almost-island).

Mistake 2 — Translating 'जनजाति' as 'common people'

What students often write/say: जन = people. जाति = caste/class.

The correct form: जनजाति = tribal community (an officially recognised indigenous group).

💡 Why

जनजाति has a precise legal meaning in modern India (Scheduled Tribe). It is NOT the same as 'common people' (आम जन / सामान्यजन). The chapter respects the legal/cultural specificity.

Mistake 3 — Saying 'कारागार' means 'school'

What students often write/say: Looks vaguely educational.

The correct form: कारागार = PRISON (कारा = cell + गार = house).

💡 Why

Don't confuse कारागार (prison) with कारखाना (factory, in Hindi) or विद्यागार (rare for library). The chapter's context — Veer Savarkar's ten years — makes clear it is a prison.

Mistake 4 — Conflating सेल्युलर (Cellular) with सेलू-लर

What students often write/say: The English loanword may get Sanskritised oddly.

The correct form: 'Cellular' here is a direct English adoption for the prison's design (each prisoner in a separate cell to prevent communication). Sanskrit treats it as a foreign word.

💡 Why

The Sanskrit version retains the English name 'Cellular' for the British architectural design — it has no Sanskrit-native synonym for that specific concept (the panopticon-like cell-by-cell layout).

Mistake 5 — Calling कालापानी 'black water' as a body of water

What students often write/say: Literally 'black' + 'water'.

The correct form: कालापानी is a NAME for the Cellular Jail / the Andamans, NOT a sea or river.

💡 Why

The metaphor: being shipped 'over the black waters' meant permanent exile from one's homeland. The name became the name of the prison itself. Translating it literally misses the cultural meaning.

📝 Self-check

Re-read the chapter once with these five mistakes in mind. Notice the places where the textbook has DELIBERATELY chosen one form over another — that's often where the trap lies.
