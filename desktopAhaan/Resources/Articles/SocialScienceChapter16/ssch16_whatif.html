History, geography and society are full of 'what if' turning points. Think about each question yourself first — then open the panel to compare your reasoning.

What if Prithviraj Chauhan had won the Second Battle of Tarain in 1192?

Think first, then open to compare

If Prithviraj had won again, Muhammad Ghuri's push into the Gangetic plains might have been stopped, and the founding of the Delhi Sultanate could have been delayed or taken a very different path. North India's politics might have stayed in the hands of Rajput and other Indian dynasties for longer. But the Ghurids could also have returned with yet another army, as they did after 1191 - so the long-term outcome is hard to predict. It shows how a single battle can become a hinge of history.

What if Nalanda and Vikramashila had not been destroyed?

Think first, then open to compare

If these great universities had survived, India's tradition of Buddhist learning - with its vast libraries, thousands of scholars and links to Tibet and Southeast Asia - might have continued for centuries longer, and Buddhism might not have declined so sharply in India. The world might have kept many manuscripts and ideas that were lost. It shows how the destruction of centres of knowledge can change the course of a whole civilisation's intellectual life.

What if Rajendra Chola had not built a strong navy?

Think first, then open to compare

Rajendra Chola's navy let the Cholas project power across the seas — raiding and trading as far as Southeast Asia — making them one of the few Indian empires to become a maritime power. Without that fleet, Chola influence would have stayed on land, and the rich sea-trade with the lands around the Bay of Bengal might have passed them by. Control of the seas, not just the land, made the Cholas exceptional.
