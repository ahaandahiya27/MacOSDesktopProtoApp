Q1. How did Mahmud of Ghazni differ from Muhammad Ghuri? (NCERT page 101)

Mahmud of Ghazni raided India about 17 times for plunder, destroying temples and carrying off booty, but he withdrew each time and did not seek to rule. Muhammad Ghuri sought territorial conquest: after defeating Prithviraj Chauhan at the Second Battle of Tarain (1192), he captured Delhi and left generals like Qutb-ud-din Aibak to hold the land, beginning lasting foreign rule.

Q2. Who was al-Biruni and why is he important? (NCERT page 104)

Al-Biruni was a Persian polymath who came to India with Mahmud around 1017. He learned Sanskrit, studied Indian texts and wrote an encyclopaedic survey of Indian religion, philosophy and science, translating the Yogasutras into Arabic. He is important as a careful, fair source who also honestly recorded the damage Mahmud's raids did to Indian learning.

Q3. Why did Rajendra Chola I send a navy against the Srivijaya Empire? (NCERT page 111)

Because Srivijaya controlled the Strait of Malacca, a key sea route for the Cholas' rich trade with China. To protect this trade, Rajendra - helped by merchant guilds - sent his navy across the Bay of Bengal, defeated Srivijaya and captured its capital, but did not occupy it; it was a punitive expedition.

Q4. What were the two Battles of Tarain and their outcomes? (NCERT page 115)

In 1191, Prithviraj Chauhan defeated Muhammad Ghuri at the First Battle of Tarain. In 1192, Ghuri returned with a larger army and won the Second Battle of Tarain, defeating and executing Prithviraj - a decisive turning point that opened north India to Ghurid rule.

Q5. Name one major change and one major continuity of this period. (NCERT page 121)

A major change was the deep Turkic invasions and the start of foreign rule in north India, with much destruction and Islam entering India's religious landscape. A major continuity was that south India and much of the north stayed free, while trade, art, literature, science and temple-building continued to flourish - the chapter's lesson being resilience.
