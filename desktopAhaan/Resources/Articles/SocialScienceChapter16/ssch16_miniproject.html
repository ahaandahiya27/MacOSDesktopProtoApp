⚔️ Build a timeline of the turning point (about 40 min)

What you need

  • A long strip of paper
  • Coloured pens
  • The chapter's dates

Steps

  1. Draw a line from 985 to 1206.
  2. Mark the invasions: Anandapala's defeat (1008), Mahmud's Somnath raid (1025), Tarain I (1191), Tarain II (1192), Bakhtiyar Khilji in the east (1205-06).
  3. On the other side of the line, mark the flourishing: Rajaraja Chola (985-1014), Rajendra's Srivijaya expedition, Bhaskaracharya (b.1114), Angkor Wat (12th c.).
  4. Use one colour for invasions and another for achievements.

👀 What you should see

You will see invasions and achievements happening side by side, which is exactly the chapter's point about change and continuity.

🧠 Why it works

Putting both kinds of event on one timeline makes the balance of destruction and resilience visible at a glance.

📜 Two invaders, one comparison chart (about 35 min)

What you need

  • A notebook or chart paper
  • A ruler

Steps

  1. Make a two-column chart: 'Mahmud of Ghazni' and 'Muhammad Ghuri'.
  2. For each, fill in: where they came from, what they wanted (plunder or rule), a key battle or raid, and what they left behind.
  3. Add one famous person linked to each period (al-Biruni for Mahmud; Qutb-ud-din Aibak for Ghuri).
  4. Write one sentence on why Ghuri marked a bigger turning point.

👀 What you should see

You will see clearly that Mahmud raided and left, while Ghuri conquered and stayed, beginning lasting foreign rule.

🧠 Why it works

A side-by-side chart turns an easily-confused pair into a clear contrast you will remember.
