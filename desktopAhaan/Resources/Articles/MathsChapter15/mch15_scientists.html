Minds Behind Finding the Unknown

Real mathematicians whose work lies underneath this chapter.

≈ 7 min read · for curious 12-year-olds

Mathematics was built by real people, often centuries apart, each adding one idea. Here are the minds behind the ideas in this chapter.

Brahmagupta (598 – 668 CE)

Indian. Gave the first general method for solving linear and quadratic equations, and rules for zero and negative numbers.

Brahmagupta, working in 7th-century Rajasthan, was among the first mathematicians anywhere to treat equations with general methods rather than case-by-case tricks. In his Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta he stated rules for solving linear equations, handled negative quantities (debts) and zero as numbers, and gave a general solution to quadratic equations centuries before such methods reached Europe. The very idea of an 'unknown' to be found by systematic operations — the heart of this chapter — owes much to the Indian algebraic tradition he helped found. The word 'algebra' itself comes later from the Arabic al-jabr, but its roots reach back to scholars like Brahmagupta and, later, Bhāskara.
