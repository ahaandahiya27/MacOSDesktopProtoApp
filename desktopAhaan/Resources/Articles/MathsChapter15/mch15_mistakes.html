Common Mistakes in Finding the Unknown

The places students slip on this chapter — and the one-line fix for each.

&#8776; 5 min read &middot; revision-tier &middot; 5 fixes

These wrong answers come back year after year. None are silly — they are almost right, which is what makes them worth knowing. Read each, then cover the fix and try to spot what is off.

1. “You can change just one side of an equation.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: You must do the SAME operation to BOTH sides, or the balance (equality) breaks.

2. “To undo '+8' you add 8.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: You SUBTRACT 8 — the inverse of addition is subtraction. To undo +8, subtract 8 from both sides.

3. “The order of undoing operations doesn't matter.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: Peel the OUTERMOST operation first: undo the +/− around the variable before the ×/÷ that multiplies it.

4. “Trial and error is the best way to solve equations.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: It works but is slow and can miss non-whole answers (like x = 2.2). The systematic inverse-operations method is reliable and fast.

5. “Once you get an answer you're done.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: Always SUBSTITUTE it back to check both sides match — it catches almost every mistake.
