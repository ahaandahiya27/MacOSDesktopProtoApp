Finding the Unknown: Worked NCERT Questions

Textbook questions from this chapter, each with a clear worked answer.

These are the kind of questions the NCERT chapter asks. Try each one yourself first, then check the worked answer.

Q1. A balanced scale has a sack of weight w plus 5 kg on the left and 12 kg on the right. Write and solve the equation.

Answer: w + 5 = 12. Remove 5 kg from both pans (subtract 5 from both sides): w = 7 kg.

Q2. Solve 2n + 1 = 99 by trial and error, then check.

Answer: Testing values: n=40 gives 81, n=50 gives 101, n=49 gives 99. So n = 49. Trial and error works but is slow.

Q3. If LHS = RHS, will the equation still hold after adding the same number to both sides?

Answer: Yes. Since both sides have the same value, performing the same operation (adding, subtracting, multiplying, or dividing by the same number) on both keeps them equal.

Q4. Solve 2n + 1 = 99 systematically.

Answer: Subtract 1 from both sides: 2n = 98. Divide both sides by 2: n = 49. Check: 2×49+1 = 99.

Q5. Solve 5x − 4 = 7.

Answer: Add 4 to both sides: 5x = 11. Divide by 5: x = 11/5 = 2.2. Check: 5×2.2 − 4 = 7.

Q6. Why is doing the same operation to both sides justified?

Answer: Because the two sides are equal (like a balanced scale). Doing the same thing to equal quantities keeps them equal — exactly like removing equal weights from both pans of a balanced scale.

Q7. A student writes 'x + 8 = 20, so x = 20 + 8 = 28.' Mend the mistake.

Answer: To undo +8, you SUBTRACT 8, not add it: x = 20 − 8 = 12. Check: 12 + 8 = 20. The error was using the wrong inverse operation.

Q8. Solve 3x − 5 = 16 and verify your answer.

Answer: Add 5: 3x = 21. Divide by 3: x = 7. Verify: 3×7 − 5 = 21 − 5 = 16 = RHS. ✓
