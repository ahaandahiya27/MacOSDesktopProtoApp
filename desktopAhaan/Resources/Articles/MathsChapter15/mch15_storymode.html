Story Mode — Finding the Unknown in Real Life

The same ideas from this chapter, but out in the world — in shops, on playgrounds, and in everyday choices.

≈ 6 min · see-it-everywhere level

Maths can feel like it lives only inside the textbook. It doesn't. Here are real situations where the ideas of "Finding the Unknown" quietly do the work.

Working back from a total

If 3 identical items plus ₹20 delivery cost ₹110, the equation 3x + 20 = 110 finds each item's price: subtract 20 (3x = 90), divide by 3 (x = ₹30). Every 'I know the total, what was each part?' question is an equation waiting to be solved.

The balance scale of algebra

The equals sign was invented in 1557 by Robert Recorde, who chose two parallel lines 'because no two things can be more equal'. Seeing an equation as a balanced scale — keep both sides equal — is the mental shift that turns arithmetic into algebra.

Self-checking with substitution

Engineers and scientists rearrange formulas constantly, then plug in test values to confirm the rearrangement is correct. The same substitution check you use on 3x − 5 = 16 guards multi-step calculations in the real world against costly errors.
