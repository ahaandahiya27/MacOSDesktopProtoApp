Finding the Unknown: Key Words

Every term in this chapter, with a one-line meaning and an example.

&#8776; quick reference &middot; 10 terms

Knowing the exact words makes the maths click. Here is every key term from the chapter, defined plainly with an example you can picture.

Equation

A statement that two expressions are equal, usually containing an unknown.

Example: 2n + 1 = 99.

In Hindi: समीकरण

Unknown (variable)

The letter-number whose value you are trying to find.

Example: n in 2n + 1 = 99.

In Hindi: अज्ञात

LHS / RHS

Left-Hand Side and Right-Hand Side of an equation.

Example: In 2n+1 = 99, LHS = 2n+1, RHS = 99.

Solution (root)

The value of the unknown that makes both sides equal.

Example: n = 49 solves 2n+1 = 99.

In Hindi: हल

Inverse operation

The operation that undoes another: subtraction undoes addition, division undoes multiplication.

Example: To undo +1, subtract 1.

Balance rule

Doing the same operation to both sides of an equation keeps it true.

Example: Subtract 1 from both sides of 2n+1 = 99.

Trial and error

Finding a solution by testing values until both sides match.

Example: Trying n = 40, 49 for 2n+1 = 99.

Substitution (checking)

Putting the solution back into the equation to confirm both sides are equal.

Example: 2×49+1 = 99 confirms n = 49.

In Hindi: प्रतिस्थापन

Isolate the unknown

To get the unknown alone on one side of the equation.

Example: From 2n+1 = 99 to n = 49.

Linear equation

An equation where the unknown appears only to the first power.

Example: 3x − 5 = 16.
