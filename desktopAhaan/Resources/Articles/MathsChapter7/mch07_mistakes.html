Common Mistakes in A Tale of Three Intersecting Lines

The places students slip on this chapter — and the one-line fix for each.

&#8776; 5 min read &middot; revision-tier &middot; 5 fixes

These wrong answers come back year after year. None are silly — they are almost right, which is what makes them worth knowing. Read each, then cover the fix and try to spot what is off.

1. “Any three lengths can form a triangle.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: Only if the sum of every two exceeds the third (triangle inequality). 10, 15, 30 cannot, since 10+15 < 30.

2. “An altitude is one of the sides of the triangle.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: An altitude is the PERPENDICULAR from a vertex to the opposite side — usually not a side itself (except in a right triangle).

3. “A triangle is acute if it has an acute angle.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: Every triangle has at least two acute angles. 'Acute' means ALL THREE angles are under 90°.

4. “A triangle can have two right angles.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: Impossible — two 90° angles already total 180°, leaving nothing for the third. At most one angle can be 90° or more.

5. “Three points always make a triangle.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: Three points that lie on one straight line (collinear) do NOT form a triangle — the sides flatten and enclose no area.
