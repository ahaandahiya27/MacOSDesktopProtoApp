A Tale of Three Intersecting Lines: Key Words

Every term in this chapter, with a one-line meaning and an example.

&#8776; quick reference &middot; 10 terms

Knowing the exact words makes the maths click. Here is every key term from the chapter, defined plainly with an example you can picture.

Triangle

A closed shape with three vertices, three sides and three angles.

Example: ∆ABC.

In Hindi: त्रिभुज

Vertex

A corner point of a triangle where two sides meet.

Example: A, B and C in ∆ABC.

In Hindi: शीर्ष

Triangle inequality

The rule that the sum of any two sides of a triangle must exceed the third side.

Example: 3+4>5 holds; 10+15>30 fails.

Altitude

The perpendicular segment from a vertex to the opposite side (the triangle's height).

Example: The drop from A to side BC.

In Hindi: शीर्षलंब

Equilateral triangle

A triangle with all three sides (and all three angles) equal.

Example: A 6-6-6 triangle, all angles 60°.

In Hindi: समबाहु त्रिभुज

Isosceles triangle

A triangle with exactly two sides equal.

Example: A 5-5-8 triangle.

In Hindi: समद्विबाहु त्रिभुज

Scalene triangle

A triangle with all three sides of different lengths.

Example: A 3-4-5 triangle.

In Hindi: विषमबाहु त्रिभुज

Acute triangle

A triangle in which all three angles are less than 90°.

Example: A 40-60-80 triangle.

Right triangle

A triangle with exactly one 90° angle.

Example: A 3-4-5 triangle has a right angle.

In Hindi: समकोण त्रिभुज

Obtuse triangle

A triangle with one angle greater than 90°.

Example: A triangle with a 120° angle.
