📐 Mini Project — Triangle or Not? The Straw Test

A hands-on activity that turns this chapter into something you actually do, not just read.

≈ 40 min · do-it-yourself level

Reading about maths and doing maths are two different things. This short project lets you see the ideas of "A Tale of Three Intersecting Lines" at work with your own hands.

What you'll need

  • Drinking straws or sticks
  • Scissors
  • A ruler

Steps

  1. Cut straws to these length-sets (in cm): {3,4,5}, {10,15,30}, {6,6,6}, {2,2,5}, {7,8,9}.
  2. For each set, predict using the triangle inequality whether a triangle can be made (do the two shortest beat the longest?).
  3. Now actually try to join each set end-to-end into a triangle.
  4. Record which sets close into a triangle and which collapse flat or won't meet.
  5. For the ones that work, classify each triangle by sides (equilateral / isosceles / scalene).

🔍 What you should notice

The sets that satisfy the triangle inequality ({3,4,5}, {6,6,6}, {7,8,9}) close into triangles; {10,15,30} and {2,2,5} fail because the two short sides can't span the long one — matching your predictions exactly.

Why it works

A triangle closes only when the two shorter sides, swung from the ends of the longest, can reach each other — which is precisely the triangle inequality. The straws make the abstract rule physical: too-short sides simply can't meet.
