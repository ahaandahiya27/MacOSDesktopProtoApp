Beyond the Book — The Day Letters Became Numbers

Why do we suddenly write maths with x and n? The story of algebra is the story of getting lazy in a brilliant way.

≈ 6 min read · curious-kid level

A "letter-number" like n stands for any number at once. That single idea is one of the most powerful in all of mathematics — and it has a long history. Read one idea at a time.

1. The word "algebra" is over 1,200 years old

Around 820 CE, the mathematician al-Khwarizmi, working in Baghdad, wrote a book whose title contained the word al-jabr — meaning "restoring" or "completing." That word travelled into Europe and became algebra. His own name, by the way, gives us the word algorithm.

💡 Try this

Write "a number, then 3 more" without algebra: you have to say it in words every single time. Now write it as n + 3. One short expression covers every number at once — that is the whole point of letter-numbers.

2. For centuries, algebra had no symbols

Early algebra was written out entirely in sentences — no x, no n, no "=". Mathematicians described problems in long paragraphs. Only in the 1500s and 1600s did people like François Viète and René Descartes start using letters for unknowns, turning pages of words into a few tidy lines.

3. A letter-number is a "promise to keep it fair"

When you write 2n + 2n = 4n, you are making a claim about every number n could ever be — 2, 100, a million, one-half. You only have to check the pattern once, with letters, instead of testing numbers forever. That is why a single algebra line can replace an infinite list.

4. Patterns hiding in plain sight

Letter-numbers make patterns visible. The sum of the first n odd numbers (1, 1+3, 1+3+5, …) is always n × n — a perfect square. Try it: 1+3+5 = 9 = 3×3. Algebra is the tool that lets you prove a pattern like this holds forever, not just for the cases you happened to test.

💡 Try this

Pick any number, double it, add 6, halve the result, then subtract your original number. You always get 3. Write the steps with a letter-number n and watch why the answer can never be anything else.
