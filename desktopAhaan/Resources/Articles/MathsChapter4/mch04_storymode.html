Story Mode — Expressions Using Letter-Numbers in Real Life

The same ideas from this chapter, but out in the world — in shops, on playgrounds, and in everyday choices.

≈ 6 min · see-it-everywhere level

Maths can feel like it lives only inside the textbook. It doesn't. Here are real situations where the ideas of "Expressions Using Letter-Numbers" quietly do the work.

One formula, every square

A carpenter who knows 'perimeter = 4 × side' never has to re-derive how much edging a square table needs — whether the side is 60 cm or 1.2 m, it's just 4 times the side. That single formula, 4q, replaces measuring and adding four edges every single time. Formulas are labour-saving devices made of letters.

Ketaki's laddu costs

Ketaki sells coconut-jaggery laddus. With coconuts at ₹35 and jaggery at ₹60 per kg, her cost for c coconuts and j kg jaggery is always 35c + 60j. She plugs in today's quantities and gets the bill instantly — no fresh arithmetic logic needed, just substitution.

Patterns in matchsticks and tiles

Builders and designers describe repeating patterns with letter-numbers: n repeats of an L-shape need 2n sticks; a border of n tiles needs a predictable count. Capturing the pattern as an expression lets them order exactly the right quantity for any size job.
