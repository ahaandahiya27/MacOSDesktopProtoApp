🗳️ Hold a real class election (about 45 min)

What you need

  • A box to act as a sealed ballot box
  • Slips of paper for ballots
  • A pen for each voter
  • A chart to display the rules and results

Steps

  1. With your class, decide one role to elect — for example, a 'class representative' for the student committee.
  2. Write down clear rules: who can stand, who can vote (one vote each), and how the winner is decided.
  3. Let candidates give a short speech about how they will advocate for the class's needs.
  4. Hand each student one blank ballot slip and have them write their choice secretly.
  5. Fold the ballots and drop them into the sealed box so votes stay secret.
  6. Open the box together and count the votes openly in front of everyone.
  7. Announce the winner, and discuss how this is like universal adult franchise in a real election.
  8. Compare your process with the Chola Uttaramerur inscriptions, which used sealed ballot boxes over a thousand years ago.

👀 What you should see

You will see how a secret, fair vote turns many individual choices into one chosen representative — and why a sealed box and clear rules make people trust the result.

🧠 Why it works

Running the vote yourself makes the principles of representative democracy — universal adult franchise, secret ballot and accountability — concrete instead of abstract, and shows why fair procedures matter as much as the vote itself.

🎭 Role-play the forms of government (about 40 min)

What you need

  • A small open space in the classroom
  • Name cards (King, Dictator, Voter, Minister, Judge, Religious leader)
  • A simple 'decision' to make, such as choosing a class trip

Steps

  1. Split the class into small groups, each assigned one form of government: democracy, absolute monarchy, dictatorship, theocracy or oligarchy.
  2. Give every group the same decision to make — for example, where to go on a class trip.
  3. The democracy group must vote, with everyone getting an equal say.
  4. The monarchy group lets one 'king' decide, the dictatorship group lets one 'dictator' decide with no discussion allowed.
  5. The theocracy group must base the decision on a set 'rule book', and the oligarchy group lets only three 'wealthy' members decide.
  6. Each group acts out making the decision in front of the class.
  7. Afterwards, discuss who had a voice and who did not in each form, and which felt fairest.

👀 What you should see

You will notice that only in the democracy group does everyone get a say and feel fairly treated, while in the others power rests with one person or a few.

🧠 Why it works

Acting out each form makes the differences in 'who decides' and 'who has a voice' vivid and memorable, directly illustrating why democracy is valued over the other forms.
