What is Government and What Does It Do?

The role a government plays in our lives, its three functions — legislative, executive and judicial — and why governments of different countries differ from one another.

  1. The Role of Government in Our LivesA government runs a country by maintaining order, providing services, defending the nation and working for the welfare of its people.
  2. The Three Functions of GovernmentEvery government makes rules (legislative), carries them out (executive) and ensures they are followed (judicial).
  3. What Makes Governments Different?Governments differ in who gives them power, how they are formed, what their parts are, and what goals they work towards.

Democracy: Rule of the People

What democracy means, the school committee story that explains representation, the fundamental principles every democracy aims for, and the difference between direct and representative democracy.

  1. What is Democracy?Democracy is 'rule of the people', where the people of a country are the source of power and authority.
  2. The School Committee Story and RepresentationChoosing representatives by voting — as students do for a school committee — is the democratic way to manage the affairs of a large group.
  3. Fundamental Principles of DemocracyEvery democracy aims for equality, freedom, representative participation, universal adult franchise, fundamental rights and an independent judiciary.
  4. Direct and Representative DemocracyIn a direct democracy citizens decide everything themselves; in a representative democracy they elect leaders to decide for them.

Forms of Representative Democracy

Why elections are central to democracy, the difference between parliamentary and presidential democracy, and the meaning of the legislature, executive, judiciary and separation of powers.

  1. Why Elections MatterRegular, fixed-interval elections let people choose their representatives and replace them, keeping the government accountable.
  2. Parliamentary and Presidential DemocracyIn a parliamentary democracy the executive comes from the legislature; in a presidential democracy the executive is elected separately.
  3. The Three Organs and Separation of PowersThe legislature makes laws, the executive implements them and the judiciary ensures they are followed — and in many democracies these work independently.

Other Forms of Government

Monarchy in its absolute and constitutional forms, theocracy, dictatorship and oligarchy — the main alternatives to democracy and how they are governed.

  1. Monarchy: Absolute and ConstitutionalA monarchy is ruled by a king or queen; the monarch holds all power in an absolute monarchy but only nominal power in a constitutional monarchy.
  2. Theocracy and DictatorshipA theocracy is ruled by religion and religious leaders; a dictatorship gives one person or small group absolute power with no legal limits.
  3. OligarchyAn oligarchy is a government where a small, powerful group — usually wealthy families or influential people — makes all the important decisions.

From the Rulers to the Ruled: History and Why Democracy Matters

Ancient republics where the ruled chose their rulers — the Lichchhavis, the Uttaramerur inscriptions, Greece and Rome — and why, despite its problems, democracy is valued and matters today.

  1. Early Republics and the Roots of Choosing RulersLong before modern democracy, republics like the Vajji mahājanapada chose leaders by merit and election, showing the ancient roots of the ruled choosing their rulers.
  2. Why Democracy MattersDemocracy is valued because the people choose and can change their government, which protects their rights and freedoms — though it has problems citizens must guard against.
