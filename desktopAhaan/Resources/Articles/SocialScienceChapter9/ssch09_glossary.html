The 17 terms to know

  • Government · सरकार — The set of institutions through which a country is run — making rules, carrying them out and ensuring they are followed.
    Example: The Government of India provides schools, defence and roads for its people.
  • Democracy · लोकतंत्र / प्रजातंत्र — A form of government that is 'rule of the people', where the people are the source of power and choose their representatives.
    Example: India is a democracy where citizens elect their representatives every five years.
  • Representative · प्रतिनिधि — A person who is chosen to act or make decisions on behalf of another person or group of people.
    Example: Each grade elected one representative to the school committee.
  • Universal adult franchise · सार्वभौमिक वयस्क मताधिकार — The principle that grants every citizen above a certain age the right to vote to choose their representative.
    Example: India gave every adult the right to vote from 1950.
  • Accountability · जवाबदेही — In a democracy, it means that the government is answerable to the people who have elected them.
    Example: If a government performs badly, voters hold it accountable by electing different representatives.
  • Separation of powers · शक्तियों का पृथक्करण — The arrangement in which the three organs of government — legislature, executive and judiciary — work independently and do not interfere in each other's functions.
    Example: In India and the USA the judiciary is independent of the executive and legislature.
  • Monarchy · राजतंत्र — A type of government ruled by a monarch (a king or queen), usually with the position inherited within the family.
    Example: Saudi Arabia is an absolute monarchy; the United Kingdom is a constitutional monarchy.
  • Theocracy · धर्मतंत्र — A form of government in which a country is ruled by the rules of religion and by religious leaders.
    Example: Vatican City and Iran have theocratic elements in their governments.
  • Dictatorship · तानाशाही — A form of government in which one person or a small group possesses absolute power, with no limits imposed by a constitution or law.
    Example: Germany under Adolf Hitler became a dictatorship after 1933.
  • Oligarchy · अल्पतंत्र — A government in which a small, powerful group — usually wealthy families or influential people — makes all the important decisions. From Greek olígos ('few') and árkhō ('to rule').
    Example: Some ancient Greek city-states were ruled by aristocratic families as oligarchies.
  • Republic · गणराज्य / गणतंत्र — A form of government in which the head of state is elected and is not a hereditary monarch.
    Example: The ancient Vajji mahājanapada was an early republic; India became a republic in 1950.
  • Kratos & arkhē (roots of government words) · शासन-सूचक यूनानी मूल — Two Greek roots name most kinds of government: kratos ('power, rule') gives demo-cracy and theo-cracy; arkhē ('rule') gives mon-archy, olig-archy and an-archy.
    Example: Knowing kratos and arkhē lets you decode almost any '-cracy' or '-archy' word.
  • Parliament · संसद — The assembly of elected representatives who debate and make a country's laws; from Old French parler ('to speak') — literally 'a place of speaking'.
    Example: In a parliamentary democracy the government is drawn from the parliament.
  • Constitution · संविधान — The supreme set of rules by which a country is governed; from Latin constituere ('to establish, set up').
    Example: A constitutional monarch must act within the limits of the constitution.
  • Suffrage · मताधिकार — The right to vote in elections; from Latin suffragium ('a vote, support').
    Example: Universal adult suffrage gives every adult citizen one equal vote.
  • Referendum · जनमत संग्रह — A direct vote in which all citizens decide a single question themselves; from Latin referendum ('a thing to be referred [to the people]').
    Example: In a referendum, voters answer one question directly rather than electing a representative.
  • Citizen · नागरिक — A full member of a state who holds its rights and duties; from Latin civis, which also gives 'civics' and 'city'.
    Example: In a democracy, power flows from the citizens.
