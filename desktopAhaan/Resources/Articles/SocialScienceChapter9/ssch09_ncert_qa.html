Q1. Which type of government does India have, and why is it called that type? (NCERT page 207)

India is a parliamentary democracy (a representative democracy of the parliamentary type). It is a democracy because the people are the source of power: they elect their representatives through universal adult franchise, and the government is accountable to them. It is parliamentary because the members of the executive — the prime minister and the council of ministers — are also members of Parliament and continue working only as long as they have the confidence of the Lok Sabha, rather than being elected separately as in a presidential system.

Q2. An independent judiciary is present in all types of democracies. State any three reasons why you think it is important for the judiciary to be independent. (NCERT page 207)

First, an independent judiciary protects every citizen's fundamental rights, so that even the government or powerful individuals cannot take them away. Second, it ensures that the laws are followed equally by ordinary citizens and by all parts of the government, so no one is above the law. Third, because it is free from the control of the rulers, it can fairly judge disputes and check the legislature and executive, striking down unjust laws or actions and upholding the separation of powers that prevents any single organ from becoming too powerful.

Q3. Democracy is different from monarchy and dictatorship. Explain. (NCERT page 208)

In a democracy the people are the source of power: they elect their representatives, enjoy equality, freedom and fundamental rights, and can change the government through regular elections, so it is accountable to them. In a monarchy a king or queen rules, usually by inheriting the position; in an absolute monarchy the monarch holds all the power and the people do not choose the government. In a dictatorship one person or a small group holds absolute power with no limits from any constitution or law, and the people cannot freely remove them. So democracy differs from both because in it power flows from the people and can be peacefully changed by them, while monarchy and dictatorship concentrate power in rulers the people did not freely choose.

Q4. Do you think democratic government is better than other forms of government? Why? (NCERT page 208)

Yes, democracy is generally considered better, and here is why. In a democracy every adult has an equal right to vote (universal adult franchise), people are treated equally and enjoy freedom of speech, power is shared through a separation of powers, and the government works for the wellbeing and prosperity of all citizens — features that other forms such as dictatorship, absolute monarchy and oligarchy do not provide. Above all, the people choose their government and can peacefully replace it through elections if it does not perform, which keeps it accountable. Democracy does have problems, such as corruption or a few people gaining too much influence, but these can be reduced if citizens stay vigilant, which is why it is still seen as the best form of government.
