History, geography and society are full of 'what if' turning points. Think about each question yourself first — then open the panel to compare your reasoning.

What if elections in a democracy were stopped forever after one vote?

Think first, then open to compare

Democracy would soon stop being real. The whole point of regular, fixed-interval elections is that the people can keep the government accountable and replace it if it performs badly. If there were no more elections, the people would lose their power to change their leaders, and the government would no longer need to listen to them. Over time, with no check on it, the government could ignore people's rights and concentrate power in a few hands — sliding toward an oligarchy or even a dictatorship. This is exactly why a democracy fixes election dates in advance, so a government cannot simply avoid facing the voters.

What if a king believed he had divine power given by God?

Think first, then open to compare

A king who believed his power came directly from God might think he was above all rules and answerable to no one. He could make laws purely as he wished, enforce them harshly, and ignore the welfare of his people, since he would feel he needed no one's permission or advice. This is the danger of an absolute monarchy. In contrast, the Indian ideal of rājadharma held that even a king's power was neither absolute nor beyond question — he was expected to rule within dharma and put the welfare of his people first, as Bhīṣhma taught Yudhiṣṭhira in the Mahābhārata and as King Chandrāpīḍa showed when he respectfully asked a poor cobbler's permission.

What if a country had no separation of powers?

Think first, then open to compare

If the same person or body made the laws, enforced them and judged disputes, there would be nothing to stop power being abused — a law could be passed, applied and 'upheld' by one hand with no check. Separation of powers splits these roles among the legislature, executive and judiciary so each can restrain the others. Without it, even an elected ruler could slide into dictatorship. The division is what keeps a democracy honest.
