Every topic has traps that catch careful students. Here are the most common ones for this chapter, paired with what is actually true, so you can sidestep them.

❌ Many kids think…

A country with a king or queen cannot be a democracy.

✅ Actually…

It depends on the type of monarchy. In an absolute monarchy (like Saudi Arabia) the king truly rules, so it is not a democracy. But in a constitutional monarchy (like the UK) the king has only nominal power while an elected parliament makes the laws — so the UK is actually a parliamentary democracy with a king.

❌ Many kids think…

India is a presidential democracy because it has a President.

✅ Actually…

India is a parliamentary democracy. Real executive power lies with the prime minister and the council of ministers, who are members of Parliament and must keep the confidence of the Lok Sabha. The USA is a presidential democracy, where the president is elected directly and works independently of the legislature.

❌ Many kids think…

Holding an election automatically makes a country a real democracy.

✅ Actually…

Elections alone are not enough. A genuine democracy also needs equality, freedom of speech, fundamental rights and an independent judiciary. Some non-democracies hold votes that are not truly free, and even real democracies can drift toward oligarchy if a small powerful group gains too much control.
