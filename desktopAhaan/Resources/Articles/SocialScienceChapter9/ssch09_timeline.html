From the rulers to the ruled: how the idea of choosing rulers grew

A few milestones in the long journey from rule by kings towards rule by the people, drawing on the examples in this chapter.

  • Early republics in India
    Ancient mahājanapadas like the Vajji (with the Lichchhavi clan) choose leaders by merit and election rather than by birth — early republics where representatives met to ensure the people's welfare.
  • Greek and Roman republics
    In the 5th and 4th centuries BCE, Greece and Rome have republics, though only some free men can vote while women, workers and enslaved people cannot.
  • Chola village elections
    The 10th-century CE Uttaramerur inscriptions in Tamil Nadu record village sabhā elections with sealed ballot boxes, qualifications, duties, and dismissal for corruption.
  • Modern democracies are established
    Country by country, modern democracies take shape — the USA (1787), Switzerland (1848), India (1947), Germany (1949), Kenya (1964) and Nepal (2008).
  • Universal adult franchise widens
    The right to vote slowly extends to all adults — India from 1950, the UK by 1928, the USA by 1965, and Swiss women only in 1971 — bringing 'rule of the people' closer to all the people.
