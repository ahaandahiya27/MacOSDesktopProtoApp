Five Wrong Answers Class 7 Students Give

The five places almost every Class 7 student trips up on Chapter 8 — and the one-line fix for each.

≈ 5 min read · revision-tier

Examiners say the same wrong answers come back year after year. None of these mistakes are silly — they are almost right. That is exactly what makes them dangerous. Read each one, cover the answer, and try to spot what is off before peeking.

1. “Air is empty — has no weight.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Air has weight. A 1 cubic metre of air at room temperature weighs about 1.2 kg. The atmospheric column above your shoulders pushes down with ~10,000 kg of force per square metre — but you don't feel it because the air inside your body pushes back equally. Sudden pressure changes (taking off in a plane, deep diving) are when you notice the air's weight on your eardrums.

2. “Wind happens because air just decides to move.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Wind always has a cause — a pressure difference. Air moves FROM high pressure TO low pressure (just like water flowing from a high tap to a lower drain). No pressure difference = no wind. The bigger the difference, the faster the wind. That's why a low-pressure cyclone draws air in at 200+ km/h, and a calm day has no pressure gradient.

3. “The eye of a cyclone is dangerous because it's the worst spot.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: The eye is the CALMEST spot in a cyclone — winds drop to near zero, sky often clears, and people who survive the first wall sometimes step out thinking the storm is over. That's dangerous — the EYE WALL right behind it has the strongest winds. Cyclone survivors in coastal India are taught: when winds suddenly stop, the second half is coming. Stay indoors another 30 minutes.

4. “Lightning hits the ground; thunder hits afterwards.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Lightning and thunder happen at exactly the same moment — they're two effects of the same bolt. Lightning is the visible electric arc; thunder is the sound of superheated air (~30,000°C) expanding explosively. You SEE lightning first because light travels million times faster than sound. Count seconds between flash and thunder; divide by 3 = km away. If less than 5 seconds, take shelter.

5. “Cyclones happen randomly.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Cyclones need very specific conditions: warm sea surface (>26.5°C), low atmospheric wind shear, distance from the equator (Coriolis force), and a pre-existing weather disturbance. Without all four, no cyclone forms. The Bay of Bengal has all four most years; the Arabian Sea less so (which is why east coast gets more cyclones than west). They aren't random — they're highly predictable from the conditions.

📝 Stretch your thinking

Pick any two of these and explain them to a younger sibling using a one-line analogy each. If you can compress a mistake into one sentence with one image, you have understood it.
