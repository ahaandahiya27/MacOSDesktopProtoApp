Winds, Storms and Cyclones

Chapter 8 · Class 7 Science

Why does wind exist?

Air is invisible, but it has weight and it moves. The Sun heats the ground unevenly — the equator gets more direct sunlight than the poles. Hot air rises because its molecules spread out and become less dense. Cooler air rushes in to take its place, and that movement is what we call wind.

In short: wind is the planet's way of evening out temperature.

Hot air rises, low pressure forms

When air heats up, it expands. The same amount of air now takes more space, so each cubic metre of it weighs less. That's why hot-air balloons float — the air inside is lighter than the air outside.

Where air rises, it leaves behind low pressure. Surrounding cooler, denser, higher-pressure air flows in. Air always moves from high pressure to low pressure.

Faster air = lower pressure (Bernoulli)

If you blow between two paper strips, they swing together, not apart. The fast-moving air between them has lower pressure than the still air outside, so the outside air pushes the strips inward. This same idea lifts aeroplane wings and gives cyclones their destructive ring.

Land breezes and sea breezes

Coastal places have a daily wind rhythm. By day, land heats faster than the sea — air over land rises, and a cool sea breeze blows in from the water. At night, the land cools faster, the sea stays relatively warm, and the breeze reverses into a land breeze.

Storms and cyclones

A cyclone is a giant low-pressure storm with winds spiralling inward. At its centre lies the eye — calm, almost windless. The fastest, most destructive winds live in the wall of clouds around the eye.

India's Meteorological Department (IMD) warns coastal towns in four stages: pre-cyclone watch (72 hrs), cyclone alert (48 hrs), cyclone warning (24 hrs), and post-landfall. The warning system saves thousands of lives every year.

Thunderstorm safety

  • Shelter inside a pucca building, away from windows.
  • Don't stand under tall trees or near metal poles.
  • If caught in the open, crouch low.
  • Avoid using plumbing or wired electronics during the storm.
Wind speed is measured with an anemometer — three spinning cups whose rotation rate converts to km/h.
