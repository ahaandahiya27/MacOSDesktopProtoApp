Q1. Fill in the blanks: (a) Wind is moving ______. (b) Winds are generated due to ______ heating on the Earth. (c) Near the Earth's surface, ______ air rises up whereas ______ air comes down. (d) Air moves from a region of ______ pressure to a region of ______ pressure.

Model answer: (a) air (b) uneven (c) warm; cool (d) high; low.

Q2. Suggest two methods to find out wind direction at a given place.

Model answer: (1) Wet a finger and hold it up — the cool side is the windward side (wind is coming from there). (2) Watch smoke from a chimney or incense stick — it drifts in the direction the wind blows. More formal: a weathercock or wind vane rotates to point toward the source of the wind. Cricket boards' wind vanes do exactly this.

Q3. State two experiences that made you think that air exerts pressure (other than those given in the text).

Model answer: (1) When you ride a fast bike or stand outside a moving car, you feel air pushing against your face — that's air pressure becoming kinetic energy. (2) When you suck juice through a straw, air pressure outside the glass pushes the juice up into the low-pressure mouth you created. Other examples: a paper sticking to a windowpane during heavy wind (high-pressure air outside, low-pressure between paper and glass), a cyclist hanging on a windy day, the wind lifting a kite.

Q4. You want to buy a house. Would you like to buy a house having windows but no ventilators? Explain your answer.

Model answer: No — a house needs ventilators (small openings high on the wall). Hot air rises and accumulates near the ceiling. Without ventilators, this hot stale air gets trapped, making the room hotter and stuffier. Ventilators let hot air escape and cool fresh air enter through lower windows — natural convection cooling. In Indian summers, ventilators can drop room temperature by 3-5°C versus closed rooms.

Q5. Explain why holes are made in hanging banners and hoardings.

Model answer: Holes let wind pass THROUGH the banner instead of pushing AGAINST it. Without holes, even a moderate wind exerts huge force on the banner (force = pressure × area). A solid 3m × 2m banner in 50 km/h wind faces ~150 kg of force — enough to rip the banner or pull down whatever it's tied to. The little holes you see in cricket-stadium banners and political hoardings are exactly this engineering trick.

Q6. How will you help your neighbours in case a cyclone approaches your village?

Model answer: (1) Listen to IMD weather radio / TV warnings and pass them to neighbours. (2) Help elderly, sick, and disabled neighbours move to a designated cyclone shelter early. (3) Help secure houses — board up windows, tie down loose objects, store water and food. (4) Move livestock to higher ground. (5) Charge phones, keep flashlight and batteries ready. (6) After the storm: don't go out during the calm eye; help with rescue and first aid after winds fully subside. (7) Avoid downed electric wires and floodwater.

Q7. What planning is required in advance to deal with the situation created by a cyclone?

Model answer: Planning at multiple levels. Individual: a 'go-bag' with food/water for 3 days, ID copies, money, torch, batteries, first aid kit. Family: knowing the nearest cyclone shelter (every coastal village has one), having transport plan, identifying vulnerable family members. Community: IMD warning subscriptions, evacuation drills, secure stockpiles. Government: storm warnings (IMD), cyclone shelters (NDMA built ~1500 since 2000), shelter signs, helpline 1078, NDRF teams pre-positioned. Without all four layers, deaths multiply.

Q8. Which of the statements given below is correct? (i) In winter the winds flow from the land to the ocean. (ii) In summer the winds flow from the land towards the ocean. (iii) A cyclone is formed by the very high pressure system with very high speed winds revolving around it. (iv) The coastline of India is not vulnerable to cyclones.

Model answer: (i) is correct. In winter, the land is cooler than the ocean (the ocean retains its summer heat longer). So warm ocean air rises, and cool land air flows out to the ocean — that's the northeast monsoon / winter wind pattern. (ii) is wrong — it's reversed. (iii) is wrong — cyclones form around LOW pressure, not high. (iv) is dramatically wrong — India's east coast is one of the world's most cyclone-vulnerable coastlines.
