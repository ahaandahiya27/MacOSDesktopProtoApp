A Class 7 Winds, Storms and Cyclones Dictionary

The 10 must-know terms from Chapter 8 — in plain English plus the Hindi name, with a one-line meaning a Class 7 student can actually use.

≈ 5 min read · revision-ready

Reading the chapter once isn't enough. Knowing the words is what makes you confident. Glance through this deck every Sunday for four weeks and these terms will be yours.

The 10 terms to know

  • Air pressure · वायुदाब — The force that air exerts on a surface, caused by the weight of the column of air above it.
    Example: At sea level, air pressure is 1 atmosphere (~10,000 kg per square metre).
  • Wind · हवा — Air moving from a region of higher pressure to a region of lower pressure.
    Example: Coastal afternoon sea breeze, monsoon winds, cyclone gales — all driven by pressure differences.
  • Sea breeze · समुद्री हवा — Daytime wind from sea to land, caused by faster land heating.
    Example: Marine Drive's afternoon breeze in Mumbai.
  • Land breeze · स्थलीय हवा — Night-time wind from land to sea, caused by faster land cooling.
    Example: Fishing boats often leave coastal villages at dusk with the land breeze.
  • Monsoon · मानसून — Large-scale seasonal wind pattern caused by differential heating of land and sea — India's summer rainfall maker.
    Example: Onset of southwest monsoon over Kerala on ~1 June, reaches Delhi by 1 July.
  • Thunderstorm · तूफान — A storm with rapid updraft of warm humid air producing tall clouds, lightning, thunder, and heavy rain.
    Example: Delhi's pre-monsoon May thunderstorms, often called 'andhi'.
  • Cyclone · चक्रवात — A large rotating storm system around a low-pressure centre, with winds 60+ km/h.
    Example: Cyclone Amphan (May 2020) hit West Bengal with 240 km/h winds.
  • Eye of the cyclone · चक्रवात की आँख — The calm low-pressure centre of a cyclone, surrounded by the strongest winds.
    Example: Survivors of cyclones often mistake the eye's calm for the end of the storm.
  • Wind vane · वायु दिशा सूचक — A device with a pointer that rotates to show the direction the wind is blowing FROM.
    Example: Cricket stadiums and weather stations use wind vanes to track wind direction.
  • Storm surge · तूफानी ज्वार — The rise in sea level along the coast during a cyclone, caused by low pressure and strong onshore winds.
    Example: Cyclone Phailin's 2013 storm surge in Odisha was 3.5 metres above normal sea level.
