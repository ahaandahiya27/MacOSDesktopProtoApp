One Curious Person Behind Chapter 8

The scientist most tied to winds, storms and cyclones — their question, their experiment, and what changed because of them.

Every chapter has a person behind it — someone who first asked the right question, ran the right experiment, or wrote down the answer. This is a one-page portrait of the scientist most tied to this chapter's content.

P. Koteswaram (with India context)

1915–2002 · Indian

🎧 In one line

First Indian Director-General of the Indian Meteorological Department; pioneered cyclone forecasting that saves thousands of lives every year.

When P. Koteswaram joined the Indian Meteorological Department in 1939, India had almost no cyclone warning. Coastal communities heard nothing until the wind hit. The 1942 Midnapore cyclone killed ~10,000 people in Bengal — silent, sudden. Koteswaram spent his career changing that. He pioneered upper-air observations using radiosondes (instruments hung off helium balloons), connecting India to the global weather-data network. He led IMD's transition to satellite-based monitoring after India's INSAT satellites launched in the 1980s. He trained generations of Indian meteorologists. By the time of Cyclone Amphan in 2020, IMD's warning system gave 7 days' advance notice with track accuracy of better than 50 km. The 2013 Cyclone Phailin (Odisha) killed only 45 people — a similar 1999 cyclone had killed 10,000. The reduction is Koteswaram's invisible legacy: every coastal Indian who survives a cyclone today owes it to a forecasting system he built.
