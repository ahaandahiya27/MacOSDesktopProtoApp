🛠️ Mini Project — Mark Mātrās in a Sanskrit Verse

A hands-on activity that turns the chapter from something you read into something you do.

≈ 20–30 min total · do-it-yourself level

The chapter taught you to hear short, long, and prolonged vowels. This project trains your eye to SEE them.

What you'll need

  • A Sanskrit verse (any from this textbook — including the chapter's कुक्कुटः रौति example, or a subhashita)
  • A pencil
  • 10 minutes

Steps

  1. Print or copy the verse onto paper, with space between words.
  2. Under each vowel, mark its mātrā:
  3. • a dot (·) for ह्रस्व (short, 1)
  4. • a dash (—) for दीर्घ (long, 2)
  5. • a curve (~) for प्लुत (prolonged, 3)
  6. Read the verse aloud, holding short vowels briefly and long vowels twice as long. Listen to how it rolls.

🔍 What you should notice

Your marked verse becomes a phonetic map. Sanskrit poetry's metre comes from this rhythm; you have just decoded it.

Why it works

Pāṇini's tradition treated mātrās as the atoms of poetry. Marking them turns a verse from prose-reading into MUSIC-reading.
