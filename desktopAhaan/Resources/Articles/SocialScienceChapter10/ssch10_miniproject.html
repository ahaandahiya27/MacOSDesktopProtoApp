📜 Design a class constitution (about 60 min)

What you need

  • A large chart paper
  • Coloured pens
  • A box for collecting suggestions
  • Your whole class

Steps

  1. As a class, brainstorm what problems or disputes commonly arise in your classroom (turns, sharing, cleaning, noise).
  2. Collect everyone's suggested rules in the suggestion box, so every student gets a voice — like a Constituent Assembly.
  3. Read out the suggestions and discuss them; group similar ones together.
  4. Write a short Preamble beginning 'We, the students of Class 7…' stating your class's shared values.
  5. List a few 'rights' (e.g., the right to be heard) and matching 'duties' (e.g., the duty to keep the room clean).
  6. Add a rule for how the class constitution itself can be changed later (your own 'amendment' process).
  7. Vote to adopt the class constitution, then display it on the wall.

👀 What you should see

You will see how hard but how fair it is to agree on rules everyone accepts, and why a constitution needs both rights and duties plus a way to be amended.

🧠 Why it works

Building rules together by discussion and voting mirrors exactly how the Constituent Assembly worked, turning the abstract idea of a constitution into a real, lived experience of consensus, rights, duties and amendment.

🔍 Hunt for the Constitution in daily life (about 45 min)

What you need

  • A notebook and pen
  • One week of everyday observation

Steps

  1. For one week, watch out for moments in your neighbourhood that show a Preamble value in action.
  2. Write down anything that shows EQUALITY (e.g., people of all castes using the same well or bus).
  3. Write down anything that shows LIBERTY (e.g., people freely expressing opinions or following their religion).
  4. Write down anything that shows JUSTICE (e.g., equal treatment regardless of gender or background).
  5. Note any moment showing FRATERNITY (people helping each other as one community).
  6. For each observation, write which Preamble word it matches and one line on why.
  7. Share your list with the class and compare findings.

👀 What you should see

You will discover that the values written in the Preamble are not just words in a book — they show up in ordinary, everyday life all around you.

🧠 Why it works

Linking abstract constitutional values to concrete daily scenes makes them memorable and real, and trains you to 'read' society through the lens of the Constitution's ideals.
