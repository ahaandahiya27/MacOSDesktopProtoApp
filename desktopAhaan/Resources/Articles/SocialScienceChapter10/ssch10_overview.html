What Is a Constitution and Why We Need One

The idea of a constitution as a country's rulebook, the four big things it lays down, why a shared rulebook everyone agrees to is essential, and why high officials pledge to uphold it.

  1. What a Constitution IsA constitution is the document that spells out a nation's basic principles and laws.
  2. Why We Need a ConstitutionWe need a constitution so that everyone agrees in advance on fair rules for running the country and settling disputes.
  3. Officials Who Pledge to Uphold the ConstitutionKey officials such as the President, Prime Minister, ministers and judges take an oath to follow and protect the Constitution.

Writing the Constitution of India

How a Constituent Assembly was formed in 1946 to write the Constitution, who led it, the role of the Drafting Committee under Dr. B.R. Ambedkar, and the key dates that gave us Republic Day.

  1. The Constituent AssemblyThe Constituent Assembly was the elected body of representatives formed in 1946 to write India's Constitution.
  2. Dr. Rajendra Prasad and the Drafting CommitteeDr. Rajendra Prasad chaired the Constituent Assembly, while a Drafting Committee headed by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar prepared the initial text.
  3. Key Dates: From Adoption to Republic DayThe Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 and came into effect on 26 January 1950, which we celebrate as Republic Day.

What Shaped the Indian Constitution

The three great influences on the Constitution — the ideals of the freedom struggle, India's own civilisational heritage, and useful learnings from the constitutions of other countries.

  1. Influence of the Freedom MovementThe ideals and experiences of India's freedom struggle were carried into the Constitution by leaders who had fought for independence.
  2. Influence of India's Civilisational HeritageAge-old Indian values like respect for differing views, the idea of duties, and 'the world is one family' shaped the Constitution.
  3. Learnings from Other CountriesIndia studied other democratic constitutions and borrowed useful ideas, like liberty-equality-fraternity from France and the independent judiciary idea from the USA.

Key Features of the Constitution

The three organs of government and separation of powers, the Fundamental Rights, Fundamental Duties and Directive Principles of State Policy, and why the Constitution is a 'living document' that can be amended.

  1. Three Organs and Separation of PowersThe Constitution sets up three organs of government — legislature, executive and judiciary — and keeps their powers separate.
  2. Rights, Duties and Directive PrinciplesThe Constitution gives citizens Fundamental Rights (enforceable promises), Fundamental Duties (responsibilities), and Directive Principles (goals for the government).
  3. A Living DocumentThe Constitution is a 'living document' because it can be changed by amendments as the country's needs evolve.

Understanding the Preamble

The Preamble as the heart of the Constitution, its description of India as a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic, and the goals it sets out — Justice, Liberty, Equality and Fraternity.

  1. The Preamble and 'We, the People'The Preamble is the opening statement of the Constitution that sums up its core guiding values.
  2. Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, RepublicThe Preamble describes India as a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic — five words that define what kind of nation India is.
  3. Justice, Liberty, Equality, FraternityThe Preamble promises every citizen Justice, Liberty, Equality and Fraternity — the four goals the Constitution seeks to secure.
