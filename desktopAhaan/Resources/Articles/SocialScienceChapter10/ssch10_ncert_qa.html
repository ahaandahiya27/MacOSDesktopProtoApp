Q1. 'The Constituent Assembly had representatives from diverse backgrounds in India.' Why do you think it was important to have a diverse set of representatives from all over India? (NCERT page 227)

It was important because India is a very large and culturally diverse country, with people of many regions, religions, castes, languages and professions. If the Constitution had been written only by one group, it would have reflected only their interests and might have been rejected by others. By including representatives from diverse backgrounds — different regions, professions and social groups, and 15 women — the Constituent Assembly made sure that the concerns of many different communities were heard and built into the Constitution. This gave the document wide acceptance and legitimacy, so people across the whole country could feel it was truly their own rulebook.

Q2. Read the statements and identify which key feature or value of the Constitution is reflected: (a) Sheena, Rajat and Harsh are excited to cast their first vote in the general elections. (b) Radha, Imon and Harpreet study in the same class in the same school. (c) Parents must make arrangements to ensure their children's education. (d) People of all castes, genders and religions can use the village well. (NCERT page 227)

(a) The right to vote in elections reflects DEMOCRACY (and universal adult franchise) — the people elect their rulers. (b) Children of different backgrounds studying together reflects EQUALITY and the Right to Education (Article 21-A). (c) Parents ensuring their children's education reflects a FUNDAMENTAL DUTY (and supports the Right to Education). (d) People of all castes, genders and religions using the same well reflects EQUALITY and JUSTICE — no discrimination on grounds of caste, religion or gender.

Q3. You have learnt that 'India is the only country that provided universal adult franchise to its citizens from the beginning.' Can you explain why India did it? (NCERT page 227)

India provided universal adult franchise — the right of every adult to vote, regardless of wealth, education, caste, religion or gender — from its very first election. India did this because the freedom struggle had been a mass movement involving people from every section of society, who had fought together for independence and self-rule. It would have been unfair to then deny any of them a say in choosing their government. The Constitution-makers believed in equality and justice for all, and trusted the ordinary people of India. By contrast, many older democracies had given the vote only gradually, first to wealthy or educated men and only much later to women and the poor. India's bold decision showed deep faith in democracy and in the equality of all its citizens.

Q4. How did the freedom struggle inspire the making of the Constitution of India? How did India's civilisational heritage inspire some of the key features in the Constitution? Explain. (NCERT page 227)

The freedom struggle inspired the Constitution in two ways. First, the ideals the freedom fighters had fought for — equality of all, justice for all, freedom, fraternity and preservation of India's cultural heritage — were written into the Constitution, because many freedom-movement leaders were themselves members of the Constituent Assembly. Second, the experience of the struggle answered practical questions, such as giving every adult the right to vote, keeping the powers of the executive, legislature and judiciary separate, and guaranteeing fundamental rights. India's civilisational heritage inspired key features too: the old acceptance of different points of view supports secularism and pluralism; respect for Nature and the well-being of all creatures appears in values like sarve bhavantu sukhinah; and the long tradition stressing the duties of people (seen in janapadas, sanghas, rajadharma and Kauṭilya's saptanga) inspired the inclusion of Fundamental Duties.
