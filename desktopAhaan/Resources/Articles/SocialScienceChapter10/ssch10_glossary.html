The 10 terms to know

  • Constitution · संविधान — The supreme document that spells out a nation's basic principles and laws, setting up the government, the rights and duties of citizens, and the country's long-term goals.
    Example: The Constitution of India came into effect on 26 January 1950.
  • Constituent Assembly · संविधान सभा — The body of elected representatives formed in 1946 to write the Constitution of India.
    Example: The Constituent Assembly had 389 members, later reduced to 299 after Partition.
  • Preamble · प्रस्तावना — The opening statement of the Constitution that sums up its core guiding values and the kind of nation India is.
    Example: The Preamble begins with the words 'We, the People of India'.
  • Sovereign · संप्रभु / प्रभुत्वसंपन्न — Having the supreme right to make decisions on internal and external matters, with no external power able to dictate the government.
    Example: India is a sovereign nation that makes its own foreign policy.
  • Secular · धर्मनिरपेक्ष — Allowing citizens complete freedom to follow any religion, with no official state religion and equal respect for all faiths.
    Example: Because India is secular, the State has no official religion.
  • Republic · गणराज्य / गणतंत्र — A form of state in which the head of state is an elected person, not a hereditary king or queen.
    Example: India is a republic with an elected President as head of state.
  • Fundamental Rights · मौलिक अधिकार — Basic rights guaranteed to citizens that must be protected, and which can be enforced through the courts.
    Example: The Right to Equality and the Right to Freedom are Fundamental Rights.
  • Fundamental Duties · मौलिक कर्तव्य — The responsibilities of citizens listed in the Constitution, such as respecting the National Flag and protecting the environment.
    Example: Protecting forests, lakes and rivers is a Fundamental Duty.
  • Directive Principles of State Policy · राज्य के नीति निदेशक तत्व — Goals and guidelines for the government to work toward, which cannot be enforced in court but guide its policies.
    Example: Ensuring a good standard of living for all is a Directive Principle.
  • Amendment · संशोधन — A change made to the Constitution, rigorously debated in Parliament before it is accepted.
    Example: Fundamental Duties were added by an amendment in 1976.
