History, geography and society are full of 'what if' turning points. Think about each question yourself first — then open the panel to compare your reasoning.

What if India had no constitution at all?

Think first, then open to compare

Without a constitution, there would be no agreed rulebook to say who governs, how power is limited, or what rights people have. Disputes would be settled by whoever was strongest, not by fair rules, and rulers could do whatever they liked because nothing would bind them. There would be no clear way to make laws, hold elections, or protect citizens who are treated unfairly, and a huge, diverse country like India could easily fall into confusion and conflict. The constitution is what lets such a varied nation be governed fairly and stay united.

What if the Constitution could never be amended?

Think first, then open to compare

If the Constitution could never change, it would slowly become outdated as the country's needs evolved. New ideas and bodies that were not thought of in 1950 — like the Fundamental Duties added in 1976 or the Panchayati Raj system added in 1992 — could never have been included. The Constitution might no longer fit modern India, and people would either have to ignore it or scrap it and start again. By making it a 'living document' that can be carefully amended after rigorous debate, the makers let it grow with the country while keeping its core values safe.

What if India had no written Constitution?

Think first, then open to compare

Without a single supreme document, there would be no agreed list of citizens' rights, no fixed rules for how the government is formed, and no neutral standard for courts to judge whether a law is fair. Those in power could change the rules to suit themselves. The Constitution sets limits on government and guarantees equality and freedom to everyone alike. It is the rulebook that makes the rule of law, rather than the rule of the strong, possible.
