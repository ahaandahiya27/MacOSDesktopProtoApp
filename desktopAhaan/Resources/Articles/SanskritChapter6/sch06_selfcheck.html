Question 1 — The warm-up

According to the famous shloka, without विद्या a person does not shine — they are compared to —

🧠 Answer in your head first

Work it out before reading on — no peeking.

Answer: a fragrance-less किंशुक flower


• The verse: विद्याहीना न शोभन्ते निर्गन्धा इव किंशुकाः.
• Showy but empty — like the किंशुक (Flame of the Forest) flower without fragrance.

Question 2 — The check

According to the wicked-vs-good shloka, the wicked use धनम् (wealth) for —

🧠 Answer in your head first

Work it out before reading on — no peeking.

Answer: मदाय (pride)


• विद्या विवादाय, धनं मदाय, शक्तिः परिपीडनाय — for the wicked.
• The good use the same three for sharing, giving, and protecting.

Question 3 — The stretch

Those without good character (शीलम्) are called by the chapter —

🧠 Answer in your head first

Work it out before reading on — no peeking.

Answer: भारभूताः (a burden on earth)


• भारभूताः = 'burden-become', glossed भारकराः.
• The chapter contrasts them with those who shine through character.

Question 4 — The trap

'श्लोकान्त्याक्षरी' is —

🧠 Answer in your head first

Work it out before reading on — no peeking.

Answer: an antyakshari game played with shlokas


• श्लोक + अन्त्य ('last') + अक्षरी ('letter game') = antyakshari with shlokas.
• The chapter's title 'क्रीडाम वयं श्लोकान्त्याक्षरीम्' = 'let us play this game'.

Question 5 — The finisher

'खलः' means —

🧠 Answer in your head first

Work it out before reading on — no peeking.

Answer: a wicked person


• Glossary: खलः = दुष्टः ('wicked').
• The villain of the contrast verse: 'खलस्य साधोः विपरीतमेतत्'.
