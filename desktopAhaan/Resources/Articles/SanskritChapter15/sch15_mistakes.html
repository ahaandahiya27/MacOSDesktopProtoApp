Common Mistakes

Five errors students often make in this chapter — and the gentle corrections that fix them.

≈ 5 min · honest-with-yourself level

Sanskrit makes precise distinctions that English glosses over — case endings, gender, tense, prefix vs augment. The mistakes below are not personal failings — they are signals that a specific detail of the language needs another look.

Mistake 1 — Saying every verb works like पठ्

What students often write/say: पठ् is the textbook example.

The correct form: Sanskrit has 10 गण (verb classes) with different conjugation patterns. पठ् is class 1 (भ्वादि), the most common — but अस्, कृ, श्रु belong to other classes with different forms.

💡 Why

Memorise पठ् as the standard, then learn the irregularities. The chapter's appendix shows pattern variations across classes — that's the point of multiple examples.

Mistake 2 — Treating परस्मैपद and आत्मनेपद as the same

What students often write/say: Both look like Sanskrit conjugations.

The correct form: They use DIFFERENT endings. 3rd singular present: parasmaipada -ति (पठति) vs ātmanepada -ते (भाषते).

💡 Why

Each verb root belongs to one (or both — उभयपद) pada-class. The lexicon tells you which. Mixing them gives wrong forms.

Mistake 3 — Saying 'अहम् करोति' (I do)

What students often write/say: Verb's wrong person.

The correct form: अहम् = 'I' → I-form needed: अहम् करोमि. करोति is 'HE does' (third person).

💡 Why

Sanskrit verbs MUST agree with subject in person and number. अहम् (1st sing) → करोमि. तत्/सः (3rd sing) → करोति. आवाम् (1st dual) → कुर्वः. वयम् (1st pl) → कुर्मः.

Mistake 4 — Reading 'अकरोत्' as 'not did'

What students often write/say: अ- might look like the negative prefix.

The correct form: अ- here is the past-tense AUGMENT (लङ्-लकार). अकरोत् = 'he did' (past tense).

💡 Why

Two different अ- in Sanskrit: (1) past-tense augment (अकरोत्), (2) negative prefix (अधर्मः = non-righteousness). Context tells you which. Past-tense verbs always have it.

Mistake 5 — Calling Sanskrit's three persons 'first, second, third' in English order

What students often write/say: Western grammar tradition orders 'I, you, he'.

The correct form: Sanskrit reverses it: प्रथमपुरुष = he/she (Western 3rd); मध्यमपुरुष = you (2nd); उत्तमपुरुष = I (1st).

💡 Why

When an Indian grammarian says 'first person', they mean WHAT WESTERN BOOKS CALL THIRD PERSON. The reversal is consistent and historic — don't translate the term word-for-word.

📝 Self-check

Re-read the chapter once with these five mistakes in mind. Notice the places where the textbook has DELIBERATELY chosen one form over another — that's often where the trap lies.
