Common Mistakes

Five errors students often make in this chapter — and the gentle corrections that fix them.

≈ 5 min · honest-with-yourself level

Sanskrit makes precise distinctions that English glosses over — case endings, gender, tense, prefix vs augment. The mistakes below are not personal failings — they are signals that a specific detail of the language needs another look.

Mistake 1 — Calling Sanskrit's case system 'six' instead of seven (plus vocative)

What students often write/say: Latin and Greek have fewer cases.

The correct form: Sanskrit has 7 कारक cases (vibhakti) + सम्बोधन (vocative) = 8 forms.

💡 Why

Don't import Latin's 6-case schema to Sanskrit. The 8-form system is one of Sanskrit's distinctive features. Vocative is usually shown alongside but often counted separately.

Mistake 2 — Saying 'देवस्य' means 'to the god'

What students often write/say: Confusion of cases.

The correct form: देवस्य = OF the god (genitive). To the god = देवाय (dative).

💡 Why

Each Sanskrit case has its own ending. Memorise the देव paradigm: देवः (nom), देवम् (acc), देवेन (inst), देवाय (dat), देवात् (abl), देवस्य (gen), देवे (loc), हे देव! (voc). Each does ONE thing.

Mistake 3 — Treating the dual number as 'two-plural'

What students often write/say: Modern languages collapse dual into plural.

The correct form: Sanskrit's dual is a SEPARATE number — for exactly TWO. देवौ = 'the two gods' (not 'a few gods').

💡 Why

Three numbers: एकवचनम् (1), द्विवचनम् (2 only!), बहुवचनम् (3+). Use the dual for pairs (eyes, hands, brothers) and exactly-two contexts. English has lost this; Sanskrit keeps it.

Mistake 4 — Reading hindi-style oblique cases into Sanskrit

What students often write/say: Hindi has 'oblique' for nouns after postpositions.

The correct form: Sanskrit does NOT have an oblique. Each role is its own case ending.

💡 Why

Hindi has compressed Sanskrit's case system into 2-3 forms + postpositions. Sanskrit retains the full 8-form system. Don't import Hindi grammar back into Sanskrit.

Mistake 5 — Forgetting the vocative ending changes

What students often write/say: Vocative often LOOKS like nominative.

The correct form: But not always: हे देव! (drops -ः), हे गुरो! (drops -ुः and adds -ओ).

💡 Why

Vocative singular for अकारान्त: drop the -ः. For उकारान्त: change -ुः to -ो (हे गुरो!). For इकारान्त: -एः to -ए (हे कवे!). Small but consistent changes — pin them.

📝 Self-check

Re-read the chapter once with these five mistakes in mind. Notice the places where the textbook has DELIBERATELY chosen one form over another — that's often where the trap lies.
