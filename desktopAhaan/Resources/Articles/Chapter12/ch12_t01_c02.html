Pollinators and Their Strategies

Why every flower looks the way it does · Class 7 Science

The flower–pollinator partnership

Every flower on Earth has evolved its shape, colour, scent, and reward for ONE PURPOSE — to convince a specific kind of animal (or use a specific natural force) to carry its pollen to another flower of the same species. The match between flower and pollinator is one of evolution's most beautiful examples of co-evolution.

The big four pollinators

1. Bees

  • Flowers attract them with: bright colours (especially blue, purple, yellow — bees can't see red), UV patterns invisible to us but obvious to them, sweet nectar reward.
  • Examples: hibiscus, marigold, mustard, sunflower.
  • Bees are responsible for ~75% of food-crop pollination worldwide. India's mango, mustard, and litchi yields all depend on bee visits.

2. Butterflies and Moths

  • Butterflies prefer bright red, orange, pink flowers with flat landing pads.
  • Moths (night-active) prefer white or pale flowers with strong fragrance.
  • Examples: lantana, jasmine, raat-ki-rani.

3. Birds (especially sunbirds in India)

  • Flowers are typically large, red or orange, tubular-shaped, scentless (birds have poor smell), with copious nectar.
  • Examples: hibiscus, banana flower, palaash (flame of the forest).

4. Wind

  • Wind-pollinated flowers don't need to attract anyone, so they save energy: small, dull, no scent, no nectar.
  • But they produce ENORMOUS quantities of light, dry pollen — millions of grains per flower, hoping a few land on the right stigma.
  • Examples: all grasses (rice, wheat, maize, sugarcane), pines, oaks.
Cross-species mismatches are common but usually inefficient. A bee visiting a hummingbird flower (or vice versa) wastes the flower's pollen. Co-evolution has fine-tuned each match for hundreds of millions of years.

Some weird specialists

  • Bats pollinate banana, agave, and durian — flowers that open at night and smell musky.
  • Beetles pollinate magnolias and water lilies — among the oldest flowering plants on Earth.
  • Water pollinates a few aquatic plants like Vallisneria — male flowers detach, float, and brush against female stigmas.
  • Flies pollinate flowers that smell like rotting meat (Rafflesia, Stinky lily). Yes, that's a thing.

Why the bee crisis matters

Bee populations globally are declining due to pesticides, monoculture farming, and habitat loss. If bees disappear, agricultural yields drop dramatically — and pollinator-dependent crops (apples, almonds, tomatoes, coffee) become rare and expensive.

Try this at home

On a sunny morning, sit near a flowering plant for 10 minutes. Count the visiting insects. You'll usually see honeybees, bumblebees, butterflies, occasionally hover-flies. Each one is unknowingly carrying pollen between flowers — running a delivery service for plants that they don't even know they're doing.

Looking ahead

Class 12 Biology 'Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants' classifies pollination strategies formally:

  • Self-pollination within the same flower (autogamy) or same plant (geitonogamy).
  • Cross-pollination between flowers of different plants (xenogamy).

And the floral adaptations that promote each (dichogamy, herkogamy, self-incompatibility, dioecy). NEET asks pollination-type and pollinator-flower-match questions every year.
