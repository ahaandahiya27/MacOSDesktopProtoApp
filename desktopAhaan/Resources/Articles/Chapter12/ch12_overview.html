Reproduction in Plants

Chapter 12 · Class 7 Science

Why do plants need to reproduce?

Every living thing dies eventually. Reproduction is how a species continues — by making new individuals that carry the parent's traits.

Two big strategies

  • Sexual reproduction — needs two parents (or two parts of one plant). Mixes genes, creates variety.
  • Asexual reproduction — one parent, identical clones. Fast and reliable.

Inside a flower

A flower is a plant's reproductive organ. The parts you need to know:

  • Sepals — green protective leaves around the bud.
  • Petals — colourful, scented — they attract pollinators.
  • Stamen — the male part. Anther (top) makes pollen; filament holds it.
  • Pistil — the female part. Stigma catches pollen; ovary contains eggs.

Pollination — getting pollen to the egg

Plants can't walk, so they hire couriers:

  • Wind — grasses, pines (lots of light, dry pollen).
  • Insects — bees, butterflies (sticky pollen, bright petals, nectar reward).
  • Birds — sunbirds, hummingbirds (large red flowers, plenty of nectar).
  • Water — water lilies (pollen designed to float).
Pollination is just delivery. Fertilisation is when the pollen tube reaches the egg and they fuse. The fertilised ovule becomes a seed; the ovary becomes a fruit.

Self vs cross pollination

  • Self pollination — pollen lands on the same flower or another flower of the same plant. Reliable but produces identical offspring.
  • Cross pollination — pollen travels to a different plant of the same species. Mixes genes → better resistance and variety.

Seed dispersal

If seeds fell straight down they'd compete with the parent plant. Nature has invented many travel methods:

  • Wind — dandelion fluff, maple wings.
  • Water — coconut floats across oceans.
  • Animals — fruit eaten and seeds dropped far away.
  • Explosion — castor pods burst open when dry.

Asexual reproduction

  • Vegetative propagation — potato tubers, rose stem cuttings, bryophyllum leaf buds.
  • Budding — yeast and hydra grow a small bump that breaks off as a new individual.
  • Fragmentation — Spirogyra algae breaks; each piece becomes a new filament.
