Five Wrong Answers Class 7 Students Give

The five places almost every Class 7 student trips up on Chapter 12 — and the one-line fix for each.

≈ 5 min read · revision-tier

Examiners say the same wrong answers come back year after year. None of these mistakes are silly — they are almost right. That is exactly what makes them dangerous. Read each one, cover the answer, and try to spot what is off before peeking.

1. “All seeds need soil to grow.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Seeds need water + warmth + (eventually) light. Soil is just convenient. A bean seed germinates fine on a wet cotton ball on a plate. Even desert seeds wait years for rain — soil's role is mostly to anchor and supply minerals AFTER germination. The seed itself contains all the energy and minerals needed for the first few days. Indian agricultural laboratories grow plants in HYDROPONICS — no soil at all, just nutrient-rich water.

2. “Fruits and vegetables are biologically different.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Botanically, a 'fruit' is what develops from the ovary of a flower after fertilisation — so it always contains seeds. By that strict definition: tomato, brinjal, capsicum, cucumber, pumpkin, lady's finger — all FRUITS. 'Vegetable' is a kitchen category, not a botanical one. 'Vegetables' are typically leaves (palak), roots (carrot), stems (potato is actually a stem), flowers (cauliflower). The mango and the tomato are botanically the same category.

3. “All flowers are colourful and have nectar.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Many flowers are tiny, green, and smelly — wind-pollinated flowers like grasses, rice, wheat, maize. They don't NEED bright colours because they're not attracting animals; wind doesn't care about colour. Maize tassels at the top of the plant ARE flowers — many people don't recognise them. India's rice and wheat fields are full of flowers nobody photographs.

4. “Sexual reproduction requires two parents (male + female animals).”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: In plants, ONE flower can have BOTH male (stamens) and female (pistil) parts. This is called a 'bisexual flower' — hibiscus, mustard, mango are examples. Some flowers can even self-pollinate. Some plants are 'monoecious' — male and female flowers on the same plant (maize). Sex in plants is much more flexible than in animals.

5. “Asexual reproduction in plants means no parents.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Asexual reproduction = ONE parent, not zero. The new plant is a genetic copy (clone) of that one parent. Sexual reproduction needs TWO parents (or two halves of one bisexual parent). The advantage of asexual: fast, no need to find a partner. The disadvantage: no variation — a disease that kills one kills the whole clone population. This is why a banana disease threatens all bananas; they're clones.

📝 Stretch your thinking

Pick any two of these and explain them to a younger sibling using a one-line analogy each. If you can compress a mistake into one sentence with one image, you have understood it.
