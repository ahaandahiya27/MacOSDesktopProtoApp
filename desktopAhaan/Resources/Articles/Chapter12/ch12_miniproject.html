Two Mini Projects for Chapter 12

Hands-on activities you can do today — with materials from the kitchen, not the lab. Each project takes about an hour and turns chapter ideas into something you can see and touch.

≈ 60 min total · hands-on

Reading is not the same as doing. These mini projects ask you to use your hands — predict, observe, and write down what surprises you. Try at least one before the next chapter test.

🌱 Bean seed time-lapse — germination day by day

~10080 min · one-sitting project

What you'll need

  • A handful of moong / rajma beans soaked overnight
  • 2 plastic cups
  • Cotton wool or tissue paper
  • Water

Steps

  1. Line the cups with damp cotton.
  2. Place 4-5 beans in each, between cotton and the cup wall (visible from outside).
  3. Keep moist — sprinkle water every day, never let dry.
  4. Place one cup in light, one in a dark cupboard.
  5. Observe and draw what you see every morning for 7 days.
  6. Compare: which grew faster? Which is greener?

👀 What you should see

Day 1: nothing visible. Day 2: tiny root pokes out. Day 3: shoot emerges; root branches. Day 4-5: shoot up, first leaves opening. Day 7: small plant! Cup in dark grows TALLER (looking for light) but is pale yellow — no chlorophyll without light.

Why it works: Seeds contain everything to start a new plant — embryo + food reserve. Water triggers germination. Roots grow toward gravity (geotropism); shoots grow against gravity. Leaves develop chlorophyll only with light. The dark experiment shows light is essential for chlorophyll, not for growth itself.

🌿 Money plant cuttings — see vegetative propagation in action

~30240 min · one-sitting project

What you'll need

  • A money plant (devil's ivy) in your house or neighbour's house
  • Scissors
  • A clear bottle of water
  • Sunny window

Steps

  1. Cut a 15 cm stem section with at least 2 leaves and 2 'nodes' (the bumps where leaves attach).
  2. Remove the lower leaf; the bare node should go underwater.
  3. Put the cutting in the bottle of water — node submerged, leaves above.
  4. Place on a sunny window (indirect light is fine).
  5. Change water every 3 days.
  6. Watch nodes for 2-3 weeks.

👀 What you should see

Within 1-2 weeks, white roots emerge from the submerged node. The plant is now technically a new individual — a clone of the parent. After 4-5 weeks, you can transplant to soil.

Why it works: Money plant stems have stored growth signals at nodes. Submerge a node in water → it forms ROOTS at that spot. The same node above water would have formed a side-shoot. A small environmental change (water vs air) triggers different organs to form from the same cells. This is exactly how rose, sugarcane, and bougainvillea are propagated commercially.

📝 How to use this

Before you start, predict what will happen. Write your prediction down. Then do the project. Compare. If your prediction was wrong, write down why — that's where real learning happens.
