Question 1 — from Chapter 12

Explain the process of pollination and fertilization in flowering plants.

🧠 Answer in your head first

Don't peek. Speak the answer out loud, then check.

Answer: Pollination is the transfer of pollen from the anther (male part) to the stigma (female part) of a flower, by agents such as wind, water, insects, or birds. After pollination, the pollen germinates on the stigma and grows a pollen tube down through the style to reach the ovule. Fertilization occurs when the male gamete from the pollen tube fuses with the egg cell in the ovule. The fertilized ovule develops into a seed, and the ovary develops into a fruit.

Why: Step 1 — Pollination: Pollen produced by the anther is transferred to the stigma.

Question 2 — from Chapter 12

Self-pollination is when:

🧠 Answer in your head first

Don't peek. Speak the answer out loud, then check.

Answer: Pollen lands on the stigma of the SAME flower or another flower on the same plant

Why: Self-pollination keeps genes within one plant — less variety.

Question 5 — from Chapter 12

Genetic diversity is the species' insurance policy. Justify why a banana farm growing ONE clone variety is at higher long-term risk than a wild banana population, and predict what could go wrong.

🧠 Answer in your head first

Don't peek. Speak the answer out loud, then check.

Answer: Wild bananas vary genetically — different plants have different disease resistance, climate tolerance, pest susceptibility. A new disease may kill some but not all. The species survives. Single-clone farms (all plants genetically identical): if a disease finds ONE weakness, it kills EVERY plant in every farm worldwide. Real example: the 'Gros Michel' banana, the world's commercial variety until the 1950s, was wiped out by Panama disease (a fungus). The replacement 'Cavendish' (95% of today's exports) is now under threat from a new strain of the same fungus. Single-clone farming trades short-term yield for long-term catastrophic vulnerability.

Why: Wild diversity: some individuals always have disease resistance.
