Pollination to Fertilisation

What actually happens after a bee leaves · Class 7 Science

Two different events, often confused

Pollination is the DELIVERY of pollen from anther to stigma. Fertilisation is the FUSION of the male nucleus from the pollen with the egg cell inside the ovary. Pollination is just transport; fertilisation is the actual reproduction.

What a pollen grain does on landing

  1. Pollen lands on a sticky stigma of the SAME species (other species' pollen is rejected — a chemical mismatch).
  2. The pollen germinates, growing a pollen tube down through the style toward the ovary.
  3. Inside the pollen are two male nuclei. They travel down the tube.
  4. The tube enters the ovary and finds an ovule with its egg cell.
  5. One male nucleus fuses with the egg → diploid (2n) zygote, which will become the embryo plant.
  6. The other male nucleus fuses with two polar nuclei in the embryo sac → triploid (3n) endosperm, which will become the seed's food store.

This is called double fertilisation, and it's unique to flowering plants (angiosperms).

The endosperm being TRIPLOID (3n, not 2n) is a guaranteed NEET fact. The two ploidy levels — diploid embryo and triploid endosperm — let flowering plants evolve faster than seed-bearing plants without endosperm.

From flower to fruit

After fertilisation:

  • Petals fall off — they've served their purpose attracting pollinators.
  • The ovule matures into a seed.
  • The ovary swells into a fruit that protects the seed and (often) helps it disperse.
  • Sometimes other floral parts contribute to the 'fruit' — in apple, most of what we eat is the swollen base (receptacle), not the ovary itself.

How long does it take?

From bee visit to ripe fruit: about a week for tomatoes, 3 weeks for chillies, 2 months for mangoes, 6 months for apples. Plants invest enormously in this process — flowers that don't get pollinated simply drop off without forming fruits.

Try this at home

Mark one fresh flower on a tomato or chilli plant with a coloured thread. Check daily. Over 4–6 weeks you'll watch petals fall, the ovary swell, and a tiny green fruit form where the flower was. Photograph it weekly to make a time-lapse of fertilisation outcomes.

Looking ahead

Class 12 'Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants' is one of the most-tested NEET chapters. You'll learn:

  • The internal structure of the embryo sac (8 nuclei, 7 cells — egg, 2 synergids, 3 antipodals, 1 central cell with 2 polar nuclei).
  • The two paths a pollen tube can take — entering via the micropyle (porogamy), the chalaza (chalazogamy), or laterally (mesogamy).
  • Seed development with the three parts: embryo, endosperm, seed coat.
  • Apomixis and polyembryony — exceptions to the standard rule.

Several NEET questions every year on this exact topic.
