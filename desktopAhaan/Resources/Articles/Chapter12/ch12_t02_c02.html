From Pollination to Fruit — The Full Plant Life Cycle

Why fruits exist (it's not for us) · Class 7 Science

Fertilisation — the key step

After pollen lands on a stigma, it grows a long pollen tube down through the style to the ovary. The pollen's male nucleus fuses with the egg in the ovule. This fusion is fertilisation — and only after this does seed development begin.

From ovule to seed

The fertilised ovule develops into a seed. Inside is a tiny embryo (the next plant) plus food reserves to fuel its first sprout. The seed coat protects the embryo from drying out and from rough handling.

From ovary to fruit

While the ovule becomes the seed, the surrounding ovary swells into the fruit. Plants 'invest' in fruit for a reason: animals eat the fruit, digest the flesh, and drop the seeds far from the parent tree — free seed dispersal!

Dispersal — why seeds travel

Mango pulp tempts squirrels. Cocklebur seeds hook into fur. Coconut floats hundreds of km on ocean currents. Dandelion seeds fly on the wind. Each shape is engineered for a particular dispersal mode.

Bananas, watermelons and grapes are bred to be seedless — convenient for humans, but each cultivar is a clone propagated by cuttings, not by seeds. If we stopped tending them, they would die out.

Try this at home

Cut open a tomato. Each seed surrounded by jelly used to be an ovule. The red flesh used to be the wall of the ovary. You're looking at fertilisation evidence on your plate.
