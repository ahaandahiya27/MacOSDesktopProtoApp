Flower Anatomy in Detail

Every petal, every stamen, every job · Class 7 Science

A flower has four whorls of parts

Every typical flower is built from four concentric rings of organs — called whorls. From outside to inside they are:

  1. Calyx — the green leaf-like sepals that protected the bud before it opened.
  2. Corolla — the colourful petals that attract pollinators.
  3. Androecium — the ring of stamens (male organs). Each stamen has an anther (which makes pollen) on top of a slender filament.
  4. Gynoecium — the central pistil (female organ). Has three parts: a stigma at the top to catch pollen, a tube (style) running down, and an ovary at the base containing the egg cells.
A flower with all four whorls is called complete; missing one is incomplete. A flower with both stamens AND pistil is bisexual (most flowers); with only one is unisexual (e.g. papaya, maize).

What each organ does

  • Sepals: protect the bud. Some (like rose) are leaf-green; others (like hibiscus) are dark; in a few species (like bougainvillea) they take on the colour usually owned by petals.
  • Petals: bright colours and sweet scents attract insects, birds, even bats. Petals also reflect ultraviolet patterns invisible to humans but obvious to bees — landing-strip-like guide marks.
  • Anther: the yellow-orange part on top of a stamen. Inside are four pollen sacs that release thousands of pollen grains when ripe.
  • Stigma: sticky surface at the top of the pistil. Catches pollen. The match has to be species-specific — a hibiscus stigma rejects rose pollen.
  • Ovary: hidden at the base. Contains one or more ovules, each with an egg cell. After fertilisation, each ovule becomes a seed and the ovary swells into a fruit.

Try this at home

On a hibiscus or roadside flower, pluck one fresh bloom. On a plate, gently peel back the parts in order. Count: 5 sepals (often fused), 5 petals, many stamens (often 5-15 forming a ring on a column), and the central pistil with one stigma at the top. India's NCERT biology practicals use hibiscus for this very reason — its parts are large and easy to separate.

Looking ahead

Class 12 Biology 'Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants' takes this anatomical inventory deep:

  • Microsporogenesis — the formation of pollen inside the anther (4 microspore mother cells → 4 microspores via meiosis → mature pollen grain with vegetative + generative nuclei).
  • Megasporogenesis — the formation of the embryo sac inside the ovule (one megaspore mother cell → 4 megaspores → 1 functional → 8-nucleate embryo sac with egg, synergids, antipodals, central cell).
  • Double fertilisation — two male gametes from one pollen tube fertilise both the egg (→ zygote, diploid) AND the central cell (→ endosperm, triploid). Unique to angiosperms.

Every one of these terms appears in NEET papers — flower-anatomy diagrams are among the most-illustrated biology topics in JEE/NEET prep books.
