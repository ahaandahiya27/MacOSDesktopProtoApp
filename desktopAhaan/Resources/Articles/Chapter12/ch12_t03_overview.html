Asexual Reproduction

Topic t03 of Chapter 12 · Reproduction in Plants · Class 7 Science

The big idea

Many plants skip the flower-and-seed route entirely. Potatoes sprout from underground tubers; rose stems grow roots when cut and planted; bryophyllum sprouts new plants from leaf-margin buds. Yeast (single-celled) and hydra reproduce by budding — a small outgrowth detaches as a new individual. Spirogyra algae break into fragments, each becoming a new filament.

Cloning Without Seeds. Asexual offspring are exact genetic clones of the parent — fast and reliable, but no genetic variety to help the population adapt to disease or climate change.

Looking ahead

Class 10 Biology covers vegetative propagation, fission (Amoeba), budding (Hydra), spore formation (Rhizopus) — all NEET-testable. Class 12 'Reproduction in Organisms' formalises the asexual vs sexual trade-off.
