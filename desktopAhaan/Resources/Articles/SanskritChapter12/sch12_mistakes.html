Common Mistakes

Five errors students often make in this chapter — and the gentle corrections that fix them.

≈ 5 min · honest-with-yourself level

Sanskrit makes precise distinctions that English glosses over — case endings, gender, tense, prefix vs augment. The mistakes below are not personal failings — they are signals that a specific detail of the language needs another look.

Mistake 1 — Spelling धाया as धैय

What students often write/say: Sanskrit's आ vs ऐ.

The correct form: धाया (wet-nurse) ends in long आ. धैर्य (a different word) ends in -ऐर्य and means 'patience'.

💡 Why

Vowel length and quality are PHONEMIC in Sanskrit — they distinguish words. आ ≠ ऐ. Listen carefully when reciting.

Mistake 2 — Saying Panna sacrificed her LIFE

What students often write/say: Loose reading of सर्वस्वत्याग.

The correct form: Panna sacrificed her SON's life. She survived; the boy in the prince's bed was her own.

💡 Why

The depth of the sacrifice is precisely THAT — losing her child for someone else's. Saying she lost her own life equates her with countless soldier-heroes; her story is unique because her sacrifice was her CHILD's life.

Mistake 3 — Translating 'दुष्टबुद्धिः' as 'foolish'

What students often write/say: बुद्धि means intellect.

The correct form: दुष्ट + बुद्धि = 'evil-minded' (one whose intellect is corrupt). NOT 'foolish'.

💡 Why

Sanskrit dis distinguishes दुष्ट (wicked) from मूर्ख (foolish) — see the Ch.6 mistakes article. The villain was clever and bad, not stupid.

Mistake 4 — Conflating 'राजगृह' (king's house) with 'राजपुत्र' (prince)

What students often write/say: Both start with राज.

The correct form: राजगृह = the royal palace. राजपुत्र = a prince.

💡 Why

The chapter mentions both: the assassin enters the राजपुत्र's शयनागार (prince's bedroom) inside the राजगृह (palace). Don't swap the place for the person.

Mistake 5 — Reading 'मारयितुम्' as 'killed'

What students often write/say: Past tense intuition.

The correct form: मारयितुम् is the INFINITIVE: 'to kill' (with -तुम् ending).

💡 Why

Sanskrit infinitives end in -तुम् universally (कर्तुम् 'to do', गन्तुम् 'to go', दातुम् 'to give'). 'Killed' would be अमारयत् (past tense). The chapter uses the infinitive: the assassin came TO KILL.

📝 Self-check

Re-read the chapter once with these five mistakes in mind. Notice the places where the textbook has DELIBERATELY chosen one form over another — that's often where the trap lies.
