The Barter System

How people exchanged goods and services directly before money existed, the many commodities used as money around the world, and a real Indian barter fair that still runs today.

  1. What is the Barter System?Barter is exchanging goods or services directly for other goods or services, without using money.
  2. Commodities Used as MoneyBefore coins, people used everyday commodities like cowrie shells, salt, cattle and cloth as money.
  3. Barter Today: The Junbeel MelaAlthough money has replaced barter almost everywhere, the Junbeel Mela in Assam still runs as a living barter fair.

Why Barter Failed: The Problems

Why barter became difficult as trade grew — the double coincidence of wants, no common measure of value, and the problems of divisibility, portability and durability.

  1. Double Coincidence of WantsBarter only works if two people each happen to want exactly what the other has to offer — a 'double coincidence of wants'.
  2. No Common Standard Measure of ValueIn barter there is no agreed unit to compare worth, so it is hard to decide a fair ratio for an exchange.
  3. Divisibility, Portability and DurabilityBarter goods are often hard to split, hard to carry and quick to spoil — the problems of divisibility, portability and durability.

Why Money? The Functions of Money

What money is, why it came into existence, and the basic functions it performs — medium of exchange, store of value, common measure of value, and standard of deferred payment.

  1. What is Money and Why It Came into ExistenceMoney is the common tool everybody accepts and uses to make or receive payments in exchange for goods and services.
  2. Money as a Medium of Exchange and Store of ValueMoney is a common medium of exchange and also a store of value that can be kept and used later.
  3. Measure of Value and Standard of Deferred PaymentMoney measures and compares the value of goods through their prices, and lets payments be made later — a standard of deferred payment.

The Journey of Money: Coins and Paper

How money evolved into metal coinage in ancient India — the kārṣhāpaṇas with their rūpa symbols, obverse and reverse motifs, Roman coins found in India — and then into paper currency issued today only by the RBI.

  1. Ancient Indian CoinageCoins were among the earliest forms of money; rulers minted them from precious metals, and ancient Indian coins were called kārṣhāpaṇas or paṇas.
  2. Coin Designs: Obverse and ReverseCoins have two sides — the obverse (head) and reverse (tail) — carrying symbols of nature, rulers and deities.
  3. Paper Money and the RBIPaper money was first used in China and brought to India in the late 18th century; today only the Reserve Bank of India may issue currency.

New Forms of Money: Going Digital

How technology brought intangible, digital money — debit and credit cards, net banking, UPI and QR-code payments — and how the whole journey of money has evolved over thousands of years.

  1. Digital Money: QR Codes, UPI and CardsDigital money is money in electronic form, paid through QR codes, UPI, debit and credit cards and net banking, with nothing to touch.
  2. The Evolution of Money: A TimelineMoney has evolved through stages — barter, commodity money, metal coinage, paper money, and now digital money.
