The journey of money: from barter to UPI

A broad timeline of how money evolved in India, from swapping goods to tapping a phone.

  • Barter (around 6000 BCE)
    People exchange goods and services directly, with no money. Commodities like cowrie shells, salt, cattle and cloth are used as early money.
  • Metal coinage (around 1000–600 BCE)
    Rulers mint coins of iron, silver, gold and copper. Ancient Indian coins (kārṣhāpaṇas/paṇas) carry punched rūpa symbols.
  • Official paper money (1861)
    As carrying coins becomes hard, paper currency takes over for larger amounts. Paper money was first used in China and brought to India in the late 18th century.
  • Digital money / cards (around 1980)
    Debit and credit cards let money move electronically between bank accounts, with nothing physical to hand over.
  • UPI (2016)
    Unified Payments Interface and QR codes let anyone — even a fruit-cart seller — receive instant digital payments straight to a bank account.
