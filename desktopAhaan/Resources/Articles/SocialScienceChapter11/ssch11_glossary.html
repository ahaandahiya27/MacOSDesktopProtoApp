The 18 terms to know

  • Barter system · वस्तु विनिमय प्रणाली — A way of exchanging goods or services for other goods and services without using money.
    Example: Swapping a spare eraser for a classmate's spare pencil.
  • Money · मुद्रा / धन — The common tool that everybody accepts and uses in order to make or receive payments in exchange for goods and services.
    Example: Coins, notes and digital balances used to buy things.
  • Transaction · लेन-देन — A piece of business done between people, especially an act of buying or selling.
    Example: Paying a shopkeeper for a notebook is a transaction.
  • Commodities · वस्तुएँ / पण्य — Products or goods that can be traded, bought and sold.
    Example: Cowrie shells, salt and cloth were once used as commodity money.
  • Double coincidence of wants · आवश्यकताओं का दोहरा संयोग — A situation where two people each have something the other wants and can exchange them directly.
    Example: A farmer who wants shoes must find a shoemaker who wants his ox.
  • Common standard measure of value · मूल्य का सामान्य मानक माप — An agreed-upon worth that helps in determining and comparing the value of goods and services.
    Example: Pricing a book at ₹100 and a toy at ₹50 lets us compare their value.
  • Divisibility / Portability / Durability · विभाज्यता / सुवाह्यता / टिकाऊपन — Divisibility is being splittable into parts; portability is being easy to carry; durability is lasting long and being storable — qualities barter goods often lack but money has.
    Example: An ox cannot be divided, is hard to carry, and wheat rots — so they make poor money.
  • Minting / Mint · टकसाल / मुद्रण — Minting is the process of producing coins; a mint is the facility that produces a nation's currency.
    Example: Special coins are minted to mark important national events.
  • Alloy · मिश्र धातु — A metal made by combining two or more metallic elements, which makes a coin strong.
    Example: Ancient coins were made of silver and copper alloys.
  • Currency / Denomination · मुद्रा / मूल्यवर्ग — Currency is the system of money used in a particular country; denominations are the units (like ₹10, ₹50, ₹100) in which coins and notes are classified.
    Example: The rupee is India's currency, with notes of ₹10, ₹50, ₹100 and more.
  • Digital money · डिजिटल मुद्रा — Money in an intangible, electronic form that cannot be touched, transferred directly between bank accounts.
    Example: Paying by scanning a QR code or using UPI.
  • QR code · क्यूआर कोड — A 'quick-response' code — a pattern of black and white squares, readable by smartphones and scanners, that holds the receiver's bank-account information for making payments.
    Example: Krishnappa's customers scan his QR code to pay him.
  • Rupee · रुपया — The name of India's currency; from Sanskrit rūpya, meaning 'wrought (stamped) silver'.
    Example: From ancient silver coins to today's digital rupee, the name 'rupee' has lasted over 2,000 years.
  • Numismatics · मुद्राशास्त्र — The study of coins, paper money and the history they reveal; from Greek nomisma ('coin, current money').
    Example: Numismatics lets historians read trade and power from old coins.
  • Obverse and reverse · मुख / पृष्ठ भाग — The two faces of a coin: the obverse ('heads', the main side) and the reverse ('tails'); from Latin obvertere ('to turn toward') and reverse ('turned back').
    Example: A coin's ruler or emblem is usually on the obverse, its value on the reverse.
  • Fiat money · आदेशिक मुद्रा / फ़िएट मुद्रा — Money that has value because the government declares it legal tender and people trust it — not because the material is precious; from Latin fiat ('let it be done').
    Example: Today's notes and coins are fiat money, valuable by trust, not by their metal or paper.
  • Legal tender · वैध मुद्रा — Money that, by law, must be accepted to settle a debt or payment within a country.
    Example: The Indian rupee is legal tender across India.
  • Inflation · मुद्रास्फीति — A general rise in prices over time, so each rupee buys a little less than before; from Latin inflare ('to blow up, inflate').
    Example: Because of inflation, the same coin buys less today than it did decades ago.
