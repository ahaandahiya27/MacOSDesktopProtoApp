History, geography and society are full of 'what if' turning points. Think about each question yourself first — then open the panel to compare your reasoning.

What if money had never been invented and we still used only barter today?

Think first, then open to compare

Daily life would be slow and difficult. To buy anything, you would need to find someone who wanted exactly what you had to offer AND had exactly what you wanted — the double coincidence of wants. There would be no agreed prices, so every deal would mean haggling over ratios. You could not easily divide goods (no cutting a piece off a cow), carry them around, or store them without spoilage. Long-distance trade and large purchases like land would be nearly impossible, salaries could not be paid in a convenient form, and saving for the future would be very hard. The economy would stay small and local.

What if anyone were allowed to print their own paper money?

Think first, then open to compare

Money would quickly lose its value and people's trust. If everyone could print notes, the supply would explode and no one could be sure a note was genuine or backed by anything, so shops would stop accepting it — destroying money's most important property, general acceptability. To prevent this, India gives the sole power to issue currency to one central authority, the Reserve Bank of India, and makes it illegal for anyone else to print currency, while adding security features to notes so genuine money can be trusted.

What if money had never been invented and we still bartered everything?

Think first, then open to compare

Barter needs a 'double coincidence of wants' — you must find someone who has what you want AND wants what you have. A teacher wanting rice would have to find a farmer who happened to want lessons. Trade would be slow and limited, and you could not easily save value for later or price very different goods against each other. Money solves all of this by being a thing everyone accepts. Without it, a complex economy could barely function.
