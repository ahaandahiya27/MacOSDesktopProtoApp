Maths can feel like it lives only inside the textbook. It doesn't. Here are real situations where the ideas of "Another Peek Beyond the Point" quietly do the work.

The metric advantage

Converting 4.56 m to centimetres is just ×100 = 456 cm — a decimal-point shift, no real multiplication. The whole metric system runs on multiplying and dividing by powers of ten, which is why scientists and most of the world prefer it over feet-and-inches (where conversions need awkward ×12).

Price per kilogram

₹4.5 for 0.5 kg of a spice means ₹4.5 ÷ 0.5 = ₹9 per kg. Shopkeepers and shoppers do this 'divide by a decimal' constantly to compare unit prices — and the answer being bigger than the price paid is exactly right.

Estimate to avoid disasters

Engineers estimate every calculation before trusting it — a misplaced decimal point or unit slip has destroyed real spacecraft. 'Look before you leap': a five-second estimate that pins the decimal point is one of the most valuable habits in all of applied maths.
