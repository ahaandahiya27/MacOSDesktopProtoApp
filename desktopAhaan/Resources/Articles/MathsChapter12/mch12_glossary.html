Another Peek Beyond the Point: Key Words

Every term in this chapter, with a one-line meaning and an example.

&#8776; quick reference &middot; 10 terms

Knowing the exact words makes the maths click. Here is every key term from the chapter, defined plainly with an example you can picture.

Decimal multiplication

Multiply ignoring the points, then place the point so decimal places equal the sum of the factors' places.

Example: 0.3 × 0.4 = 0.12.

Decimal division

Make the divisor whole by scaling both numbers, then divide as usual.

Example: 4.5 ÷ 0.5 = 45 ÷ 5 = 9.

Decimal places

The number of digits to the right of the decimal point.

Example: 0.45 has 2 decimal places.

Point shift

Moving the decimal point when multiplying/dividing by powers of ten.

Example: × 100 shifts the point 2 places right.

Estimate

A rough value found by rounding, used to check a calculation's size.

Example: 2.5 × 1.4 ≈ 3 to 4.

In Hindi: आकलन

Order of magnitude

The rough power-of-ten size of a number.

Example: 3.5 is order of magnitude 'ones'.

Quotient

The result of a division.

Example: In 45 ÷ 5 = 9, the quotient is 9.

In Hindi: भागफल

Power of ten

10, 100, 1000, … — used to shift decimal points.

Example: 1000 = 10³.

Scaling (a division)

Multiplying dividend and divisor by the same number, which leaves the quotient unchanged.

Example: 4.5/0.5 = 45/5.

Reasonableness check

Comparing an answer with an estimate to catch errors.

Example: 3.5 fits the estimate 3-4; 35 doesn't.
