🛒 Mini Project — Decimal Shopping Check

A hands-on activity that turns this chapter into something you actually do, not just read.

≈ 30 min · do-it-yourself level

Reading about maths and doing maths are two different things. This short project lets you see the ideas of "Another Peek Beyond the Point" at work with your own hands.

What you'll need

  • Some grocery price labels or a bill
  • A pen
  • Optional: a calculator to verify

Steps

  1. Pick three priced-by-weight items (e.g. ₹/kg) and a quantity for each in decimals (like 0.5 kg, 2.5 kg).
  2. For each, ESTIMATE the cost first by rounding (e.g. 2.5 × ₹38 ≈ 2.5 × 40 ≈ ₹100).
  3. Now compute the exact cost by decimal multiplication (multiply, then place the point).
  4. Check the exact answer falls near your estimate — if it's off by a factor of ten, you misplaced the point.
  5. Bonus: work out the price per kg for an item sold in a fractional weight using decimal division.

🔍 What you should notice

Your estimates land close to the exact costs, and any decimal-point slip jumps out because it's ten times too big or small. Estimating first reliably guards the point.

Why it works

Decimal multiplication is whole-number multiplication plus point placement, and point errors change the size by factors of ten. A rounded estimate pins the order of magnitude, so it catches exactly the kind of error the algorithm is prone to.
