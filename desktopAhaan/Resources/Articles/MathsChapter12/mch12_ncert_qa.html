Textbook questions from this chapter, each with a clear worked answer.

These are the kind of questions the NCERT chapter asks. Try each one yourself first, then check the worked answer.

Q1. Express the place values in 27.53.

Answer: 27.53 = 2 tens, 7 units (ones), 5 tenths, and 3 hundredths. As a sum: 20 + 7 + 5/10 + 3/100.

Q2. Compute 0.3 × 0.4 and explain the decimal-place rule.

Answer: Multiply 3 × 4 = 12. The factors have 1 + 1 = 2 decimal places, so the answer has 2: 0.12. (0.3 = 3/10, 0.4 = 4/10, product 12/100 = 0.12.)

Q3. Compute 4.56 × 10, × 100 and × 1000.

Answer: Shift the point right by the number of zeros: 4.56 × 10 = 45.6, × 100 = 456, × 1000 = 4560.

Q4. Express 50 g, 100 g, 25 g and 250 g in kilograms as decimals.

Answer: Divide by 1000 (1 kg = 1000 g): 50 g = 0.05 kg, 100 g = 0.1 kg, 25 g = 0.025 kg, 250 g = 0.25 kg.

Q5. Compute 45.6 ÷ 10 and 456 ÷ 100.

Answer: Shift the point left: 45.6 ÷ 10 = 4.56, and 456 ÷ 100 = 4.56.

Q6. Compute 4.5 ÷ 0.5.

Answer: Multiply both by 10 to clear the divisor's point: 45 ÷ 5 = 9. Dividing by a number less than 1 gives a result larger than the dividend.

Q7. Why is it useful to estimate 2.5 × 1.4 before computing it exactly?

Answer: The estimate (≈ 3 to 4) tells you where the decimal point belongs. Since the exact digits are 350, the point must give 3.5 (in range), not 35 or 0.35. Estimation catches point-placement errors.

Q8. Compute the area of a 2.5 m by 1.4 m mat.

Answer: 2.5 × 1.4: 25 × 14 = 350, with 2 decimal places → 3.5 m².
