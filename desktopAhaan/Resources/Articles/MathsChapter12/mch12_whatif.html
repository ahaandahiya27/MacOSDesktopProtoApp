"What If?" — Another Peek Beyond the Point

Hypothetical questions with no single right answer. Think hard before you read the discussion.

≈ 7 min read · brain-stretcher level

The textbook tells you what is. The best way to really understand an idea is to ask what if? Pick a question below, pause and think for a minute, then read one possible discussion.

What if 1 — India used a non-decimal measure system?

The question: What if 1 metre were 12 'units' and 1 unit were 9 'bits'?

🧠 Think first

How hard is it to convert 2.5 metres?

One discussion path: Every conversion would need awkward multiplying, like old feet-and-inches. Because metric units are powers of ten, converting is just sliding the decimal point — that is the whole convenience of base ten.

What if 2 — percentages were out of 60, not 100?

The question: What if 'per cent' meant 'per sixty'?

🧠 Think first

Would 30% still equal a quarter? a half?

One discussion path: All the easy links (25% = 1/4, 50% = 1/2, 0.25) would break, because they depend on hundredths. 'Per hundred' is what ties percentages, decimals and fractions together so neatly.

What if 3 — multiplying by 10 didn't shift the point?

The question: What if 3.5 × 10 needed long multiplication every time?

🧠 Think first

How much slower would decimal arithmetic be?

One discussion path: Every scaling would be a chore. The shortcut works only because our system is built on tens — the digits keep their pattern and just the point moves.

📝 Stretch your thinking

None of these has one 'correct' answer — they are for arguing about. Try one on a friend or a parent and see whether their discussion path differs from yours.
