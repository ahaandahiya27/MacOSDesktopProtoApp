Common Mistakes in Another Peek Beyond the Point

The places students slip on this chapter — and the one-line fix for each.

&#8776; 5 min read &middot; revision-tier &middot; 5 fixes

These wrong answers come back year after year. None are silly — they are almost right, which is what makes them worth knowing. Read each, then cover the fix and try to spot what is off.

1. “To multiply decimals you must line up the points (like addition).”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: For multiplication you do NOT line up points. Multiply as whole numbers, then place the point by counting total decimal places.

2. “0.3 × 0.4 is bigger than 0.3.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: It's 0.12 — SMALLER. Multiplying by a number less than 1 shrinks the value.

3. “Multiplying by 100 adds two zeros to a decimal.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: It SHIFTS the point two places right: 4.56 × 100 = 456 (not 4.5600).

4. “4.5 ÷ 0.5 is smaller than 4.5.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: It's 9 — BIGGER. Dividing by a number less than 1 increases the result.

5. “When dividing by a decimal, scale only the dividend.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: Scale BOTH numbers by the same power of ten (so the quotient is unchanged) until the divisor is whole.
