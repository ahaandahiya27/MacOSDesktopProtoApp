The 10 terms to know

  • Age of Reorganisation · पुनर्गठन का युग — The period after the breakup of the Maurya Empire (roughly 2nd century BCE to 3rd century CE) when regions were rearranged into many new, competing kingdoms.
    Example: The Shungas, Satavahanas and Cholas all rose during the Age of Reorganisation.
  • Tributary kingdom · करद राज्य / सामंत राज्य — A kingdom that pays tribute to and accepts the overlordship of a more powerful empire while keeping some of its own identity.
    Example: Many post-Maurya kingdoms had earlier been tributary kingdoms under the Mauryas.
  • Matrimonial alliance · वैवाहिक गठबंधन — An alliance arranged through a marriage, generally between two members of royal families, to cement friendship between two kingdoms.
    Example: A king might offer his daughter in marriage to a neighbouring royal family to secure a matrimonial alliance.
  • Ashvamedha yajna · अश्वमेध यज्ञ — A Vedic horse-sacrifice ritual performed by rulers to declare themselves a supreme king; land the freed horse crossed unchallenged was claimed by the king.
    Example: Pushyamitra Shunga performed the ashvamedha yajna to establish himself as a most powerful ruler.
  • Stupa · स्तूप — A dome-shaped Buddhist monument, often decorated with carved railings and reliefs depicting religious stories.
    Example: The Shungas added carved railings to the Bharhut Stupa.
  • Inscription · अभिलेख / शिलालेख — Writing carved into stone, metal or other lasting material, often recording a ruler's deeds, gifts or beliefs.
    Example: The Hathigumpha inscription records King Kharavela's accomplishments year after year.
  • Sangam literature · संगम साहित्य — The oldest literature of south India: anthologies of Tamil poems expressing love, heroism and generosity, which gave the 'Sangam Age' its name.
    Example: Historians consult Sangam literature to understand ancient southern society.
  • Rock-cut architecture · शैलकृत स्थापत्य — A style of architecture in which rooms, pillars, shrines and sculptures are carved directly out of solid rock.
    Example: The Udayagiri-Khandagiri caves and the Karla caves are examples of rock-cut architecture.
  • Silk Route · रेशम मार्ग — A network of ancient trade routes connecting China with the Mediterranean world, passing through Central Asia and Persia.
    Example: The Kushanas controlled significant sections of the Silk Route, so trade grew.
  • Cultural confluence · सांस्कृतिक संगम — The flowing together and blending of different cultures to create new shared styles, while keeping a dominant local character.
    Example: Gandhara art is a confluence of Greco-Roman and Indian styles.
