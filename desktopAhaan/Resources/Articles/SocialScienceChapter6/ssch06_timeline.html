The Age of Reorganisation: from 185 BCE to the 3rd century CE

A chronological walk through the key rulers and dynasties of the post-Maurya age, crossing from BCE into CE. Notice how several kingdoms ruled at the same time in different parts of the subcontinent.

  • c. 185 BCE — Maurya breakup
    The last Maurya emperor is assassinated by his commander-in-chief, Pushyamitra Shunga, who founds the Shunga dynasty over north and central India.
  • 2nd century BCE — Satavahanas rise
    The Satavahanas come to rule large parts of the Deccan, building wealth on the Krishna-Godavari farmland and on sea trade reaching Rome.
  • 2nd century BCE — Indo-Greeks
    Indo-Greek rulers, descended from Alexander's satraps, take the weakened northwest and blend Greek and Indian culture.
  • 1st century BCE — Kharavela of Kalinga
    The Chedi king Kharavela, the Jain 'monk-king', records his deeds in the Hathigumpha inscription near Bhubaneswar.
  • Late 2nd c. BCE–5th c. CE — Shakas
    The Shakas (Indo-Scythians) replace the Indo-Greeks; the Shaka Samvat calendar is developed during their period.
  • 2nd century CE — Kushanas & Kanishka
    The Central Asian Kushanas, peaking under Kanishka, control the Silk Route; Gandhara and Mathura art flourish.
  • 2nd c. BCE–3rd c. CE — Sangam Age (South)
    In the south the Cheras, Cholas and Pandyas flourish; Sangam Tamil poetry defines the era. Karikala builds the Grand Anicut.
  • 3rd century CE — Satavahanas fragment
    The Satavahana Empire breaks into smaller kingdoms due to weak central control and economic decline, opening the way for new regional powers.
