Every topic has traps that catch careful students. Here are the most common ones for this chapter, paired with what is actually true, so you can sidestep them.

❌ Many kids think…

The Maurya Empire was conquered and destroyed by foreign invaders.

✅ Actually…

The Maurya Empire first broke up from WITHIN — its last emperor was assassinated around 185 BCE by his own commander-in-chief, Pushyamitra Shunga. Foreign invaders (Indo-Greeks, Shakas, Kushanas) only entered later, through the northwest that the breakup had left weak.

❌ Many kids think…

The foreign invaders wiped out Indian culture and replaced it with their own.

✅ Actually…

It was largely the other way round. Although they arrived as conquerors, the Indo-Greeks and Kushanas were much influenced by India's rich culture; their art, coins and even their gods became a blend, with Indian themes staying dominant. India absorbed and transformed the newcomers.

❌ Many kids think…

An age full of wars between kingdoms must have been a time of decline with no culture.

✅ Actually…

Despite constant political conflict, the Age of Reorganisation saw remarkable cultural exchange — new art styles like Gandhara and Mathura, flourishing trade, and the composition of great Sanskrit texts like the Mahabharata and Ramayana. Conflict and cultural flowering happened together.
