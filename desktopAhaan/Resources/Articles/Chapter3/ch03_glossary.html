A Class 7 Fibre to Fabric Dictionary

The 10 must-know terms from Chapter 3 — in plain English plus the Hindi name, with a one-line meaning a Class 7 student can actually use.

The 10 terms to know

  • Fleece · ऊन का बाल — The whole coat of wool covering a sheep, removed in one piece during shearing.
    Example: A single sheep's annual fleece can weigh 4–5 kg.
  • Shearing · ऊन कतरना — The process of cutting off the wool fleece from a sheep, painlessly, using clippers.
    Example: Sheep are shorn once a year in early summer; the wool grows back over the next 12 months.
  • Scouring · धुलाई — Washing the sheared wool to remove grease, dust, and dirt — the second step of wool processing.
    Example: Scouring uses hot water and detergent in big tanks; the recovered grease becomes lanolin (used in lotions).
  • Sericulture · रेशम कीट पालन — The rearing of silkworms for the production of silk fibre.
    Example: Karnataka and West Bengal are India's largest mulberry-sericulture states.
  • Cocoon · कोकून — The silk casing a silkworm caterpillar spins around itself before transforming into a pupa.
    Example: One cocoon yields a continuous thread 600–900 metres long when reeled.
  • Reeling · रीलिंग — Unwinding the silk thread from a softened cocoon onto a reel.
    Example: Reeling is done in hot water that dissolves the sticky sericin holding the cocoon threads together.
  • Sorter's disease (Anthrax) · वूल सॉर्टर रोग — A serious lung infection from inhaling Bacillus anthracis spores in raw wool — historically common among wool sorters.
    Example: Modern wool processing sterilises raw fleece, making sorter's disease nearly extinct in the industry.
  • Pashmina · पश्मीना — The fine inner wool of the Changthangi goat from Ladakh, used in luxurious shawls.
    Example: Pashmina fibre is under 16 microns thick — the human eye can barely see a single strand.
  • Synthetic fibre · कृत्रिम रेशा — A man-made fibre produced from petroleum-based chemicals through polymerisation.
    Example: Polyester, nylon, and acrylic are the three most common synthetic fibres in clothing today.
  • Microplastic · सूक्ष्म प्लास्टिक — Tiny plastic fragments (less than 5 mm) shed from synthetic clothing in every wash, polluting water and soil.
    Example: A single 6 kg load of polyester clothes can release 700,000 microfibres into the wash water.
