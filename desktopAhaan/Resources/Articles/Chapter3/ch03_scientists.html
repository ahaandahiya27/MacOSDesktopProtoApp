Every chapter has a person behind it — someone who first asked the right question, ran the right experiment, or wrote down the answer. This is a one-page portrait of the scientist most tied to this chapter's content.

Tiruvallur Subramanya Sadasivan

1913–2001 · Indian

🎧 In one line

Built India's modern sericulture and silkworm-disease research; brought scientific rigour to a 4000-year-old industry.

When India became independent in 1947, sericulture was a craft industry — millions of rural families rearing silkworms with practices passed down for generations, often losing entire batches to disease without knowing why. T. S. Sadasivan, working from his lab in Madras and later at the Central Sericultural Research and Training Institute in Mysore, brought microbiology to the silk world. He identified the bacterial and viral diseases that wiped out silkworm crops, developed sterilisation methods that any village could use, and trained a generation of scientists who scaled silkworm yields several-fold. Today India is the world's second-largest silk producer (after China), and Karnataka's mulberry farms support over a million families. The silk saree your mother wears is downstream of his microscope.
