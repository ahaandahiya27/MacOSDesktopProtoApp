Five Wrong Answers Class 7 Students Give

The five places almost every Class 7 student trips up on Chapter 3 — and the one-line fix for each.

≈ 5 min read · revision-tier

Examiners say the same wrong answers come back year after year. None of these mistakes are silly — they are almost right. That is exactly what makes them dangerous. Read each one, cover the answer, and try to spot what is off before peeking.

1. “Wool is the same thing as fur.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Both come from animal hair, but wool specifically refers to the curly, scaled, often crimped fibre that can be spun into yarn — found on sheep, goats, camels, alpacas, llamas. Dog fur, cat fur, and human hair are straight and smooth — they don't spin into yarn easily. The scales on wool are what let it lock together when spun, which is also why it can felt.

2. “Cotton and silk are both plants.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Cotton is a plant fibre (cellulose, from the cotton plant boll). Silk is an animal fibre (protein, from a caterpillar's cocoon). They feel similar but are chemically very different — cotton burns to ash, silk burns smelling like burnt hair (because of the protein), and that smell test alone can tell them apart.

3. “Synthetic fibres are entirely new — natural fibres can't compete.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Synthetic fibres are durable and cheap, but they don't breathe (sweaty in summer), don't biodegrade, and shed microplastics in every wash. Natural fibres (cotton, wool, silk, linen) breathe, regulate temperature, biodegrade, and feel better on skin. The trend in recent years has reversed — premium clothing is moving BACK to natural fibres for both comfort and environmental reasons.

4. “Silk is only made by silkworms.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: The silkworm (Bombyx mori) makes commercial mulberry silk, but other species produce other silks: tasar moth, eri moth, muga moth — all different species. Spiders also make silk, in seven different kinds for different uses (web frame, sticky capture, egg sac, etc.). Spider silk is the strongest natural fibre known.

5. “Anthrax is just a wartime weapon.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Anthrax (sorter's disease) is naturally caused by spores of Bacillus anthracis that live on raw fleece. Before modern hygiene, wool sorters in India and Europe regularly inhaled the spores while combing dirty fleece and got severe lung infections. Today, sterilisation and protective masks make the risk near-zero — but the bacterium is the same one studied for biological weapons.

📝 Stretch your thinking

Pick any two of these and explain them to a younger sibling using a one-line analogy each. If you can compress a mistake into one sentence with one image, you have understood it.
