Beyond the Book — A Compass, a Ruler, and Three Impossible Puzzles

With just two tools you can build astonishing figures — and there are three famous shapes that, no matter how clever you are, you can never build.

≈ 6 min read · curious-kid level

A construction in geometry means drawing an exact figure using only a compass and an unmarked straightedge — no measuring numbers allowed. Those two simple tools turn out to be both powerful and, in a few cases, powerless. Read one idea at a time.

1. Why no measurements are allowed

The ancient Greek rules let you draw a straight line between two points and a circle of any radius — nothing more. The straightedge has no markings. This makes constructions a pure logic puzzle: every figure must be justified by geometry, not by reading a ruler. A bisected angle built this way is exactly bisected, not "near enough."

💡 Try this

With a compass, draw a circle, then keep the same radius and step around the edge marking points. You will land on six points that form a perfect hexagon. The radius of a circle fits exactly six times around it — a tidy gift of geometry.

2. The three puzzles no one could ever solve

The Greeks set three challenges: trisect any angle into three equal parts, double the cube (build a cube with twice the volume), and square the circle (make a square with the same area as a given circle) — all with just compass and straightedge. People tried for over 2,000 years. In the 1800s, mathematicians finally proved all three are impossible with those tools. The puzzle was not too hard; it simply could not be done.

3. Only three shapes tile the floor by themselves

A tiling (or tessellation) covers a flat surface with no gaps and no overlaps. Of all the regular shapes, only three can do it alone: the equilateral triangle, the square, and the regular hexagon. Pentagons, for instance, always leave gaps. The reason is angles — the corners meeting at a point must add up to exactly 360°.

4. The bees figured it out

Honeybees build their honeycomb out of hexagons, and it is no accident. Among the three shapes that tile perfectly, the hexagon encloses the most space using the least wax for its walls. The bees are solving a real optimisation problem — getting maximum storage for minimum material.

💡 Try this

Try to tile a sheet of paper with regular pentagons drawn the same size. You will always be left with awkward gaps. Then try hexagons — they lock together perfectly, just like a honeycomb.
