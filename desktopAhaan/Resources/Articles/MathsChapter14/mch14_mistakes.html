Common Mistakes in Constructions and Tilings

The places students slip on this chapter — and the one-line fix for each.

&#8776; 5 min read &middot; revision-tier &middot; 5 fixes

These wrong answers come back year after year. None are silly — they are almost right, which is what makes them worth knowing. Read each, then cover the fix and try to spot what is off.

1. “You need a ruler to find the exact midpoint of a segment.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: A compass-and-straightedge perpendicular bisector finds it exactly, no measuring — equal arcs guarantee equidistance.

2. “If you can bisect an angle, you can also trisect it with compass and straightedge.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: Bisecting is easy, but trisecting a general angle is provably IMPOSSIBLE with these tools. You can make 30° (half of 60°) but not 20° (a third).

3. “Any regular polygon can tile the plane.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: Only those whose interior angle divides 360° — triangles (60°), squares (90°), hexagons (120°). Pentagons (108°) cannot.

4. “At a tiling point the angles can add to a bit less or more than 360°.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: They must add to EXACTLY 360°. Less leaves a gap; more forces an overlap.

5. “Constructed figures are just careful drawings.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: They are EXACT by geometry (equal radii, equidistance), not by measurement — that's what makes constructions special.
