Constructions and Tilings · Mini Project

📐 Mini Project — Construct & Tile

A hands-on activity that turns this chapter into something you actually do, not just read.

≈ 45 min · do-it-yourself level

Reading about maths and doing maths are two different things. This short project lets you see the ideas of "Constructions and Tilings" at work with your own hands.

What you'll need

  • A compass
  • An unmarked ruler (straightedge)
  • Plain paper
  • Coloured paper for cut-out tiles

Steps

  1. Construct the perpendicular bisector of a drawn segment using equal arcs; check it crosses at the midpoint at 90°.
  2. Construct a 60° angle, then bisect it to get 30°.
  3. Cut several identical paper squares; try to tile a region with no gaps — see four meet at each point (4×90°=360°).
  4. Cut identical regular pentagons (trace a template); try to tile — observe the gaps left (108° doesn't divide 360°).
  5. Cut equilateral triangles or hexagons and confirm they DO tile, counting how many meet at each vertex.

🔍 What you should notice

Your constructions are exact without measuring. Squares, triangles and hexagons tile cleanly (angles total 360° at each vertex), while pentagons leave stubborn gaps.

Why it works

Equal radii force equidistance, making constructions exact. Tiling works only when the corner angles meeting at a point fill exactly 360°; shapes whose angle divides 360° (60°, 90°, 120°) tile, while 108° pentagons cannot.
