"What If?" — Constructions and Tilings

Hypothetical questions with no single right answer. Think hard before you read the discussion.

≈ 7 min read · brain-stretcher level

The textbook tells you what is. The best way to really understand an idea is to ask what if? Pick a question below, pause and think for a minute, then read one possible discussion.

What if 1 — regular pentagons could tile the floor?

The question: What if five-sided tiles fit with no gaps?

🧠 Think first

Add up the corner angles meeting at a point — do they reach 360°?

One discussion path: Three pentagons leave a gap (3 × 108° = 324°) and four overlap, so they can't tile. Only triangles, squares and hexagons fit perfectly, because their angles divide 360° exactly.

What if 2 — your straightedge had markings?

The question: What if you were allowed to measure during a construction?

🧠 Think first

Could you then trisect any angle?

One discussion path: Surprisingly, yes — a marked ruler makes the 'impossible' trisection possible. The classical puzzles are impossible only with an unmarked straightedge. The rules of the game decide what can be built.

What if 3 — bees built square cells, not hexagons?

The question: What if a honeycomb were made of squares?

🧠 Think first

Which shape stores most honey for the least wax?

One discussion path: Squares would work but waste wax; among shapes that tile, the hexagon encloses the most space for the least wall. The bees are quietly solving an optimisation problem.

📝 Stretch your thinking

None of these has one 'correct' answer — they are for arguing about. Try one on a friend or a parent and see whether their discussion path differs from yours.
