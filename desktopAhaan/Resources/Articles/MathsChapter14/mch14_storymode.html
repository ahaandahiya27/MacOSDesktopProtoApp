Maths can feel like it lives only inside the textbook. It doesn't. Here are real situations where the ideas of "Constructions and Tilings" quietly do the work.

Why honeycombs are hexagonal

Bees build hexagonal cells because three hexagons (3 × 120° = 360°) meet perfectly at each point with no wasted space, using the least wax wall for the most honey. The 360°-at-a-vertex rule, plus efficiency, explains a shape bees 'chose' millions of years before mathematicians proved it optimal.

Constructions without measuring

Draughtsmen, carpenters and masons construct exact right angles, midpoints and 60° angles using only a compass (or string) and straightedge — no protractor. The perpendicular bisector alone lets you find a point equally far from two others, used from temple layout to GPS.

Islamic geometric art and Escher

The intricate tilings of Islamic architecture and the impossible-looking tessellations of M.C. Escher both obey the same 360°-at-a-vertex rule you learn here. Tiling theory connects simple angle arithmetic to some of the world's most beautiful art.
