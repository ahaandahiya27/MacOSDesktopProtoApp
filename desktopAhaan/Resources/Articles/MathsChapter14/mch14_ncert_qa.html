Constructions and Tilings: Worked NCERT Questions

Textbook questions from this chapter, each with a clear worked answer.

&#8776; practice &middot; 8 questions

These are the kind of questions the NCERT chapter asks. Try each one yourself first, then check the worked answer.

Q1. How do you construct the perpendicular bisector of a segment XY?

Answer: From X and from Y, draw arcs of equal radius (more than half XY) above and below the line. Join the two points where the arcs intersect. This line bisects XY at right angles, because each intersection is equidistant from X and Y.

Q2. Why does the perpendicular bisector pass through the midpoint of XY?

Answer: Every point on it is equidistant from X and Y. The only point of XY equidistant from both ends is the midpoint, so the bisector crosses XY exactly there.

Q3. How can you construct a 60° angle with compass and straightedge?

Answer: Draw a ray; from its endpoint swing an arc; from where the arc meets the ray, swing the same radius to cut the arc; join the endpoint to that intersection. The angle is 60° because the construction makes an equilateral triangle, all of whose angles are 60°.

Q4. How do you construct a 30° angle?

Answer: Construct a 60° angle, then bisect it (arc across both arms, equal arcs from the crossings, join to the corner). Half of 60° is 30°.

Q5. What does it mean to tile (tessellate) a region?

Answer: To cover the region completely with shapes leaving no gaps and no overlaps, like floor tiles.

Q6. What condition must the angles meeting at a point satisfy in a tiling?

Answer: They must sum to exactly 360°. Less than 360° leaves a gap; more than 360° forces an overlap.

Q7. Why do squares and regular hexagons tile the plane, but regular pentagons do not?

Answer: Four squares (4×90°) and three hexagons (3×120°) each total 360° at a point. A regular pentagon's angle is 108°, and no whole number of 108° angles equals 360° (3 give 324°, 4 give 432°), so pentagons cannot tile alone.

Q8. How many equilateral triangles meet at each vertex in a triangular tiling?

Answer: Six, since each angle is 60° and 6 × 60° = 360°.
