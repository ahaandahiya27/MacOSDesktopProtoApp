Five Wrong Answers Class 7 Students Give

The five places almost every Class 7 student trips up on Chapter 15 — and the one-line fix for each.

≈ 5 min read · revision-tier

Examiners say the same wrong answers come back year after year. None of these mistakes are silly — they are almost right. That is exactly what makes them dangerous. Read each one, cover the answer, and try to spot what is off before peeking.

1. “We see things because our eyes give off light.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Eyes are RECEIVERS, not emitters. Light from a source (Sun, bulb) bounces off an object; some of that bounced light enters our eyes; the retina converts it to a signal our brain reads. Ancient Greeks (Plato) believed eyes shot out 'visual rays'. Modern science: no rays come out of your eyes. Test: in pitch dark, you see nothing — because there's nothing for your eyes to receive.

2. “Mirrors swap left and right.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Mirrors don't swap left-right or up-down. They swap FRONT and BACK. Your reflection's nose is at the SAME position as your nose (left or right). What flips is the depth axis — your reflection 'faces you' (its front is your front). This makes lifting your left hand look like lifting the mirror person's right hand — but only because they're facing you. A mirror image is a depth-reversed image, not a side-reversed one.

3. “A prism creates colours.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: A prism does NOT create colours. White light from the Sun is already a MIXTURE of all colours; the prism just separates them (because each colour bends by a different angle on entering glass). Newton proved this by recombining the colours with a second prism → white light again. The colours were always there; the prism is just the analyser.

4. “The rainbow is in the sky / has a beginning and end.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: A rainbow is an OPTICAL ILLUSION — a circle of light at a specific angle from the Sun-anti-point (the point exactly opposite the Sun). Each person sees a DIFFERENT rainbow (the one positioned for their eyes). You can never reach a rainbow's end — as you move, it moves with you. From an aeroplane, you can sometimes see a full CIRCLE rainbow because the ground doesn't block the bottom half.

5. “Glass and water always slow light down by the same amount.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Different substances slow light by different amounts. This is what 'refractive index' measures. Water slows light to 75% of its speed in vacuum (μ = 1.33). Glass slows it to ~67% (μ = 1.5). Diamond, dramatically, slows it to ~41% (μ = 2.42) — that's why diamonds sparkle so much (high refraction + reflection). Light bends more when the slowdown is greater.

📝 Stretch your thinking

Pick any two of these and explain them to a younger sibling using a one-line analogy each. If you can compress a mistake into one sentence with one image, you have understood it.
