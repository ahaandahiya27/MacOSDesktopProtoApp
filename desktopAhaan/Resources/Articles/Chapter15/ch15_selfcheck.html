Question 1 — from Chapter 15

A ray of light is incident at an angle of 60° on a surface. Find the angle of reflection in degrees.

🧠 Answer in your head first

Don't peek. Speak the answer out loud, then check.

Answer: 60°

Why: Apply the first law of reflection: angle of incidence = angle of reflection.

Question 2 — from Chapter 15

Why does a doctor use a concave mirror to examine ears, teeth, or the throat?

🧠 Answer in your head first

Don't peek. Speak the answer out loud, then check.

Answer: It forms a magnified, erect image when the object is close

Why: When an object is between a concave mirror and its focus, the image is virtual, erect, and enlarged.

Question 5 — from Chapter 15

Compare how a FIBRE OPTIC cable carries internet data with how a copper wire carried telephone signals 50 years ago. Explain WHY fibre is faster AND more efficient.

🧠 Answer in your head first

Don't peek. Speak the answer out loud, then check.

Answer: Copper wire: carries voice as electrical voltage variations. Signal weakens with distance (resistance heats wire, loses energy); needs amplification every few km. Bandwidth limited to ~kHz for voice. Fibre optic: carries digital data as PULSES OF LIGHT through glass. Light experiences total internal reflection at the fibre boundary, bouncing along without leaking. Almost zero signal loss; signal travels 100+ km without amplification. Bandwidth: terahertz (millions × kHz). Same modulation principle (turn signal on/off rapidly), vastly different physical carrier (light vs. electricity). Light is faster (3×10⁸ m/s vs. signal speed in copper), lossless over distance, and carries enormously more bandwidth.

Why: Copper: electrical signal, lossy, low bandwidth.
