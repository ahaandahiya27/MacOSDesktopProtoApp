A Class 7 Light Dictionary

The 10 must-know terms from Chapter 15 — in plain English plus the Hindi name, with a one-line meaning a Class 7 student can actually use.

The 10 terms to know

  • Reflection · परावर्तन — Bouncing back of light from a surface.
    Example: A mirror reflects light to form your image.
  • Refraction · अपवर्तन — Bending of light as it passes from one medium to another.
    Example: A spoon in water looks bent because of refraction at the water-air boundary.
  • Dispersion · विक्षेपण — Splitting of white light into its constituent colours.
    Example: A glass prism disperses sunlight into a rainbow spectrum.
  • Convex mirror · उत्तल दर्पण — A mirror whose reflecting surface curves outward; gives wider field of view but smaller image.
    Example: Car side mirrors and shopping mall corner mirrors.
  • Concave mirror · अवतल दर्पण — A mirror whose reflecting surface curves inward; can magnify when object is close.
    Example: Dentist's mirror; shaving mirror.
  • Convex lens · उत्तल लेंस — A lens that is thicker in the middle; converges light to a focus.
    Example: Magnifying glass; lens in your eye.
  • Concave lens · अवतल लेंस — A lens that is thinner in the middle; diverges light.
    Example: Spectacles for short-sighted people (myopia correction).
  • Virtual image · आभासी प्रतिबिम्ब — An image that appears to be at a place where light doesn't actually reach; can't be projected on a screen.
    Example: Your reflection in a plane mirror is a virtual image.
  • Real image · वास्तविक प्रतिबिम्ब — An image formed where light actually converges; CAN be projected on a screen.
    Example: Image on a cinema screen, formed by the projector's lens.
  • Periscope · पेरिस्कोप — A device using two parallel mirrors to see over an obstacle or around a corner.
    Example: Submarines use periscopes to see above water while underwater.

💬 How to use this

Cover the right side of each row. Read just the term. See if you can speak the meaning out loud in one sentence. Move on when you can do that for every term — not before.
