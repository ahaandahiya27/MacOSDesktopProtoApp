Beyond the Book — Three Mind-Stretchers About Light

Three ideas that don't show up in the NCERT textbook but will change how you see this chapter.

≈ 9 min read · curious-kid level

The chapter teaches you light travels in straight lines, bounces off mirrors (reflection), and bends through lenses. This article goes a step further — three stretch topics that lead naturally into Class 8 and beyond. Read one a day; don't try to absorb them all at once.

1. Lens formula — predicting where the image forms

Class 7 says lenses converge or diverge light. Class 9 introduces the LENS FORMULA: 1/v - 1/u = 1/f. Where u = object distance from lens, v = image distance, f = focal length (a lens property). All distances signed (sign convention varies; here, object distances negative, image distances positive for real images). Example: object at 30 cm from a +10 cm convex lens. 1/v - 1/(-30) = 1/10. So 1/v = 1/10 - 1/30 = 2/30. v = 15 cm — image forms 15 cm beyond lens, REAL and inverted. The camera in your phone, the projector in your school auditorium, your eye's natural lens — all obey this formula. MAGNIFICATION m = v/u = h'/h tells you size + orientation.

2. Total internal reflection — the physics behind BharatNet's optical fibres

Class 7 says light refracts at boundaries. Class 10 reveals a special case. When light travels from DENSE medium (water, glass) to LESS DENSE (air) at a steep angle, it can be TOTALLY REFLECTED back — no refraction at all. The threshold is called the CRITICAL ANGLE: for water-air, ~48.6°. For glass-air, ~42°. Above this angle, 100% internal reflection. This is the principle of OPTICAL FIBRES — light enters one end of a glass strand at a shallow angle; it hits the wall at ABOVE-critical angles, totally reflects, bounces along the fibre, exits the other end. India's BharatNet has laid 6 lakh km of these. ENT doctors use endoscopes (fibre bundles) to see inside your body via the throat. Same physics.

3. Wave optics — why CDs make rainbows and Newton's rings

Class 7 says white light splits into colours in a prism. Class 12 reveals light is a WAVE with specific wavelengths — red ~700 nm, violet ~400 nm. INTERFERENCE: when two wave-fronts overlap, they ADD or CANCEL — creating bright + dark bands. Why CDs show rainbows: the closely-spaced tracks (microscopic ridges) act as a 'diffraction grating' — they split white light into spectral colours. Same effect: oil films on water (the iridescent sheen), butterfly wings, the gem-like surfaces of beetles. Newton's rings: place a curved glass on a flat glass; the tiny air gap between them creates circular interference patterns. India's homegrown CSIR optics labs use these patterns to measure microscopic surface defects to a fraction of light wavelength.
