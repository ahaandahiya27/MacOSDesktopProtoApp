Q1. Fill in the blanks. (a) An image that cannot be obtained on a screen is called ______. (b) Image formed by a ______ mirror is always of the same size as that of the object. (c) An image formed by a ______ mirror is always virtual and smaller in size. (d) A ______ image is always erect. (e) An image formed by a plane mirror is always ______ image, of the ______ size and is ______ from the mirror as the object is in front of it.

Model answer: (a) virtual (b) plane (c) convex (d) virtual (e) virtual, same, same distance.

Q2. Mark 'T' if statement is true and 'F' if it is false. (a) We can obtain an enlarged and erect image by a convex mirror. (b) A concave lens always forms a virtual image. (c) We can obtain a real, enlarged and inverted image by a concave mirror. (d) A real image cannot be obtained on a screen. (e) A concave mirror always forms a real image.

Model answer: (a) F — convex mirrors always form virtual, smaller images. (b) T — concave lenses always form virtual, smaller images. (c) T — concave mirrors can do this when object is between F and C. (d) F — a real image CAN be obtained on a screen (that's the definition). (e) F — concave mirrors form virtual images when the object is between mirror and focus.

Q4. State the characteristics of the image formed by a plane mirror.

Model answer: (1) Same size as the object. (2) Same distance behind the mirror as the object is in front. (3) Virtual (can't be projected on screen). (4) Erect (upright). (5) Laterally inverted (left appears right and vice versa — or technically depth-reversed, but appears to most observers as left-right swap).

Q5. Find out the letters of the English alphabet or any other language known to you in which the image formed in a plane mirror appears exactly like the letter itself.

Model answer: Letters that look identical to their mirror image: A, H, I, M, O, T, U, V, W, X, Y. These letters are vertically symmetric (left half mirrors right half perfectly). Try writing your name and look at it in a mirror — only symmetric letters look 'right'. In Hindi: अ, ओ have some symmetry; in Devanagari most letters do NOT mirror well. This is why ambulances write the word 'AMBULANCE' backwards on the front — so cars seeing it in their rearview mirror see it the right way around.

Q6. What is a kaleidoscope?

Model answer: A kaleidoscope is a tube with three flat mirrors arranged inside forming a triangular prism, with coloured beads or paper bits at one end and a viewing hole at the other. Light from the object end reflects multiple times between the three mirrors, creating beautiful symmetrical patterns. Each tiny movement of the bead end produces a completely new pattern because the geometry of reflections changes. Invented by David Brewster in 1816; based purely on multiple reflection. The same principle is used in kaleidoscope-style geometry tools in textile design.

Q7. Draw a labelled sketch of a human eye.

Model answer: Key labelled parts: CORNEA (transparent outer covering, does most of the focusing), IRIS (coloured ring, controls how much light enters), PUPIL (the black centre hole through the iris), LENS (clear focuses fine-tunes the focus, can change shape), CILIARY MUSCLES (change lens shape), RETINA (light-sensitive layer at the back where image forms), OPTIC NERVE (carries signal to brain), BLIND SPOT (where optic nerve attaches, no light-sensitive cells). The eye is a self-focusing camera with biological autofocus.

Q8. Sun appears red at sunrise and sunset. Why?

Model answer: At sunrise/sunset, the Sun's light travels a LONGER path through Earth's atmosphere (slanted angle vs straight overhead at noon). The longer path scatters more of the shorter-wavelength colours (blue, violet) AWAY from your line of sight. What remains, reaching your eye, is mostly the longer-wavelength colours: red, orange, yellow. So the Sun appears reddish. Same reason the sky around the Sun is yellow/orange at this time. This is Rayleigh scattering — Class 7's first hint at what makes the sky blue.
