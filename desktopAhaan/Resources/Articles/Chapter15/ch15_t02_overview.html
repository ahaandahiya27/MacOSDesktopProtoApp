Refraction, Prism & Rainbow

Topic 2 of Chapter 15 · Class 7 Science

The big idea

Topic 1 was about light bouncing — reflection. This topic is about light bending — what happens when light leaves one transparent material and enters another (air to water, air to glass, water to air). The bending is called refraction, and it explains pools that look shallower than they are, the rainbow that appears after rain, and the colours hiding inside white sunlight.

One sentence: when light changes speed it changes direction. Light is fastest in vacuum, slower in air, slower still in water, slower still in glass.

Where you've already seen refraction

  • A pencil dipped in a glass of water looks broken at the surface.
  • The legs of someone standing in a swimming pool look shorter.
  • A coin at the bottom of a glass of water looks closer to the surface than it really is.
  • Stars seem to twinkle because air pockets at different temperatures bend their light by different amounts.

The prism magic trick

A glass prism doesn't just bend a ray of light — it bends each colour by a slightly different amount. Violet bends the most, red the least. So a single beam of white light spreads out into a fan of seven colours: V·I·B·G·Y·O·R (Violet, Indigo, Blue, Green, Yellow, Orange, Red). That's called dispersion.

The rainbow is many prisms in the sky

A rainbow is what happens when a million raindrops act like a million tiny prisms after a shower. Each drop refracts the sunlight, reflects it off its back, and refracts it again on the way out — splitting it into colours that we see at a precise angle.
