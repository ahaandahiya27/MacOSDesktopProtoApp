Reflection & Mirrors

Topic 1 of Chapter 15 · Class 7 Science

The big idea

Light travels in straight lines, but when it hits a smooth surface — a polished mirror, a still puddle of water, a window pane — it bounces back. That bouncing is called reflection, and the rule for how it bounces is simple, exact, and the same in every situation.

Law of reflection: the angle the light hits the mirror at equals the angle it leaves at. Both angles are measured from the normal — an imaginary line drawn at 90° to the mirror.

Three kinds of mirrors

The shape of a mirror's surface decides what kind of image you see:

  • Plane mirrors are flat — you see yourself life-size, the right way up, but mirrored left-to-right. The mirror in your bathroom is a plane mirror.
  • Concave mirrors curve inward like the inside of a spoon. Hold one close to your face and you see yourself larger. Hold it far away and the image flips upside down.
  • Convex mirrors curve outward. They make everything look smaller, but they show a wider view. That's why they're used on car wing mirrors and at the entrance of shops.
