Light

Chapter 15 · Class 7 Science

What is light?

Light is a form of energy that travels in straight lines at the highest speed in the universe — about 300,000 km per second. We see objects because light bounces off them and enters our eyes.

Reflection — light bouncing off mirrors

When light strikes a smooth surface, it bounces. The Law of Reflection says:

Angle of incidence = angle of reflection. Both are measured from the normal — an imaginary line at 90° to the mirror's surface.

Three kinds of mirrors:

  • Plane mirror — flat. Image same size, upright, behind the mirror. Used in bathrooms and dressing rooms.
  • Concave mirror — curves inward. Close-up object → magnified, upright (used in shaving mirrors, dentist mirrors, makeup mirrors). Far object → smaller, inverted (used in telescope mirrors).
  • Convex mirror — curves outward. Always gives smaller, upright image with a wider field of view. Used in car side mirrors and shop security.

Refraction — light bending

When light passes from one transparent material to another (air to water, air to glass), it changes speed and bends. That's why a pencil in a glass of water looks broken at the surface.

Prism and the rainbow

White light is actually a mix of seven colours: Violet, Indigo, Blue, Green, Yellow, Orange, Red — remember as VIBGYOR. A glass prism bends each colour by a different amount, fanning them out. The same thing happens in millions of raindrops — that's a rainbow.

Lenses — bending light to make images

  • Convex (converging) lens — thicker in the middle. Converges light. Used in magnifying glasses, cameras, microscopes — and the lens of your own eye.
  • Concave (diverging) lens — thinner in the middle. Spreads light apart. Used in spectacles for short-sightedness.

Mirrors with a purpose

  • Periscope — two mirrors at 45° let submarines see above water.
  • Kaleidoscope — three mirrors form symmetric patterns from a few coloured beads.
  • Headlight — concave mirror with a bulb at its focus → parallel beam.
