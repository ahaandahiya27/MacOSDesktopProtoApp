Refraction

Chapter 15 · Topic 2 · Concept 1

Light slows down in glass and water

In a vacuum, light travels at the universe's speed limit — about 300,000 km per second. In air it's almost as fast. But in water it slows to about 75% of that, and in glass to about 66%. Whenever light moves from a fast medium into a slow one (or vice versa), it changes direction at the boundary. That bending is refraction.

Why does it bend? Imagine a marching band stepping from a hard road onto a sandy beach at an angle. The marchers who hit the sand first slow down first, so the whole line pivots. Light at a boundary does exactly that.

Bending rule

  • From a faster medium into a slower medium (e.g. air → water): the ray bends towards the normal.
  • From a slower medium into a faster medium (e.g. water → air): the ray bends away from the normal.
  • If the ray hits the boundary straight on (along the normal), it doesn't bend — only its speed changes.

The pencil-in-water illusion

Dip a straight pencil into a glass of water at an angle. Light bouncing off the underwater part travels up through the water, then bends at the surface as it enters the air. Your eye traces the bent rays back as straight lines — so you "see" the underwater part shifted to a different position. The brain interprets the shift as a broken pencil.

Why a pool looks shallower than it is

Same trick: light from a coin at the bottom of a pool refracts as it leaves the water. Your eye traces the rays back in straight lines and places the coin closer to the surface than its real depth. A 2-metre-deep pool looks more like 1.5 m. People often misjudge — and slip — because of this illusion.

Refractive index — a Class 10 preview

Each transparent material has a single number that says "how much it slows light down". It's called the refractive index (n).

  • Air ≈ 1.00 (essentially the reference)
  • Water ≈ 1.33
  • Glass ≈ 1.50
  • Diamond ≈ 2.42 (this is why diamonds sparkle — they bend light a lot)

Try this at home

The reappearing coin. Drop a coin into an opaque bowl. Step back until the rim hides the coin from view. Without moving your head, ask someone to slowly pour water into the bowl. The coin "appears" — water refraction bends the light around the rim into your eye. (Bonus: you can also use a transparent glass and stir slowly to see the pencil-bend.)

Looking ahead

In Class 10 (Light — Reflection & Refraction) you'll meet the law that exactly describes this bend: Snell's Law, n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂. In Class 12 / JEE Physics, refraction becomes the basis of:

  • Total Internal Reflection and the critical angle (why a diamond sparkles; how optical fibres carry the internet).
  • Apparent depth — actual depth divided by refractive index.
  • Mirage formation — hot road appearing wet because of refraction in heated air layers.

NEET Biology uses the same principles to describe how the cornea + lens of the human eye refract light onto the retina.
