Lenses & Optical Instruments

Topic 3 of Chapter 15 · Class 7 Science

The big idea

Topic 1 was light bouncing. Topic 2 was light bending as it crossed boundaries. Topic 3 puts those ideas to work in real devices — pieces of curved glass and clever mirror arrangements that let us magnify the very small, see around the corner, or build symmetric patterns out of a few coloured beads.

One sentence: a lens is a piece of curved glass that bends light by refraction; instruments like the periscope and the kaleidoscope use plane mirrors to redirect or multiply images.

Two kinds of lens, two completely different jobs

  • A convex (converging) lens is thicker in the middle. It gathers parallel rays of light and brings them to a single focal point. It magnifies things you hold close to it — like a magnifying glass — and forms real, inverted images of things that are far away.
  • A concave (diverging) lens is thinner in the middle. It spreads parallel rays apart. It always gives a smaller, upright, virtual image, no matter how far or close the object.

Two instruments built from plane mirrors

  • A periscope uses two plane mirrors set at 45°. Light from above the wall (or above the water) bounces off the top mirror, travels down a tube, bounces off the bottom mirror, and into your eye. Submarines use this to see above water without surfacing.
  • A kaleidoscope uses two or three plane mirrors arranged in a triangular tube, with a few coloured beads at one end. Every reflection of the beads in every mirror creates a stunning symmetric pattern that changes when you twist the tube.

Looking ahead

Lenses become formal in Class 10 with the lens formula 1/v − 1/u = 1/f, sign conventions, and the power of a lens P = 1/f measured in dioptres (D). Class 10's "Human Eye" chapter explains myopia (corrected with concave lenses) and hypermetropia (corrected with convex lenses).

In Class 12 / JEE Physics you'll meet the lensmaker's equation, the combined-lens formula, and design problems for the simple microscope, compound microscope, and the astronomical telescope. NEET Biology uses the same physics to describe how the eye's cornea + crystalline lens together project a real, inverted image onto the retina.
