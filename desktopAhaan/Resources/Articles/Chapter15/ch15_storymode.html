Story Mode — Light in Five Scenes

5 short scenes from real life that bring this chapter to a kitchen table, a garden, a classroom — somewhere you can picture.

≈ 10 min read · narrative-tier

Textbook examples can feel abstract. These five scenes don't happen in a textbook — they happen in places you go. Read them in order; each scene tightens the chapter's big idea by another notch.

Scene 1 — Why a car's right rear-view mirror says 'objects closer than they appear'

A car's right rear-view mirror is a CONVEX mirror — bulging outward. Convex mirrors give a wider field of view (you can see more lanes at once) but make objects look smaller and farther. That's why the warning is printed: a car you see at 50 m in this mirror is actually at maybe 35 m. Convex mirrors are also at every Indian road blind-spot and shopping mall corner — wide-angle is more important than accurate distance.

Scene 2 — Why a spoon looks bent in your dal

Dip a spoon into your dal. The part underwater appears BENT at the surface. Reason: light bends (refracts) as it passes from water to air. Your brain assumes light travels in straight lines, so it 'sees' the spoon where the lines APPEAR to come from — not where the spoon actually is. This is refraction in action — same physics that makes ponds look shallower than they are.

Scene 3 — Rainbows after monsoon showers over Goa

After a heavy rain in Goa, late afternoon, if you stand with the Sun behind you, you'll see a rainbow over the sea. The Sun's light enters each raindrop, refracts as it enters water, reflects off the back of the drop, then refracts again as it exits. The different colours bend by different amounts — red bends least, violet most — separating white light into a spectrum. Each colour appears as an arc at a specific angle from the Sun. You only see your OWN rainbow — the one positioned for YOUR eye.

Scene 4 — BharatNet optical fibre carrying internet to villages

When you load a YouTube video in your village, the data travels (most of the way) through hair-thin glass strands — optical fibres. Light pulses bounce inside the fibre by total internal reflection. The light enters at one end, bounces along the inside surface (never escaping because the angle is too shallow), and emerges at the other end. India's BharatNet initiative laid 6 lakh km of optical fibre to connect 2.5 lakh village panchayats. Light carries your WhatsApp.

Scene 5 — Why the IIA Hanle Observatory is in Ladakh

The Indian Institute of Astrophysics built its main telescope at Hanle, Ladakh (4500 m altitude). Why so remote? At high altitude, the air is thin — less refraction, sharper images. No city lights for hundreds of km — dark skies. Cold and dry — no humidity to scatter light. The 2-metre telescope there sees stars billions of light-years away. Class 7 lenses → Hanle's enormous lens system → galaxies. Same physics; different scale.
