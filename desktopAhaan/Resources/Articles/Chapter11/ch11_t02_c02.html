Inside a Kidney — Nephrons, Filtration and Reabsorption

How blood is cleaned 60 times a day · Class 7 Science

Two fist-sized super-filters

Each kidney is shaped like a bean, about 12 cm long, holding nearly 1 million nephrons. Together, the two kidneys filter your entire blood volume (~5 litres) about 60 times every day.

Step 1 — filtration in the glomerulus

Blood enters a tiny coil of capillaries called the glomerulus. High pressure forces water, salts, glucose and urea OUT of the blood through tiny pores. Big things like red blood cells and proteins stay in.

Step 2 — reabsorption in the tubule

The filtered fluid runs through a long tubule. As it does, all the glucose, most of the water, and most of the salts are reabsorbed back into the blood. Without this step you'd urinate ~170 litres a day — instead you make only ~1.5 litres.

Step 3 — the final urine

What's left after reabsorption is the urine: water, urea, excess salts, and waste. It travels via the ureter → bladder → urethra → out. The kidney has just done in a few seconds what would take an industrial filter plant several hours.

If even one kidney is healthy, you can live a completely normal life — which is why kidney transplants from a living donor are so common. The donor's remaining kidney enlarges and takes over the work of two.

Try this at home

Drink a glass of water and time how long until you feel the urge to urinate (usually 30–60 min). That's roughly how long your kidneys take to process the extra water and signal the bladder.

Looking ahead
