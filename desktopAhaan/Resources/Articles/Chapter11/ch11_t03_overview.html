Transport in Plants

Topic t03 of Chapter 11 · Transportation in Animals and Plants · Class 7 Science

The big idea

Plants don't have a heart, but they move water and food just as effectively. Water enters through the roots, climbs the xylem all the way to the leaves, and evaporates out through stomata — a process called transpiration. That evaporation creates suction that pulls more water up, like sipping through a straw powered by sunlight. Phloem carries sugars made in the leaves to wherever the plant needs energy or storage.

Xylem, Phloem, Transpiration. Unlike the one-way xylem, phloem can flow in any direction — sugars travel from leaves DOWN to roots in summer, and UP from stored roots in spring.
