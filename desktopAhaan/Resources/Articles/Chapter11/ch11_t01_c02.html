Blood Components

RBCs, WBCs, platelets, plasma — and what each one does · Class 7 Science

Blood isn't just red fluid

If you spin a tube of blood in a centrifuge for 10 minutes, it separates into layers:

  • Bottom 45%: dark red — red blood cells (RBCs).
  • Thin middle layer (1%): whitish — white blood cells (WBCs) and platelets.
  • Top 54%: straw-yellow liquid — plasma.

Each component does a different job; all four are essential.

RBCs — the oxygen ferries

  • Shaped like flattened doughnuts (biconcave discs) — about 7 μm across.
  • 5 million per cubic millimetre of blood. You make 2 million NEW ones every second.
  • Packed with haemoglobin, an iron-based pigment that grabs O₂ in the lungs and releases it at body tissues.
  • One RBC carries up to 1 BILLION oxygen molecules at a time.
  • Live for ~120 days, then the spleen recycles them.
  • Have no nucleus (unlike most cells) — they're packed with as much haemoglobin as possible.
The red of blood comes from iron at the centre of haemoglobin molecules — the same iron that makes rust red. That's why low haemoglobin (anaemia) leaves you pale and tired: less oxygen reaches your muscles.

WBCs — the immune army

Much fewer than RBCs (only 5,000–10,000 per mm³) but much larger (12-20 μm). Five main types, each with a job:

  • Neutrophils (most common) — engulf bacteria.
  • Lymphocytes (B-cells, T-cells) — make antibodies and kill virus-infected cells.
  • Monocytes / macrophages — eat dead cells and debris.
  • Eosinophils — fight parasites and allergens.
  • Basophils — release histamine in inflammation.

WBCs are mobile — they can leave the blood vessels and crawl through tissues to reach infection sites.

Platelets — the wound-pluggers

  • Tiny cell fragments (2-4 μm). Not full cells.
  • 150,000–450,000 per mm³.
  • When a vessel is damaged, platelets stick to the damaged wall and to each other, forming a plug.
  • They release chemicals that trigger the cascade producing fibrin — the protein mesh that catches RBCs and forms the visible blood clot.

Plasma — the liquid carrier

Mostly water (~92%), plus dissolved:

  • Proteins (~7%): albumin (keeps fluid in vessels), globulins (antibodies), fibrinogen (clotting).
  • Glucose (food for cells).
  • Salts (Na⁺, K⁺, Ca²⁺, etc).
  • Hormones (chemical messengers from glands).
  • Urea and other wastes on their way to the kidneys.
  • Dissolved CO₂ being carried back to the lungs.

Try this at home

Most schools have prepared blood-smear slides. Under a microscope you'll see thousands of small red discs (RBCs), an occasional large white-bodied cell with a darkly-stained nucleus (WBC), and tiny irregular speckles (platelets) — exactly the proportions described above.

Looking ahead

Class 12 Biology 'Body Fluids and Circulation' covers all four blood components in clinical detail — leukaemia, anaemia, thalassemia, sickle-cell anaemia, haemophilia. NEET tests these every year.
