A Class 7 Transportation in Animals and Plants Dictionary

The 10 must-know terms from Chapter 11 — in plain English plus the Hindi name, with a one-line meaning a Class 7 student can actually use.

The 10 terms to know

  • Heart · हृदय — The muscular organ that pumps blood through the body; in humans, a four-chambered fist-sized muscle.
    Example: An average human heart beats ~100,000 times per day, pumping ~7,500 litres of blood.
  • Artery · धमनी — A blood vessel that carries blood AWAY from the heart, usually oxygenated.
    Example: The aorta, the body's biggest artery, is as wide as a garden hose.
  • Vein · शिरा — A blood vessel that carries blood BACK to the heart, usually deoxygenated.
    Example: Veins have valves that prevent backflow against gravity.
  • Capillary · केशिका — The smallest blood vessels — single cell thick — where actual exchange of nutrients/gases happens.
    Example: An adult body has ~40 billion capillaries totalling ~80,000 km in length.
  • Haemoglobin · हीमोग्लोबिन — The iron-containing molecule in red blood cells that carries oxygen.
    Example: Anaemia is low haemoglobin — common in India, often due to iron deficiency.
  • Kidney · गुर्दा — Two bean-shaped organs that filter blood and produce urine.
    Example: Each kidney has ~1 million nephrons; together they filter ~180 litres of blood plasma per day.
  • Nephron · नेफ्रॉन — The microscopic filtering unit of the kidney.
    Example: Each kidney has ~1 million nephrons, each like a tiny filter + selective re-absorption tube.
  • Xylem · जाइलम — The plant tissue that carries water and minerals from roots to leaves.
    Example: Cut a celery stalk in coloured water — the colour rises through xylem visibly.
  • Phloem · फ्लोएम — The plant tissue that carries food (sugar) from leaves to roots and storage organs.
    Example: Ringing a tree (cutting away a strip of bark, including phloem) eventually kills it because roots starve.
  • Transpiration · वाष्पोत्सर्जन — The evaporation of water from plant leaves through stomata.
    Example: A mature mango tree can transpire 200+ litres of water on a hot day — the equivalent of running a tap for hours.
