Question 1 — from Chapter 11

What is heartbeat? What are systole and diastole?

🧠 Answer in your head first

Don't peek. Speak the answer out loud, then check.

Answer: Heartbeat is the continuous rhythmic contraction and relaxation of the heart that pumps blood through the body. The contraction phase is called systole; the relaxation phase is called diastole. The normal human heartbeat is about 70-72 times per minute at rest.

Why: Heartbeat = rhythmic pumping cycle of the heart.

Question 2 — from Chapter 11

Pulse is the:

Why: When the heart contracts, it sends a pressure wave through the arteries.

Question 3 — from Chapter 11

Compare a HEART ATTACK with a STROKE — both involve blood-supply failure, but to different organs and with different consequences. Explain the parallels and the differences.

🧠 Answer in your head first

Don't peek. Speak the answer out loud, then check.

Answer: Both: a blood vessel gets blocked (usually by a clot), starving downstream tissue of oxygen. Heart attack: blockage in a CORONARY artery (the heart's own blood supply) → heart muscle tissue dies. Symptoms: chest pain, shortness of breath. Damage: muscle scar reduces pumping capacity. Stroke: blockage in a BRAIN artery → brain tissue dies. Symptoms: sudden weakness on one side, slurred speech, vision loss. Damage: lost function depends on which brain region died (paralysis, language loss, etc.). Treatment for both: clot-busting drugs within ~3 hours, then surgery if needed. Speed matters in BOTH — every minute, more tissue dies.

Why: Parallel: vessel blocked → downstream tissue starves.

Question 5 — from Chapter 11

A leaf is connected to the rest of the tree by a single PETIOLE (stalk) containing both xylem and phloem in concentric rings. Predict what happens if you cut HALFWAY through the petiole (severing xylem only) vs. cutting all the way through.

🧠 Answer in your head first

Don't peek. Speak the answer out loud, then check.

Answer: Halfway cut (xylem severed, phloem intact): water transport from roots STOPS for this leaf → leaf wilts within hours and eventually dies. Phloem still connects, so sugars from neighboring leaves can reach this dying leaf, but without water, photosynthesis stops + tissue collapses. Full cut (both xylem and phloem): leaf dies faster — no water AND no sugar input. Tree responds by abscission (drops the leaf within 1-2 weeks via the abscission zone). The petiole reveals the dual-transport design: lose either, lose the leaf; lose both, immediate consequence.

Why: Identify the two transport systems: xylem (up to leaf with water) + phloem (down/sideways with sugars).
