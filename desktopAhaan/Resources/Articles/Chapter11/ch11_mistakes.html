Five Wrong Answers Class 7 Students Give

The five places almost every Class 7 student trips up on Chapter 11 — and the one-line fix for each.

≈ 5 min read · revision-tier

Examiners say the same wrong answers come back year after year. None of these mistakes are silly — they are almost right. That is exactly what makes them dangerous. Read each one, cover the answer, and try to spot what is off before peeking.

1. “All veins carry blue blood; all arteries carry red blood.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Veins are NOT blue — they only look blue through skin because of how light scatters. Inside, veins carry dark red deoxygenated blood; arteries carry bright red oxygenated blood. There's ONE exception: the pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood (lung to right ventricle) and pulmonary vein carries oxygenated (lung to left atrium). 'Arteries carry oxygenated blood' is mostly true except here.

2. “The heart is on the left side of the chest.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Your heart is approximately in the MIDDLE of your chest, slightly behind the breastbone, tilted leftward. The 'left-side' feeling comes from the left ventricle (the most muscular pumping chamber) being on the left, and its strong contraction is what you feel as 'heart on the left'. The heart organ itself spans both sides.

3. “Drinking lots of water flushes out toxins.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Kidneys filter blood and produce a constant amount of urine regardless of how much you drink, within reason. Drinking too much water just produces more dilute urine, not 'more toxin removal'. Kidneys excrete what your body has produced (urea, excess salts) — they don't pull random poisons from your body. Drink for thirst; over-drinking can dangerously dilute blood sodium.

4. “Plants drink water like animals.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Plants don't 'drink' actively. Water enters root hairs by osmosis, then climbs xylem because the leaves are LOSING water through transpiration. The lost water creates suction that pulls water up — like sipping through a straw. The plant doesn't consciously drink; transpiration evaporation passively powers the whole system.

5. “Sweating is just water leaving the body.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Sweat is mostly water but also contains salts (sodium, potassium), urea, and trace amounts of other excretory products. Heavy sweating loses electrolytes — that's why ORS / nimbu-pani-with-salt is given after dehydration. Sweat also helps cool the body (evaporation removes heat) and lubricate skin. Sweat is part of the excretion system, not just air-conditioning.

📝 Stretch your thinking

Pick any two of these and explain them to a younger sibling using a one-line analogy each. If you can compress a mistake into one sentence with one image, you have understood it.
