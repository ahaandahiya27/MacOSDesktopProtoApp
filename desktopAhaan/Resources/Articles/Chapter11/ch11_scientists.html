Every chapter has a person behind it — someone who first asked the right question, ran the right experiment, or wrote down the answer. This is a one-page portrait of the scientist most tied to this chapter's content.

Sushruta

circa 600 BCE · Indian (Ancient)

🎧 In one line

Wrote the world's first systematic description of the circulatory system + the body's energy channels — and pioneered surgery 2500 years before modern medicine.

Sushruta lived in Varanasi around 600 BCE. He compiled the Sushruta Samhita, a medical encyclopaedia of 184 chapters. It describes the human body as a network of marma points (vital energy centres, often near major blood vessels) and srotas (channels of fluid flow). Sushruta described 700 medicinal plants, 1100 conditions, 121 surgical instruments, and 300+ surgical procedures including cataract removal, plastic surgery (rhinoplasty — rebuilding a cut nose from forehead skin, still called the 'Indian method'), and the removal of bladder stones. He even described, in poetic terms, the rhythm of the pulse and what it could reveal about the body's state — a thousand years before William Harvey 'discovered' circulation in 1628 Europe. Sushruta's pulse diagnosis, modified, is still part of Ayurvedic practice in India today. He's the patron of Indian surgery; AIIMS Delhi's surgery department building is named after him.
