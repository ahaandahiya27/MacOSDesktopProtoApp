Three "What Ifs?" About Transportation in Animals and Plants

Hypothetical questions with no single right answer. Think hard before you read the discussion.

≈ 7 min read · brain-stretcher level

The textbook tells you what is. The best science teachers also ask what if? Pick any one of the questions below. Pause. Think for at least one minute before scrolling. Then read the discussion.

What if 1 — What if your kidneys stopped working?

🧠 Think first

Pause. Write down your one-sentence guess before reading.

One discussion path: Within hours, urea and other wastes would build up in your blood — a condition called uraemia. Within days, you'd feel sick, weak, confused. Without treatment, death within ~2 weeks. Treatment: dialysis (artificial kidney machine that filters your blood 3 times a week) or kidney transplant. India has ~200,000 dialysis patients and ~5000 kidney transplants per year. Your kidneys are silent — you don't notice them until they're gone.

What if 2 — What if humans had two-chambered hearts like fish?

🧠 Think first

Pause. Write down your one-sentence guess before reading.

One discussion path: We'd be much less active and probably cold-blooded. A two-chambered heart only pumps blood in one circuit (heart → gills → body → heart). This means oxygen-poor blood mixes with oxygen-rich blood, and the pump can't generate enough pressure to deliver oxygen at high rates. Fish work because they're slow-moving and in cool water. Humans need a 4-chambered heart with double circulation to support our high metabolic rate, large body, and warm-blooded existence. Heart evolution = activity level.

What if 3 — What if plants didn't transpire?

🧠 Think first

Pause. Write down your one-sentence guess before reading.

One discussion path: They wouldn't be able to grow tall. Transpiration is the only way water reaches the top of a tall tree — there's no pump. Without it, even a small mango tree (10 m) couldn't get water to the top leaves. Plants would all be ground-cover height. They'd also overheat — transpiration is plant's air-conditioning. The global water cycle would change: forests transpire ~25% of all rainfall back to atmosphere; without that, monsoons would weaken significantly. Tropical rainforests like Amazon are essentially giant water pumps.

💬 How to use this

These are not exam questions. The answer marked ‘one discussion path’ is one valid line of reasoning — your own may be different and still good. Practising this kind of thinking is how scientists become scientists.
