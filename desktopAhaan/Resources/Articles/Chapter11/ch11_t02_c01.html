How Kidneys Filter Blood

Two bean-shaped lifesavers · Class 7 Science

The body's chemistry purifier

Every cell in your body produces waste — old proteins, broken-down hormones, excess salt, leftover urea from protein metabolism. If these chemicals built up, they'd poison you within days. Your two kidneys remove them by filtering all your blood, dozens of times a day.

The numbers are remarkable

  • Each kidney is roughly the size of your fist.
  • Together they filter 180 litres of blood per day — more than your body's total blood volume (5 L) cycling through 36 times.
  • From those 180 L, only 1.5 L leaves the body as urine. The other 99.2% is reabsorbed.

How filtration works

Inside each kidney are about a million microscopic filtering units called nephrons. Each nephron has three parts:

  1. Glomerulus — a tight knot of capillaries inside a cup-shaped Bowman's capsule. Blood pressure forces water + small molecules (glucose, salt, urea) OUT of the capillaries into the capsule. Large molecules (proteins, blood cells) stay in.
  2. Tubule (Loop of Henle) — a long winding tube. The body REABSORBS what it wants to keep — water, glucose, useful salts — back into nearby capillaries.
  3. Collecting duct — the leftover liquid (urine) drains here and out to the bladder.
The kidney's two-step trick — filter EVERYTHING out, then pull most of it back in — sounds wasteful but works brilliantly. It catches all wastes without having to identify them individually.

What kidneys keep balanced

  • Water — drink more → kidneys excrete more; drink less → kidneys retain water.
  • Salt — eat salty food → more salt in urine.
  • Urea — the waste from protein breakdown. About 30 g per day.
  • pH — kidneys keep blood pH near 7.4, even when you eat acidic or alkaline foods.

When kidneys fail

Kidney failure means wastes build up rapidly. Patients require dialysis — an external machine that does the kidney's job, 3 times a week, 4 hours each session. Or a kidney transplant. India has one of the world's highest rates of kidney disease, often linked to diabetes and high blood pressure.

Try this at home

Drink 500 mL of water all at once. Note the time. Within 30–60 minutes you'll need to urinate. That's your kidneys filtering the extra water out of your blood. Drink no extra water for several hours — your kidneys will reabsorb more water and your urine becomes darker yellow (more concentrated).
