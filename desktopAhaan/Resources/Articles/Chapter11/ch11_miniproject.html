Two Mini Projects for Chapter 11

Hands-on activities you can do today — with materials from the kitchen, not the lab. Each project takes about an hour and turns chapter ideas into something you can see and touch.

≈ 60 min total · hands-on

Reading is not the same as doing. These mini projects ask you to use your hands — predict, observe, and write down what surprises you. Try at least one before the next chapter test.

🥬 Celery xylem in colour — see plant pipes work

~240 min · one-sitting project

What you'll need

  • A fresh celery stalk with leaves
  • A glass of water
  • Red or blue food colour
  • Knife (adult help)

Steps

  1. Pour water into the glass, add ~20 drops of food colour. Mix.
  2. Trim 2 cm off the bottom of the celery for a fresh cut.
  3. Place the stalk in the coloured water.
  4. Leave for 2-4 hours.
  5. Slice the stalk crossways. You'll see coloured dots — the xylem tubes that carried the dye up.
  6. Cut the leaves — coloured veins are visible.

👀 What you should see

Coloured dots in the stalk cross-section and coloured veins in the leaves, all from water rising through xylem.

Why it works: Transpiration from the leaves creates suction in the xylem tubes. The plant doesn't know the water is dyed; it just sucks. The dye rides along as a tracer, making the invisible xylem network visible. Same principle works for white roses dipped in coloured water — the petals colour.

❤️ Find your pulse — count your own heart rate

~15 min · one-sitting project

What you'll need

  • A watch with a second hand or a stopwatch (phone is fine)
  • Your wrist or neck

Steps

  1. Sit quietly for 2 minutes (let your heart rate settle).
  2. Place 2 fingers (not thumb — it has its own pulse) on your wrist (radial artery, just below thumb) OR neck (carotid, just below jaw).
  3. Count beats for 60 seconds. That's your resting heart rate.
  4. Now do 30 jumping jacks. Immediately count beats for 60 seconds — that's exercise rate.
  5. Sit. Recount at 1 min, 2 min, 5 min. Watch recovery.
  6. Plot your numbers — resting, peak, 1 min, 2 min, 5 min.

👀 What you should see

Resting: 70-90 bpm for a Class 7 student. Peak after exercise: 130-180. Recovery to within 20 of resting by 5 minutes.

Why it works: You're feeling your heart's pulse wave travelling through arteries. Exercise demands more oxygen → muscles signal need → heart speeds up. Fast recovery (under 5 min) indicates good cardiovascular fitness. Athletes' resting rates can be 50 or below; sedentary people may have 90+ resting rates. Your own pulse is a window into your heart's efficiency.

📝 How to use this

Before you start, predict what will happen. Write your prediction down. Then do the project. Compare. If your prediction was wrong, write down why — that's where real learning happens.
