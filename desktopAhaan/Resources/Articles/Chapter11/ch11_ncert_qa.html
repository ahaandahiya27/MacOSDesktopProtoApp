Q1. Match the structures given in Column I with functions given in Column II. (Stomata, xylem, root hairs, leaf veins).

Model answer: Stomata — gas exchange and transpiration. Xylem — water transport from roots to leaves. Root hairs — absorption of water and minerals from soil. Leaf veins — distribution of water and food within the leaf. Each is a tube or pore engineered for one specific function in plant transport.

Q3. Why is transport of substances necessary in a plant or in an animal?

Model answer: Living things are made of many cells, each of which needs nutrients, oxygen, and a way to remove waste. The mouth eats food but every cell in your big toe needs glucose. The lungs breathe but every cell of your brain needs oxygen. Without transport, only cells close to the food/air source could function. In animals, blood + heart carries it. In plants, xylem carries water + minerals upward; phloem carries glucose to roots and storage. Transport is what allows multicellular life.

Q4. What will happen if there are no platelets in the blood?

Model answer: Platelets are blood cells that help blood clot at injuries. Without them, even a small cut wouldn't stop bleeding — you'd lose blood continuously. People with low platelet count (e.g., dengue fever patients in their critical stage) need platelet transfusions urgently. The platelet count drops below ~20,000 (normal: 150,000–400,000) raises serious bleeding risk. Without platelets, surgery would be impossible.

Q5. What are stomata? Give two functions of stomata.

Model answer: Stomata (singular: stoma) are tiny mouth-shaped openings on the underside of leaves, surrounded by two specialised guard cells that open and close them. Two functions: (1) Gas exchange — CO₂ enters for photosynthesis, oxygen exits. (2) Transpiration — water vapour exits, which (a) cools the leaf and (b) creates suction that pulls water up from roots. Both functions are essential; without stomata a plant would suffocate AND wilt.

Q6. Does transpiration serve any useful function in the plants? Explain.

Model answer: Yes — transpiration is essential, not just water loss. Three functions: (1) Suction pull — evaporation from leaves creates negative pressure that pulls water up xylem from roots. Without transpiration, water couldn't reach the top of tall trees. (2) Cooling — water evaporating from leaves absorbs heat, like sweat cooling skin. Leaves can be 5-10°C cooler than air. (3) Mineral transport — water carries dissolved minerals from roots upward; without water movement, leaves would starve of nutrients.

Q7. What are the components of blood?

Model answer: Blood has four components. (1) Plasma — the pale yellow liquid (~55% of blood), mostly water with dissolved nutrients, hormones, waste, and proteins. (2) Red blood cells — biconcave discs containing haemoglobin to carry oxygen. (3) White blood cells — fight infection and produce antibodies. (4) Platelets — small fragments that form clots at injuries. Each component has a specific job: plasma transports, RBCs deliver oxygen, WBCs defend, platelets repair.

Q8. Why is blood needed by all the parts of a body?

Model answer: Blood does several critical jobs: (1) Delivers oxygen from lungs to every cell. (2) Delivers glucose and other nutrients from digestion to every cell. (3) Removes CO₂ (waste from cell respiration) back to lungs. (4) Carries urea (protein breakdown waste) to kidneys. (5) Carries hormones from glands to target organs. (6) White blood cells reach infections; platelets reach wounds. (7) Distributes heat from body core to extremities. No single tissue can do its job without blood reaching it.
