Transportation in Animals and Plants

Chapter 11 · Class 7 Science

The body is a city; blood is its road network

Every cell in your body needs food, water, and oxygen — and needs to send waste away. Your circulatory system is the delivery service that handles both.

The four-chambered heart

Your heart has two halves and four rooms:

  • Right atrium → right ventricle sends used (oxygen-poor) blood to the lungs.
  • Left atrium → left ventricle pumps fresh (oxygen-rich) blood to the whole body.

A child's resting heart beats around 80 times a minute — about 100,000 beats per day.

Blood — four ingredients

  • Red blood cells (RBC) — carry oxygen using haemoglobin.
  • White blood cells (WBC) — fight germs.
  • Platelets — clot blood when you cut yourself.
  • Plasma — liquid that carries everything else: food, hormones, waste.

Three kinds of vessels

  • Arteries — thick-walled, carry blood AWAY from the heart at high pressure.
  • Veins — thinner, with valves to stop backflow, carry blood BACK to the heart.
  • Capillaries — tiny, one-cell-thick walls where O₂ and food slip into tissues.

The kidneys — body's filter

Your two bean-shaped kidneys filter about 180 litres of blood every day, removing urea and extra water as urine. Useful nutrients go back to the blood.

Plants don't have a heart, but they have xylem (water-up pipes) and phloem (sugar both ways).

How plants drink — without a pump

Water enters through the roots, climbs the xylem all the way to the leaves, and evaporates out through tiny pores called stomata. That evaporation, called transpiration, creates a suction that pulls more water up — like sipping through a straw, powered by sunlight and dry air.

Phloem — the sugar highway

Sugars made in the leaves by photosynthesis travel down (or up) the phloem to wherever the plant needs energy or storage — fruits, roots, growing buds. Unlike xylem, phloem can flow in any direction.
