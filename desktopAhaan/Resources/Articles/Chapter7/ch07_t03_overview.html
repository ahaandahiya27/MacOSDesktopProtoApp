Climate Change and Animal Survival

How a warming world is reshaping habitats and what we can do about it

≈ 4 min read · for curious 12-year-olds

Earth's climate has always changed -- ice ages came and went over millions of years. But today, climate change is happening much faster than ever before, driven largely by human activities. Animals that took thousands of generations to adapt are now facing rapid shifts in temperature, rainfall, and food availability. Can they keep up?

Why This Topic Matters Now

Scientists around the world are observing alarming changes. Polar ice is melting, coral reefs are bleaching white, forests are catching fire more often, and many species are moving to new areas or declining in numbers. Understanding these changes is the first step toward doing something about them.

This topic connects the science of weather and climate (Topic 1) and animal adaptations (Topic 2) to the urgent real-world challenge of protecting our planet's biodiversity.

Concepts in This Topic

  1. Climate Change and Wildlife -- Shifting Habitats As temperatures rise, animals are forced to move, change their timing, or face extinction.
  2. Coral Bleaching -- When Oceans Get Too Warm The colourful underwater cities of coral reefs are turning white -- find out why and what it means.
  3. Conservation -- Helping Animals Survive a Changing World National parks, breeding programmes, and everyday actions that make a difference for wildlife.

In the News

India's Project Tiger, launched in 1973, has helped tiger numbers recover from about 1,800 to over 3,600 in recent years. Conservation works -- but it needs continued effort as climate change creates new challenges for tiger habitats.

What Can You Do?

Even small actions matter: save electricity, plant trees, reduce waste, and spread awareness. Every bit of carbon dioxide we prevent from entering the atmosphere helps slow down climate change and gives animals more time to adapt.
