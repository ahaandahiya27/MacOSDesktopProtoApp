Question 3 — from Chapter 7

A friend says: 'Weather and climate are the same thing — just one is short and the other is long.' Evaluate this claim. Is the only difference duration, or is there a deeper distinction?

🧠 Answer in your head first

Don't peek. Speak the answer out loud, then check.

Answer: Partly correct, but missing the deeper point. Weather IS short-term and climate IS long-term, but the distinction is also about VARIABILITY vs. AVERAGE. Weather is the instantaneous state — today it's 32°C and raining. Climate is the long-run statistical pattern — Delhi has hot dry May (avg 41°C) and cool dry January (avg 14°C), every year. A single hot day in January doesn't change Delhi's climate; it's just unusual weather. Climate tells you what to PACK for a trip (clothes for the season); weather tells you what to WEAR today (umbrella or not). The relationship: climate = average + range of weather over decades.

Why: Identify the friend's framing: 'same thing, different duration'. Test it.

Question 5 — from Chapter 7

A migratory bird flies 10,000 km from Siberia to South India each winter. List THREE physiological challenges it faces and explain how it has evolved to handle each.

🧠 Answer in your head first

Don't peek. Speak the answer out loud, then check.

Answer: (1) Energy storage. Solution: pre-migration overeating to deposit fat (fat = 9 cal/g vs. carbohydrates' 4 cal/g, lighter to carry). Migratory songbirds can DOUBLE their body mass before departing. (2) Navigation across thousands of km. Solution: hardwired sky compass (sun + stars) + magnetic field sensing + landmark memory. Many species use multiple cues redundantly. (3) Sleep without crashing. Solution: 'unihemispheric sleep' — one brain hemisphere sleeps while the other stays alert. Some birds (frigates) literally fly for weeks without true sleep. Evolved redundancies make 10,000 km possible.

Why: Three challenges: fuel, navigation, fatigue.
