A Class 7 Weather, Climate and Adaptations of Animals to Climate Dictionary

The 10 terms to know

  • Weather · मौसम — The short-term state of the atmosphere — temperature, rainfall, wind, humidity over hours to days.
    Example: Today's weather in Mumbai is rainy with 30°C and high humidity.
  • Climate · जलवायु — The long-term average weather pattern of a region, typically measured over 30+ years.
    Example: Rajasthan has a hot-arid climate; Kerala has a tropical-wet climate.
  • Adaptation · अनुकूलन — A trait that helps an organism survive better in its environment, evolved over generations.
    Example: The thick blubber of a polar bear is an adaptation to extreme cold.
  • Migration · प्रवास — Long-distance seasonal movement of animals between two regions, usually for feeding and breeding.
    Example: Siberian cranes migrate 5000 km from Siberia to Bharatpur each winter.
  • Hibernation · शीतनिद्रा — Winter dormancy — slowing of body processes to conserve energy when food is scarce.
    Example: Frogs hibernate in mud during a Himalayan winter; their heart rate drops to 1-2 beats per minute.
  • Tropical · उष्णकटिबंधीय — The region near the equator, characterised by high temperature and high rainfall year-round.
    Example: Western Ghats and Sundarbans are India's tropical-climate regions.
  • Polar · ध्रुवीय — The Arctic and Antarctic regions, with extreme cold and long darkness in winter.
    Example: Snow leopards live in the cold Himalayas; true polar species (polar bear, penguin) are at much higher latitudes.
  • Monsoon · मानसून — The seasonal wind pattern that brings most of South Asia its rainfall — June to September.
    Example: Mawsynram in Meghalaya gets ~12 metres of monsoon rain per year — the world's highest.
  • Coral bleaching · मूंगा विरंजन — The loss of colourful algae from coral when sea temperature rises, leaving the coral white and starved.
    Example: Lakshadweep coral lost 70% of its colour and biomass in the 2016 and 2020 marine heatwaves.
  • Climate change · जलवायु परिवर्तन — Long-term shifts in global weather patterns, primarily driven by human-released greenhouse gases.
    Example: India's monsoon has become more erratic since 1980 — heavy rain in fewer days, longer dry gaps.
