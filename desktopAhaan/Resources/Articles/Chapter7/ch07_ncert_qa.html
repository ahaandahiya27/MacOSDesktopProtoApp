Q1. Name the two regions of the Earth where extreme conditions of climate are found. Mention any two adaptations of animals living in these regions.

Model answer: Polar regions (Arctic, Antarctic) and Tropical rainforests (near the equator). Polar adaptations: (1) Thick fur AND insulating fat layer to retain body heat. (2) White colour (camouflage in snow + minimises heat loss). Tropical rainforest adaptations: (1) Sharp claws and prehensile tails for climbing trees (e.g., monkeys). (2) Loud calls that travel through dense vegetation (e.g., howler monkeys, gibbons).

Q2. How do animal adaptations help us understand the importance of climate for them?

Model answer: Adaptations are the visible proof that climate is a powerful force shaping life. The polar bear's white fur, thick blubber, and small ears didn't appear by accident — they exist because millions of years of selection killed bears who lacked them in cold environments. The same logic explains every adaptation: snow leopard's wide paws (snow), camel's hump (desert), elephant's large ears (heat radiation), fish's gills (water). Each animal's body is essentially a long answer to the question 'How do I survive this climate?'

Q3. What are migratory birds? Why do they migrate to other places?

Model answer: Migratory birds are species that travel long distances seasonally between two regions — typically a breeding ground (with long days and abundant food in summer) and a wintering ground (where food is still available when the breeding ground freezes). They migrate to escape winter scarcity, to find food, and to breed safely. Famous example: Siberian cranes fly 5000 km from Siberia to Bharatpur (Rajasthan) every winter. Most Indian states host at least a few species of winter migrant birds.

Q4. Which of the two changes daily — weather or climate? Explain.

Model answer: Weather changes daily — sometimes hour to hour. Today might be sunny, tomorrow rainy, the day after windy. Climate is the long-term average over many years (typically 30+). Mumbai's climate is 'tropical wet' year-round, even though any individual day could be sunny, cloudy, or stormy. Confusing the two leads to errors: a single cold winter doesn't disprove climate change (which is a 30-year warming TREND), and a single hot day doesn't make a city's climate hot.

Q5. The tropical region has a special weather. State its main features.

Model answer: Tropical region (near the equator) features: (1) Hot all year — temperatures consistently 25-35°C with little seasonal variation. (2) Heavy rainfall — often 200+ cm annually in tropical rainforests. (3) High humidity — air is constantly damp because warm air holds more water vapour. (4) Dense vegetation — equatorial rainforests like Western Ghats, Amazon, Congo basin support more species per hectare than anywhere else on Earth. (5) Day length is roughly 12 hours throughout the year (no big seasonal change in sunlight).

Q6. What are the two main adaptations seen in polar animals?

Model answer: Insulation and behaviour. (1) Insulation: a thick layer of fur or feathers PLUS a layer of fat (blubber) under the skin. Polar bears have both — black skin under white fur absorbs the small sunlight that reaches them, and 10 cm of blubber. (2) Body shape and colour: short ears, short tails, small extremities (less surface area to lose heat), white or pale colour for camouflage AND less heat loss. Many also have specific behaviours — huddling together (penguins), digging snow caves (polar bears with cubs), or migrating to slightly warmer waters in winter (Arctic foxes).

Q7. Choose the correct option. (a) Heavy rain (b) High humidity (c) Cold climate (d) Hot climate — which of these is NOT a feature of a tropical region?

Model answer: (c) Cold climate. Tropical regions near the equator are characterised by heavy rain, high humidity, and hot climate. Cold climate is the opposite — found in polar and high-altitude regions. So 'cold climate' is the odd one out.

Q8. Why is February-May considered to be the best season for visiting Kerala according to climate?

Model answer: February to May is the warm-dry period BEFORE the monsoon arrives in June. Temperatures are 22-32°C — warm enough for beaches but not yet scorching. Rainfall is minimal, so beaches and hill stations (like Munnar) are accessible. The monsoon (June-September) brings heavy rain that floods roads and closes some sites. October-January brings the lighter northeast monsoon. So Feb-May is the sweet spot — sunny, warm, dry, accessible. Tourism in Kerala spikes during exactly this window.
