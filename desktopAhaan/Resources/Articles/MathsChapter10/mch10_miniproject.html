Operations with Integers · Mini Project

🔴 Mini Project — Token-Bag Integer Machine

A hands-on activity that turns this chapter into something you actually do, not just read.

≈ 35 min · do-it-yourself level

Reading about maths and doing maths are two different things. This short project lets you see the ideas of "Operations with Integers" at work with your own hands.

What you'll need

  • Two colours of small counters (or paper circles) — say green for +1 and red for −1
  • A bowl or 'bag'
  • Paper to record

Steps

  1. Model addition: to compute (+5)+(−3), put 5 green and 3 red into the bag; remove every green-red 'zero pair'; count what's left.
  2. Record the result and its sign. Repeat for (−6)+(+2), (+4)+(+3), (−5)+(−1).
  3. Model multiplication: for 4×(−2), put 2 reds in, four times; count the result.
  4. For (−4)×(−2): lay down zero pairs, then REMOVE 2 reds four times; count the greens left over.
  5. Write the sign rule you discover for each kind of product.

🔍 What you should notice

Zero pairs cancel cleanly in addition; in multiplication, removing red (negative) tokens leaves green (positive) ones — so (−)×(−) comes out positive, just as the sign rule says.

Why it works

The tokens make additive inverses physical: a green and a red sum to zero. A negative multiplier means 'remove', and removing negatives leaves positives behind — a hands-on demonstration that two negatives multiply to a positive, forced by how addition and removal interact.
