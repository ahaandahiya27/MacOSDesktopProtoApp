Story Mode — Operations with Integers in Real Life

The same ideas from this chapter, but out in the world — in shops, on playgrounds, and in everyday choices.

≈ 6 min · see-it-everywhere level

Maths can feel like it lives only inside the textbook. It doesn't. Here are real situations where the ideas of "Operations with Integers" quietly do the work.

Bank balances go both ways

A deposit is a positive integer, a withdrawal a negative one. Deposit ₹500 then withdraw ₹300: (+500)+(−300) = +200 change. Withdraw more than you have and the balance goes negative — integers are exactly the maths of money that can go below zero.

Why removing debt is a gain

If a ₹2 debt is cancelled (removed) 4 times, your balance rises by ₹8: (−4)×(−2) = +8. The puzzling 'negative times negative is positive' is just 'taking away something bad makes things better', four times over.

Temperatures above and below zero

In Shimla in January the temperature might be +3° at noon and fall to −4° at night — a change of −7°. Integer addition and subtraction describe every rise and fall across the zero of the freezing point.
