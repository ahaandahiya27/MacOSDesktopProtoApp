Operations with Integers: Worked NCERT Questions

Textbook questions from this chapter, each with a clear worked answer.

These are the kind of questions the NCERT chapter asks. Try each one yourself first, then check the worked answer.

Q1. A carrom coin at 0 is struck 4 units right, then 3 units right. Where does it end up, and what is the general formula for two same-direction strikes a and b?

Answer: It ends at 4 + 3 = 7 units from 0. In general the final position is P = a + b.

Q2. Compute (+5) + (−3) and (−5) + (+3) using zero pairs.

Answer: (+5)+(−3): three zero pairs cancel, leaving +2. (−5)+(+3): three zero pairs cancel, leaving −2. Opposite signs partly cancel; the larger-magnitude sign remains.

Q3. What is the additive inverse of an integer, and give the inverse of −12.

Answer: The additive inverse of a number is the number with the same magnitude but opposite sign; it sums with the original to 0. The inverse of −12 is +12, since −12 + 12 = 0.

Q4. Using the token model, what is 4 × (−2)?

Answer: 4 × (−2) means placing 2 negative tokens into an empty bag 4 times, giving 8 negatives, i.e. −8.

Q5. Why is (−4) × (−2) equal to +8?

Answer: A negative multiplier means removing tokens. (−4) × (−2) removes 2 negative tokens four times; removing negatives (after laying down zero pairs) leaves their positive partners, giving 8 positives, i.e. +8.

Q6. State the sign rules for multiplying two integers.

Answer: (+)(+)=+, (+)(−)=−, (−)(+)=−, (−)(−)=+. Same signs give a positive product, different signs a negative product; the magnitude is the product of the magnitudes.

Q7. Does swapping the multiplier and multiplicand change the product's sign?

Answer: No. Multiplication is commutative: a × b = b × a for all integers, and the sign is the same before and after swapping (same-sign stays +, different-sign stays −).

Q8. Compute (−9) × 7 and 8 × (−3).

Answer: (−9) × 7 = −63 (different signs → −, 9×7 = 63). 8 × (−3) = −24 (different signs → −, 8×3 = 24).
