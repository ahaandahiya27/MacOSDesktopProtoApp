Common Mistakes in Operations with Integers

The places students slip on this chapter — and the one-line fix for each.

&#8776; 5 min read &middot; revision-tier &middot; 5 fixes

These wrong answers come back year after year. None are silly — they are almost right, which is what makes them worth knowing. Read each, then cover the fix and try to spot what is off.

1. “Adding two integers always makes a bigger number.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: Adding a negative makes it smaller: (+5)+(−3)=+2, less than 5. Opposite signs partly cancel.

2. “(−)×(−) = − (two negatives make a negative).”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: (−)×(−) = + . Removing negative tokens leaves positives; two negatives multiplied give a POSITIVE.

3. “Subtraction is different from addition for integers.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: Subtracting is adding the opposite: 5 − (−3) = 5 + 3 = 8. Every subtraction is an addition in disguise.

4. “The sign of a product depends on which number is bigger.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: It depends only on the SIGNS: same signs → +, different → −. Magnitude just sets the size.

5. “Only zero has an opposite.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: EVERY integer has an opposite (additive inverse): the opposite of −12 is 12, of 7 is −7. This is what makes subtraction always possible.
