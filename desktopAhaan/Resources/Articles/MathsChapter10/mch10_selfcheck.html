Question 1 — The warm-up

A carrom coin at 0 is struck 4 units right, then 3 units right. Where does it end up, and what is the general formula for two same-direction strikes a and b?

🧠 Answer in your head first

Work it out before you read on — no peeking.

Answer: It ends at 4 + 3 = 7 units from 0. In general the final position is P = a + b.

Question 2 — The check

Compute (+5) + (−3) and (−5) + (+3) using zero pairs.

🧠 Answer in your head first

Work it out before you read on — no peeking.

Answer: (+5)+(−3): three zero pairs cancel, leaving +2. (−5)+(+3): three zero pairs cancel, leaving −2. Opposite signs partly cancel; the larger-magnitude sign remains.

Question 3 — The stretch

What is the additive inverse of an integer, and give the inverse of −12.

🧠 Answer in your head first

Work it out before you read on — no peeking.

Answer: The additive inverse of a number is the number with the same magnitude but opposite sign; it sums with the original to 0. The inverse of −12 is +12, since −12 + 12 = 0.

Question 4 — The trap

Using the token model, what is 4 × (−2)?

🧠 Answer in your head first

Work it out before you read on — no peeking.

Answer: 4 × (−2) means placing 2 negative tokens into an empty bag 4 times, giving 8 negatives, i.e. −8.

Textbook page 28.

Question 5 — The finisher

Why is (−4) × (−2) equal to +8?

🧠 Answer in your head first

Work it out before you read on — no peeking.

Answer: A negative multiplier means removing tokens. (−4) × (−2) removes 2 negative tokens four times; removing negatives (after laying down zero pairs) leaves their positive partners, giving 8 positives, i.e. +8.
