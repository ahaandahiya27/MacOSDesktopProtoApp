The 10 terms to know

  • Urbanisation · नगरीकरण — The growth and spread of towns and cities, and of the organised life (trade, crafts, administration) that goes with them.
    Example: India's 'Second Urbanisation' began in the Ganga plains in the 1st millennium BCE.
  • Janapada · जनपद — A territory settled by a clan and led by a rajaa; from Sanskrit jana ('people') + pada ('foot'), meaning 'where the people have set foot'.
    Example: Each clan came to be associated with its own janapada.
  • Mahaajanapada · महाजनपद — A 'great janapada' — a larger state formed when janapadas merged; the frequent list names 16 of them.
    Example: Magadha and Kosala were powerful mahaajanapadas.
  • Moat · खाई / परिखा — A deep, wide ditch surrounding a fort or fortified city and filled with water, used for defence.
    Example: A moat ran outside the fortifications of mahaajanapada capitals.
  • Sabhaa / Samiti · सभा / समिति — Assemblies or councils where the clan's matters were discussed; the words first appear in the Vedas.
    Example: A good rajaa took advice from the sabhaa and samiti.
  • Gana / Sangha · गण / संघ — A mahaajanapada that was not a monarchy, where the assembly took major decisions and selected the rajaa; an 'early republic'.
    Example: Vajji and Malla were ganas or sanghas.
  • Punch-marked coins · आहत मुद्रा — India's first coins, made of silver with symbols punched (stamped) into the soft metal.
    Example: Each mahaajanapada generally issued its own punch-marked coins.
  • Varna · वर्ण — One of four broad social categories from Vedic texts: Brahmins, Kshatriyas, Vaishyas and Shudras.
    Example: Kshatriyas were the varna expected to defend the land.
  • Jaati · जाति — An occupational community whose specific skills and customs are passed from generation to generation, often subdividing into sub-jaatis.
    Example: A community of weavers or metalworkers formed a jaati.
  • Uttaraapatha & Dakshinaapatha · उत्तरापथ / दक्षिणापथ — Two great ancient routes — the northern route (Uttaraapatha) and the southern route (Dakshinaapatha) — used for trade, pilgrimage and travel.
    Example: The Dakshinaapatha ran from Kaushaambi across the Vindhyas to the south.
