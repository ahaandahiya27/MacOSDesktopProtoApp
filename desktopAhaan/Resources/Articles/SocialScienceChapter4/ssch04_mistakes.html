Every topic has traps that catch careful students. Here are the most common ones for this chapter, paired with what is actually true, so you can sidestep them.

❌ Many kids think…

All the mahaajanapadas were ruled by all-powerful kings.

✅ Actually…

Many were monarchies, but at least two — Vajji (Vrijji) and Malla — were ganas or sanghas, 'early republics' where the assembly took major decisions by discussion and vote and even selected the rajaa.

❌ Many kids think…

The Harappan (Indus) cities used iron tools and weapons.

✅ Actually…

The Harappans mastered copper and bronze. Iron metallurgy became widespread only later, by the late 2nd millennium BCE, and it was a key technology of the SECOND Urbanisation, not the first.

❌ Many kids think…

The varna-jaati system was always rigid and unchanging.

✅ Actually…

In the early period it was more flexible — people and communities could change occupations when circumstances (like a drought) demanded. The system became rigid only over time, especially during British rule.

❌ Many kids think…

The gana-sangha republics were full democracies where every person could vote.

✅ Actually…

They were a big step away from one all-powerful king, but not modern democracies. Decisions were taken jointly by an assembly of the heads of leading families (the 'rajaas'), so it was rule by a group — closer to an oligarchy. Ordinary people, and usually women, did not vote.

❌ Many kids think…

Coins were invented so that people could become rich.

✅ Actually…

Punch-marked coins were invented to make TRADE easier. A standard-weight piece of metal that everyone trusted was far simpler than swapping goods by barter. Coins were a tool for exchanging things fairly, not a goal in themselves.
