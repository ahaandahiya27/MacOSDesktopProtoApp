🏰 Design your own mahaajanapada capital (about 75 min)

What you need

  • A large sheet of paper or cardboard
  • Pencil, ruler and colours
  • Optional: clay or playdough for a 3D model
  • The chapter's descriptions of forts, moats and gateways

Steps

  1. Choose a spot for your city near a river and good farmland, and mark it on your sheet.
  2. Draw a strong rampart (wall) all around the city.
  3. Add a moat — a wide water-filled ditch — outside the wall.
  4. Make the gateways in the wall narrow, and add little guard posts beside them.
  5. Inside, lay out broad streets, a market, houses, a king's hall and a place for the assembly (sabhaa).
  6. Add a label explaining why each defensive feature (wall, moat, narrow gate) is there.
  7. Give your capital a name and decide whether it is a monarchy or a gana/sangha, and write one line on how its ruler is chosen.

👀 What you should see

You will see how every part of an ancient capital — its location, walls, moat and narrow gates — had a clear purpose for defence, trade and governance.

🧠 Why it works

Designing the city yourself forces you to use each fact from the chapter (moats, ramparts, narrow gates, assemblies) as a real decision, so the reasons behind ancient city planning stick in your memory far better than just reading them.

🪙 Make your own 'punch-marked' coins (about 50 min)

What you need

  • Air-dry clay, playdough, or aluminium foil
  • A few blunt-tipped tools (a pencil, a bottle cap, a key) to make 'punch' marks
  • Paper and pen to record your design
  • A partner or classmate to trade with

Steps

  1. Flatten small pieces of clay into little coin shapes.
  2. Design 2-3 simple symbols to stand for your own 'mahaajanapada'.
  3. Carefully 'punch' your symbols into each coin using the blunt tools.
  4. Make several coins, all carrying your symbols.
  5. Ask a partner to make coins with their own different symbols.
  6. Now 'trade' goods (pencils, erasers, drawings) using each other's coins.
  7. Afterwards, discuss: how did using coins make trading easier than swapping goods directly?

👀 What you should see

You will find that coins make trade much easier than barter, and that the punched symbols quickly tell everyone which 'region' a coin came from.

🧠 Why it works

By punching symbols and trading with them, you re-create exactly why India invented punch-marked coins — to make growing trade easier — and why symbols mattered for identifying and trusting a coin.
