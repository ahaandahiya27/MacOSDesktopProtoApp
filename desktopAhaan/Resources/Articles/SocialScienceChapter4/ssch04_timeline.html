From cities lost to cities reborn: 1900 BCE to 300 BCE

A chronological journey through the period the chapter covers, from the end of India's First Urbanisation to the close of the mahaajanapada age.

  • c. 1900 BCE — First Urbanisation declines
    In the early 2nd millennium BCE the Harappan (Indus / Sindhu-Sarasvati) civilisation disintegrates; cities are abandoned or revert to village life, beginning a roughly thousand-year gap with little urban life.
  • End of 2nd millennium BCE — Janapadas rise
    Clans settle into territories called janapadas, each led by a rajaa who takes counsel from an assembly of elders (sabhaa or samiti).
  • Late 2nd millennium BCE — Iron becomes widespread
    Iron tools spread, allowing larger-scale agriculture, while iron weapons strengthen armies — fuelling the new urbanisation.
  • 8th-7th centuries BCE — The 16 mahaajanapadas
    Janapadas merge into bigger states; the frequent list names 16 mahaajanapadas, from Gandhaara to Anga to Ashmaka, with fortified, moated capital cities.
  • 1st millennium BCE — Second Urbanisation, coins & routes
    Cities flourish in the Ganga plains and spread outward; the first punch-marked coins appear, and the Uttaraapatha and Dakshinaapatha routes connect India.
  • c. 400 BCE — Cities and kingdoms in the south
    Cities emerge in the south; the Cholas, Cheras and Pandyas arise, known from archaeology and ancient Tamil literature.
  • c. 300-200 BCE — One land; mahaajanapadas end
    Almost the whole Subcontinent becomes one interconnected land trading even with Central and Southeast Asia; the mahaajanapadas cease to exist, giving way to the great empires.
