Galileo and the Pendulum

How a swinging lamp changed timekeeping · Class 7 Science

Pisa Cathedral, 1583

A 19-year-old medical student named Galileo Galilei sat bored during a long sermon at Pisa Cathedral. A great chandelier hung from the ceiling, swinging gently in the draft. Galileo timed the swings using his own pulse. He noticed something nobody had noticed before: regardless of whether the chandelier swung a lot or a little, each swing took the same amount of time.

The famous insight

The TIME for one swing of a pendulum is constant — it does NOT depend on:

  • How far you pull the bob aside (the amplitude).
  • How heavy the bob is (the mass).

It depends ONLY on:

  • The length of the string.
T = 2π √(L / g)
where T is the period (one swing), L is the length, g is gravitational acceleration (~9.8 m/s²). A 1-metre pendulum has a period of about 2 seconds.

Why the period depends on √L (not L itself)

Quadruple the string length, and the period only doubles (since √4 = 2). Halve the string, and the period drops to about 70% of what it was. This non-linear relationship is the first encounter with a 'square-root law' kids meet in physics.

Pendulum clocks — the 300-year revolution

Galileo's son started designing a pendulum clock, but it was Christiaan Huygens in 1656 who built the first working one. Suddenly clocks went from being accurate to a few minutes per day to being accurate to a few SECONDS per day — a 1,000-fold improvement. For 300 years, until quartz watches arrived in the 20th century, pendulum clocks were the world's most accurate timepieces.

Why pendulum clocks need adjustment by latitude

'g' varies slightly across Earth (9.78 m/s² at the equator, 9.83 at the poles, due to Earth's bulge). A pendulum clock made to keep perfect time in London will run slightly slow when shipped to the equator. Sailors had to manually shorten the pendulum to compensate.

Try this at home

Tie a metal washer or a key to a 50-cm string. Hold the other end against a doorframe. Set it swinging. Time 20 full swings with a stopwatch; divide by 20 to get the period — should be around 1.4 seconds. Now HALVE the string to 25 cm and repeat: the period drops to about 1.0 seconds (not 0.7 — because √2 ≈ 1.4).

Looking ahead

Class 11 Physics 'Oscillations' formalises this as Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM). The pendulum's equation is derived from the restoring force F = -mg·sin(θ) ≈ -mgθ for small angles. You learn about phase, amplitude, energy in SHM. JEE asks T = 2π√(L/g) every year, often with twists — like 'what's the period of a pendulum in a freely-falling lift?' (answer: infinity, since effective g = 0). Class 12 connects to coupled oscillators and wave mechanics — the foundation of all sound, light, and quantum physics.
