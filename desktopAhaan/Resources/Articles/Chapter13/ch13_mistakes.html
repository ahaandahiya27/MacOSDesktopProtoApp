Five Wrong Answers Class 7 Students Give

The five places almost every Class 7 student trips up on Chapter 13 — and the one-line fix for each.

≈ 5 min read · revision-tier

Examiners say the same wrong answers come back year after year. None of these mistakes are silly — they are almost right. That is exactly what makes them dangerous. Read each one, cover the answer, and try to spot what is off before peeking.

1. “If something isn't moving, its speed is zero.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Speed is zero at THIS INSTANT, yes. But things that aren't moving can still have momentum stored (a child holding the swing in air before letting go), and can start moving the next instant. Also: 'not moving' depends on reference. You sitting on a bus going 60 km/h: your speed relative to bus = 0; your speed relative to road = 60. Speed always needs a reference frame.

2. “A heavier pendulum bob takes longer to swing.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Surprisingly, NO. The period of a simple pendulum depends ONLY on its LENGTH and the strength of gravity. A 10g bob and a 1kg bob on the same string swing with the same period. This was Galileo's discovery in 1602 — he timed a chandelier's swing with his pulse during a church service.

3. “All clocks measure 'real' time the same way.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Different clocks measure different things. A sundial measures the Sun's position (solar time). A pendulum clock measures swings (mechanical time). An atomic clock measures cesium atom vibrations (atomic time). These don't perfectly agree! Earth's spin is slightly irregular; we add 'leap seconds' to atomic clocks every few years to keep them aligned with solar time. There's no single 'real' time — there are several agreed conventions.

4. “A distance-time graph going up means moving faster.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: A STEEPER line means faster (slope = speed). A higher line doesn't necessarily mean faster — it just means farther from start. A line going UP means moving AWAY. A line going DOWN means moving BACK toward start. A FLAT line means stationary. Pay attention to slope, not height.

5. “Speed and velocity are the same.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: In Class 7 they're treated as same, but later you'll learn: SPEED is just a number (50 km/h). VELOCITY is speed + direction (50 km/h NORTH). Two cars at 50 km/h in opposite directions have same speed but opposite velocities. This matters when they collide.

📝 Stretch your thinking

Pick any two of these and explain them to a younger sibling using a one-line analogy each. If you can compress a mistake into one sentence with one image, you have understood it.
