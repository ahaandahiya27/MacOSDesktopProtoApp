Instruments and the History of Time

Topic t03 of Chapter 13 · Motion and Time · Class 7 Science

The big idea

Humans have measured time for 4,000 years. Sundials (2000 BCE, ±15 min) gave way to water and sand clocks, then pendulum clocks (1656, ±seconds/day), then quartz watches (1927, ±seconds/year), then atomic clocks (1955, ±1 second in 100 million years). Modern GPS satellites carry atomic clocks; a clock error of 1 microsecond would put your GPS off by 300 metres.

Sundials to Atomic Clocks. Each new generation of clock was 1,000 times more accurate than the last — and each unlocked new science, navigation and commerce.

Looking ahead

Class 11 Physics 'Units and Measurements' uses the atomic clock as the definition of the second. JEE Physics applies precision-measurement ideas in error analysis and uncertainty problems.
