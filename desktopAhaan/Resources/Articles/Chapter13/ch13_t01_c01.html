What is Speed?

Distance per unit time — the simplest physics equation · Class 7 Science

The world's simplest equation

How fast is a thing moving? You only need two measurements: how far it went (distance) and how long it took (time). Speed is one divided by the other.

Speed = Distance ÷ Time   (or, with symbols: s = d / t)
SI unit: metres per second (m/s). Everyday unit: kilometres per hour (km/h).
Conversion: 1 m/s = 3.6 km/h. So 60 km/h ≈ 16.7 m/s.

Two kinds of speed

The single number you've just calculated is usually the average speed — total distance divided by total time. But a real journey isn't uniform: you slow down at traffic lights, speed up on highways. The speed AT one instant — what the speedometer shows — is the instantaneous speed.

A 100 km trip in 2 hours has an average speed of 50 km/h. Inside those 2 hours, your instantaneous speed varied from 0 (at red lights) to maybe 90 km/h (on the open road). Same average, very different ride.

Typical speeds (so you have a feel)

  • Walking: 5 km/h (≈ 1.4 m/s)
  • Cycling (kid): 15 km/h
  • Car on a city street: 40 km/h
  • Car on the highway: 80-100 km/h
  • Indian Express train: 100-130 km/h
  • Vande Bharat / Bullet train: 160-300 km/h
  • Airliner: 900 km/h
  • Sound at sea level: 1,235 km/h (343 m/s)
  • Light: 1.08 BILLION km/h (3 × 10⁸ m/s)

Try this at home

Use a stopwatch and a measured 10-metre stretch (hallway, garden path). Time everyone in your family walking it, jogging it, sprinting it. Compute their speeds in m/s. Make a tiny bar chart on paper. You'll find adults walk at about 1.3 m/s, kids more like 1.0, and sprinting peaks around 5-6 m/s for most untrained people.

Looking ahead

Class 11 'Motion in a Straight Line' formalises this with two BIG new ideas:

  • Speed (scalar) vs velocity (vector). Velocity carries direction; speed doesn't. If you walk 10 m east in 5 s and 10 m back in 5 s, your average speed is 2 m/s but your average velocity is ZERO (you ended where you started).
  • Acceleration — the rate of change of velocity. The three equations of motion for constant acceleration: v = u + at, s = ut + ½at², v² = u² + 2as.

These two ideas open the door to all of mechanics. JEE Physics asks "kinematics" questions every year, including problems where the speed-vs-velocity distinction is the trick.
