A Land of Many Lands: India's Location

Where India sits on the globe, why it is called a subcontinent, the five regions geographers use to describe it, and the natural barriers that frame it.

  1. The Indian SubcontinentIndia is the seventh-largest country in the world and the centre of a region called the Indian Subcontinent.
  2. The Five Physiographic RegionsGeographers describe India in five great regions: the mountains, the plains, the desert, the peninsula and the islands.
  3. India's Natural BoundariesMountains, a desert, and seas form natural walls that separate India from the rest of Asia.

The Great Mountain Zone: The Himalayas

How the Himalayas were born from a slow collision of land, their three parallel ranges, why they are the 'Water Tower of Asia', and the surprising cold desert of Ladakh hidden among them.

  1. How the Himalayas Were FormedThe Himalayas rose when the Indian landmass drifted north and crashed into Asia, crumpling the land upward.
  2. The Three Ranges of the HimalayasThe Himalayas have three parallel belts: the high Himadri, the gentler Himachal, and the low Shivalik hills.
  3. The Water Tower of AsiaSnow and glaciers in the Himalayas feed great rivers like the Ganga, Indus and Brahmaputra, supplying water to hundreds of millions of people.
  4. The Cold Desert of LadakhLadakh is a cold desert high in the Himalayas, with very little rain, freezing winters and a moon-like landscape.

Plains, Deserts and Old Hills

The fertile Northern Plains where most Indians live, the hot Thar Desert and how people adapt to it, and the ancient Aravalli Hills that hold back the sand.

  1. The Northern (Gangetic) PlainsThe flat, fertile Northern Plains are built by Himalayan rivers and are home to a large part of India's population.
  2. The Thar Desert and Human AdaptationThe Thar is a hot, sandy desert in northwestern India where people have cleverly adapted to scarce water.
  3. The Aravalli HillsThe Aravallis are among the world's oldest mountains and act as a natural wall that stops the Thar Desert from spreading east.

The Peninsular Plateau and the Coasts

The ancient triangular plateau of the south, its bordering Western and Eastern Ghats, the rivers and tribal communities it supports, and India's long coastlines with their great river deltas.

  1. The Peninsular Plateau and the GhatsSouthern India is a very old, triangular plateau bordered by two mountain ranges, the Western and Eastern Ghats.
  2. Plateau Rivers, Minerals and Tribal CommunitiesThe peninsular plateau is rich in minerals and forests, sends most of its rivers east to the Bay of Bengal, and is home to many tribal communities.
  3. India's Coastlines and DeltasIndia has over 7,500 km of varied coastline, with a swift-river West Coast and a wide-delta East Coast.

Islands, Sundarbans and the Northeast

India's two great island groups, the unique mangrove delta of the Sundarbans, the rainy hills of the Northeast, and how such diverse regions still form one united country.

  1. The Indian IslandsIndia has two major island groups — the coral Lakshadweep in the Arabian Sea and the volcanic Andaman and Nicobar Islands in the Bay of Bengal.
  2. The Sundarbans DeltaThe Sundarbans is a unique mangrove forest in the delta of the Ganga and Brahmaputra, shared by India and Bangladesh and home to the Royal Bengal Tiger.
  3. The Hills of the Northeast and India's Unity in DiversityThe rainy hills of the Northeast complete India's diversity, and despite such varied regions India remains one united country.
