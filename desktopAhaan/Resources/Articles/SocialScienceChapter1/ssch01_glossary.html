The 17 terms to know

  • Subcontinent · उपमहाद्वीप — A large, distinct landmass that is part of a continent but set apart from the rest of it by natural features.
    Example: India and its neighbours form the Indian Subcontinent.
  • Plateau · पठार — A landform that rises above the surrounding land and has a more or less flat top, often with steep sides.
    Example: The Deccan Plateau in southern India.
  • Peninsula · प्रायद्वीप — A piece of land surrounded by water on three sides.
    Example: Southern India is a peninsula bordered by three seas.
  • Delta · डेल्टा / नदीमुख — A triangular or fan-shaped landform built where a river deposits its sediment as it enters a larger body of water.
    Example: The Sundarbans lies in the Ganga–Brahmaputra delta.
  • Estuary · ज्वारनदमुख — A funnel-shaped river mouth where fresh river water mixes with salty sea water.
    Example: The Narmada and Tapti form large estuaries on the West Coast.
  • Archipelago · द्वीपसमूह — A group or chain of islands.
    Example: Lakshadweep is an archipelago of 36 coral islands.
  • Tributary · सहायक नदी — A smaller river or stream that flows into and joins a larger river.
    Example: The Bhagirathi is a tributary of the Ganga.
  • Sand dune · बालू का टीला — A hill or ridge of sand built up and shaped by the wind.
    Example: Thar Desert dunes can rise as high as 150 metres.
  • Lagoon · अनूप / लैगून — A body of water separated from a larger body of water by a natural barrier such as a sandbar.
    Example: Chilika Lake on the East Coast is a lagoon.
  • Himalaya · हिमालय — From Sanskrit hima ('snow') + ālaya ('abode'), meaning 'abode of snow' — India's great northern mountain range.
    Example: The Himalayas are the 'Water Tower of Asia'.
  • Deccan · दक्कन / दक्षिण पठार — From Sanskrit dakṣiṇa ('south'), through Prakrit dakkhiṇa — the large, ancient plateau that makes up most of southern India.
    Example: The flat Deccan Plateau lies between the Western and Eastern Ghats.
  • Ghat · घाट — A Hindi/Marathi word for a flight of steps or a steep mountain pass that leads down to a plain or river — used for the two ranges edging the Deccan Plateau.
    Example: The tall Western Ghats run like a wall along the west coast; the lower Eastern Ghats are broken by rivers.
  • Alluvium · जलोढ़ मिट्टी / कछार — From Latin alluvius ('washed against') — the fine, fertile soil that rivers carry and drop along their courses, also called alluvial soil.
    Example: The Northern Plains are so productive because the Ganga and its tributaries spread thick alluvium over them.
  • Glacier · हिमनद / ग्लेशियर — A slow-moving river of ice on a high mountain; in Hindi himnad, from Sanskrit hima ('snow') + nada ('river').
    Example: Himalayan glaciers melt slowly all summer and keep the great rivers flowing even when it does not rain.
  • Coral island · प्रवाल द्वीप — A low island built up over ages by tiny sea animals called corals; coral is pravāla in Sanskrit.
    Example: The Lakshadweep islands are coral islands ringed by clear, shallow lagoons.
  • Mangrove · ज्वारीय वन / मैंग्रोव — A salt-tolerant tidal forest that grows where river deltas meet the sea; the Sundarbans is named after its sundari trees (Bengali sundar 'beautiful' + ban 'forest').
    Example: The Sundarbans mangroves are the home of the Royal Bengal Tiger.
  • Monsoon · मानसून / मौसमी हवाएँ — The seasonal winds that reverse direction with the seasons and bring India most of its rain; from Arabic mausim ('season'), the source of Hindi mausam.
    Example: When the summer monsoon hits the Western Ghats, it pours rain over the waterfalls along the west coast.
