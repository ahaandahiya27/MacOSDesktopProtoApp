What if the Himalayas did not exist?

Think first, then open to compare

Without the Himalayan wall, cold, dry winds from Central Asia would sweep into India, making the north far colder in winter. The monsoon clouds would no longer be trapped, so India would get much less rain and large areas could turn dry or desert-like. There would be no stored snow to feed the great perennial rivers, so the Ganga, Indus and Brahmaputra would shrink or dry in the dry season, and the fertile, crowded Northern Plains might never have formed. India's climate, farming and history would be completely different.

What if the Aravalli Hills were completely flattened by mining?

Think first, then open to compare

The Aravallis act as a natural wall that slows the eastward spread of the Thar Desert's sand. If they were levelled, blowing sand and dryness could creep east toward cities like Delhi, harming farms and worsening dust storms. The region would also lose forests, wildlife and the local rainfall patterns the hills help create. This is why protecting and re-greening the Aravallis is so important.
