🗺️ Build a relief map of India (about 90 min)

What you need

  • A large sheet of cardboard
  • Atta dough or modelling clay
  • Paints (white, green, yellow, brown, blue)
  • A pencil and a physical map of India to copy

Steps

  1. Draw the outline of India on the cardboard using the physical map.
  2. Build up clay to show the high Himalayas in the north (make them the tallest).
  3. Keep the Northern Plains flat and low, just south of the mountains.
  4. Raise a gentle triangular block for the Deccan Plateau in the south.
  5. Pinch up a ridge for the Western Ghats and lower bumps for the Eastern Ghats.
  6. Paint by height: white for snow peaks, brown/yellow for highlands and desert, green for plains, blue for seas and rivers.
  7. Add labels for the five regions and a small legend explaining your colours.

👀 What you should see

You will see at a glance how the land steps down from the towering Himalayas to the flat plains and the plateau, and why rivers flow the way they do.

🧠 Why it works

A relief model turns flat map colours into real height, so the connection between altitude, rivers and climate becomes obvious. Building it by hand fixes the five regions and their positions firmly in memory.

💧 A one-day Thar water diary (about 60 min)

What you need

  • A notebook and pen
  • A measuring jug or marked bottle

Steps

  1. For one whole day, write down every time you or your family use water (brushing, bathing, cooking, washing, cleaning).
  2. Estimate how many litres each use takes, using the measuring jug to check a few.
  3. Add up your household's total for the day.
  4. Now imagine you live in the Thar Desert, where women may walk far to fetch water.
  5. List three changes you could make to use less water (e.g., reuse rinse water on plants, turn off the running tap).
  6. Write what a 'taanka' or 'kund' is and how it would help your imagined desert home.

👀 What you should see

You will be surprised how much water a normal day uses — and how clever Thar Desert habits like reusing rinse water really are.

🧠 Why it works

Measuring your own use makes the desert's water scarcity real and personal, turning an abstract fact into a habit you can change. It connects the geography of the Thar to everyday choices.
