India's long journey: how the land took shape

A few key moments in the deep history of India's landscape, from an ancient supercontinent to today.

  • Part of Gondwana
    India is joined to a giant southern supercontinent called Gondwana, with Africa as a neighbour.
  • Aravallis already ancient
    The Aravalli mountains form — about 2.5 billion years ago — making them among the oldest mountains on Earth.
  • India drifts north
    India breaks away from Gondwana and slowly moves north across the ocean toward Asia.
  • Collision with Eurasia
    About 50 million years ago, India collides with Eurasia; the crumpling land begins to raise the Himalayas.
  • Still rising today
    India keeps pushing north at about 5 cm a year, so the Himalayas are still growing and the region still has earthquakes.
