Every topic has traps that catch careful students. Here are the most common ones for this chapter, paired with what is actually true, so you can sidestep them.

❌ Many kids think…

All deserts are hot places full of sand.

✅ Actually…

A desert is defined by very LOW rainfall, not by heat. Ladakh is a COLD desert where winters fall below -30°C. Dryness, not temperature, makes a place a desert.

❌ Many kids think…

The Himalayas were formed long ago and are now finished growing.

✅ Actually…

India is still pushing into Asia at about 5 cm a year, so the Himalayas are still rising by a few millimetres each year — and the region still has earthquakes because of it.

❌ Many kids think…

A delta and an estuary are the same thing.

✅ Actually…

A delta is fertile land BUILT UP where a river drops lots of sediment into a calm sea (as on the East Coast). An estuary is a funnel-shaped river mouth where fresh and salt water mix without building much land (as on the West Coast).

❌ Many kids think…

The Western and Eastern Ghats are two matching walls of the same height.

✅ Actually…

They are very different. The Western Ghats are taller and almost unbroken, running like a wall close to the west coast. The Eastern Ghats are lower and broken into chunks, because east-flowing rivers like the Godavari and Krishna cut right through them on their way to the Bay of Bengal.

❌ Many kids think…

All of India's big rivers behave the same way through the year.

✅ Actually…

Himalayan rivers like the Ganga are 'perennial' — fed by melting snow and glaciers, they flow even in the dry season. Most peninsular rivers like the Godavari depend on rain, so they swell during the monsoon and shrink in summer.
