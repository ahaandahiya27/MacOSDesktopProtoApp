Weather, Seasons and Climate

The everyday confusion between weather and climate cleared up, how seasons fit in, India's extra rainy season and the six ṛitus, and a tour of the many climate types found across India.

  1. Weather versus ClimateWeather is what we feel hour by hour; climate is the long-term pattern of weather a region has over many decades.
  2. Seasons and the Six ṚitusSeasons recur every year as the Earth revolves around the Sun, and India traditionally divides the year into six ṛitus.
  3. The Many Climates of IndiaIndia has many climate types at once — alpine, temperate, subtropical, arid, tropical wet, semi-arid and tropical — matching its diverse land.

Factors Determining the Climate

The five forces that decide a region's climate — latitude, altitude, nearness to the sea, winds and topography — plus the idea of a microclimate and urban heat islands.

  1. LatitudePlaces near the Equator (low latitudes) are hotter, and places near the poles (high latitudes) are colder, because of the angle of the Sun's rays.
  2. AltitudeTemperature decreases as altitude increases, which is why high places like hill stations and Himalayan peaks are cool or freezing.
  3. Proximity to the SeaPlaces near the sea have mild, even temperatures because the sea acts as a moderator, while inland places have more extreme summers and winters.
  4. WindsWinds move masses of warm or cool, dry or moist air, affecting a region's temperature, humidity and rainfall.
  5. Topography and MicroclimatesThe shape of the land (topography) shapes climate — mountains can block winds or force rain — and small areas can have their own microclimate.

The Monsoons

What the monsoon really is, how the unequal heating of land and ocean drives it, the southwest (summer) and northeast (winter) monsoons, how the rains advance across India, and the role of the Western Ghats.

  1. What the Monsoon Is and How It FormsThe monsoon is a system of seasonal winds, driven by the simple fact that land heats and cools faster than the ocean, that brings India's rains.
  2. Southwest and Northeast MonsoonsThe summer (southwest) monsoon brings the main rains from sea to land, and the winter (northeast) monsoon reverses, bringing dry winds plus some rain to the east and south.
  3. How the Monsoon Advances and the Western GhatsThe monsoon rains advance from India's southern tip in early June up to the whole country by mid-July, and the Western Ghats make the west coast far wetter than the Deccan.

Climate, Our Lives and Climate Change

How climate shapes culture, festivals and the economy, what 'monsoon failure' does, and what climate change is — its human causes through the greenhouse effect and the need for mitigation and resilience.

  1. Climate, Culture and the EconomyOur lives, festivals, food and economy are deeply tied to climate, and a 'monsoon failure' can hurt farming, water supply and prices.
  2. Climate Change and the Greenhouse EffectClimate change is a long-term shift in climate, now driven mainly by humans burning fossil fuels, which adds greenhouse gases that trap extra heat.
  3. Mitigation, Adaptation and ResilienceGovernments and communities respond to climate change through mitigation (slowing warming) and by building resilience and adaptation.

Climates and Disasters

How India's weather can turn extreme — cyclones, floods (including glacial bursts), landslides and forest fires — what causes them, the role of human actions, and how the IMD and NDRF help prepare and respond.

  1. CyclonesA cyclone is an intense low-pressure storm that forms over the sea, common on India's eastern coast, with a calm cloudless centre called the 'eye'.
  2. Floods, Glacial Bursts and Urban FloodingFloods happen when water overflows onto dry land — from heavy monsoon rain, overflowing rivers, glacial bursts in the mountains, or poor drainage in cities.
  3. Landslides, Forest Fires and Disaster ResponseLandslides and forest fires are climate-linked disasters worsened by human actions, and India relies on the NDRF to rescue and respond.
