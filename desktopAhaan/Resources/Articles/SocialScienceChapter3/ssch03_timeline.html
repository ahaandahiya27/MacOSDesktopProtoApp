A year of the monsoon: arrival, advance and retreat

Follow the monsoon through the year — from the hot build-up of summer, through the rains sweeping up India, to the winter reversal.

  • Summer heat builds the low
    As summer begins, the Asian landmass heats up fast and forms a powerful low-pressure system, while the cooler ocean stays at higher pressure.
  • Rains reach the south (early June)
    Moist southwest-monsoon winds are drawn from the sea to the land; the rains typically reach India's southern tip in early June.
  • Monsoon advances northward (to mid-July)
    Over several weeks the rains move northward, covering the entire subcontinent by mid-July, heaviest on the west coast against the Western Ghats.
  • Winter reversal begins
    As winter approaches the land cools faster than the ocean, the land gains high pressure, and the winds reverse to blow from land to sea.
  • Northeast monsoon
    The dry winter (northeast) monsoon brings cold, dry weather to most of the land, but part crossing the Bay of Bengal rains on parts of east and south India.
