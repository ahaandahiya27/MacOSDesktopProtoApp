The 20 terms to know

  • Weather · मौसम — The state of the atmosphere experienced from hour to hour or day to day — such as rain, sunshine or wind — which keeps changing.
    Example: It was windy and rainy this morning, but sunny by afternoon.
  • Climate · जलवायु — The long-term pattern of weather a region experiences over a long period, at least several decades.
    Example: The Thar Desert has an arid climate with very little rainfall.
  • Ṛitu · ऋतु — A season in the traditional Indian calendar, which divides the year into six ṛitus.
    Example: Varṣhā is the rainy ṛitu, celebrated in many monsoon festivals.
  • Latitude · अक्षांश — A measure of how far a place is north or south of the Equator, increasing as you move away from it toward the poles.
    Example: Kanniyakumari is at a low latitude near the Equator, so it is warm all year.
  • Altitude · ऊँचाई / तुंगता — The height of a place above sea level; temperature decreases as altitude increases.
    Example: Shimla is cool because of its high altitude.
  • Topography · स्थलाकृति — The totality of the physical surface features of an area, which may include mountains, hills, valleys, slopes and coasts.
    Example: The topography of the Western Ghats forces monsoon winds to rise and rain.
  • Microclimate · सूक्ष्म जलवायु — A climate localised in a small area that differs from the surrounding region in temperature, humidity or precipitation.
    Example: An 'urban heat island' is a city's warmer microclimate caused by concrete and little vegetation.
  • Monsoon · मानसून / मॉनसून — Seasonal winds over the Indian Ocean and surrounding regions; commonly used to mean the seasonal rains they bring. From the Arabic 'mausim', meaning 'season'.
    Example: Most of India's rain comes from the summer southwest monsoon.
  • Cyclone · चक्रवात — An intense low-pressure storm that forms over the sea, with strong inward-spiralling winds and a calm, cloudless centre called the 'eye'.
    Example: The eastern coast of India is hit by several cyclones each year.
  • Greenhouse effect · हरितगृह प्रभाव — The natural trapping of the Sun's heat by gases like carbon dioxide in the atmosphere, which keeps Earth warm enough for life.
    Example: Burning fossil fuels adds greenhouse gases, strengthening the greenhouse effect and warming the planet.
  • Mitigation · शमन — The adoption of steps to help slow down global warming and reduce the causes of climate change.
    Example: Planting trees and using renewable energy are forms of climate mitigation.
  • Resilience · लचीलापन / प्रत्यास्थता — The capacity to withstand or recover quickly from difficulties.
    Example: Communities build resilience so they can bounce back faster after a flood.
  • Equator · भूमध्य रेखा — From Latin aequator ('equaliser') — the imaginary line that divides the Earth into equal northern and southern halves; places near it receive the most direct sunlight all year.
    Example: Lands near the Equator are hot because the Sun's rays strike them almost straight down.
  • Maritime climate · समुद्री जलवायु — From Latin mare ('sea') — the mild, even climate of places near the sea, which warms and cools slowly.
    Example: Mumbai's maritime climate keeps it pleasant all year, never very hot or very cold.
  • Continental climate · महाद्वीपीय जलवायु — From Latin continentem ('continuous land') — the climate of inland places far from the sea, with very hot summers and very cold winters.
    Example: Nagpur, far inland, has a continental climate and bakes in summer.
  • Windward · पवनाभिमुख — The side of a hill or mountain that faces the wind, where moist air is forced up, cools and drops heavy rain.
    Example: The windward, western slopes of the Western Ghats are drenched by the monsoon.
  • Leeward · पवनविमुख — The sheltered side of a mountain, away from the wind; from Old English hleo ('shelter'). The air arriving here has already lost its moisture.
    Example: The leeward, eastern side of the Western Ghats is much drier.
  • Rain shadow · वृष्टि छाया — The dry region on the leeward side of a mountain, left in the 'shadow' of the rain that fell on the windward side.
    Example: The interior Deccan lies in the rain shadow of the Western Ghats.
  • Loo · लू — The hot, dry, dusty wind that blows across the plains of north India on summer afternoons (Hindi लू).
    Example: People stay indoors at midday in May to avoid the scorching loo.
  • El Niño · एल नीन्यो — Spanish for 'the (Christ) child' — a periodic warming of the Pacific Ocean that can weaken India's monsoon and cause poor rains.
    Example: In an El Niño year, India's farmers worry the monsoon may be weak.
