Q1. What is the difference between weather and climate? (NCERT page 65)

Weather is what we experience from day to day or hour to hour — it might be windy, rainy, hot or dry — and it keeps changing constantly. Climate, on the other hand, is the long-term pattern of weather that a particular region experiences over a long period of time, at least several decades. So the key difference is the timescale: weather is short-term and changeable, while climate is the steady, average pattern over many years. For example, a rainy afternoon in Delhi is weather, but saying 'Delhi has hot summers and cool winters' describes its climate.

Q2. Why do places near the ocean have milder temperatures than places far away from it? (NCERT page 65)

Because the sea acts as a moderator of temperature. Water heats up and cools down much more slowly than land, so the sea keeps nearby coastal places from becoming too hot in summer or too cold in winter, giving them mild, even temperatures with a small temperature range. Places far inland have no sea to even out the heat, so they warm up and cool down faster, giving them hotter summers and colder winters and a much larger temperature range. For instance, coastal Mumbai has a range of about 14°C, while inland Nagpur (at a similar latitude) has a range of about 34°C.

Q3. What role do monsoon winds play in affecting India's climate? (NCERT page 65)

Monsoon winds are central to India's climate and life. In summer, the warm southwest monsoon winds blow from the sea toward the hot land, carrying moisture that falls as the heavy rains on which most of India's farming depends — filling rivers, soaking the soil and helping crops grow. In winter, the winds reverse: the northeast monsoon blows from the land to the sea, bringing mostly dry, cold conditions, though part of it crosses the Bay of Bengal, picks up moisture, and brings rain to parts of east and south India. So the monsoon winds decide India's rainy season and shape its agriculture, water supply and economy.

Q4. Why is Chennai warm or hot throughout the year, while Leh is cold? (NCERT page 65)

Two factors explain the difference: latitude and altitude. Chennai lies at a low latitude, close to the Equator, where the Sun's rays strike almost directly and concentrate their heat, so it is warm or hot all year; it is also at low elevation near the warm sea. Leh, in Ladakh, is at a higher latitude in the far north AND at a very high altitude in the Himalayas — and since temperature falls as altitude increases (the air is thinner and farther from the warm ground), Leh stays cold throughout the year. So Chennai is hot mainly because of low latitude, while Leh is cold mainly because of high altitude (and high latitude).
