History, geography and society are full of 'what if' turning points. Think about each question yourself first — then open the panel to compare your reasoning.

What if land and the ocean heated up at exactly the same speed?

Think first, then open to compare

There would be no monsoon. The monsoon exists because land heats up (and cools down) faster than the ocean, creating a difference in pressure between the hot land and the cooler sea that pulls in moist winds. If both heated at the same rate, there would be no pressure difference to drive the seasonal winds, so the moist sea winds would not be drawn inland and India would lose its main source of rain. Farming, rivers and life across the subcontinent would be completely different and far drier.

What if the Western Ghats were not there?

Think first, then open to compare

The west coast would get much less rain. Right now the Western Ghats force the moist southwest-monsoon winds to rise, cool and dump heavy rain on their western slopes, while the Deccan behind them stays drier in a rain shadow. Without the Ghats, the winds would not be lifted so sharply over the coast, so the coast's tropical-wet climate (great for rice and spices) would weaken, while the rain might spread further inland. The sharp contrast between the lush coast and the semi-arid Deccan would largely disappear.

What if the monsoon failed to arrive one year?

Think first, then open to compare

Most Indian farming depends on monsoon rain, so a failed monsoon would mean drought: crops would wither, reservoirs and wells would run low, and food prices would rise sharply. Rivers fed by rain would shrink, hurting drinking water and hydro-power. This is why monsoon forecasting matters so much, and why India builds dams and irrigation to store water against a weak monsoon year. A single missing rainy season can ripple through the whole economy.
