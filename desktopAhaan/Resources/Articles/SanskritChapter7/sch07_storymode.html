Three short moments from an ordinary kid's life where the chapter's idea suddenly appears.

≈ 6 min · see-it-everywhere level

The chapter lives in the textbook for an hour a week. The IDEA inside the chapter shows up everywhere — at breakfast, on the playground, in a song, in a hospital, on a news bulletin. Here are three small scenes from Aria's life where the chapter walked off the page.

In a temple

Aria visits a temple with her parents. The priest chants ईशावास्यमिदं सर्वम् over the offering. Aria recognises the line — the chapter's opening sentence. The same verse, in the same form, in active use centuries after it was composed.

Looking at a tree

Aria's mother says, 'Don't pluck flowers carelessly.' Aria asks why. Her mother says, 'Everything has the divine in it.' Aria thinks: that is exactly ईशावास्यमिदं सर्वम्. The verse becomes practical advice about flowers.

Gandhi quote on a wall

Aria sees Gandhi's words on a school wall: '...if only this one verse survived, Hinduism would live forever.' The verse is the chapter's verse. Aria stops to read it again, more carefully this time.
