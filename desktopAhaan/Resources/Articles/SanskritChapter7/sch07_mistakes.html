ईशावास्यम् इदं सर्वम् · Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Five errors students often make in this chapter — and the gentle corrections that fix them.

≈ 5 min · honest-with-yourself level

Sanskrit makes precise distinctions that English glosses over — case endings, gender, tense, prefix vs augment. The mistakes below are not personal failings — they are signals that a specific detail of the language needs another look.

Mistake 1 — Reading 'ईश्' in ईशावास्यम् as 'ish/like'

What students often write/say: ईश looks like the English '-ish' suffix.

The correct form: ईश here = 'Lord' (from the root ईश् 'to rule, own'). Same root as ईश्वर.

💡 Why

Sanskrit ईश has nothing to do with English -ish. It is one of the names of the Supreme. ईश्वर ('Lord') is its derivative.

Mistake 2 — Conflating ब्रह्म, ब्रह्मन्, and ब्रह्मा

What students often write/say: All three look similar.

The correct form: ब्रह्म = neuter, the Absolute (impersonal). ब्रह्मन् = masculine, a priest. ब्रह्मा = masculine, the creator-god.

💡 Why

Sanskrit gender + spelling distinguishes three distinct concepts. The chapter's ईशावास्यम् refers to NEUTER ब्रह्म — the Absolute that pervades all. Mixing up the three is a common error in religious-text translation.

Mistake 3 — Saying 'सर्वशक्तिमान्' means 'has all powers'

What students often write/say: Close, but the suffix matters.

The correct form: सर्व + शक्ति + -मत् (नोमिनेटिवे -मान्) = 'possessing all power' = ALL-POWERFUL / OMNIPOTENT.

💡 Why

The -मत्/-वत् suffix means 'possessing'. So सर्वशक्तिमान् doesn't 'have powers' (plural items); he POSSESSES THE QUALITY of all-power (omnipotence).

Mistake 4 — Translating 'दास्यन्ति' as 'they give'

What students often write/say: Looks like a regular present-tense verb.

The correct form: दास्यन्ति is FUTURE tense ('they SHALL/will give'). Present is ददति or दत्ते (different forms).

💡 Why

The -स्य- before the personal ending marks Sanskrit's future tense. दा (root) + स्य (future marker) + न्ति (3rd pl) = 'they will give'.

Mistake 5 — Treating 'यक्षाः', 'गन्धर्वाः', 'किन्नराः' as gods (देवाः)

What students often write/say: All are divine beings.

The correct form: They are SEMI-divine — distinct classes attending the gods, not gods themselves.

💡 Why

Sanskrit cosmology has a hierarchy: देव (gods) → upa-deva (semi-divine: यक्ष, गन्धर्व, किन्नर, अप्सरस्) → manuṣya (humans) → asura (anti-gods). The chapter names semi-divine beings, not full gods.

📝 Self-check

Re-read the chapter once with these five mistakes in mind. Notice the places where the textbook has DELIBERATELY chosen one form over another — that's often where the trap lies.
