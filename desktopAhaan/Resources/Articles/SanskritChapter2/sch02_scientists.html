Minds Behind नित्यं पिबामः सुभाषितरसम्

Sanskrit's living tradition was built by real people. Here is one of them — whose work the chapter quietly stands on.

≈ 7 min read · for curious 12-year-olds

Every Sanskrit chapter you read is the visible tip of a much longer history. Behind each verse, each word-table, each story is at least one person who made the next step possible. This article names one of them.

Bhartṛhari (around 5th century CE (dates uncertain))

Indian. Poet-philosopher whose three centuries of Sanskrit verse shaped India's subhashita tradition.

Bhartṛhari is the traditional author of the Śatakatraya — the 'three centuries' of Sanskrit verse: Nīti-śataka (100 verses on ethics), Śṛṅgāra-śataka (100 on love), and Vairāgya-śataka (100 on renunciation). Many of the chapter's subhashitas, including the famous 'five va-qualities' and 'six faults' verses, are widely attributed to him. Legend says he was originally a king who renounced his throne after a personal disillusionment and spent the rest of his life writing wisdom-verses. Scholars debate the historical details, but the literary impact is undisputed — his three hundred verses are among the most-quoted lines in all of Sanskrit literature.

💡 What they gave us

Bhartṛhari shows that even a short, pointed verse — if well-made — can outlive empires. His four-line gems are still memorised by Sanskrit students 1,500 years later.

📝 Stretch your thinking

The figures behind Sanskrit's tradition rarely worked alone — Pāṇini built on Yāska, Patañjali built on Pāṇini, Śaṅkara built on the Upaniṣads, Bhartṛhari built on centuries of prior verse. Every great mind sits on top of another. Whose 'shoulders' would you most want to stand on?
