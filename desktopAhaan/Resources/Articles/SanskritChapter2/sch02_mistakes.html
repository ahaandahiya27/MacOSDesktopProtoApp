Common Mistakes

Five errors students often make in this chapter — and the gentle corrections that fix them.

≈ 5 min · honest-with-yourself level

Sanskrit makes precise distinctions that English glosses over — case endings, gender, tense, prefix vs augment. The mistakes below are not personal failings — they are signals that a specific detail of the language needs another look.

Mistake 1 — Listing seven faults instead of six

What students often write/say: Students add laziness AND procrastination as two different items — but दीर्घसूत्रता already names procrastination.

The correct form: The shloka names exactly six: निद्रा, तन्द्रा, भयम्, क्रोधः, आलस्यम्, दीर्घसूत्रता.

💡 Why

Count carefully. आलस्यम् (laziness) and दीर्घसूत्रता (procrastination) ARE different — laziness is general inactivity; procrastination is delaying SPECIFIC tasks. But they are still each one item; together they make two of the six.

Mistake 2 — Saying 'चत्वारः वकाराः' (four va-s) instead of पञ्च (five)

What students often write/say: Easy to lose count of the va-words.

The correct form: Five: विद्यया, वपुषा, वाचा, वस्त्रेण, विनयेन.

💡 Why

All five start with व: knowledge, body, speech, dress, humility. Count by tapping fingers — विद्या-वपुस्-वाणी-वस्त्र-विनय — five.

Mistake 3 — Confusing 'दीर्घसूत्रता' with 'दीर्घायुष्'

What students often write/say: Both start with दीर्घ ('long').

The correct form: दीर्घसूत्रता = 'long-thread-ness' = procrastination. दीर्घायुष् = 'long-lived' = blessing of long life.

💡 Why

Sanskrit compounds: दीर्घ + सूत्र + ता (long thread, abstract) vs दीर्घ + आयुस् (long lifespan). Same first word, very different meanings.

Mistake 4 — Saying 'बुद्धिः जलेन शुध्यति' (intellect cleaned by water)

What students often write/say: The famous purification verse: by what is each part cleaned?

The correct form: बुद्धिः ज्ञानेन शुध्यति (intellect by KNOWLEDGE). गात्राणि (body) is what water cleans.

💡 Why

The verse pairs different parts with different means: water-body, truth-mind, austerity-soul, knowledge-intellect. Mixing them defeats the lesson.

Mistake 5 — Treating 'सुभाषितम्' as feminine

What students often write/say: The -आ in some uses might look feminine.

The correct form: सुभाषितम् is NEUTER (ends in -अम्, declines like ज्ञानम्).

💡 Why

Sanskrit gender is grammatical, not biological. The -अम् ending here marks the NEUTER form (नपुंसकलिङ्ग). Plural सुभाषितानि (neuter), not सुभाषिताः.

📝 Self-check

Re-read the chapter once with these five mistakes in mind. Notice the places where the textbook has DELIBERATELY chosen one form over another — that's often where the trap lies.
