Every topic has traps that catch careful students. Here are the most common ones for this chapter, paired with what is actually true, so you can sidestep them.

❌ Many kids think…

An empire is just a really big kingdom ruled directly by one king.

✅ Actually…

An empire is many kingdoms layered under one emperor. The smaller kings usually keep their thrones as tributaries (vassals) who pay tribute and accept the emperor's overlordship — it is the layered, top-down authority, not just size, that makes it an empire.

❌ Many kids think…

Alexander the Great conquered the whole of India.

✅ Actually…

Alexander reached only the northwest of India (around 327–325 BCE), defeated Porus in Punjab, and turned back when his tired soldiers refused to advance toward the Ganga. His direct political impact on India was limited; his bigger effect was opening Indo-Greek cultural contact.

❌ Many kids think…

Prakrit and Brahmi are the same thing, both languages.

✅ Actually…

Prakrit is a language (what people spoke); Brahmi is a script (a way of writing). Ashoka's edicts were mostly in the Prakrit language, written in the Brahmi script. A language is what you speak; a script is what you write it in.

❌ Many kids think…

Ashoka fought the Kalinga War in order to spread Buddhism.

✅ Actually…

It was the other way round. He attacked Kalinga to EXPAND his empire by conquest. The terrible bloodshed there shocked him afterwards, and that remorse led him to turn toward dhamma and peace. The war caused the change of heart; it was not fought for it.

❌ Many kids think…

An emperor personally governed every town and village in his empire.

✅ Actually…

No emperor could do that. An empire was held together in layers — tributary (vassal) kings kept their thrones, satraps or governors ran provinces, and a bureaucracy of officials collected taxes and kept order on the emperor's behalf. He ruled through others, not directly everywhere.
