From mahājanapadas to the Mauryas: the rise of India's first empire

Key moments in the rise of empires in north India, from the age of the sixteen great kingdoms to the end of the Maurya empire. (All dates are BCE — Before the Common Era — so larger numbers are earlier.)

  • Age of the mahājanapadas (c. 600 BCE)
    North and central India is divided into sixteen large kingdoms, the mahājanapadas, with their assembly systems. King Ajātaśhatru helps make Magadha a dominant power.
  • Rise of the Nandas (c. 5th century BCE)
    Mahāpadma Nanda founds the Nanda dynasty, unifies many kingdoms across eastern and northern India, issues coins and maintains a large army.
  • Alexander reaches India (c. 327–325 BCE)
    Alexander of Macedonia, having conquered the Persian Empire, defeats Porus in Punjab; his tired soldiers refuse to advance toward the Ganga, so he turns back.
  • Maurya empire founded (c. 321 BCE)
    Chandragupta Maurya, helped by Kauṭilya, overthrows the unpopular Dhana Nanda, makes Pāṭaliputra his capital, defeats the Greek satraps and expands the empire.
  • Reign of Ashoka (268–232 BCE)
    Ashoka expands the empire to almost the whole subcontinent, then renounces war after the Kalinga campaign and spreads dhamma through rock and pillar edicts.
  • End of the Maurya empire (c. 185 BCE)
    About half a century after Ashoka, his successors cannot hold the empire together; smaller kingdoms break away and the Maurya empire ends.
