🗺️ Map the rise of an empire (about 75 min)

What you need

  • An outline map of India (printed or drawn)
  • Coloured pencils or sketch pens
  • A pencil and ruler
  • The maps in the chapter (Figs. 5.5, 5.12, 5.13, 5.17) to copy from

Steps

  1. Lightly draw the Ganga and Son rivers and mark the location of Magadha (south Bihar) and its capital Pāṭaliputra.
  2. In one colour, shade the area of the Nanda empire using the chapter's Nanda map.
  3. In a second colour, shade the larger Maurya empire (northwest to the Deccan, almost the whole subcontinent under Ashoka).
  4. Mark the two great trade routes — the Uttarapatha (north) and Dakṣhiṇapatha (south).
  5. Place dots and labels for a few Ashokan edict sites (such as Girnar, Sanchi, Dhauli and Sarnath).
  6. Add a small legend explaining each colour and symbol.
  7. Write two sentences on what your map shows about how the empire grew over time.

👀 What you should see

You will see clearly how the empire grew from Magadha around the Ganga, expanded under the Nandas, and reached almost the whole subcontinent under the Mauryas — and how trade routes and edict sites spread across that territory.

🧠 Why it works

Drawing the territory in stages turns dates and names into a visible story of expansion, fixing the geography of the Nanda and Maurya empires firmly in memory and showing the link between rivers, trade routes and political power.

📜 Write your own 'edict' (about 50 min)

What you need

  • A large sheet of brown or grey paper (to look like rock or pillar)
  • A marker or sketch pen
  • A pencil for drafting
  • Optional: a printout of part of an Ashokan edict from the chapter

Steps

  1. Read one of Ashoka's edicts quoted in the chapter (for example, his instructions to city magistrates to be fair).
  2. Notice the values it expresses: fairness, kindness, care for people and animals, and welfare.
  3. Imagine you are a ruler who wants your people to follow good values.
  4. Draft three or four short, clear 'rules' for your people, each promoting one good value (honesty, kindness, helping others, caring for nature).
  5. Write them neatly on your 'rock' sheet, beginning like Ashoka with a title for yourself.
  6. Decorate the edges with simple symbols (a wheel, a lion, a tree) like Mauryan art.
  7. Display your edict and explain to a friend why you chose those values.

👀 What you should see

You will understand why Ashoka chose simple, public messages: short clear rules in everyday words can reach and influence many people, which is exactly how his edicts worked.

🧠 Why it works

By composing your own edict you step into Ashoka's role as a 'great communicator' and feel why he used plain language (Prakrit) and visible stone — communication and shared values help hold a large, diverse community together.
