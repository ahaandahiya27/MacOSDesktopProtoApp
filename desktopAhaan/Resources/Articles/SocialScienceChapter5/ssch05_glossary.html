The 10 terms to know

  • Empire · साम्राज्य — A collection of smaller kingdoms or territories over which one powerful ruler exerts supreme power; from the Latin imperium, 'supreme power'.
    Example: The Maurya empire stretched from the northwest to the Deccan plateau.
  • Tributary (Vassal) · करद / सामंत — A ruler or state that has submitted to an emperor and pays tribute as a sign of submission, loyalty or respect.
    Example: Tributary kings paid the emperor gold, grain or elephants to keep their thrones.
  • Guild (Śhreni) · श्रेणी — A powerful association of traders, craftsmen, moneylenders or farmers that pooled resources and made its own rules.
    Example: A weavers' guild set quality standards and shared market information.
  • Mahājanapadas · महाजनपद — The sixteen large kingdoms of north and central India, with assembly systems, from which Magadha rose to dominance.
    Example: Magadha was one of the sixteen mahājanapadas.
  • Satrap · क्षत्रप — A governor of a province in the Persian and Greek empires, left by the overlord to manage far-off territories, holding significant power.
    Example: Chandragupta defeated the Greek satraps left by Alexander in the northwest.
  • Arthaśhāstra · अर्थशास्त्र — Kauṭilya's famous treatise, literally 'the science of governance and economics', covering defence, administration, justice and welfare.
    Example: The saptānga theory comes from the Arthaśhāstra.
  • Saptānga · सप्तांग — Kauṭilya's concept of the seven parts that make up a kingdom: king, ministers, territory and people, forts, treasury, army and justice, and allies.
    Example: A weakness in any one of the seven saptānga limbs harms the whole state.
  • Edict · राजाज्ञा / अभिलेख — An official declaration issued by an authority, such as a king; Ashoka's edicts were engraved on rocks and pillars.
    Example: Ashoka's Kalinga edict records his remorse after the war.
  • Dharma (Dhamma) · धर्म — Moral law and one's ethical and religious duties; at a deeper level, living in harmony with the order of the universe (ṛitam) — duty, law, truth, order and ethics together.
    Example: Ashoka's edicts encouraged people to follow dhamma.
  • Dharmachakra · धर्मचक्र — The 'wheel of dharma' symbolising the Buddha's teachings; it appears on the Sarnath Lion Capital and at the centre of India's national flag.
    Example: The blue wheel on the Indian flag is the dharmachakra.
