History, geography and society are full of 'what if' turning points. Think about each question yourself first — then open the panel to compare your reasoning.

What if Dhana Nanda had ruled wisely instead of oppressing his people?

Think first, then open to compare

If Dhana Nanda had been a fair and popular ruler, the Nanda empire might have lasted much longer and resisted Chandragupta Maurya's challenge. Kauṭilya, who turned against the Nandas only after being insulted and seeing their oppression, might never have helped overthrow them. Without the people's resentment to exploit, Chandragupta would have found it far harder to seize Magadha, and India's first great empire might have stayed Nanda rather than Maurya. This shows how a ruler's loss of his people's trust — not just military defeat — can decide the fate of an empire.

What if Ashoka had ignored the suffering of the Kalinga war and kept conquering?

Think first, then open to compare

If Ashoka had felt no remorse after Kalinga, he might have continued waging aggressive wars to expand further. India's history could have been very different: the powerful state-backed spread of Buddhism to Sri Lanka, Thailand and Central Asia might never have happened, so Buddhism might have remained a smaller, mainly Indian tradition. There would be no dhamma edicts teaching peace, kindness and tolerance, and Ashoka would be remembered as just another conqueror rather than as a ruler who chose welfare over war. The Sarnath Lion Capital — now India's national emblem — is tied to this Buddhist, dhamma-focused phase of his reign.

What if Ashoka had not turned to dhamma after the Kalinga War?

Think first, then open to compare

The bloodshed at Kalinga so shook Ashoka that he gave up conquest for dhamma — a policy of tolerance, welfare and non-violence broadcast on rock and pillar edicts across the empire. Without that change, the Mauryas might have kept expanding by war, and we would lose one of history's rare examples of a powerful ruler choosing peace at the height of his strength. His edicts, which still teach us today, would never have been carved.
