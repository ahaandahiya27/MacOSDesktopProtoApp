One Curious Person Behind Chapter 2

The scientist most tied to nutrition in animals — their question, their experiment, and what changed because of them.

≈ 4 min read · biography

Every chapter has a person behind it — someone who first asked the right question, ran the right experiment, or wrote down the answer. This is a one-page portrait of the scientist most tied to this chapter's content.

Charaka

circa 100 BCE – 200 CE · Indian (Ancient)

🎧 In one line

Wrote the first systematic Indian account of digestion as fire — and described what we now call enzymes, hormones, and the gut microbiome two thousand years before modern medicine.

Long before microscopes, an Indian physician named Charaka was already writing about how the body turned food into life. His treatise, the Charaka Samhita, treats digestion as a fire (agni) burning inside the body — different agnis in different organs, each transforming food into the body's seven essential tissues. Charaka described why food stays in the stomach for a fixed time, why some people digest dairy easily and others don't, and why the same meal causes different effects in different bodies. He described the consequences of weak digestive fire (today: low stomach acid, poor enzymes), gas formation (today: gut microbiome imbalance), and even prescribed buttermilk and certain spices as digestive aids — exactly the kind of fermented + carminative food modern nutritionists still recommend. The Sushruta Samhita, written around the same time, complemented Charaka's internal-medicine view with detailed anatomy of the digestive organs. India had a model of digestion that worked, in practical terms, for two millennia before Beaumont's gastric experiments in 1822.
