A Class 7 Nutrition in Animals Dictionary

The 10 must-know terms from Chapter 2 — in plain English plus the Hindi name, with a one-line meaning a Class 7 student can actually use.

The 10 terms to know

  • Ingestion · अंतर्ग्रहण — The first step of nutrition: taking food into the body through the mouth.
    Example: When you bite into a roti, ingestion has begun.
  • Digestion · पाचन — The breaking down of complex food into simpler molecules the body can absorb.
    Example: Saliva digesting starch into maltose is the first step of digestion.
  • Absorption · अवशोषण — The movement of digested nutrients from the small intestine into the blood.
    Example: Glucose passing through the villi wall into a blood capillary is absorption.
  • Assimilation · स्वांगीकरण — Using absorbed nutrients to build new tissue, repair damage, and store energy.
    Example: Amino acids becoming muscle proteins after a workout is assimilation.
  • Egestion · बहिःक्षेपण — Removing undigested food from the body as solid waste.
    Example: Going to the toilet is the egestion step.
  • Pepsin · पेप्सिन — A protein-digesting enzyme released by the stomach lining; works best in acid.
    Example: Pepsin breaks the protein in your egg-and-cheese sandwich into smaller chains called peptides.
  • Bile · पित्त — A greenish fluid made by the liver and stored in the gall bladder; breaks fats into tiny droplets so enzymes can digest them.
    Example: After a ghee-rich meal, bile flows from the gall bladder into the small intestine.
  • Villi · रसांकुर — Millions of tiny finger-like projections lining the inner wall of the small intestine; they multiply the absorption surface.
    Example: Each villus is less than a millimetre long but absorbs nutrients into a network of blood capillaries inside it.
  • Peristalsis · क्रमाकुंचन — Wave-like muscular contractions that push food along the digestive tract.
    Example: Peristalsis is why you can swallow even when standing upside down — it doesn't need gravity.
  • Pseudopodia · कूटपाद — Temporary 'fake feet' that single-celled organisms like Amoeba extend to engulf food or move.
    Example: An Amoeba spotting a smaller microbe extends pseudopodia around it, surrounding it like a bag.
