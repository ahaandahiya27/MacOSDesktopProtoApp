Five Wrong Answers Class 7 Students Give

The five places almost every Class 7 student trips up on Chapter 2 — and the one-line fix for each.

≈ 5 min read · revision-tier

Examiners say the same wrong answers come back year after year. None of these mistakes are silly — they are almost right. That is exactly what makes them dangerous. Read each one, cover the answer, and try to spot what is off before peeking.

1. “Most digestion happens in the stomach.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: The stomach does about 30% of digestion — it churns food, kills germs with acid, and starts protein digestion with pepsin. The real heavy lifting happens in the small intestine, where bile, pancreatic enzymes, and intestinal enzymes finish digesting carbohydrates, proteins, and fats. The small intestine is also where 95% of absorption happens.

2. “Saliva is just for moistening food.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Saliva does moisten food, but it ALSO contains an enzyme called amylase that begins digesting starch right in your mouth. Try chewing plain roti for 30 seconds — it starts to taste sweet because amylase has converted some starch into maltose. So digestion really begins before you swallow.

3. “Longer intestines mean better digestion.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Length depends on diet, not 'better'. Herbivores like cows have very long intestines (cow's small intestine is ~40 metres) because grass cellulose is tough and slow to digest. Carnivores like cats and tigers have short intestines (a tiger's is just ~5 metres) because meat is easy to digest. Humans, being omnivores, are in between — about 6–7 metres.

4. “We absorb nutrients in the stomach.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: The stomach is a digestion + storage organ; it absorbs almost nothing (only some water, alcohol, and certain medicines). Almost all nutrient absorption happens in the small intestine, where the inner wall has billions of finger-like villi and microvilli that multiply the absorption surface to the size of a tennis court folded inside you.

5. “The appendix is a useless organ.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: For a long time doctors thought so. Current research shows the appendix probably acts as a 'safe house' for the helpful gut bacteria — when you get severe diarrhoea and lose your gut microbiome, the appendix re-seeds it. People who've had their appendix removed are slightly more prone to certain gut infections, supporting this idea.

📝 Stretch your thinking

Pick any two of these and explain them to a younger sibling using a one-line analogy each. If you can compress a mistake into one sentence with one image, you have understood it.
