The five steps of nutrition

Nutrition has five steps: ingestion, digestion, absorption, assimilation, and egestion.

≈ 8 min read · for curious 12-year-olds

Think of food going on a journey through your body. First, you put it in your mouth — that's ingestion. Then your body breaks it into tiny pieces — that's digestion. The useful bits cross into your blood — absorption. Your cells use those bits to grow and run — assimilation. Whatever's left over comes out the other end — egestion. Five steps. Every meal you eat goes through all of them.

Food in your hand isn't usable by your cells — its molecules are too big and locked in complex structures. The five steps form a pipeline: get food in, break it down, ship it to cells, use it, get rid of leftovers. Skip any step and the system breaks. This concept explains how your digestive system accomplishes this, step by step.

What this concept means

Nutrition is the process by which an organism takes in food and uses it for growth, energy, and repair. It involves five sequential steps: (1) Ingestion — taking food into the body; (2) Digestion — breaking complex food into simpler molecules using mechanical action and digestive enzymes; (3) Absorption — passing the simple nutrients from the alimentary canal into the bloodstream; (4) Assimilation — utilising the absorbed nutrients in body cells to release energy or build tissue; (5) Egestion — removing undigested waste from the body.

Why it works this way

Food in your hand isn't usable by your cells — its molecules are too big and locked in complex structures. The five steps form a pipeline: get food in, break it down, ship it to cells, use it, get rid of leftovers. Skip any step and the system breaks.

💡 Did you know?

Some animals run this pipeline at extreme speed. A shrew (a tiny mouse-like mammal) can ingest, digest, and egest a meal in just 3 hours — it has to, because it eats nearly its own body weight every day to stay alive. At the other extreme, a python can take 6 weeks to fully digest a deer. The same five steps, vastly different time scales, all driven by metabolism.

Real-world applications

  1. Eating a roti — Bite (ingestion), chew + saliva (digestion starts), the simple sugars cross into blood in the small intestine (absorption), your cells convert them to energy (assimilation), the fibre passes out (egestion).
  2. Why people skip breakfast and feel tired — No ingestion → no glucose absorbed → cells run low on fuel → you feel sluggish until lunch.
  3. Constipation — When the large intestine absorbs too much water from waste, egestion becomes painful — the same five-step pipeline, with the last stage struggling.
  4. Milk protein digestion — A baby's body ingests milk, the stomach digests proteins via pepsin, small intestine absorbs amino acids, body uses them to build muscle, unused bits are egested.
  5. Rice and vegetables meal — Boiled rice ingested, both carbs and plant fibres are digested partially in mouth and stomach, simple sugars absorbed in small intestine, body uses for energy, fibre mostly egested.
  6. Chocolate after exercise — Quick ingestion of sugar, rapid absorption in small intestine, glucose assimilated by muscles for recovery energy, minimal waste to egestion.
  7. Diarrhoea during fever — Infection → small intestine absorbs too quickly → water passes through → egestion of loose stools before the other steps finish properly.
  8. Medicinal herbs brewing — Ingesting herb tea → digestion extracts active compounds → absorption into blood → assimilation by cells → egestion of the plant matter.
  9. Cow eating grass — Ingestion of grass → ruminal digestion (with microbe help) → absorption in the abomasum and intestines → assimilation for milk/muscle → egestion of manure.
  10. Earthworm composting — Ingestion of dead leaves → grinding in gizzard + enzyme digestion → nutrient absorption across intestine → assimilation for growth → egestion enriches soil.

🌍 In your body right now

You can feel your own nutrition happening right now. Your last meal is breaking down in your stomach using HCl acid and pepsin enzyme. Some nutrients are entering your bloodstream through your small intestine. Some are being stored in your liver as glycogen to use later. Each step is happening in parallel in different parts of your body simultaneously.

🧪 Try this at home

Place some bread in your mouth without chewing for 30 seconds. Notice it begins to taste sweet — that's salivary amylase breaking down starch into maltose. This proves digestion starts in the mouth.

🧠 Check your understanding

Question: What would happen if your stomach didn't produce hydrochloric acid?

Answer: Food wouldn't be broken down chemically. Only the mechanical churning would happen, leaving larger particles that are harder for the small intestine to absorb. Proteins wouldn't be denatured by pepsin, and you'd lose a major source of nutrition.

🧠 Try another

Question: Why is the small intestine much longer than the large intestine?

Answer: The small intestine is where most digestion finishes and absorption happens — it needs maximum surface area (about 6 meters long). The large intestine is only for water reabsorption and waste storage, so it doesn't need to be long.

🧠 One more

Question: Does the liver digest food, or does it do something else?

Answer: The liver doesn't digest food directly. Instead, it produces bile (which breaks down fats), stores glucose, detoxifies poisons, and processes amino acids. It's more of a chemical factory and warehouse than a digestive organ.
