How the human body turns food into fuel

Explore how animals take in, break down, and use food for survival

≈ 15 min read overview · 12 concepts

Nutrition in humans happens in five steps along a long tube — the alimentary canal — from the mouth all the way to the anus. Each organ has a specific job and a specific juice.

In this topic, you'll learn the actual mechanisms — the organs, enzymes, acids, and muscular movements — that turn a meal into fuel your body can use.

Whether you're curious about how your own stomach works, or how evolution has shaped digestion across the animal kingdom, this topic covers the diversity of animal nutrition.

What you'll learn in this topic

  • Every animal eats, but not all animals eat the same way.
  • The human digestive system is a specialized tube with specific organs.
  • Digestion is both mechanical and chemical.
  • Nutrition includes absorption and assimilation, not just digestion.
  • Different animals have evolved different digestive systems.
  • Understanding digestion helps explain health and disease.

Why this topic matters

Nutrition is the process that keeps every animal alive. Understanding it explains why different animals have different bodies, why you get hungry at certain times, why some foods are easier to digest than others, and why malnutrition causes disease.
