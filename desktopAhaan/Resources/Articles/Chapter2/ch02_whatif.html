Three "What Ifs?" About Nutrition in Animals

Hypothetical questions with no single right answer. Think hard before you read the discussion.

≈ 7 min read · brain-stretcher level

The textbook tells you what is. The best science teachers also ask what if? Pick any one of the questions below. Pause. Think for at least one minute before scrolling. Then read the discussion.

What if 1 — What if you had only molars and no incisors or canines?

🧠 Think first

Pause. Write down your one-sentence guess before reading.

One discussion path: You could still grind food, but biting off chunks of an apple or roti would be impossible. You'd have to pre-cut everything with a knife. Worse, your jaw alignment would change because incisors and canines act as guides during chewing. Animals show this clearly: cows have no upper front teeth (they tear grass with a horny pad against lower incisors), and they get away with it because they live on soft pre-chewed grass. A human on a chana-and-roti diet without front teeth would lose efficiency fast.

What if 2 — What if your stomach acid was 10 times stronger?

🧠 Think first

Pause. Write down your one-sentence guess before reading.

One discussion path: Your stomach already produces hydrochloric acid at a pH of around 1.5 — strong enough to dissolve metal. Multiplying that by ten would mean pH ~0.5, the strength of car battery acid. The mucus barrier protecting your stomach lining would fail; you'd develop ulcers within hours. The acid would damage your oesophagus when burping. On the upside, you'd digest food faster and be virtually immune to most foodborne infections (bacteria can't survive that pH). Wild animals like vultures have stomach pH around 1.0 — they can safely eat rotten meat that would kill any human.

What if 3 — What if humans could digest cellulose like cows?

🧠 Think first

Pause. Write down your one-sentence guess before reading.

One discussion path: Grass, leaves, dry hay — all of that would become food. We could live on lawn clippings the way cows do. But there's a cost: we'd need a much longer intestine (cows are roughly 8–10 times our gut length per body size), or extra stomach chambers, or both. Our body shape and lifestyle would change. We'd also lose protein density per kilogram of food — cellulose is mostly carbohydrate, so we'd need to eat HUGE amounts daily. A cow eats 30+ kg of grass per day. Termites get around this with help: they don't digest cellulose themselves — bacteria and protists in their gut do it for them. So even 'digesting cellulose' is mostly symbiosis, not a personal superpower.

💬 How to use this

These are not exam questions. The answer marked ‘one discussion path’ is one valid line of reasoning — your own may be different and still good. Practising this kind of thinking is how scientists become scientists.
