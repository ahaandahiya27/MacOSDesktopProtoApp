"What If?" — Number Play

Hypothetical questions with no single right answer. Think hard before you read the discussion.

≈ 7 min read · brain-stretcher level

The textbook tells you what is. The best way to really understand an idea is to ask what if? Pick a question below, pause and think for a minute, then read one possible discussion.

What if 1 — every number were even?

The question: What if odd numbers didn't exist?

🧠 Think first

Could you ever split a group into two unequal halves? Could parity arguments work?

One discussion path: Half of arithmetic's structure would vanish — no parity, no 'odd one out', no proof that five odds can't sum to 30. Odd and even together give numbers their hidden two-colour pattern.

What if 2 — the Virahāṅka–Fibonacci sequence started 1, 2?

The question: What if the rule stayed 'add the last two' but began differently?

🧠 Think first

Would the same numbers appear?

One discussion path: You would get a different but related sequence (2, 3, 5, 8…). The rule, not the start, is what matters — and starting points change which numbers you visit. Patterns are defined by their rule.

What if 3 — a cryptarithm had two answers?

The question: What if SEND + MORE = MONEY could be solved two different ways?

🧠 Think first

Would it still be a good puzzle?

One discussion path: A well-made cryptarithm has exactly one solution, found by logic. Two answers would mean the clues weren't tight enough — the fun is that careful reasoning pins down a single code.

📝 Stretch your thinking

None of these has one 'correct' answer — they are for arguing about. Try one on a friend or a parent and see whether their discussion path differs from yours.
