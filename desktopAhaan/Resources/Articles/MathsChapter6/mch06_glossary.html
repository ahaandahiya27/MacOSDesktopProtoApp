Number Play: Key Words

Every term in this chapter, with a one-line meaning and an example.

&#8776; quick reference &middot; 10 terms

Knowing the exact words makes the maths click. Here is every key term from the chapter, defined plainly with an example you can picture.

Parity

Whether a whole number is even or odd.

Example: 6 has even parity; 7 has odd parity.

Even number

A number that can be split into pairs with nothing left over; a multiple of 2.

Example: 8 = 4 pairs.

In Hindi: सम संख्या

Odd number

A number that is one more than a multiple of 2; leaves one over when paired.

Example: 7 = 3 pairs + 1.

In Hindi: विषम संख्या

Proof

A general argument showing a statement is true for all cases, not just examples.

Example: (2m+1)+(2n+1)=2(m+n+1) proves two odds sum to even.

In Hindi: उपपत्ति

Virahāṅka–Fibonacci sequence

The sequence 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, … where each term is the sum of the previous two.

Example: 5 + 8 = 13.

Recurrence

A rule that gives each term of a sequence from earlier terms.

Example: F(n) = F(n−1) + F(n−2).

Cryptarithm

A puzzle where letters stand for digits and you deduce which digit each letter is.

Example: T+T+T = UT gives T = 5.

Sequence

An ordered list of numbers following a rule.

Example: 0, 1, 1, 2, 4, 1, 5 from the height game.

In Hindi: अनुक्रम

Invariant

A feature (like parity) that stays predictable and lets you rule out impossibilities.

Example: The parity of a sum of odds depends only on how many odds.

Golden ratio

The number (about 1.618) that consecutive Virahāṅka–Fibonacci terms approach in ratio.

Example: 13/8 ≈ 1.625, 21/13 ≈ 1.615.
