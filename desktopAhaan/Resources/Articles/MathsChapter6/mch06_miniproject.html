Number Play · Mini Project

🪙 Mini Project — Parity Detective

A hands-on activity that turns this chapter into something you actually do, not just read.

≈ 30 min · do-it-yourself level

Reading about maths and doing maths are two different things. This short project lets you see the ideas of "Number Play" at work with your own hands.

What you'll need

  • A handful of coins or counters
  • Paper and pen

Steps

  1. Grab any handful of coins and split them into pairs. If none are left over, the count is even; if one is left over, it's odd.
  2. Take two odd handfuls, combine them, and pair again — record whether the total is even or odd.
  3. Repeat with three odd handfuls, then four. Note the pattern.
  4. Now predict, WITHOUT counting, the parity of a sum of seven odd handfuls. Then check.
  5. Try to write the reason as a sentence: 'An odd number of odd groups gives a ___ total because ___.'

🔍 What you should notice

You'll find an even count of odd handfuls always pairs up perfectly (even total), while an odd count always leaves one stranded (odd total) — and you can predict it before counting.

Why it works

Each odd group is 'pairs plus one leftover'. When you combine groups, the leftovers try to pair with each other; an even number of leftovers pairs perfectly, an odd number strands one. So the parity of the total depends only on how many odd groups there are — a parity argument, made with coins.
