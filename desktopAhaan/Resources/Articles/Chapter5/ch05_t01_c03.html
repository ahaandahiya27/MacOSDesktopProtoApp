Indicators — How We Tell Acids from Bases

Chemical detectives that change colour to reveal a substance’s true identity

≈ 6 min read · for curious 12-year-olds

Imagine you have two clear liquids in identical glasses. One is lemon juice (an acid), the other is a baking-soda solution (a base). They look the same. You cannot tell them apart just by staring. But drop in a special substance called an indicator, and the liquids suddenly turn different colours — revealing which is the acid and which is the base. Indicators are like the secret agents of chemistry!

What is an indicator?

An indicator is a substance that changes colour when it comes in contact with an acid or a base. The colour change tells you whether the substance being tested is acidic, basic, or neutral.

There are two main types of indicators:

  • Natural indicators — obtained from plants (like litmus, turmeric, red cabbage).
  • Synthetic (artificial) indicators — made in laboratories (like phenolphthalein, methyl orange).

Litmus — the most famous indicator

Litmus is extracted from lichens (small plant-like organisms that grow on rocks and tree bark). It is the oldest and most widely used indicator in schools and labs. Litmus is available in two forms:

  • Litmus solution — a purple liquid.
  • Litmus paper — strips of paper soaked in litmus solution. They come in two colours: red and blue.

Memory trick

Think: “Bases make Blue” — both start with B. So a base turns red litmus blue. And acids do the opposite: they turn blue litmus red.

Turmeric — an indicator in your kitchen

The bright yellow turmeric (haldi) powder that your family uses in cooking is a natural indicator! Here is how it works:

  • In an acid: turmeric stays yellow (no change).
  • In a base: turmeric turns reddish-brown (or dark red).

Have you ever noticed that a turmeric stain on a white shirt turns reddish when you wash it with soap? That happens because soap is a base, and it changes the colour of turmeric!

Try this at home

Make a turmeric indicator: mix a pinch of turmeric powder in a little water and soak a white paper strip in it. Let it dry. Now dip the strip into soapy water — watch it turn red! Dip another strip into lemon juice — it stays yellow. You have just identified an acid and a base using a kitchen ingredient.

Other common indicators

Phenolphthalein

This is a synthetic indicator often used in labs:

  • In acids: colourless
  • In bases: pink (or magenta)

Methyl orange

  • In acids: red (or orange-red)
  • In bases: yellow

Red cabbage juice

Red cabbage juice is a brilliant natural indicator because it shows a range of colours:

  • Strong acid: red
  • Weak acid: pink
  • Neutral: purple
  • Weak base: blue-green
  • Strong base: yellow-green

The colour-changing magic of red cabbage

Red cabbage contains a pigment called anthocyanin. This pigment changes its molecular shape depending on how acidic or basic the solution is, which is what causes the wide range of colour changes. It is like a pH rainbow!

China rose (Gudhal) indicator

The petals of the China rose (hibiscus) flower can also be used as an indicator:

  • In acids: turns dark pink (magenta)
  • In bases: turns green

To make the indicator, soak a few China rose petals in warm water for a few minutes until the water becomes coloured. Use this coloured water as your indicator solution.

Why do we need indicators?

You might wonder: why not just taste a substance to see if it is sour or bitter? The answer is safety. Many acids and bases are extremely dangerous — they can burn your skin, damage your eyes, or poison you. Indicators let scientists identify acids and bases without ever needing to taste or touch the substance. They are a safe, reliable, and quick method of testing.

Quick check — What is an indicator?

Answer: An indicator is a substance that changes colour when it comes in contact with an acid or a base, helping us identify whether a substance is acidic, basic, or neutral.

Quick check — A turmeric stain on your shirt turns red when washed with soap. Why?

Answer: Soap is a base. Turmeric is a natural indicator that turns reddish-brown (or red) in the presence of a base. So the turmeric stain changes colour when it meets the basic soap solution.

Quick check — Which indicator gives a range of colours from red to green?

Answer: Red cabbage juice. It contains anthocyanin, a pigment that changes colour across a wide pH range — from red in strong acids to yellow-green in strong bases.
