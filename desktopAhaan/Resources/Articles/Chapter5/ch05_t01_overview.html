Chapter 5 · Topic 1 Overview

Acids and Bases

Meet the two great chemical families and learn how to tell them apart

≈ 4 min read · for curious 12-year-olds

Every liquid around you — lemon juice, shampoo, tap water, vinegar — can be sorted into one of three groups: acidic, basic (alkaline), or neutral. In this topic you will discover what makes a substance acidic or basic, learn about indicators that reveal their identity like secret-agent tools, and explore the pH scale that scientists use to measure exactly how acidic or basic something is.

What you will explore

  1. Acids — The Sour Family What acids are, common examples, and their key properties.
  2. Bases — The Bitter, Soapy Family What bases are, how they feel and taste, and everyday examples.
  3. Indicators — How We Tell Acids from Bases Litmus paper, phenolphthalein, and other chemical detectives.
  4. The pH Scale — Measuring Acidity and Alkalinity A numbered scale from 0 to 14 that tells you exactly how acidic or basic a substance is.
  5. Strong vs Weak Acids and Bases Why battery acid is dangerous but lemon juice is safe to drink.
  6. Natural Indicators — Turmeric, Litmus, and More Kitchen ingredients that double as chemistry tools.
  7. Acids and Bases in the Kitchen How baking soda, vinegar, curd, and lemon juice work their magic in recipes.

Where to start?

If you are new to this topic, begin with Concept 1 (Acids) and Concept 2 (Bases) to understand the basics. Then jump to Concept 3 (Indicators) to learn how scientists test for them. After that, read in any order you like!
