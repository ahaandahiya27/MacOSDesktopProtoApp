A Class 7 Acids, Bases and Salts Dictionary

The 10 must-know terms from Chapter 5 — in plain English plus the Hindi name, with a one-line meaning a Class 7 student can actually use.

≈ 5 min read · revision-ready

Reading the chapter once isn't enough. Knowing the words is what makes you confident. Glance through this deck every Sunday for four weeks and these terms will be yours.

The 10 terms to know

  • Acid · अम्ल — A substance that tastes sour, turns blue litmus red, and releases H⁺ ions in water.
    Example: Lemon juice (citric acid), curd (lactic acid), vinegar (acetic acid).
  • Base · क्षार — A substance that tastes bitter, feels soapy, turns red litmus blue, and releases OH⁻ ions in water.
    Example: Soap, baking soda, antacid tablets, milk of magnesia.
  • Indicator · सूचक — A substance that changes colour to tell whether a solution is acidic, basic, or neutral.
    Example: Litmus, methyl orange, phenolphthalein, red cabbage extract, turmeric.
  • Litmus · लिटमस — A natural dye from lichens, used as a simple acid-base indicator (blue → red in acid, red → blue in base).
    Example: Litmus paper strips are the lab's quickest pH check.
  • Neutralisation · उदासीनीकरण — The reaction between an acid and a base producing salt and water.
    Example: Antacid + stomach acid → salt + water + CO₂; the burn stops.
  • Salt · लवण — An ionic compound formed when an acid neutralises a base.
    Example: Common salt (NaCl) is formed by neutralising HCl with NaOH.
  • Sulphuric acid (H₂SO₄) · गंधक का अम्ल — The most important industrial acid, used in fertilisers, batteries, dyes, and detergents.
    Example: India produces ~6 million tonnes of H₂SO₄ per year — used mostly in DAP fertiliser.
  • Hydrochloric acid (HCl) · हाइड्रोक्लोरिक अम्ल — The acid in your stomach — kills germs in food and helps digest protein.
    Example: Stomach pH is ~1.5; this acid would dissolve metal but is contained safely by mucus.
  • Slaked lime · बुझा हुआ चूना — Calcium hydroxide, Ca(OH)₂ — the base in chuna paste in paan and in whitewash.
    Example: Whitewashing walls uses slaked lime; over weeks it absorbs CO₂ and hardens into calcium carbonate (chalk).
  • pH (preview) · पी एच — A 0-14 scale measuring how acidic or basic a solution is — 7 is neutral, below is acidic, above is basic. (You'll meet this formally in Class 10.)
    Example: Lemon juice pH ~2; pure water pH 7; soap pH ~10; oven cleaner pH ~13.
