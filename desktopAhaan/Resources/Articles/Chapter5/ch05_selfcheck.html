Question 3 — from Chapter 5

Three colourless solutions A, B, C are on a table. You drop blue litmus into each: A turns red, B stays blue, C stays blue. You drop red litmus into each: A stays red, B stays red, C turns blue. Identify the nature of A, B, and C, and explain how the TWO-litmus test rules out ambiguity that a single test would leave.

🧠 Answer in your head first

Don't peek. Speak the answer out loud, then check.

Answer: A is an ACID (only acids turn blue litmus red; red stays red — consistent). C is a BASE (only bases turn red litmus blue; blue stays blue — consistent). B is NEUTRAL (neither litmus changed colour — neither acid nor base). Why both tests matter: a single test is ambiguous. If only blue litmus were used, B and C would BOTH stay blue — you couldn't tell base from neutral. Red litmus distinguishes them: bases turn it blue, neutrals don't. Two tests give a unique fingerprint for each of the three categories.

Why: Recall the rules: acids turn BLUE litmus red. Bases turn RED litmus blue. Neutrals leave both unchanged.

Question 4 — from Chapter 5

Common salt (NaCl) is the salt formed from:

🧠 Answer in your head first

Don't peek. Speak the answer out loud, then check.

Answer: HCl + NaOH

Why: Hydrochloric acid + sodium hydroxide → sodium chloride + water.

Question 5 — from Chapter 5

Ocean acidification mainly harms which type of marine life?

🧠 Answer in your head first

Don't peek. Speak the answer out loud, then check.

Answer: Shell-builders (corals, oysters, sea-urchins)

Why: Acidified seawater dissolves calcium carbonate (CaCO₃) — the mineral shells and skeletons of corals, molluscs, sea urchins.
