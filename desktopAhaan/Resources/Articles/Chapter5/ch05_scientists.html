Svante Arrhenius (with India context)

1859–1927 · Swedish

🎧 In one line

Defined acids and bases by what they release in water — the modern definition every Class 10 student in India still learns.

In the 1880s, a young Swedish chemist named Svante Arrhenius proposed a strange idea: when salt or acid dissolves in water, it BREAKS APART into smaller charged particles called ions. His PhD examiners almost failed him — the idea sounded crazy. But Arrhenius pushed on. He defined acids as substances that release H⁺ ions in water, bases as substances that release OH⁻ ions. Neutralisation became elegant chemistry: H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O. In 1903 he won the Nobel Prize for this theory. The 1903 Nobel arrived in India through college chemistry textbooks within years; by the time independence-era scientists at IISc Bangalore and IIT-affiliated institutions started research, every Indian chemistry student learned 'acids release H+, bases release OH-'. That one-line definition still anchors the Class 10 acids/bases chapter you'll meet next year.
