Beyond the Book — Three Mind-Stretchers About Acids, Bases and Salts

Three ideas that don't show up in the NCERT textbook but will change how you see this chapter.

≈ 9 min read · curious-kid level

The chapter teaches you lemon juice, soap water, and table salt — three things from your kitchen that introduce three big chemical families. This article goes a step further — three stretch topics that lead naturally into Class 8 and beyond. Read one a day; don't try to absorb them all at once.

1. The pH scale — measuring how acidic something is

In Class 7 you saw indicators tell us acid/base. Class 8 introduces the pH SCALE — 0 to 14. pH 0-6: acidic (lower = more acidic). pH 7: neutral (pure water). pH 8-14: basic. Each unit is 10× — pH 3 is 100× more acidic than pH 5. Common Indian-context values: lemon juice pH 2, vinegar pH 3, tomato pH 4, rain pH 5.6, milk pH 6.7, blood pH 7.4, soap pH 9, ammonia pH 11, drain cleaner pH 14. Your stomach holds the strongest natural acid on Earth (pH 2). Your blood is maintained at exactly 7.4 — outside this narrow range, you die. The whole biochemistry of life depends on pH precision. Indian agricultural soils are pH-tested before fertiliser choice.

2. Salt hydrolysis — why some salts are acidic and others basic

Class 7 says salt = acid + base. Class 10 reveals a twist — not all salts dissolve to give NEUTRAL water. Salt from STRONG acid + STRONG base (NaCl from HCl + NaOH): dissolves neutral, pH 7. Salt from WEAK acid + strong base (sodium acetate from acetic acid + NaOH): dissolves BASIC, pH ~8 (the base is more dominant). Salt from strong acid + WEAK base (ammonium chloride from HCl + NH₃): dissolves ACIDIC, pH ~5. This is HYDROLYSIS — water molecules react with salt ions. Practical Indian context: alum (potash alum, used in water purification) dissolves slightly acidic — kills some bacteria. Sodium bicarbonate (baking soda) dissolves slightly basic — neutralises stomach acid in indigestion remedies.

3. Brønsted-Lowry vs Lewis — three acid-base theories

Class 7's acid-base definition (Arrhenius) is the simplest: acids release H⁺ in water, bases release OH⁻. Class 11 expands to BRØNSTED-LOWRY: acids DONATE H⁺ (proton); bases ACCEPT H⁺. This broader definition includes ammonia (NH₃ accepts H⁺ from water, forming NH₄⁺ — basic) which Arrhenius couldn't explain. Then LEWIS theory generalises further: acids ACCEPT an electron pair; bases DONATE one. Now even substances without H⁺ can be acids (BF₃, AlCl₃) — used in industrial chemistry. Each theory is more general. NEET tests all three. Lewis theory underpins modern catalysis, biology (enzyme-substrate binding), and even photosynthesis machinery — all involve Lewis acid-base interactions.
