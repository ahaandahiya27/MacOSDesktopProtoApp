Neutralisation — when acid meets base

When an acid reacts with a base, they neutralise each other to produce a salt and water, releasing some heat.

≈ 2 min read · for curious 12-year-olds

If you mix lemon juice (acid) with baking soda water (base), you'll see bubbling and feel slight warmth. They've cancelled each other out — the sour and bitter both disappear. This is neutralisation. The general rule is: Acid + Base → Salt + Water + Heat. Doctors use this when you have stomach acidity (they give you an antacid like Eno or Digene). Farmers use it on acidic soil (they add lime). It's one of the most useful chemical reactions on Earth.

The textbook version

Neutralisation is the chemical reaction in which an acid reacts with a base to produce salt and water. Heat is released in the process. The general equation is: Acid + Base → Salt + Water (+ Heat). For example: HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O. Here hydrochloric acid + sodium hydroxide → sodium chloride (table salt) + water. Neutralisation is used in many real-life situations: antacid tablets (a base) neutralise stomach acid to relieve heartburn; lime (Ca(OH)₂) is added to acidic soil; baking soda paste relieves the burning of an ant sting (formic acid).

Why does this happen?

Acid means excess H⁺ ions. Base means excess OH⁻ ions. When they meet, H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O — they cancel. The remaining ions (Na⁺ from the base, Cl⁻ from the acid, for example) pair up to form a salt. New bonds release energy as heat.

Where you see this in the real world

  • Antacid tablet for heartburn — When you have heartburn, your stomach has too much HCl. An antacid (containing magnesium hydroxide or calcium carbonate — bases) reacts with the acid and the burning stops within minutes. [Human body]
  • Lime on acidic soil — Some farmlands become acidic due to fertiliser overuse or natural rainfall. Spreading powdered lime (Ca(OH)₂) neutralises the acid, restoring pH for crop growth. [Plants]
  • Baking soda on a wasp sting — Wasp venom is alkaline (basic). Apply vinegar (acid) to neutralise it. Bee venom is acidic — apply baking soda paste (base). Knowing which sting is which can give the right relief. [Human body]

Going deeper

Neutralisation is exothermic — it releases heat because new chemical bonds in water and salt are stronger than the original bonds in the acid and base. The amount of heat released per mole is the heat of neutralisation, around -57 kJ/mol for any strong acid + strong base in water — surprisingly constant because the underlying reaction is always H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O. Titration is a quantitative technique that exploits neutralisation: a base of known concentration is added drop by drop to an acid of unknown concentration with an indicator until the colour change reveals exact equivalence. Used in pharmaceuticals, water testing, and food chemistry.

Beyond the book

The Apollo astronauts on the Moon used lithium hydroxide canisters to remove CO₂ from their cabin air — the LiOH (a base) reacted with the acidic CO₂ to form lithium carbonate. When Apollo 13's air system failed, ground engineers improvised an adapter from spare LiOH canisters and a flight manual cover — the famous 'square peg in a round hole' fix that saved three lives. Neutralisation chemistry, in space.
