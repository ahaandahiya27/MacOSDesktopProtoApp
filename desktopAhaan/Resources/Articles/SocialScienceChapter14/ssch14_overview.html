What Makes a Neighbour?

A neighbour is not only a country sharing a land border. India's 15,100 km land boundary touches seven neighbours, while her 11,100 km coastline and peninsular reach into the Indian Ocean make many sea-linked nations maritime neighbours. India's central position drives regionalism and her role as the region's first responder.

  1. Who Is a Neighbour? Land and Maritime NeighboursA neighbour is not only a country that shares a land border with India — countries linked across the sea are maritime neighbours too.
  2. India's Maritime Position and the Indian OceanIndia's long coastline and peninsular shape push deep into the Indian Ocean, placing her on the busiest sea routes between Southeast Asia, West Asia and Africa.
  3. Regionalism: Being a Good NeighbourRegionalism means neighbouring countries working together for peace, stability and shared progress — not just respecting borders.

India's Land-Based Neighbours: The North and West

India's north-western and northern land frontier meets China (across the Himalayas), Pakistan (born of the 1947 Partition) and Afghanistan (an ancient land-link severed in 1947). These relationships braid deep cultural exchange — especially Buddhism and ancient trade routes — with modern trade, tension and developmental cooperation.

  1. India and China: Separated by the HimalayasIndia and China, two of Asia's largest nations, share a Himalayan border and deep cultural links through Buddhism, alongside trade and periods of tension.
  2. India and Pakistan: A Shared Heritage, A Divided HistoryIndia and Pakistan share one of South Asia's most complex relationships, marked by the 1947 Partition and conflict, yet bridged by shared culture and pilgrimage like the Kartarpur Corridor.
  3. India and Afghanistan: An Ancient Land-Linked FriendAfghanistan, once a direct land neighbour, has ancient ties to India through the Uttarapatha trade route and shared Buddhist-Hindu heritage, now complicated by the 1947 creation of Pakistan.

Himalayan and Eastern Neighbours

To the east and along the Himalayas, India meets Bangladesh (born 1971, sharing language, rivers and the Sundarbans), Nepal (the open border and the 1950 Treaty), Bhutan (Drukyul, hydropower and Gross National Happiness), and Myanmar (the gateway to Southeast Asia). Buddhism, shared rivers and connectivity projects bind them to India.

  1. India and Bangladesh: A Newborn NeighbourBangladesh, born in 1971, shares with India a common history, the Bangla language, the longest of India's land borders, transboundary rivers and the Sundarbans.
  2. India and Nepal: The Open Border in the Lap of the HimalayasIndia and Nepal share an open border (no visa or passport needed), deep Hindu pilgrimage ties, and the 1950 Treaty of Peace and Friendship.
  3. India and Bhutan: The Land of the Thunder DragonBhutan ('Drukyul'), a landlocked Himalayan kingdom, links to India through Vajrayana Buddhism, hydroelectric cooperation, and pioneered the Gross National Happiness index.
  4. India and Myanmar: The Gateway to Southeast AsiaMyanmar (formerly Burma) is India's land-and-sea bridge to Southeast Asia, linked by Buddhism, north-eastern border states, and connectivity projects like the Trilateral Highway.

Maritime Neighbours Across the Seas: Sri Lanka and the Maldives

Across the southern seas lie India's nearest maritime neighbours: Sri Lanka (just 32 km away across the Palk Strait, linked by Ashokan Buddhism and Tamil culture) and the Maldives (1,100+ low islets sharing Dhivehi and South Indian culture, with India as first responder). Binding the whole neighbourhood are the three schools of Buddhism — Theravada, Mahayana and Vajrayana — that grew in India and spread across Asia.

  1. India and Sri Lanka: The Nearest Maritime NeighbourSri Lanka, just 32 km across the Palk Strait, is India's nearest maritime neighbour, linked by Buddhism (brought by Ashoka's children), Hinduism and deep Tamil cultural ties.
  2. India and the Maldives: A Nation of IsletsThe Maldives, over 1,100 low islets near Lakshadweep, shares South Indian-rooted culture and language (Dhivehi) with India, which acts as its trusted first responder in crises.
  3. The Three Schools of Buddhism: India's Gift to Her NeighboursThree schools of Buddhism — Theravada, Mahayana and Vajrayana — grew in ancient India and spread to her neighbours, shaping the religious life of much of Asia.

The Wider Maritime Neighbourhood and Regional Cooperation

India's wider maritime neighbourhood stretches from Thailand (Ayutthaya from Ayodhya) through Malaysia, Singapore and Indonesia (Borobudur, Garuda) to Iran and Oman in the west (Avesta, the 5,000-year copper trade, Chabahar). Ancient routes — Uttarapatha, Dakshinapatha, the Silk and spice routes — first connected this world, and modern bodies like SAARC (1985) try to revive that cooperation.

  1. India and Thailand: From Ayodhya to AyutthayaThailand's history echoes India's: kingdoms named after Indian cities (Dvaravati, Ayutthaya), kings named Rama, and shared Buddhist-Hindu culture seen even at Bangkok's Suvarnabhumi Airport.
  2. India, Malaysia, Singapore and Indonesia: The Golden Lands of Southeast AsiaAcross Southeast Asia, Indian culture shaped Malaysia (Brahmi-based script, Indian-origin community), Singapore ('Singapuram', Tamil an official language) and Indonesia (Borobudur, Garuda on its currency).
  3. India, Iran and Oman: The Ancient Western NeighboursTo the west, India's ancient ties reach Iran (shared Indo-Iranian heritage, the Avesta and Rigveda, Chabahar Port) and Oman (5,000-year-old copper trade from the Indus-Sarasvati civilisation).
  4. SAARC and the Ancient Trade Routes That Connected IndiaAncient routes (Uttarapatha, Dakshinapatha, the Silk Route and spice routes) connected India to her neighbours, while modern bodies like SAARC (1985) revive that cooperation today.
