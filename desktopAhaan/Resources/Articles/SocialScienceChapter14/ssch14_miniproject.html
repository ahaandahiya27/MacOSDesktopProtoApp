🗺️ Map your neighbourhood (about 40 min)

What you need

  • A blank outline map of South Asia and the Indian Ocean
  • Coloured pencils
  • A ruler

Steps

  1. Label India and all seven land neighbours: Pakistan, Afghanistan, China, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh, Myanmar.
  2. Mark the maritime neighbours: Sri Lanka, the Maldives, and the wider group (Thailand, Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, Iran, Oman).
  3. Draw arrows showing one cultural flow to each neighbour (e.g. Buddhism to Sri Lanka, language to the Maldives, place names to Thailand).
  4. Colour land neighbours in one shade and maritime neighbours in another.

👀 What you should see

You will see India ringed by land neighbours to the north and maritime neighbours across the seas, with cultural arrows fanning out in every direction.

🧠 Why it works

Drawing the flows makes visible the chapter's main idea — that India is a centre connected to a whole neighbourhood by culture, not just borders.

🍲 A neighbourhood food and word hunt (about 35 min)

What you need

  • A notebook
  • Help from family or the internet

Steps

  1. Pick three neighbours from the chapter (e.g. the Maldives, Nepal, Thailand).
  2. Find one food and one word each that they share with India (e.g. Maldivian roshi like roti; Dhivehi 'raajje' from Sanskrit 'raja').
  3. Write down what the shared item is and trace where it came from.
  4. Present your findings as a small 'connections chart'.

👀 What you should see

You will discover that everyday foods and words quietly carry centuries of contact between India and her neighbours.

🧠 Why it works

It turns the abstract idea of 'cultural ties' into concrete things you can taste and say, reinforcing how culture crosses borders.
