Q1. Explain who a maritime neighbour is with two examples with respect to India. (NCERT page 29)

A maritime neighbour is a country connected to another by a shared sea or ocean, even without a direct land border. For India, Sri Lanka (just 32 km away across the Palk Strait) and the Maldives (about 130 km from Minicoy in Lakshadweep) are immediate maritime neighbours. The wider maritime neighbourhood also includes Iran, Oman, Thailand, Malaysia, Singapore and Indonesia.

Q2. How has Buddhism created links with India's neighbours? Give examples. (NCERT page 32)

Buddhism began in India and spread peacefully to neighbours. It reached China in the 1st century CE (monks Faxian, Xuanzang, Bodhidharma, Kumarajiva); Sri Lanka in the 3rd century BCE through Ashoka's children Mahendra and Sanghamitra; Bhutan in the 8th century CE through Guru Padmasambhava; and shaped Thailand, Myanmar (Theravada), Indonesia (the Borobudur stupa) and Afghanistan (the Bamiyan Buddhas).

Q3. What does the 'open border' policy mean, and how does the India–Nepal open border affect people living along it? (NCERT page 39)

An open border means people can cross without a visa or passport. Along the India–Nepal border this lets families stay connected and lets people move freely for work, education, trade and pilgrimage, creating vibrant markets and thriving trade. It rests on the 1950 Treaty of Peace and Friendship, and both countries cooperate to keep it safe.

Q4. The chapter says, 'Being neighbours is not just about geography.' Explain with an example. (NCERT page 49)

It means cultural, spiritual and economic ties make neighbours, not only shared borders. For example, India and Thailand never shared a border, yet Thai kings are named Rama and the kingdom of Ayutthaya was named after Ayodhya. India and Oman, across the Arabian Sea, have traded for over 5,000 years. Such links make countries neighbours beyond mere geography.

Q5. What are the different ways in which India has helped smaller countries in her neighbourhood? (NCERT page 48)

India has acted as the region's first responder: rushing aid to the Maldives during the 2004 tsunami, the 2014 Malé water crisis and COVID-19; building infrastructure in Afghanistan (the Parliament building and Zaranj-Delaram highway); developing hydropower with Bhutan; restoring the Ananda temple in Myanmar; and helping set up the Indian Ocean tsunami warning system for the whole region.
