History, geography and society are full of 'what if' turning points. Think about each question yourself first — then open the panel to compare your reasoning.

What if India had no coastline and was completely landlocked?

Think first, then open to compare

India would lose her maritime neighbourhood entirely — no easy ties to Sri Lanka, the Maldives, Indonesia or Oman, and no role guarding the Indian Ocean's busy oil and cargo routes. Trade would depend on crossing other countries' land, and India could not be the region's quick 'first responder' by sea. Much of her ancient cultural spread to Southeast Asia, which travelled by ship, might never have happened.

What if borders were drawn only by culture and connection instead of politics?

Think first, then open to compare

The map would look very different. Bangla-speaking regions of India and Bangladesh might form one zone; Tamil-linked areas of India, Sri Lanka and Singapore might join; the Buddhist Himalayan belt from Ladakh through Bhutan to Tibet might be one cultural country. It shows how today's political borders cut across older, deeper webs of shared language, faith and trade.
