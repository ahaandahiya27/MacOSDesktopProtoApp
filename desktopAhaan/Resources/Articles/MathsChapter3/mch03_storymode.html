Story Mode — A Peek Beyond the Point in Real Life

The same ideas from this chapter, but out in the world — in shops, on playgrounds, and in everyday choices.

≈ 6 min · see-it-everywhere level

Maths can feel like it lives only inside the textbook. It doesn't. Here are real situations where the ideas of "A Peek Beyond the Point" quietly do the work.

Paise are hundredths of a rupee

₹4.45 means 4 rupees and 45 paise — and a paisa is exactly one hundredth of a rupee. The two-decimal format of money is the decimal system made concrete: the first place after the point is ten-paise, the second is single paise. Every price tag you read is a decimal place-value lesson.

The metric system is decimals all the way down

1 metre = 10 decimetres = 100 centimetres = 1000 millimetres. Each step down is one more decimal place, which is why 1.25 m is simply 125 cm — you just move the point. The whole metric system was designed around tenfold subdivision so conversions never need awkward multiplication.

Sprint times decided by hundredths

Usain Bolt's 100 m world record is 9.58 seconds. The difference between gold and silver in a sprint is often a few hundredths of a second — 9.58 vs 9.63. Hundredths aren't abstract here; they're the margin of an Olympic medal.
