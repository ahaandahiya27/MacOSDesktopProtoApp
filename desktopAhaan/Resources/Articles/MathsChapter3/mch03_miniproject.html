📏 Mini Project — Make Your Own Tenths Ruler

A hands-on activity that turns this chapter into something you actually do, not just read.

≈ 40 min · do-it-yourself level

Reading about maths and doing maths are two different things. This short project lets you see the ideas of "A Peek Beyond the Point" at work with your own hands.

What you'll need

  • A strip of stiff paper or card about 20 cm long
  • A pen and a normal ruler
  • Scissors (optional)

Steps

  1. Mark a 1-unit length anywhere on your strip (say, 10 cm = 1 unit).
  2. Divide that unit into 10 equal parts using your ruler — each part is one tenth.
  3. Label the marks 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, … 1.0.
  4. Now pick small objects (an eraser, a coin, a key) and measure each in your tenths.
  5. Challenge: pick one tenth-interval and divide IT into ten — you've just made hundredths. Measure something to a hundredth.

🔍 What you should notice

You'll find most objects don't land on a whole mark — they sit between tenths, which is exactly why hundredths (and finer) are needed for precise measurement.

Why it works

Splitting a unit into ten equal parts is the physical act behind decimal tenths; splitting a tenth into ten again makes hundredths. Building the ruler by hand shows that decimals are just finer and finer rulers, each made by the same 'divide by ten' step.
