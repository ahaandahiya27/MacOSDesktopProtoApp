Mathematics was built by real people, often centuries apart, each adding one idea. Here are the minds behind the ideas in this chapter.

Simon Stevin (1548 – 1620)

Flemish (worked in the Dutch Republic). Popularised decimal fractions in Europe with his 1585 booklet De Thiende.

Stevin was an engineer and mathematician who argued that ordinary people — clerks, surveyors, soldiers — would find tenths and hundredths far easier than awkward fractions. His short book De Thiende ('The Tenth', 1585) showed how, and within a couple of generations decimals had transformed European arithmetic, accounting and measurement.
