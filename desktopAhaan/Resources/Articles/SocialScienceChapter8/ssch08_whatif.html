History, geography and society are full of 'what if' turning points. Think about each question yourself first — then open the panel to compare your reasoning.

What if there had been no tradition of long-distance pilgrimage in ancient India?

Think first, then open to compare

Without pilgrimage, far fewer ordinary people would have travelled across the subcontinent, so the great network of sacred sites would not have knit the land together. There would have been much less contact between distant regions, fewer chances to notice the shared deities, stories and values beneath India's many languages and customs, and weaker overlap between sacred routes and trade routes. India's strong sense of being 'one country and one culture', and much of its cultural and economic integration, might never have developed as it did.

What if people stopped regarding rivers, mountains and forests as sacred?

Think first, then open to compare

If Nature were seen only as a resource — rivers as irrigation water, mountains as ore, forests as timber — it would be far easier to exploit and pollute them, as David Suzuki warned. Sacred groves would lose the belief that protects their biodiversity and water; rivers like the Yamuna and Kaveri, already polluted, would have even fewer defenders; and communities would lose a powerful moral reason to care for their environment. The sacred-geography worldview gives people a deep motivation to protect Nature that pure economics often does not.
