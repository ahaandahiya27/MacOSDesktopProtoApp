🗺️ Map India's sacred networks (about 60 min)

What you need

  • An outline political map of India
  • Coloured pens or pencils (at least four colours)
  • A list of the chār dhām, the 12 jyotirlingas and a few tīrthas
  • A ruler

Steps

  1. Draw or print a clean outline map of India.
  2. In one colour, mark the four chār dhām sites at the southern, northern, eastern and western corners and join them.
  3. In a second colour, plot a few of the 12 jyotirlingas you can find.
  4. In a third colour, plot a few Shakti pīṭhas, including any that fall in present-day Bangladesh or Pakistan.
  5. In a fourth colour, mark famous tīrthas like Rameswaram, Haridwar, Bodh Gaya and Pandharpur.
  6. Draw light lines connecting sites a pilgrim might visit in one long journey.
  7. Write one sentence under the map explaining how these networks make the whole land 'sacred geography'.

👀 What you should see

You will see that the sacred sites are spread to every corner and region of India, so the lines joining them crisscross the entire country.

🧠 Why it works

Plotting the networks turns an abstract idea into a visible pattern: when you see holy sites in all four corners and across the map, it becomes obvious how pilgrimage forced people to travel everywhere and helped weave the land together.

🌳 Investigate a local sacred place or grove (about 90 min)

What you need

  • A notebook and pen
  • Permission from a parent or teacher to ask questions
  • Optional: a camera or phone for photos

Steps

  1. List the sacred sites in your own region — a temple, mosque, church, shrine, sacred tree or grove.
  2. Choose one and find out why it is considered sacred.
  3. Speak to elders in your family or community and ask if any stories or legends are connected with it.
  4. Note who looks after it and what activities are allowed or forbidden there.
  5. Observe and record any plants, birds, animals or water bodies around it.
  6. Write a short essay of about 150 words describing the place, its story and why it matters.
  7. Add one suggestion for how the community could help keep it clean and protected.

👀 What you should see

You will discover that even an ordinary-looking local site carries stories, rules and a web of meaning, and that its sacred status often helps protect the Nature around it.

🧠 Why it works

Researching a real place makes the chapter's big idea personal: you experience first-hand how a community's reverence turns a patch of land, a tree or a stream into a protected, meaningful space — sacred geography in your own neighbourhood.
