A pilgrim's three-month journey across India

Following the kind of long pilgrimage the historian Dharampal described — villagers from north of Lucknow travelling to the far south and back to the Himalayas — to see how a single journey covers the whole sacred land.

  • Set out from home
    A small group of villagers from north of Lucknow gathers food — atta, ghee and sugar — and earthen pots, and sets out on a pilgrimage that will last about three months, with no modern transport.
  • South to Rameswaram
    They travel all the way to the far south, to Rameswaram, crossing regions with unfamiliar languages, clothing and foods, but noticing the things they share with the people they meet.
  • Other sacred sites along the way
    Along the route they visit other tīrthas, where their pilgrimage routes often overlap with the paths of traders and merchants exchanging goods.
  • Hurrying north to Haridwar
    Turning north, they pass through Delhi but do not stop to sightsee — they only change trains, because they must reach Haridwar, a sacred site on the Ganga in the Himalayas.
  • A dip in the sacred Ganga
    At Haridwar they take a sacred dip in the Ganga, completing the outer journey across the whole land and, with it, the inner journey of devotion.
  • Home again, the land made sacred
    They return home having travelled from the far south to the Himalayas. Journeys like theirs, repeated for 3,000 years, made the entire geography of India sacred and helped bind it into one culture.
