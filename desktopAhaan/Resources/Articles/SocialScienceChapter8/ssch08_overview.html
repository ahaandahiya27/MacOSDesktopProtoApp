What Is 'Sacredness'?

What it means for something to be sacred, how a place, a journey or even land can be holy, and how every religion in India — those born here and those that came from outside — marks its own sacred sites.

  1. The Meaning of SacrednessSacredness means finding something of deep religious or spiritual significance — holy, divine, and worthy of respect and reverence.
  2. Pilgrimage, Shrine and TīrthaA pilgrimage is a journey to a sacred place; a shrine is a holy spot; a tīrtha is a 'crossing place' that lets one cross from ordinary life to a higher, spiritual life.
  3. Sacred Sites of Many Faiths in IndiaAlmost every religion in India has its own sacred places — both faiths that came from outside India and those that were born here.

Pilgrimages and Tīrthas

The ancient, continuous tradition of pilgrimage across India, the difficult hilltop journeys that mirror an inner path, and the Jain tradition of tīrthas linked to the Tīrthankaras.

  1. An Ancient and Continuous Tradition of PilgrimageFor at least 3,000 years Indians have crisscrossed the subcontinent on pilgrimage, treating it as both an outer journey and an inner one.
  2. Difficult Journeys to Hilltop ShrinesMany shrines sit on hilltops reached by hard treks, and the difficulty of the climb symbolises the difficulties of the inner spiritual path.
  3. Tīrthas in the Jain TraditionIn Jainism, tīrthas are linked to the Tīrthankaras — the supreme preachers of dharma — and to the trees, ponds, hills and mountains they visited.

Sacred Geography and Pilgrimage Networks

How sacred sites are deliberately spread across the whole country in networks like the chār dhām, the 12 jyotirlingas and the 51 Shakti pīṭhas, how this makes the land itself sacred, and how pilgrimage routes overlapped with trade to integrate the subcontinent.

  1. Networks: Chār Dhām, Jyotirlingas and Shakti PīṭhasSacred sites are organised into networks — the four chār dhām, the 12 jyotirlingas and the 51 Shakti pīṭhas — that cover the entire map of India.
  2. Sacred Geography and the Integration of IndiaBecause sacred networks crisscross the whole subcontinent, the land itself becomes sacred, and pilgrims travelling between sites helped weave India's diverse cultures together.
  3. From Pilgrimage to TradePilgrimage routes and trade routes often overlapped, so holy travel and the exchange of goods strengthened each other.

Sacred Ecology: Rivers, Confluences and Mountains

How tīrthas sit in Nature and make the landscape sacred, the worship of rivers and their confluences since Vedic times, the great Kumbh Mela, and sacred mountains seen as gateways to heaven.

  1. Sacred Rivers and SangamsRivers have been worshipped in India since Vedic times, and their sources, tributaries and confluences (sangams) are treated as sacred.
  2. The Kumbh MelaThe Kumbh Mela is a vast pilgrimage held at four river sites linked to the legend of the amṛita (divine nectar), where a sacred dip is considered most auspicious.
  3. Sacred Mountains as Gateways to HeavenMountains are linked with deities, legends and heroes worldwide, and their height makes them a symbolic gateway from earth to heaven.

Trees, Sacred Groves and Sacred Geography Beyond India

The sacred peepul tree and the epics that scatter shrines across the land, the sacred groves that protect Nature, examples of sacred geography in other parts of the world, and the duty to conserve our sacred places today.

  1. Sacred Trees and the EpicsThe peepul (Ficus religiosa) is sacred to several Indian religions, and the epics scatter shrines across the land wherever their heroes are said to have passed.
  2. Sacred GrovesSacred groves are forests protected as the abodes of deities; their sacred status has made them rich reservoirs of biodiversity and water.
  3. Sacred Geography Beyond India and Conserving the SacredSacred geography exists worldwide, and recognising land, rivers and mountains as sacred is a powerful tool for protecting Nature today.
