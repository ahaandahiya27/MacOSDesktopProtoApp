The 17 terms to know

  • Sacredness · पवित्रता — Finding something of deep religious or spiritual significance — holy or divine and worthy of respect and reverence.
    Example: A shrine, a pilgrimage route, or even an entire stretch of land can have sacredness.
  • Pilgrimage · तीर्थयात्रा — A journey to a sacred place that is significant within a religion or belief system.
    Example: The 21-day Pandharpur wārī to the Vithoba temple is a pilgrimage.
  • Tīrtha · तीर्थ — Literally a place to cross a river; symbolically, a sacred place where one can cross from ordinary life to a higher, spiritual life.
    Example: Pilgrims undertake tīrthayātrās to tīrthas such as Haridwar and Rameswaram.
  • Shrine · मंदिर / दरगाह — A place regarded as holy because of its associations with the divine, a sacred relic, or a spiritual figure.
    Example: The Dargah Sharif of Ajmer is a shrine visited by people of many faiths.
  • Relic · अवशेष — A part of a saint's or spiritual figure's body, or one of their belongings, kept as an object of reverence.
    Example: The Great Stūpa at Sanchi is a relic stūpa associated with the Buddha.
  • Tīrthankara · तीर्थंकर — In Jainism, a supreme preacher of dharma — literally 'one who makes a tīrtha', guiding the crossing from ordinary to higher life.
    Example: Jain tīrthas like Girnar are linked to places where Tīrthankaras attained liberation.
  • Sangam · संगम — The confluence where two or more rivers meet, regarded as an especially sacred 'crossing place'.
    Example: Prayagraj sits at the sangam of the Ganga, Yamuna and the invisible Sarasvati.
  • Jyotirlinga · ज्योतिर्लिंग — One of the 12 highly auspicious sacred shrines dedicated to Śhiva, each with its own mythology and name.
    Example: The 12 jyotirlingas are spread across India as a pilgrimage network.
  • Shakti pīṭha · शक्ति पीठ — One of the 51 sacred sites where, by tradition, body parts of the divine mother (Satī) fell, covering the whole subcontinent.
    Example: The 51 Shakti pīṭhas reach even into present-day Bangladesh and Pakistan.
  • Sacred grove · देव वन / पवित्र उपवन — A natural forest protected by a community as the abode of a deity, where activities like hunting, felling and mining are forbidden.
    Example: A 'kāvu' in Kerala or an 'oraṇ' in Rajasthan is a sacred grove rich in biodiversity.
  • Puṇyakṣhetra · पुण्यक्षेत्र — Sacred space — a natural landscape (riverbank, forest, mountain) perceived as holy, where geography, culture and spirituality fuse.
    Example: Because tīrthas sit in Nature, the surrounding landscape is seen as puṇyakṣhetra.
  • Chār Dhām · चार धाम — The four holy abodes that ring India — Badrinath (north), Dwarka (west), Puri (east) and Rameswaram (south); from Sanskrit chār ('four') + dhām ('divine abode').
    Example: A Chār Dhām pilgrimage carries a devotee to all four corners of India.
  • Kumbh Mela · कुम्भ मेला — A vast riverbank pilgrimage-fair held on a sacred calendar; from Sanskrit kumbh ('pot, pitcher') + mela ('gathering, fair'), recalling the pot of amṛita.
    Example: The Kumbh Mela rotates between Haridwar, Prayagraj, Nashik and Ujjain.
  • Bodhi tree · बोधि वृक्ष — The peepal tree under which the Buddha is said to have attained enlightenment; from Sanskrit bodhi ('awakening, enlightenment').
    Example: Pilgrims visit Bodh Gaya to honour the Bodhi tree.
  • Peepal · पीपल — A sacred fig tree (Ficus religiosa) revered across Indian faiths; the Buddha's Bodhi tree was a peepal.
    Example: A peepal is often left standing and worshipped even in the middle of a field.
  • Banyan · बरगद / वट — A giant sacred fig tree (Ficus benghalensis) whose hanging roots grow into new trunks; the English name comes from the 'banias' (traders) who gathered in its shade.
    Example: A single old banyan can shade a whole village meeting.
  • Conservation · संरक्षण — Protecting and keeping nature, biodiversity or heritage safe for the future; from Latin conservare ('to keep, preserve').
    Example: Sacred groves achieve conservation through belief rather than law.
