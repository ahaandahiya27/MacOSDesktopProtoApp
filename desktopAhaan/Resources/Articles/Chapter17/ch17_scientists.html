One Curious Person Behind Chapter 17

The scientist most tied to forest: our lifeline — their question, their experiment, and what changed because of them.

Every chapter has a person behind it — someone who first asked the right question, ran the right experiment, or wrote down the answer. This is a one-page portrait of the scientist most tied to this chapter's content.

Sunderlal Bahuguna

1927–2021 · Indian

🎧 In one line

Gandhian environmentalist who led the Chipko movement and built India's modern conservation conscience — saving Himalayan forests with the slogan 'Ecology is the permanent economy'.

Sunderlal Bahuguna was born in 1927 in Marora village, Tehri-Garhwal. As a young man he met Gandhi and Sarla Devi, who inspired him to lifelong activism. He settled in a forest hut by the Bhagirathi (Ganga) river with his wife Vimla. In the 1970s, when government contracts to log Himalayan forests caused floods and landslides downstream, Sunderlal joined the women's CHIPKO movement — villagers hugging trees to stop loggers. He fasted for 84 days in 1995 to demand a ban on green felling above 1000 m. He WON. He then led the (unsuccessful but historic) Tehri Dam protest, walking 4900 km from Kashmir to Kohima studying the Himalayas. He coined the slogan 'Ecology is the permanent economy' — meaning: any economic growth that destroys ecosystems is borrowing against the future. He was awarded Padma Vibhushan (2009). He died of COVID at 94 in May 2021. The forests in Uttarakhand, the language of forest rights in Indian law, the National Forest Policy 1988 — all bear his fingerprint. He took the science of Class 7's Chapter 17 and turned it into political resistance.
