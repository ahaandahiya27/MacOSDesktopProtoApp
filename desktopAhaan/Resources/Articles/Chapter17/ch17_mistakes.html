Five Wrong Answers Class 7 Students Give

The five places almost every Class 7 student trips up on Chapter 17 — and the one-line fix for each.

≈ 5 min read · revision-tier

Examiners say the same wrong answers come back year after year. None of these mistakes are silly — they are almost right. That is exactly what makes them dangerous. Read each one, cover the answer, and try to spot what is off before peeking.

1. “Forests are just lots of trees.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: A forest is a vast INTERACTING SYSTEM. Trees, shrubs, climbers, herbs, fungi, bacteria, insects, birds, mammals, soil microbes, decomposers — all linked by food webs and nutrient cycles. Remove any one (e.g., bees), the whole system suffers. A monoculture plantation (rows of one tree type) is NOT a forest — it's a tree farm. Real forests have hundreds of species per square km.

2. “Cutting down a tree is fine if I plant a new one to replace it.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: A 100-year-old tree and a new sapling are NOT equivalent. The old tree stores 50+ tonnes of carbon; the sapling stores almost none for 20+ years. The old tree houses dozens of species (insects, birds, fungi); the sapling houses few. Compensatory afforestation policies count number of trees, not ecological value — a serious flaw. 'Plant a tree' is good; 'don't cut the old tree' is much better.

3. “Decomposers are gross or unimportant.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Decomposers are ESSENTIAL — they are the cleanup crew of the planet. Without fungi and bacteria, dead leaves, dead bodies, dead trees would pile up and no nutrients would return to soil. Plants would starve within years. The 'gross' brown layer on the forest floor is doing the work that keeps trees alive. Indian Ayurveda recognises this — many fungi are also used medicinally.

4. “Forests grow back after a fire.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Some forests DO recover from natural fires (e.g., Australian eucalyptus). But Indian rainforests like Western Ghats do NOT — once burnt, they may be replaced by grasses or invasive plants. The complex ecosystem (specific trees + specific fungi + specific animals) took millions of years to develop; once disrupted, it doesn't 'grow back'. Recent forest fires in Uttarakhand and Odisha have permanently degraded thousands of hectares.

5. “Tigers are aggressive predators that should be killed.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Tigers are KEYSTONE species. They control deer populations (deer eat saplings; too many deer kill the forest). They keep prey species healthy by eliminating sick/weak animals. Removing tigers causes 'trophic cascade' — deer explode, saplings die, forest collapses. Project Tiger (1973) saved ~1700 wild tigers in India today. Tiger sightings are extremely rare; tiger ATTACKS on humans are mostly because human habitations are encroaching on tiger habitat.

📝 Stretch your thinking

Pick any two of these and explain them to a younger sibling using a one-line analogy each. If you can compress a mistake into one sentence with one image, you have understood it.
