A Class 7 Forest: Our Lifeline Dictionary

The 10 terms to know

  • Forest canopy · वन छत्र — The upper layer of a forest formed by interlocking branches of trees.
    Example: The canopy of a Western Ghats rainforest is so dense that little sunlight reaches the ground.
  • Biodiversity · जैव विविधता — The variety of plants, animals, and microorganisms in an area.
    Example: Western Ghats has ~5000 plant species, including 1700 endemic.
  • Decomposer · अपघटक — An organism (mostly fungi or bacteria) that breaks down dead matter into simpler compounds.
    Example: Mushrooms decompose dead trees over months.
  • Humus · ह्यूमस — The dark, nutrient-rich layer on forest soil formed from decomposed leaves and matter.
    Example: Garden compost mimics natural forest humus.
  • Deforestation · वनोन्मूलन — The clearing of forests for timber, agriculture, mining, or construction.
    Example: Aravalli forests have shrunk by 80% in 30 years due to mining and real estate.
  • Afforestation · वनीकरण — Planting trees in areas where there were no forests before.
    Example: Government's CAMPA scheme funds afforestation projects across India.
  • Sanctuary · अभयारण्य — An area where wild animals are protected from hunting and habitat destruction.
    Example: Periyar Tiger Reserve in Kerala protects tigers, elephants, and Nilgiri tahr.
  • Endemic species · स्थानिक प्रजाति — A species found in only one geographic region and nowhere else on Earth.
    Example: Lion-tailed macaque is endemic to Western Ghats.
  • Keystone species · मुख्य प्रजाति — A species whose presence is essential for the survival of many others in an ecosystem.
    Example: Tigers, elephants, bees — all keystone species in Indian forests.
  • Carbon sequestration · कार्बन प्रच्छादन — The process by which forests absorb and store atmospheric CO₂.
    Example: A mature tree sequesters ~25 kg of CO₂ per year, fighting climate change.
