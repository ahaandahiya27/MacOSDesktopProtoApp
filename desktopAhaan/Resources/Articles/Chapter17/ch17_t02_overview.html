Decomposers and the Soil-Forest Cycle

Topic t02 of Chapter 17 · Forests: Our Lifeline · Class 7 Science

The big idea

When leaves and animals die, fungi, bacteria, worms and termites break them down into humus — a dark, spongy material rich in nutrients. The nutrients return to the soil and feed the next generation of plants. Forests act as living water tanks too: canopies break the force of rain, roots hold soil, and water seeps slowly into the ground rather than flooding away.

Where Nothing Is Wasted. Forests absorb CO₂ and release O₂ — they are Earth's lungs. They also filter the air of dust and pollutants and stabilise local climate.
