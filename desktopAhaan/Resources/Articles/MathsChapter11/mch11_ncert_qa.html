Finding Common Ground: Worked NCERT Questions

Textbook questions from this chapter, each with a clear worked answer.

These are the kind of questions the NCERT chapter asks. Try each one yourself first, then check the worked answer.

Q1. Sameeksha tiles a 12 ft by 16 ft room with the largest equal square tiles. What size tile, and why?

Answer: The tile side must divide both 12 and 16 (a common factor); the largest such, the HCF, gives the fewest tiles. Common factors of 12 and 16 are 1, 2, 4; the HCF is 4, so 4 ft tiles.

Q2. What is the HCF (GCD) of two numbers?

Answer: It is the greatest number that divides both of them exactly — the highest of their common factors.

Q3. Lekhana packs 84 kg and 108 kg of rice (separately) into equal largest bags. Find the bag size and number of bags.

Answer: Bag size = HCF(84,108) = 12 kg. Bags = 84/12 + 108/12 = 7 + 9 = 16 bags.

Q4. What is the LCM of two numbers? Find LCM(6,8).

Answer: The LCM is the smallest number that is a multiple of both. Multiples of 6 (6,12,18,24…) and of 8 (8,16,24…) first meet at 24, so LCM(6,8) = 24.

Q5. Find the HCF and LCM of 12 and 18 by prime factorisation.

Answer: 12 = 2²·3, 18 = 2·3². HCF = common primes to the lowest power = 2·3 = 6. LCM = all primes to the highest power = 2²·3² = 36.

Q6. State and verify the relationship between HCF, LCM and the product of two numbers, using 12 and 18.

Answer: HCF × LCM = product of the numbers. For 12 and 18: HCF = 6, LCM = 36, and 6 × 36 = 216 = 12 × 18. Verified.

Q7. The HCF of two numbers is 6 and their product is 216. Find their LCM.

Answer: Using HCF × LCM = product: 6 × LCM = 216, so LCM = 216 ÷ 6 = 36.

Q8. Why does the largest tile use the fewest tiles?

Answer: A larger tile covers more area per tile, so fewer are needed to cover the same floor. Taking the HCF as the side gives the largest tile that still fits both dimensions exactly, hence the fewest tiles.
