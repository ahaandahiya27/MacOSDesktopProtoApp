Finding Common Ground: Key Words

Every term in this chapter, with a one-line meaning and an example.

&#8776; quick reference &middot; 10 terms

Knowing the exact words makes the maths click. Here is every key term from the chapter, defined plainly with an example you can picture.

Factor

A number that divides another exactly, leaving no remainder.

Example: 4 is a factor of 12.

In Hindi: गुणनखंड

Multiple

A number obtained by multiplying a given number by a whole number.

Example: 24 is a multiple of 6.

In Hindi: गुणज

Common factor

A number that is a factor of two or more numbers.

Example: 4 is a common factor of 12 and 16.

Highest Common Factor (HCF/GCD)

The greatest number that divides two or more numbers exactly.

Example: HCF(12,16) = 4.

In Hindi: महत्तम समापवर्तक

Least Common Multiple (LCM)

The smallest number that is a multiple of two or more numbers.

Example: LCM(6,8) = 24.

In Hindi: लघुत्तम समापवर्त्य

Prime number

A number greater than 1 with exactly two factors: 1 and itself.

Example: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11.

In Hindi: अभाज्य संख्या

Prime factorisation

Writing a number as a product of prime numbers.

Example: 12 = 2² × 3.

Greatest Common Divisor (GCD)

Another name for the HCF — the greatest number dividing both.

Example: GCD(12,16) = 4.

Common multiple

A number that is a multiple of two or more numbers.

Example: 24 is a common multiple of 6 and 8.

HCF×LCM identity

For two numbers, HCF × LCM equals their product.

Example: 6 × 36 = 12 × 18.
