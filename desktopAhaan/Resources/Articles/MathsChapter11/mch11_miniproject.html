🟦 Mini Project — Tile the Room (HCF) & Sync the Bells (LCM)

A hands-on activity that turns this chapter into something you actually do, not just read.

≈ 40 min · do-it-yourself level

Reading about maths and doing maths are two different things. This short project lets you see the ideas of "Finding Common Ground" at work with your own hands.

What you'll need

  • Graph/squared paper
  • A pencil
  • A ruler

Steps

  1. Draw a 12 by 16 rectangle on squared paper (each square = 1 ft).
  2. Try filling it exactly with equal squares of side 1, 2, 3, 4. Which sizes fit with no gaps? Which is largest?
  3. Confirm the largest exact-fit square has side 4 — the HCF of 12 and 16 — and count how many tiles.
  4. Now mark a timeline: tick every 6 units for one bell, every 8 for another. Where do the ticks first land together?
  5. Confirm they coincide at 24 — the LCM of 6 and 8.

🔍 What you should notice

Only common factors (1,2,4) tile the room exactly, and the largest (HCF=4) needs the fewest tiles. The two bell-tick patterns first align at 24, the LCM — showing HCF for 'largest exact share' and LCM for 'first coincidence'.

Why it works

A tile fits both dimensions only if its side divides both (common factor); the largest is the HCF. Repeating ticks coincide only at a common multiple, and first at the least one — the LCM. The grid and timeline make both ideas visible at once.
