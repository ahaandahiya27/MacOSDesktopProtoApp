Minds Behind Finding Common Ground

Real mathematicians whose work lies underneath this chapter.

≈ 7 min read · for curious 12-year-olds

Mathematics was built by real people, often centuries apart, each adding one idea. Here are the minds behind the ideas in this chapter.

Euclid (c. 300 BCE)

Greek (Alexandria). Invented the Euclidean algorithm for the highest common factor.

In Book VII of The Elements, Euclid described a quick method for finding the highest common factor of two numbers by repeated division. Now called the Euclidean algorithm, it is still taught today and still runs inside computers — one of the oldest algorithms in everyday use.

A method over 2,000 years old can still be the fastest one we have.
