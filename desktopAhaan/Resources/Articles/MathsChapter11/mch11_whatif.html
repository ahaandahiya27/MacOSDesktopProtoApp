"What If?" — Finding Common Ground

Hypothetical questions with no single right answer. Think hard before you read the discussion.

≈ 7 min read · brain-stretcher level

The textbook tells you what is. The best way to really understand an idea is to ask what if? Pick a question below, pause and think for a minute, then read one possible discussion.

What if 1 — two repeating events never coincided?

The question: What if a 6-minute bell and an 8-minute bell never rang together?

🧠 Think first

Is that possible?

One discussion path: They must coincide — at the least common multiple, 24 minutes. Any two regularly repeating events always line up eventually; the LCM tells you exactly when.

What if 2 — you tiled a floor with the smallest tile?

The question: What if you ignored the HCF and used tiny tiles?

🧠 Think first

How many tiles, and how many cuts?

One discussion path: You would need far more tiles and still might not fit the walls evenly. The HCF gives the largest tile that fits a whole number of times — the neatest, fewest-tile answer.

What if 3 — every pair of numbers had HCF 1?

The question: What if no two numbers ever shared a factor above 1?

🧠 Think first

Could you ever simplify a fraction?

One discussion path: Fractions could never be reduced, and 'common ground' between numbers would vanish. Shared factors are what let us simplify, compare, and find common denominators.

📝 Stretch your thinking

None of these has one 'correct' answer — they are for arguing about. Try one on a friend or a parent and see whether their discussion path differs from yours.
