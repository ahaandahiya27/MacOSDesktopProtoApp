Maths can feel like it lives only inside the textbook. It doesn't. Here are real situations where the ideas of "Finding Common Ground" quietly do the work.

Tiling a floor with the fewest tiles

To tile a 12 ft × 16 ft room with equal square tiles and no cutting, the side must divide both 12 and 16 — and the largest such tile (HCF = 4 ft) uses the fewest tiles. HCF answers every 'largest equal piece that fits exactly' question, from tiles to ribbon-cutting.

When do the bells coincide?

Two temple bells ringing every 6 and 8 minutes sound together every LCM = 24 minutes. The same LCM logic tells you when synced traffic lights realign, when two gears return to their starting positions, and when repeating work shifts coincide.

Common denominators

Adding 1/6 + 1/8 needs a common denominator — and the smallest is LCM(6,8) = 24. Rewrite as 4/24 + 3/24 = 7/24. Every time you add fractions with different denominators, you're quietly computing an LCM.
