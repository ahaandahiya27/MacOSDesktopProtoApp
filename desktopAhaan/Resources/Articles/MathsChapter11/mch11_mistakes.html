Common Mistakes in Finding Common Ground

The places students slip on this chapter — and the one-line fix for each.

&#8776; 5 min read &middot; revision-tier &middot; 5 fixes

These wrong answers come back year after year. None are silly — they are almost right, which is what makes them worth knowing. Read each, then cover the fix and try to spot what is off.

1. “The HCF is the biggest of the two numbers.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: The HCF is the biggest number that DIVIDES both — it's never bigger than the smaller number. HCF(12,16) = 4, not 16.

2. “The LCM is the product of the two numbers.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: Only when they share no common factor. In general LCM = product ÷ HCF, so LCM(6,8) = 48/2 = 24, not 48.

3. “HCF and LCM are the same kind of thing.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: HCF divides INTO the numbers (small, for sharing); LCM is what the numbers divide into (large, for cycles). They're opposite directions.

4. “For HCF take the highest powers of the primes.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: For HCF take the LOWEST power of each COMMON prime. Highest powers (of ALL primes) give the LCM.

5. “A bigger tile means more tiles.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: A bigger tile means FEWER tiles. That's why the largest common factor (HCF) gives the fewest tiles for an exact fit.
