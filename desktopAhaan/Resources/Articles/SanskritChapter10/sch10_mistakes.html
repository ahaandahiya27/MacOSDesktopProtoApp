Common Mistakes

Five errors students often make in this chapter — and the gentle corrections that fix them.

≈ 5 min · honest-with-yourself level

Sanskrit makes precise distinctions that English glosses over — case endings, gender, tense, prefix vs augment. The mistakes below are not personal failings — they are signals that a specific detail of the language needs another look.

Mistake 1 — Saying 'दशमः त्वम् असि' means 'you are tenth (in line)'

What students often write/say: It can be read as a queue position.

The correct form: Vedāntic reading: 'YOU are the tenth (the missing one you've been looking for).'

💡 Why

The traveler's revelation is metaphysical — the SELF is what was missing all along. The parable serves Vedānta's तत्त्वमसि tradition. Reading it as just queue-ordering misses the depth.

Mistake 2 — Counting 'दशमः' as 'the tenth one in the order'

What students often write/say: Same as above but at the literal level.

The correct form: Yes, ordinally 'tenth' — but each boy thought the tenth was MISSING. The point is that the missing tenth was the COUNTER.

💡 Why

The parable depends on each boy forgetting to count himself. The label 'tenth' is positional; the WHO of the tenth is the discovery.

Mistake 3 — Treating गणयति (counts) as 'sings' or 'speaks'

What students often write/say: Other gana-roots exist.

The correct form: गण् (कलनम्) = to count, enumerate. Different root from गा (sing).

💡 Why

Listen for the consonant: गण् vs गा. Different roots, totally different families. गणयति 'he counts'; गायति 'he sings'.

Mistake 4 — Reading 'पथिकः' as 'pathiya' or rendering as a name

What students often write/say: It could look like a proper noun.

The correct form: पथिकः = 'a wayfarer / traveler'. Common noun, not name.

💡 Why

Sanskrit forms common nouns with -इकः suffix often: पथिकः (wayfarer), यायावरः (wanderer), अध्वगः (traveller). The chapter's पथिकः is anonymous — the unnamed wayfarer is the parable's teacher figure.

Mistake 5 — Saying प्रहृष्टाः means 'surprised'

What students often write/say: Surprise is one of the emotions in the scene.

The correct form: प्रहृष्टाः = delighted, overjoyed (from हृष् 'to thrill with joy').

💡 Why

Sanskrit distinguishes आश्चर्य (surprise), विस्मय (wonder), and हर्ष (joy). The boys' state on discovering the tenth is HARSHA — joy at recovering what was 'lost.'

📝 Self-check

Re-read the chapter once with these five mistakes in mind. Notice the places where the textbook has DELIBERATELY chosen one form over another — that's often where the trap lies.
