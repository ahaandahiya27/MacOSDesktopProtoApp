A Class 7 Heat Dictionary

The 10 must-know terms from Chapter 4 — in plain English plus the Hindi name, with a one-line meaning a Class 7 student can actually use.

The 10 terms to know

  • Heat · ऊष्मा — The total energy of vibrating atoms inside an object. Heat flows from a hotter object to a colder one.
    Example: When you boil water, heat from the stove flows into the water.
  • Temperature · तापमान — A measure of how fast the atoms of an object are vibrating on average; not the same as heat.
    Example: Your body temperature is normally about 37°C.
  • Conduction · चालन — Heat transfer through a solid material by direct contact, atom to atom.
    Example: A metal spoon left in hot tea soon feels hot at the handle.
  • Convection · संवहन — Heat transfer through a fluid (liquid or gas) by the fluid itself moving — hot rising, cool falling.
    Example: Hot air above a candle rises straight up because of convection.
  • Radiation · विकिरण — Heat transfer by electromagnetic waves; needs no material between source and receiver.
    Example: The Sun heats Earth through 150 million km of empty space — only radiation can cross that gap.
  • Conductor (thermal) · ऊष्मा चालक — A material that allows heat to flow through it easily.
    Example: Copper, aluminium, and silver are good thermal conductors.
  • Insulator (thermal) · ऊष्मा रोधक — A material that resists heat flow.
    Example: Wood, plastic, wool, and air are good insulators.
  • Specific heat capacity · विशिष्ट ऊष्मा धारिता — The amount of heat needed to raise 1 kg of a substance by 1°C; the property that makes water special.
    Example: Water's specific heat is ~5 times higher than air's — that's why oceans warm and cool slowly.
  • Sea breeze · समुद्री हवा — Wind blowing from sea to land during the day, due to faster heating of land.
    Example: Marine Drive's afternoon breeze is a textbook sea breeze.
  • Cryogenics · हिमनशास्त्र — The science of materials and processes at extremely low temperatures, usually below -150°C.
    Example: Liquid nitrogen used in ISRO's launches is at -196°C — cryogenic fuel.
