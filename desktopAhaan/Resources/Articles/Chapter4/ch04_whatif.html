Three "What Ifs?" About Heat

Hypothetical questions with no single right answer. Think hard before you read the discussion.

≈ 7 min read · brain-stretcher level

The textbook tells you what is. The best science teachers also ask what if? Pick any one of the questions below. Pause. Think for at least one minute before scrolling. Then read the discussion.

What if 1 — What if water had a low specific heat capacity like sand?

🧠 Think first

Pause. Write down your one-sentence guess before reading.

One discussion path: Oceans would heat up to over 60°C every summer and freeze every winter. Coastal cities would have insane temperature swings between day and night. Marine life would die — fish, coral, plankton all require stable temperatures. Monsoons depend on the slow ocean warming/cooling; without it, India might never get a regular rainy season. Water's high heat capacity is what makes life-friendly weather possible — a chemistry quirk we owe everything to.

What if 2 — What if you tried to fry an egg on the bonnet of a car parked in the Delhi sun?

🧠 Think first

Pause. Write down your one-sentence guess before reading.

One discussion path: Egg whites need 60°C to start cooking. A black car bonnet under direct Delhi summer sun (45°C air) hits 75–80°C in 30 minutes. So yes, the egg WILL slowly cook — at the edges first, then the centre — over maybe 30–45 minutes. White cars stay closer to 50–55°C; the egg barely warms. This is also why never leave kids or pets in a parked car: the interior can exceed 70°C in 30 minutes, an oven-grade environment.

What if 3 — What if Earth was 5°C hotter on average?

🧠 Think first

Pause. Write down your one-sentence guess before reading.

One discussion path: This is close to the worst-case climate-change projection for 2100. The Arctic and Antarctic would mostly melt — sea levels rise 60+ metres, drowning Kolkata, Mumbai, Chennai. Many heat-sensitive crops fail; wheat yields drop in the northern plains. The monsoon pattern shifts unpredictably. Diseases spread to higher altitudes. We'd need cooling-the-house-just-to-survive infrastructure even in cities that don't currently use AC. Five degrees doesn't sound like much but at planetary scale it reshapes everything.

💬 How to use this

These are not exam questions. The answer marked ‘one discussion path’ is one valid line of reasoning — your own may be different and still good. Practising this kind of thinking is how scientists become scientists.
