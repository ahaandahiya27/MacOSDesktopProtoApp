Five Wrong Answers Class 7 Students Give

The five places almost every Class 7 student trips up on Chapter 4 — and the one-line fix for each.

≈ 5 min read · revision-tier

Examiners say the same wrong answers come back year after year. None of these mistakes are silly — they are almost right. That is exactly what makes them dangerous. Read each one, cover the answer, and try to spot what is off before peeking.

1. “Heat and temperature are the same thing.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Heat is the total energy of all the moving atoms in an object — it depends on size. Temperature is the average speed of those atoms — it doesn't depend on size. A bathtub of warm water has WAY more heat than a teaspoon of boiling water, even though the teaspoon is at a higher temperature. The bathtub will boil an egg if you pour it on the egg; the teaspoon won't even cook the surface.

2. “Wool fabric is hot, so it gives off heat.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Wool doesn't produce heat — it TRAPS heat. The fibre has tiny air pockets that act as insulators, slowing the heat from leaving your body. Your body is the actual heat source. That's why wool works to keep you warm in winter (traps your body heat) but you'd freeze in a wool sweater in Antarctica if your body wasn't producing heat to trap.

3. “Sweating wets you so you get cold.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Sweat doesn't cool you by being wet — it cools you when it EVAPORATES. Liquid water needs a lot of energy to become vapour, and it takes that energy from your skin. Just being wet (think of sitting in a wet swimsuit in still air) doesn't cool you much. What cools is the moment droplets evaporate. That's why fans help — they speed evaporation.

4. “The sun heats Earth by conduction (because space is connected).”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Conduction needs material to carry heat. Space between sun and Earth is empty — no air, no molecules. Sun's heat reaches Earth ONLY by radiation: electromagnetic waves that travel through vacuum. Once those waves hit Earth, they convert to heat in soil, water, our skin. That's also how a microwave heats your roti — radiation, then conversion to heat in water molecules.

5. “Cold flows into things, like a flu virus.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: There's no such thing as 'cold' moving anywhere. Heat flows from hot to cold; what feels like cold entering you is actually heat leaving you. Touching ice doesn't mean cold goes into your hand — heat goes from your hand to the ice. Your brain interprets the loss as 'cold'. Cold is the ABSENCE of heat, not a separate substance.

📝 Stretch your thinking

Pick any two of these and explain them to a younger sibling using a one-line analogy each. If you can compress a mistake into one sentence with one image, you have understood it.
