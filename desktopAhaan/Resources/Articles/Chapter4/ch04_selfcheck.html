Question 5 — from Chapter 4

A new building in Rajasthan has a ROOF GARDEN (a layer of soil and plants on the roof). Predict how this changes the building's indoor temperature in 45°C summer afternoons, and why this strategy works mechanically.

🧠 Answer in your head first

Don't peek. Speak the answer out loud, then check.

Answer: Indoor temperature drops 5-8°C compared to bare-concrete roof. Mechanisms: (1) Plants reflect more sunlight than concrete (higher albedo). (2) Soil layer insulates against heat conduction into the building. (3) Plants TRANSPIRE — releasing water vapour absorbs heat (latent heat of vaporisation). (4) Shade from foliage protects soil surface from direct sun. The combined effect: less heat reaches the roof slab, less conducts through to the ceiling, indoor stays cooler. Same physics as why a forest floor is cooler than open ground.

Why: Identify the heat path: sun → roof → ceiling → indoor air.
