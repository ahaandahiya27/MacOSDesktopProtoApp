How Heat Travels

Conduction, convection, and radiation — heat's three highways

≈ 5 min read · for curious 12-year-olds

Heat never sits still. It is always on the move, flowing from hot places to cold ones. But it does not travel in just one way — it has three distinct methods, and each one shows up in your daily life more often than you might think.

In this topic you will explore conduction (heat flowing through solids), convection (heat moving through liquids and gases), and radiation (heat travelling as invisible waves through empty space). You will also see how engineers use these ideas to design thermos flasks, cooking pans, and even understand sea breezes.

Concepts in this topic

  1. Conduction Touch a metal railing on a hot day and feel conduction in action — heat flowing through solid materials.
  2. Convection Watch water boil and you see convection currents — hot liquid rising, cool liquid sinking.
  3. Radiation The Sun heats Earth across 150 million km of empty space. No medium needed — that is radiation.
  4. Thermos Flask A thermos fights all three modes of heat transfer at once. See how its clever design works.
  5. Cooking Vessels Copper bottoms and wooden handles — every part of a cooking pan is designed around heat transfer.
  6. Sea Breeze and Land Breeze Why does the wind blow from the sea during the day and from the land at night? Convection explains it all.
