The 17 terms to know

  • State · राज्य / राष्ट्र — A political organisation with the authority to govern a defined territory and population; it has four parts - population, territory, government and sovereignty.
    Example: India is a sovereign state.
  • Government · सरकार — The group of people or system that runs the country by making, enforcing and resolving disputes about laws; one part of the state.
    Example: A new government is formed after elections.
  • Sovereignty · संप्रभुता — The full power of a state to make and enforce its own laws without any outside control.
    Example: Sovereignty means no other country can interfere in India's internal affairs.
  • Democracy · लोकतंत्र — A system of 'rule by the people', where people choose their government through free and fair elections.
    Example: India is the world's largest democracy.
  • Republic · गणराज्य — A form of government where the head of state is elected (not hereditary) and rulers' powers are limited by a constitution.
    Example: India became a republic on 26 January 1950.
  • Rule of law · विधि का शासन — The principle that everyone, including the government, must follow the law.
    Example: The judiciary protects the rule of law.
  • Matsya nyaya · मत्स्य न्याय — The 'law of the fishes' - the idea that without law and order, the powerful prey on the weak.
    Example: A government prevents matsya nyaya by upholding the law.
  • Bureaucracy · नौकरशाही — The permanent executive of non-elected, exam-selected officers (civil servants) who run the government.
    Example: The IAS and IPS are part of India's bureaucracy.
  • Judicial review · न्यायिक समीक्षा — The judiciary's power to examine laws and government actions and strike down those that violate the Constitution.
    Example: Through judicial review, courts can cancel an unconstitutional law.
  • Right to Information (RTI) · सूचना का अधिकार — A law through which citizens can ask the government for information about its actions, bringing transparency and accountability.
    Example: A citizen filed an RTI to learn how road money was spent.
  • Decentralisation · विकेंद्रीकरण — Spreading out power and decision-making so local matters are decided locally rather than concentrated in one place.
    Example: India's three tiers of government reflect decentralisation.
  • Legislature · विधायिका — The body that makes the laws; from Latin lex/legis ('law') + latio ('a bringing, proposing').
    Example: Parliament is India's national legislature.
  • Executive · कार्यपालिका — The organ that carries out and enforces the laws; from Latin exsequi ('to carry out, follow through').
    Example: The executive includes ministers and the officials who put laws into action.
  • Judiciary · न्यायपालिका — The organ that interprets the laws and settles disputes; from Latin judex ('judge'), from jus ('law') + dicere ('to say').
    Example: The judiciary acts as a 'watchdog' over the other two organs.
  • Federalism · संघवाद — A system in which power is shared between a central government and state/local governments; from Latin foedus ('treaty, league').
    Example: India's three tiers of government make it a federal country.
  • Regulator · नियामक — A body or rule-maker that sets and enforces fair rules for an activity; from Latin regula ('rule').
    Example: As a regulator, the government sets safety rules for food and medicines.
  • Territory · राज्यक्षेत्र — The defined land (and waters) over which a state has authority — one of the four parts of a state; from Latin terra ('land').
    Example: A state must have a clear territory with recognised borders.
