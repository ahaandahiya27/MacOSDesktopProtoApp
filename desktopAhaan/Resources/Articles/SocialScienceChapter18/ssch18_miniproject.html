🏛️ Map the three organs in your daily life (about 40 min)

What you need

  • A notebook
  • A pen

Steps

  1. Make three columns: Legislature, Executive, Judiciary.
  2. For one week, note any news item or daily event and decide which organ it involves (a new law? a scheme being delivered? a court case?).
  3. Under the Executive, mark whether it was the political executive (a minister) or permanent executive (a bureaucrat/teacher/health worker).
  4. Share your chart and discuss which organ you encounter most in daily life.

👀 What you should see

You will likely find you meet the permanent executive (teachers, health workers, officials) most often, even though the legislature and judiciary shape the rules behind them.

🧠 Why it works

Linking the three organs to real events makes an abstract structure concrete and shows how government touches daily life.

📝 Be an active citizen: design a grievance (about 35 min)

What you need

  • A notebook
  • Optional: look up your local Public Grievances Portal

Steps

  1. Identify one real local problem (a broken streetlamp, a dirty park, a pothole).
  2. Decide which tier of government is responsible (usually local).
  3. Write a clear, polite grievance describing the problem, location and what you want done.
  4. List which tools you could use - grievance portal, RTI, media, writing to a representative - and why.

👀 What you should see

You will see that most everyday problems belong to the local government and that citizens have several real tools to get them fixed.

🧠 Why it works

It turns the chapter's 'and You' message into practice, showing democracy as something citizens actively do.
