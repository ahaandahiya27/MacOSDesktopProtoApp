History, geography and society are full of 'what if' turning points. Think about each question yourself first — then open the panel to compare your reasoning.

What if one organ of government could control the other two?

Think first, then open to compare

If, say, the executive controlled the legislature and the judiciary, there would be no checks and balances. The same people would make the laws, carry them out, and judge whether they were broken - so they could never be held accountable. They might pass unfair laws, ignore citizens' rights, and escape punishment. This is why the Constitution keeps the three organs separate and the judiciary independent: so that power is checked and no one becomes too powerful. The separation of powers is what stops a democracy from sliding into dictatorship.

What if India had only a central government and no state or local governments?

Think first, then open to compare

A single government in Delhi would struggle to manage 1.4 billion people, thousands of villages and towns, and many languages and local needs. Decisions about local roads, water and streetlamps would be slow and out of touch, because distant officials cannot know every area's needs. Decentralisation - the three tiers of union, state and local government - solves this by keeping local decisions close to the people, making government faster, better informed and more accountable. This is exactly why India is a federal country with a constitutional 'third tier' of local government.
