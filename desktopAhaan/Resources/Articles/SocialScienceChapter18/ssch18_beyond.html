Separation of powers: how to stop power going to one head [Class 10]

Why split government into three organs at all? Why not let one strong body do everything? The answer is an old and powerful idea called the 'separation of powers'. If the same people made the laws, carried them out, AND judged whether they were broken, there would be nothing to stop them from abusing their power - they could make unfair laws and then protect themselves from any consequence. By giving law-making to the legislature, law-implementing to the executive, and law-judging to an independent judiciary, each organ can check the others. This system of 'checks and balances' means power is shared, not hoarded. The judiciary's power of judicial review - to strike down laws that break the Constitution - is the sharpest of these checks. The chapter's point that 'no part of the government becomes too powerful' is exactly this idea. It is one of the most important inventions in the history of government, and it is what allows a democracy to limit even its own elected leaders.

Why democracy is more than voting: the rule of law and minority rights [Class 10]

Many people think democracy simply means 'the majority decides'. But the chapter shows that real democracy is more subtle - and more humane. If the majority could do absolutely anything, it could vote to take away the rights of a smaller group, and that group would have no protection. This is called the 'tyranny of the majority' or the 'dominance of the majority'. A democratic REPUBLIC solves this by adding the rule of law: a Constitution that guarantees everyone's fundamental rights and limits what any majority may do. So elected leaders cannot pass laws against freedom of speech, religion or equality, no matter how many votes they have. The apartment-delivery example in the chapter shows this in miniature - a majority rule was overturned to protect the sick and elderly. The deep lesson is that democracy needs two things together: the power of the people to choose, AND the rule of law to protect everyone, including the few. One without the other is incomplete.
