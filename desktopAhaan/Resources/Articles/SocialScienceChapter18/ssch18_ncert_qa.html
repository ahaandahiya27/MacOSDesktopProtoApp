Q1. What is the difference between a state and a government? (NCERT page 146)

A state is the larger, permanent entity with four parts - population, territory, government and sovereignty. A government is just one part of the state: the group or system that runs the country by making, enforcing and resolving disputes about laws. Governments change after elections, but the state remains - like teachers changing while the school stays.

Q2. What makes India a democratic republic? (NCERT page 149)

India is a democracy because its people choose their government through free and fair elections ('rule by the people'). It is a republic because its head of state (the president) is elected, not a hereditary king or queen, and rulers' powers are limited by the Constitution. Being both makes India a democratic republic.

Q3. What are the three organs of government and their functions? (NCERT page 152)

The legislature makes laws and represents the people; the executive implements the laws and makes policy (with a political executive of elected ministers and a permanent executive of bureaucrats); and the judiciary, the independent watchdog, ensures laws are followed, interprets them, protects rights, and through judicial review safeguards the Constitution.

Q4. How can a citizen make the government do what it is meant to do? (NCERT page 157)

Through grievance redressal (e.g. the Public Grievances Portal), the Right to Information (RTI) Act for transparency, the media, joining civil society organisations (NGOs), writing to representatives, and - most importantly - voting in elections.

Q5. Why does India have three tiers of government, and what is decentralisation? (NCERT page 162)

Because India is too vast and diverse for one central government to run alone, it has union, state and local governments. Decentralisation means spreading out power so local problems are solved locally (close to the people, faster and more accountably) while big issues like defence go to higher levels.
