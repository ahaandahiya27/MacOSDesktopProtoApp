Five Wrong Answers Class 7 Students Give

The five places almost every Class 7 student trips up on Chapter 6 — and the one-line fix for each.

≈ 5 min read · revision-tier

Examiners say the same wrong answers come back year after year. None of these mistakes are silly — they are almost right. That is exactly what makes them dangerous. Read each one, cover the answer, and try to spot what is off before peeking.

1. “Burning is a physical change because the substance just disappears.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Burning is the most dramatic chemical change. Wood + oxygen → CO₂ + water vapour + ash. The mass doesn't disappear — it just leaves as invisible gas. If you collected all the CO₂ and water vapour from burning 100 g of wood, you'd have ~150 g (the extra mass came from oxygen). The chemistry equation balances exactly; that's the law of mass conservation.

2. “If you can see it change, it must be chemical.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Visible change tells you SOMETHING happened, but doesn't say what. Ice melting (visible) is physical — water unchanged. Bread mould growing (visible) is chemical — new substances. The test is: can you reverse it easily? Ice ↔ water = physical. Mould → bread = impossible = chemical.

3. “Crystallisation creates new substances.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Crystallisation only rearranges existing substances into orderly patterns. Salt dissolved in water IS salt; salt crystallised out IS still salt — same molecules, more orderly arrangement. No new compound formed. This is why crystallisation is a physical change, not chemical. Industrial use: purifying drugs, sugar, salt all rely on this clean physical-only step.

4. “Galvanised iron doesn't rust because it's a different metal.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Galvanised iron IS iron — just with a thin coating of zinc. The zinc is more reactive than iron. When water and oxygen attack, the zinc rusts FIRST (sacrificially), forming a protective oxide layer that stops further damage to the iron underneath. Once the zinc is gone, the iron rusts normally. The buckets, sheets, and tin-cans of India are mostly galvanised iron.

5. “Cooking is just heating food.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Cooking involves many chemical changes happening simultaneously. Maillard reaction (browning of roti or paneer) is sugars + amino acids forming new flavour molecules. Egg whites going from clear to opaque is proteins denaturing irreversibly. Starch gelatinising in rice is water + starch forming a new gel structure. All these are chemical changes — you can't 'uncook' them.

📝 Stretch your thinking

Pick any two of these and explain them to a younger sibling using a one-line analogy each. If you can compress a mistake into one sentence with one image, you have understood it.
