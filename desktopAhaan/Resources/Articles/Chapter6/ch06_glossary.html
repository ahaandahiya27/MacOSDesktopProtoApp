A Class 7 Physical and Chemical Changes Dictionary

The 10 terms to know

  • Physical change · भौतिक परिवर्तन — A change in form, shape, or state without altering the substance's chemical identity.
    Example: Ice melting, water boiling, tearing paper, sugar dissolving in tea.
  • Chemical change · रासायनिक परिवर्तन — A change that creates one or more new substances with new properties; usually irreversible.
    Example: Burning wood, rusting iron, cooking egg, curdling milk.
  • Rusting · जंग लगना — The chemical change where iron combines with oxygen and water to form reddish-brown iron oxide.
    Example: A nail left outside in the monsoon becomes covered with rust within a week.
  • Crystallisation · क्रिस्टलीकरण — A purification process where dissolved solids reform into pure regular-shaped crystals as solvent evaporates or the solution cools.
    Example: Tamil Nadu's salt pans produce edible salt by crystallisation of seawater.
  • Galvanisation · गैल्वेनीकरण — Coating iron with a thin layer of zinc to protect it from rusting; the zinc rusts first, sacrificially.
    Example: Galvanised iron sheets and buckets, common across rural India, last 5x longer than uncoated iron.
  • Combustion · दहन — A chemical change where a substance reacts rapidly with oxygen, releasing heat and light.
    Example: A burning candle, a phuljhari sparkler, the engine of an autorickshaw.
  • Reactant · अभिकारक — A substance that takes part in (and is changed by) a chemical reaction.
    Example: In rusting, iron and oxygen and water are the reactants.
  • Product · उत्पाद — A substance formed by a chemical reaction.
    Example: In rusting, the product is iron oxide (rust).
  • Maillard reaction · मेलार्ड अभिक्रिया — A chemical reaction between sugars and amino acids during cooking, producing brown colour and savoury flavours.
    Example: Tandoor roti turning golden-brown, paneer browning in a pan, dosa edges becoming crisp.
  • Photosynthesis · प्रकाश संश्लेषण — The chemical reaction by which green plants combine CO₂ and water in sunlight to make glucose and release oxygen.
    Example: Every leaf in your garden does this from sunrise to sunset.
