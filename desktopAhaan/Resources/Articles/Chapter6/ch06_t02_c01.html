How crystallization works

Crystallization is the process of obtaining pure crystals of a substance from its hot saturated solution as it cools.

≈ 2 min read · for curious 12-year-olds

Dissolve as much salt or sugar as you can in hot water — making a saturated solution. Then let it cool slowly. Beautiful crystals will form on the bottom or sides of the cup. That's crystallization. The water can hold less dissolved substance when cool, so the extra comes out as solid crystals. The slower the cooling, the bigger and more perfect the crystals. This is how rock candy (mishri) is made.

The textbook version

Crystallization is the process by which pure crystals of a solid substance are obtained from its hot saturated solution. When a hot saturated solution is allowed to cool slowly, the solubility of the dissolved substance decreases, and the excess substance separates out as pure crystals. The technique is widely used to: (1) purify impure salts and chemicals, (2) obtain large single crystals for research, (3) produce table salt from seawater (salt pans use evaporative crystallization). Crystallization is a physical change — no new substance is formed, just a separation.

Why does this happen?

A hot saturated solution holds more solute than the same liquid can hold cold. As it cools, the surplus separates out as solid. Pure substances tend to form ordered crystal arrangements; impurities can't fit and stay dissolved.

Where you see this in the real world

  • Sea salt from evaporation pans — In coastal Tamil Nadu, seawater is led into shallow ponds. Sun evaporates the water; salt crystallizes out. The salt collected is then washed and refined into table salt. [Weather]
  • Mishri (rock candy) at home — Boil sugar in water until thick syrup forms. Suspend a string in it and leave for days. Sugar crystals grow on the string — that's homemade rock candy. [Kitchen]
  • Snowflakes in winter — When water vapour in the air cools below 0 °C, it crystallizes directly into the hexagonal lattice we recognise as snowflakes. Each one is a crystal grown atom by atom from the gas phase. [Weather]

Going deeper

Crystallization works because solubility is temperature-dependent. Hot water can dissolve more solid than cold water for most substances. As the solution cools, the saturation point drops, and the excess solute precipitates as crystals. The ordered crystal structure naturally excludes impurities — they remain in the mother liquor. Geometry of the crystal (cubic for salt, hexagonal for snow) reflects the underlying atomic arrangement. Industrial crystallization is the basis for sugar refining (cane juice → sugar crystals), pharmaceutical purification (drug crystals are easier to package and dose), and even semiconductor manufacturing (silicon crystals are the foundation of every chip).

Beyond the book

Sir William Bragg and his son Lawrence Bragg won the 1915 Nobel Prize for showing that X-rays diffract through crystals — letting us see the arrangement of atoms inside. This technique (X-ray crystallography) revealed the double-helix structure of DNA in 1953, and continues to be the most powerful tool for working out the 3D shape of new drugs and proteins. Every crystal is a window into atomic arrangement.
