Antoine Lavoisier (with India context)

1743–1794 · French

🎧 In one line

Discovered that matter is neither created nor destroyed in any chemical reaction — the law of mass conservation, still the foundation of every chemistry chapter.

In the 1770s, a French chemist named Antoine Lavoisier did something nobody had thought to do: he WEIGHED everything before and after a chemical reaction. He sealed iron filings in a glass container with measured air, heated the container, and then weighed it again. The total mass was unchanged. The iron had turned into rust (gained weight), but the air had lost weight by exactly the same amount. The conclusion: chemistry rearranges atoms, never creates or destroys them. This Law of Mass Conservation is the deepest rule in every chemistry chapter you'll ever read — Class 7, Class 10, Class 11, NEET, JEE. Lavoisier's work reached India through colonial-era science colleges (Calcutta in 1857, Bombay in 1854) and now sits as the silent foundation of every balanced equation in your Class 10 textbook.
