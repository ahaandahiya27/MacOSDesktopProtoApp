Chapter 6 · Topic 1 Overview

Two Kinds of Change

Physical changes keep the same substance; chemical changes create something brand new.

Topic overview · for curious 12-year-olds

Every second of every day, matter is changing. Ice melts, wood burns, iron rusts, and food cooks. But scientists group all these changes into just two categories: physical and chemical. Understanding the difference is one of the most important ideas in all of chemistry.

What This Topic Covers

Topic 1 has eight concept articles. Work through them in order, or jump to whichever one you need. Each article is a self-contained lesson with examples, activities, and quiz questions.

  1. Physical Change -- Same Stuff, Different Shape What happens when you melt ice or break a stick? The substance stays the same.
  2. Chemical Change -- New Substance, New Properties Burning paper creates ash and gas -- totally new materials.
  3. Rusting of Iron -- The Famous Chemical Change Why does iron turn reddish-brown? Meet iron oxide.
  4. Signs of a Chemical Reaction Colour change, gas bubbles, heat, and more -- how to spot a chemical change.
  5. Burning, Cooking, and Digestion as Chemical Changes Three everyday processes that are actually chemistry in action.
  6. Reversible vs Irreversible Changes Can you undo a change? Sometimes yes, sometimes no.
  7. Galvanisation and Preventing Rust How a thin coat of zinc saves bridges and buildings.
  8. Physical vs Chemical -- Tricky Cases Dissolving sugar, curdling milk -- are they physical or chemical?

Study tip

Keep a small notebook handy. As you read each article, jot down one example of a physical change and one example of a chemical change. By the end of this topic, you will have a fantastic revision list!
