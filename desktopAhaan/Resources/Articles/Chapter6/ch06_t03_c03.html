Photosynthesis and respiration — life's chemical engines

Plants run PHOTOSYNTHESIS to build sugar from CO₂ + water using sunlight; all living cells run RESPIRATION to burn that sugar with O₂ for energy — the two opposite reactions cycle the same atoms around the biosphere.

≈ 3 min read · for curious 12-year-olds

Every plant cell with chlorophyll runs PHOTOSYNTHESIS: it absorbs CO₂ from air, water from soil, and sunlight, and makes SUGAR + OXYGEN. Every living cell — yours, a fish's, a bacterium's — runs RESPIRATION: it burns SUGAR with OXYGEN to release ENERGY + CO₂ + WATER. Look at the two equations side by side and they're MIRROR IMAGES. Plants build the sugar; animals (and at night, plants themselves) burn it. The same atoms — carbon, hydrogen, oxygen — cycle endlessly between living things and the air. Photosynthesis stores sunlight in chemical bonds; respiration releases it for life's work.

The textbook version

Two complementary biochemical processes: (1) Photosynthesis (in chloroplasts of green plants and some bacteria): 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + sunlight → C₆H₁₂O₆ (glucose) + 6 O₂. Reactants come from air (CO₂), soil (water), and Sun (energy); products are glucose (stored in plants as starch) and O₂ (released to atmosphere). (2) Respiration (in mitochondria of all eukaryotes, and in cytoplasm of prokaryotes): C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂ → 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + energy (~36 ATP molecules). Reactants are glucose and O₂; products are CO₂ and water + cellular energy currency (ATP). The two equations are essentially the reverse of each other. Together they cycle ~ 120 billion tonnes of carbon between atmosphere and biosphere every year.

Why does this happen?

Photosynthesis 'captures' sunlight energy by building it into the chemical bonds of glucose. Respiration 'releases' that energy by breaking those bonds back down with O₂. Together, the two run a closed carbon-and-oxygen cycle, with sunlight as the only energy input.

Where you see this in the real world

  • Plant in sunlight vs darkness — Submerge a plant in water, expose to bright sun, count gas bubbles — you're seeing real-time O₂ release from photosynthesis. [Plants]
  • Heavy breathing while running — Your body's increased glucose burn during exercise needs more O₂ — you breathe faster to supply it and exhale more CO₂. [Human body]
  • Yeast bread rising — Yeast in bread dough respires sugar anaerobically, releasing CO₂ that makes the bread rise — same respiration biochemistry, no oxygen needed. [Kitchen]

Going deeper

Photosynthesis comprises light reactions (chlorophyll absorbs photons, generates ATP and NADPH, splits water releasing O₂ — the only known biological route to O₂) and the Calvin cycle (CO₂ is fixed into 3-phosphoglycerate via RuBisCO, the planet's most abundant protein). Respiration comprises glycolysis (cytosol, 2 ATP), the citric acid cycle (mitochondrial matrix, NADH/FADH₂ generation), and oxidative phosphorylation (inner mitochondrial membrane, ATP synthesis driven by proton gradient and chemiosmosis — Mitchell 1961 Nobel). C4 and CAM plants have engineered variants of photosynthesis suited to high-temperature or arid environments. Climate change is shifting the balance: warmer temperatures favour respiration (faster) over photosynthesis (acclimation lags), pushing some ecosystems from net carbon sinks to sources.
