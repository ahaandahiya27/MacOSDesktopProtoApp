Physical and Chemical Changes

Everything around you is changing -- but not all changes are the same!

Chapter overview · for curious 12-year-olds

Think about what happens when you tear a piece of paper versus when you burn it. Both are changes, but they are very different. In this chapter, you will discover the difference between physical and chemical changes, learn how crystals form, and explore the chemistry happening right in your kitchen and in nature every single day.

Big Ideas at a Glance

  • Physical changes alter the form or appearance of a substance but do not create a new substance. They are usually reversible.
  • Chemical changes produce one or more entirely new substances with new properties. They are usually irreversible.
  • Crystallisation is a physical change used to obtain pure solid crystals from a solution.
  • Many everyday processes -- cooking, rusting, digestion, burning -- are chemical changes.
  • Galvanisation protects iron from rusting by coating it with zinc.
