Banks and Your Money

Financial infrastructure - the network of banks, payment systems, stock markets and other institutions - moves and manages money, complementing physical infrastructure. At its heart is the bank: a financial institution that collects deposits and gives loans, offering savings, current and fixed deposit accounts. Banks pay interest on savings, and through the magic of compounding - earning interest on interest, like the doubling rice grains on a chessboard - small amounts can grow into surprisingly large sums over time.

  1. Financial InfrastructureFinancial infrastructure is the network of banks, payment systems, stock markets and other financial institutions that helps people, businesses and the government move and manage money.
  2. What Banks Do, and Types of AccountsA bank collects money as deposits and lends it out as loans; it offers different accounts - savings (earns interest), current (for businesses), and fixed deposit (locked for a period, higher interest).
  3. Interest and the Magic of CompoundingInterest is the extra money a bank pays you for keeping your savings; through compounding - earning interest on interest - small amounts can grow into surprisingly large sums over time.

Loans, Credit, and How Banks Earn

A loan is money borrowed from a bank, repaid later with interest; banks give credit to people (for homes, education) and businesses (for machinery, expansion). Banks earn by charging borrowers a higher interest rate than they pay savers - the 'spread' - and keep some deposits as reserves rather than lending everything. Banking has also become more inclusive: the Jan Dhan Yojana (2014) gave crores their first bank account, while post offices and institutions like NABARD bring finance to rural India.

  1. Loans and CreditA loan is money borrowed from a bank to be repaid later with interest; banks give loans (credit) to people and businesses for purposes like buying a house, funding education, or expanding a business.
  2. How Banks Make MoneyBanks earn by charging a higher interest rate on the loans they give than the interest they pay on deposits; this difference (spread) is their income, and they keep some deposits as reserves rather than lending all of it.
  3. Banking for All: Jan Dhan and Other InstitutionsThe Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana (2014) gave crores of Indians their first bank account with no minimum balance; beyond banks, post offices and institutions like NABARD also provide financial services.

The Reserve Bank of India

With so many banks, India needs one institution to keep order: the Reserve Bank of India (RBI), established in 1935 and the 'banker to banks' since 1949. As India's central bank, it supervises the banking system, holds banks' accounts and lends to banks and the government - so important it is compared to Kubera, the god of wealth. The RBI alone prints and distributes India's currency and fixes the benchmark interest rate at which it lends to banks, letting it manage the nation's money and keep the financial system stable.

  1. The RBI: Banker to BanksThe Reserve Bank of India (RBI), established in 1935, is India's central bank - the 'banker to banks' - which supervises the banking system, holds banks' accounts, and lends to banks and the government.
  2. What the RBI ControlsThe RBI prints and distributes India's currency and fixes the benchmark interest rate at which it lends to banks - powers that let it manage the nation's money and keep the financial system stable.

Moving Money: Cheques, UPI and the Stock Market

Money moves between accounts in many ways. A cheque is a paper order to pay, slow and needing a bank visit; electronic transfers are instant. UPI lets people send money instantly from a phone - it powered India's cashless boom and is now 'India's gift to the world', adopted by many countries. The stock market lets people buy shares (units of company ownership) at exchanges like the Bombay Stock Exchange (1875), helping companies raise money and savers invest. With digital convenience comes the risk of cyber fraud, so we must guard our PINs and OTPs and report fraud via helpline 1930.

  1. Cheques and Electronic TransfersMoney can be moved between accounts using a cheque - a paper instrument ordering the bank to pay someone - or, faster, through electronic modes that transfer money instantly from sender to receiver.
  2. UPI and the Digital Payments RevolutionUPI (Unified Payments Interface) lets people send money instantly from a phone; it powered India's cashless boom (especially during COVID-19) and has become 'India's gift to the world', adopted by many countries.
  3. The Stock Market and SharesA share is a small unit of ownership in a company; people buy and sell shares at a stock exchange (like the Bombay Stock Exchange, founded 1875), letting companies raise money and savers invest for growth.
  4. Staying Safe with Digital MoneyDigital payments are convenient but carry the risk of cyber fraud, so we must protect our bank details, never share OTPs or PINs, and report fraud (e.g. via the helpline 1930).
