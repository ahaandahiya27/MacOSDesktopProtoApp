Q1. What is financial infrastructure? How does it complement physical infrastructure? (NCERT page 194)

Financial infrastructure is the network of banks, payment systems, stock markets and other financial institutions that helps move and manage money. It complements physical infrastructure because economic activities supported by roads and railways are driven by money transactions, which financial infrastructure makes possible; it also funds the building of physical infrastructure. By promoting savings, credit and investment, it boosts the economy.

Q2. How does having a bank account help people? (NCERT page 195)

A bank account keeps money safe, earns interest so savings grow, and gives access to loans, easy payments and government benefits. Through schemes like Jan Dhan Yojana, accounts let workers receive wages, students receive scholarships and farmers borrow - all directly, cutting out middlemen.

Q3. What are the advantages and disadvantages of compound interest for savers and borrowers? (NCERT page 197)

For savers, compounding is an advantage: earning interest on interest makes savings grow faster over time, especially if started early. For borrowers, it is a disadvantage if a loan is not repaid: the unpaid interest is added to the debt, so the amount owed grows faster and faster - which is why debt should be repaid promptly.

Q4. Why is the fixed deposit account's interest higher than a savings account's? (NCERT page 196)

Because in a fixed deposit the money is locked in for a fixed period (like 3 or 5 years), so the bank can use it for longer without the risk of sudden withdrawal. In return for locking the money away, the bank rewards the depositor with a higher interest rate than a savings account, which allows withdrawals.

Q5. How does the stock market help mobilise savings, and how do companies benefit from issuing shares? (NCERT page 208)

The stock market lets individuals invest their savings by buying shares, hoping the value will grow - so idle savings are put to use. Companies benefit by issuing shares to raise money (funds) for their operations without taking a loan, in exchange for giving buyers part-ownership. Thus savings flow to companies that need capital.
