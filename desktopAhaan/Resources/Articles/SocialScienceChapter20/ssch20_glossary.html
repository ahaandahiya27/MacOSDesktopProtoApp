The 17 terms to know

  • Financial infrastructure · वित्तीय अवसंरचना — The network of banks, payment systems, stock markets and other institutions that helps move and manage money.
    Example: Banks and UPI are part of India's financial infrastructure.
  • Bank · बैंक — A financial institution that collects money as deposits and lends money as loans.
    Example: Navdeep deposits his savings in a bank.
  • Deposit · जमा — Money placed in a bank account that can be withdrawn and often earns interest.
    Example: Navdeep made a deposit of Rs 3,000.
  • Interest · ब्याज — The extra money a bank pays a depositor (or charges a borrower) over time for the use of money.
    Example: A savings account earns interest on the money kept in it.
  • Compounding · चक्रवृद्धि — Earning interest on interest already added, so money grows faster over time.
    Example: The chessboard rice story shows the power of compounding.
  • Loan · ऋण — An amount borrowed from a bank to be repaid later with interest.
    Example: Rima took a loan for her bamboo business.
  • Reserve Bank of India (RBI) · भारतीय रिज़र्व बैंक — India's central bank, the 'banker to banks', established in 1935; it prints currency and supervises the banking system.
    Example: The RBI fixes the benchmark interest rate.
  • Benchmark interest rate · मानक ब्याज दर — The base interest rate the RBI fixes for lending money to commercial banks.
    Example: When the RBI changes the benchmark rate, loan rates change too.
  • Cheque · चेक — A paper instrument that orders a bank to pay a named person from your account.
    Example: You can pay a friend by writing a cheque.
  • UPI · यूपीआई — Unified Payments Interface - an instant electronic payment system used on a smartphone.
    Example: People pay shops instantly using UPI.
  • Share · शेयर — A unit of ownership in a company; buying one makes you a part-owner.
    Example: Buying a share of a company makes you a part-owner.
  • Bank (root) · बैंक — From Italian banca ('bench') — medieval moneylenders worked at benches in markets; a failed one had his banca rotta ('broken bench'), the origin of 'bankrupt'.
    Example: The word 'bank' literally comes from the moneychanger's bench.
  • Credit · ऋण / उधार — Money lent in the trust that it will be repaid; from Latin credere ('to believe, to trust').
    Example: A bank gives credit because it trusts the borrower to repay with interest.
  • Finance · वित्त — The management of money — saving, lending and investing; from Latin finis ('end'), once meaning the settling of a debt.
    Example: Banks are the heart of a country's financial system.
  • Inflation · मुद्रास्फीति — A general rise in prices over time, so money buys less; the RBI tries to keep it in check; from Latin inflare ('to blow up').
    Example: If inflation rises too fast, the RBI may raise interest rates to cool it.
  • Dividend · लाभांश — The share of a company's profit paid to each shareholder; from Latin dividendum ('a thing to be divided').
    Example: When a company does well, it may pay its shareholders a dividend.
  • Principal · मूलधन — The original sum of money lent or saved, before any interest is added; from Latin principalis ('first, chief').
    Example: On a loan you repay the principal plus interest.
