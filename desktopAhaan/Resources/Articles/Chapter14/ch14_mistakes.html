Five Wrong Answers Class 7 Students Give

The five places almost every Class 7 student trips up on Chapter 14 — and the one-line fix for each.

≈ 5 min read · revision-tier

Examiners say the same wrong answers come back year after year. None of these mistakes are silly — they are almost right. That is exactly what makes them dangerous. Read each one, cover the answer, and try to spot what is off before peeking.

1. “Current is consumed in a bulb.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Current is NOT consumed. The same amount of current that enters the bulb leaves the bulb. What's consumed is ENERGY (electrons lose energy as they pass through the bulb filament, energy released as heat + light). Think of it like a water turbine — water that enters also exits; the WATER isn't consumed, the PRESSURE drop powers the turbine. Same with current and voltage.

2. “AC and DC current are 'different kinds of electricity'.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Both are electric current — just different patterns. DC (Direct Current): electrons flow in one direction continuously. Batteries, USB chargers — DC. AC (Alternating Current): electrons reverse direction 50 times per second (in India). Power grids — AC. The reason for AC: it's easier to step up/down with transformers, so power can travel long distances at high voltage then be stepped down at homes. Same electrons, same physics — just flow patterns.

3. “A fuse 'stops too much electricity'.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: A fuse is a deliberately weak link — a thin wire that MELTS when too much current passes through. When it melts, the circuit breaks, stopping current entirely. The fuse SACRIFICES itself to save the rest of the wiring (which would otherwise overheat and start a fire). After a fuse blows, you replace it — and ideally find out WHY too much current flowed. Modern MCBs (Miniature Circuit Breakers) do the same job but reset instead of needing replacement.

4. “Magnets and electricity are separate things.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: They're INTERTWINED — that's the deepest discovery in physics history. Moving electric charges create magnetic fields. Moving magnets create electric currents. These are TWO ASPECTS OF THE SAME THING — electromagnetism. Maxwell showed in 1865 that light itself is a wave of electromagnetism. Class 7 introduces the first link (Ørsted's compass). Class 12 reveals the full unity.

5. “Rubber-soled shoes always protect you from electric shock.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Rubber soles add SOME resistance, but at high voltages (anything above 50 V) they can still allow current through. Genuine electrical work uses INSULATED tools and gloves rated for the voltage. The myth that 'rubber slippers protect you from anything' has killed people. Hospital corridors and electrical substations have dedicated insulating mats for a reason — chappals alone aren't safety equipment.

📝 Stretch your thinking

Pick any two of these and explain them to a younger sibling using a one-line analogy each. If you can compress a mistake into one sentence with one image, you have understood it.
