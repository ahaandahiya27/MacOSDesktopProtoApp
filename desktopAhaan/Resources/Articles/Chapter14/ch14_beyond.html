Beyond the Book — Three Mind-Stretchers About Electric Current and its Effect

Three ideas that don't show up in the NCERT textbook but will change how you see this chapter.

≈ 9 min read · curious-kid level

The chapter teaches you electric current is the flow of charge through a conductor. This article goes a step further — three stretch topics that lead naturally into Class 8 and beyond. Read one a day; don't try to absorb them all at once.

1. Ohm's law — the relationship between V, I, R

Class 7 says current flows through circuits. Class 9 quantifies it with OHM'S LAW: V = I × R. Voltage (V, volts) = Current (I, amperes) × Resistance (R, ohms). Example: a 12V battery connected to a 4Ω resistor gives current I = V/R = 12/4 = 3 A. Double the voltage → double the current (for same R). Double the resistance → halve the current (for same V). This single formula governs every electrical device — phone charger, fan, fridge. Ohm's law isn't a 'law of nature' like gravity; some devices (diodes, bulbs at high power) deviate. But for resistors and most household wiring, it holds beautifully. Indian electricians use this to size wires — too-thin wire = too much resistance = wasted heat = fire risk.

2. Series vs parallel — why your house wiring is parallel

Class 9 introduces V=IR for one resistor. Class 10 builds circuits with multiple. SERIES: resistors end-to-end. Same current through all. Total R = R₁ + R₂ + R₃. If one breaks, ALL stop (old Christmas lights). PARALLEL: resistors side-by-side, each with full voltage. Total R = 1 / (1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + ...) — always LESS than smallest. If one breaks, others keep working. Indian household wiring is PARALLEL — your fridge keeps running when you flip a light off. Each bulb gets full 230V; switches independently. The main fuse protects the whole house from total current exceeding safe limit. Why solar panels are wired in series (boost voltage) but inverters connect in parallel (boost current) — engineering trade-offs.

3. Electromagnetic induction — how generators make all our electricity

Class 7 says current → magnetism (Ørsted). Class 12 reveals the REVERSE: magnetism → current. ELECTROMAGNETIC INDUCTION (Faraday, 1831): moving a magnet through a coil INDUCES a current in the coil. Faster motion = stronger current. This is how EVERY GENERATOR works — a rotating magnet (or coil) inside a stationary coil (or magnet) creates current. The Tehri Hydroelectric Dam: water spins a turbine, which spins a magnet, which induces current in surrounding coils. Same in coal plants, nuclear plants, wind turbines. EVERY KILOWATT of Indian grid electricity is electromagnetic induction. Bicycle dynamo (dynamo light): tiny induction generator. LENZ'S LAW: induced current opposes the change that caused it — Newton's third law of electromagnetism.
