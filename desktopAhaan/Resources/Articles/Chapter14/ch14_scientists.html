Every chapter has a person behind it — someone who first asked the right question, ran the right experiment, or wrote down the answer. This is a one-page portrait of the scientist most tied to this chapter's content.

Mokshagundam Visvesvaraya

1861–1962 · Indian

🎧 In one line

Engineer who built India's first hydroelectric power station and modern dam — turning Ørsted's electromagnetism into electricity for villages.

Visvesvaraya was born in 1861 in a poor Brahmin family in Chikkaballapur (Karnataka). He walked 6 km daily to school. Through scholarships he became an engineer, then Chief Engineer of Mysore state. In 1932 he completed the Krishna Raja Sagara dam on the Cauvery — its hydroelectric station was India's first major electric power supply, lighting Bengaluru and Mysore. He invented automatic flood gates (still used at Khadakwasla dam in Pune). He chaired the first nationally-funded engineering college (now IISc Bangalore). He was knighted (Sir M.V.) and given Bharat Ratna. He lived to be 101. Every September 15 — his birthday — is India's National Engineer's Day. Visvesvaraya didn't discover the equations; he TURNED them into infrastructure. The current flowing through your home tonight, the LED bulb that just clicked on, the rural village lit under Saubhagya Yojana — all run on the engineering tradition he founded. Ørsted's compass moved in Denmark in 1820; Visvesvaraya's turbines moved water in Karnataka in 1932 — same physics, civilization-scale impact.
