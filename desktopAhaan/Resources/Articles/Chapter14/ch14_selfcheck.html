Question 1 — from Chapter 14

What is an electric circuit? Name its main components.

🧠 Answer in your head first

Don't peek. Speak the answer out loud, then check.

Answer: An electric circuit is a closed path through which electric current flows. Main components: (1) Source — battery or cell that provides energy. (2) Conductor — wires (usually copper) that carry current. (3) Switch — to control whether the circuit is open or closed. (4) Load — the device that uses electricity (bulb, fan, motor). (5) Optional safety — fuse or MCB.

Why: Definition: a closed loop allowing current to flow.

Question 4 — from Chapter 14

What is an electromagnet? How is it made?

🧠 Answer in your head first

Don't peek. Speak the answer out loud, then check.

Answer: An electromagnet is a temporary magnet made by passing electric current through a coil of insulated wire wound around a soft iron core. When current flows, the iron core becomes magnetic and can attract iron objects. When current stops, the magnetism disappears.

Why: Take a soft iron rod or nail.

Question 5 — from Chapter 14

A solar panel produces DC electricity. India's homes use AC. Explain WHY this mismatch exists, what device bridges them, and the energy cost of the conversion.

🧠 Answer in your head first

Don't peek. Speak the answer out loud, then check.

Answer: Solar cells convert photon energy directly into DC (the photoelectric effect produces direct current naturally). Homes use AC because Tesla's AC distribution won the 'war of currents' in the 1890s — AC can be stepped up to high voltages for long-distance transmission (low loss) and stepped down for safe home use, all via transformers. DC can't easily change voltage. The bridge: INVERTER — converts solar DC to grid-compatible AC. Cost: ~5-15% energy lost in conversion (heat). A 5 kW solar panel delivers ~4.5 kW after inversion. Modern inverters: ~95% efficient. Compromise: efficiency vs. compatibility with the established AC grid.

Why: Solar physics: DC output natural.
