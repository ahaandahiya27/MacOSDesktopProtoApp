Question 1 — from Chapter 16

What is the water cycle? Briefly describe its main steps.

🧠 Answer in your head first

Don't peek. Speak the answer out loud, then check.

Answer: The water cycle is the continuous movement of water between the Earth's surface and atmosphere. Main steps: (1) Evaporation — water turns into vapour from oceans/lakes/soil. (2) Transpiration — plants release water vapour. (3) Condensation — vapour cools high up, forming clouds. (4) Precipitation — water falls as rain/snow. (5) Runoff and infiltration — water flows back to oceans or seeps underground.

Why: Define: ongoing cycle of water moving through atmosphere, surface, underground.

Question 2 — from Chapter 16

Rainwater harvesting refers to:

🧠 Answer in your head first

Don't peek. Speak the answer out loud, then check.

Answer: Collecting and storing rainwater for use or to recharge groundwater

Why: Rainwater harvesting captures rain from roofs and surfaces and stores it in tanks or directs it into pits/wells that recharge groundwater.

Question 5 — from Chapter 16

Design an intervention for a school where ONE major water-wastage problem is leaking taps in bathrooms. Quantify the daily waste from a single dripping tap, predict annual school-wide impact, and recommend interventions.

🧠 Answer in your head first

Don't peek. Speak the answer out loud, then check.

Answer: Single dripping tap: ~1 drip/second = ~5 mL/min = 7 L/day. School with 20 leaky taps = 140 L/day = 51,000 L/year = enough to fill 5 large tankers. Interventions: (1) Quarterly maintenance check of all taps; replace washers (cost ~Rs 10 each). (2) Install lever taps (less wear than rotary, fewer drips). (3) Educate students to report leaks immediately (eyes everywhere). (4) Audit water bill monthly; sudden increases signal new leaks. Combined: 95%+ of leak waste avoidable.

Why: Single tap drip: ~7 L/day (one drip per second).
