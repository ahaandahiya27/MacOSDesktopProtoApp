Water: A Precious Resource

Chapter 16 · Class 7 Science

The Earth is wet — but most of it is unusable

Around 70% of Earth's surface is covered in water, but:

  • ~97% is salty ocean water.
  • ~2% is locked away in glaciers and polar ice.
  • Less than 1% is fresh, liquid water we can actually use.
Of every 100 litres of water on Earth, only about 1 litre is fresh — and of that 1 litre, most is underground, frozen, or trapped in deep aquifers. The amount in rivers and lakes is tiny.

Where does our water come from?

Four big sources:

  • Rivers and lakes (surface water).
  • Rain — refills everything.
  • Glaciers (slowly melting).
  • Groundwater — water hidden under the soil, in saturated rock layers called aquifers.

The water table

If you dig down anywhere, eventually you reach water — that level is called the water table. Wells tap into it. Rain seeps down and tops it up. Pumps suck it out. If we pump faster than rain can replenish, the water table drops and wells go dry.

Irrigation — which method wastes the least?

  • Drip irrigation — water dripped right at the roots. Most efficient.
  • Sprinkler irrigation — sprayed over fields. Medium efficient; lots of evaporation.
  • Flood irrigation — whole field soaked. Easiest but wastes the most.

Rainwater harvesting

Most rooftops drain rain into the street. With gutters and a storage tank, the same rain can fill a tank for gardens and washing — or be sent into the ground through a soak-pit to recharge the aquifer.

Bawdis — India's stepwell genius

For thousands of years, dry-region India built stepwells like Chand Baori and Rani-ki-Vav. Some are nine storeys deep. Villagers walked down to the water table even in the driest months, while monsoon runoff was stored for the year ahead.

What can a kid do?

  • Close the tap while brushing teeth.
  • Bucket bath instead of shower.
  • Fix leaking taps fast.
  • Reuse water from washing vegetables for plants.
  • Tell one friend why water matters. World Water Day is 22 March.
