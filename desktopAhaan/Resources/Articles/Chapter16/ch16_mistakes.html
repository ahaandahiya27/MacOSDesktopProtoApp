Five Wrong Answers Class 7 Students Give

The five places almost every Class 7 student trips up on Chapter 16 — and the one-line fix for each.

≈ 5 min read · revision-tier

Examiners say the same wrong answers come back year after year. None of these mistakes are silly — they are almost right. That is exactly what makes them dangerous. Read each one, cover the answer, and try to spot what is off before peeking.

1. “Water cannot run out because rain keeps coming.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: FRESH water can absolutely run out — locally, even globally. Only 2.5% of Earth's water is fresh; of that, 70% is locked in glaciers/polar ice. Available fresh water is <1% of total water. Population growth + climate change + groundwater overuse = shrinking supply. Chennai 2019, Cape Town 2018, California 2014 droughts prove fresh water can hit zero. Rain comes back, but not necessarily where humans live, when they need it, in usable form.

2. “Groundwater is infinite — there's always more if you dig deeper.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Groundwater forms over hundreds to thousands of years as rain seeps slowly down. Modern pumping can drain in decades what took millennia to fill. Once an aquifer is empty, it doesn't refill quickly — and in some cases the rock layers COMPACT permanently after being drained, never able to hold water again. Punjab, Rajasthan, Haryana are losing groundwater FAST. The CGWB (Central Ground Water Board) labels these areas 'critical' or 'over-exploited'.

3. “Bottled water is purer than tap water.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Government-supplied tap water in Indian metros is filtered and treated to drinkable standards. Bottled water companies use the same source, sometimes just filter it more (or sometimes not), and charge 100× more. Many bottled brands have failed BIS tests in independent surveys. The big problem with bottled water isn't purity — it's PLASTIC waste. India consumes ~25 billion plastic bottles per year. A water filter at home + steel bottle is cheaper, purer, and zero-plastic.

4. “Watering plants in the morning vs evening doesn't matter.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: EVENING/early-morning watering is MUCH better. In the heat of midday, water evaporates faster than plants can absorb — wasted. Watering in cool morning (before 9 am) or evening (after 5 pm) gives plants time to absorb. Saves ~40% water for the same plant growth. Drip irrigation in farms is usually timed for cool hours.

5. “A leaky tap loses just a few drops — not a problem.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: One drip per second from a tap = ~5 litres per day = 1825 litres per year. A leaky cistern in your bathroom can waste 20+ litres per day. A whole apartment building with multiple leaks: tens of thousands of litres wasted yearly. Fixing a tap washer (₹10) saves more than running a tap for a month. Tiny leaks scale up enormously when you count households and time.

📝 Stretch your thinking

Pick any two of these and explain them to a younger sibling using a one-line analogy each. If you can compress a mistake into one sentence with one image, you have understood it.
