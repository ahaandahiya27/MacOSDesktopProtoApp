How Much Drinkable Water Earth Actually Has

Why 'water everywhere' doesn't mean 'water to drink' · Class 7 Science

The shocking numbers

Earth looks blue from space because more than 70% of its surface is water. So why is water scarcity a global crisis?

Of all the water on Earth:
  • 97.5% is salty ocean water — undrinkable without expensive desalination.
  • 1.7% is frozen in polar ice caps and glaciers — unreachable.
  • 0.8% is groundwater — hidden under the soil in aquifers.
  • 0.01% is the surface fresh water (lakes, rivers, swamps, atmosphere) we can easily use.

Put another way

If all of Earth's water fit in a 1-litre jug, only 30 mL would be fresh water — about two tablespoons. Of that, only ~0.6 mL would be the surface water in rivers and lakes that we can easily reach. The other 29 mL is frozen or buried deep underground.

Where freshwater lives

  • Glaciers and polar ice: The biggest freshwater storage. Greenland and Antarctica alone hold ~70% of all fresh water — frozen.
  • Groundwater: Stored in aquifers (saturated rock layers underground). The Indo-Gangetic plain has one of the world's largest aquifer systems, but it's being depleted faster than rain can refill it.
  • Lakes and rivers: A tiny fraction of total water — but it's the part we depend on the most for agriculture and drinking.
  • Soil moisture and atmosphere: Always being recycled through the water cycle.

Why this matters in India

India has 18% of the world's population but only 4% of its freshwater. About 600 million Indians already face high to extreme water stress. Cities like Bengaluru, Chennai, and Delhi have come within weeks of running out of water during peak summer drought years. Punjab and Haryana's wheat-and-rice belt is pumping groundwater faster than the monsoon can replenish.

Try this at home

Measure exactly 1 litre of water in a jug — call this 'all of Earth's water'. Now carefully pour out 30 mL (six teaspoons) into a glass — that's the freshwater. From that 30 mL, isolate just 0.6 mL (a tiny drop) — that's the surface water humanity directly uses. Look at the ratio. That single picture should change how you turn a tap on tomorrow.

Looking ahead

Class 11 Geography goes into the hydrological cycle quantitatively. Class 12 Civics covers UN Sustainable Development Goal 6 (Clean Water and Sanitation for All by 2030) and India's progress toward it. The Jal Jeevan Mission (launched 2019, aims at piped water to every rural home by 2024) is a focus area in CBSE board questions.
