Q1. Mark T (true) or F (false): (a) Water vapour gets into air by evaporation only. (b) The water table does not get affected by the depth of a well. (c) Rain is the only source of fresh water on Earth. (d) Water shortage is a problem faced only by people living in rural areas.

Model answer: (a) F — water vapour gets into air also by transpiration (from plants). (b) F — wells drilled below the water table give water; above it, no water. Depth matters. (c) F — fresh water also comes from glaciers, springs, rivers (which originate from springs and rain together). (d) F — urban areas like Chennai 2019 face worse shortages because dense populations need huge water.

Q2. Explain how groundwater is recharged.

Model answer: Rainwater that falls on porous land surfaces (soil, sand, broken rock) doesn't flow away — it INFILTRATES into the ground. Drop by drop, it works through tiny gaps between soil particles, eventually reaching an underground 'aquifer' (a layer of water-holding rock or sand). The water table is the top of this saturated zone. Over months and years, billions of raindrops accumulate. Recharge is FAST in sandy/gravelly areas, SLOW in clay. Recharge is BLOCKED in concrete-paved cities (water runs off into drains, not into ground). Rainwater harvesting systems deliberately route runoff into recharge pits to feed groundwater.

Q3. There are ten tubewells in a lane of fifty houses. What could be the long term impact on the water table?

Model answer: Ten tubewells pumping continuously will withdraw water faster than rain can recharge the local aquifer. Over years, the water table will DROP — first by metres, then by tens of metres. Each tubewell will need to be drilled deeper periodically. Eventually, some tubewells will run dry. Neighbouring lanes' tubewells will also be affected because groundwater connects across underground rock. This is exactly what happened in Punjab, Rajasthan, and parts of Tamil Nadu. The solution: rainwater harvesting in every house + reducing per-house consumption + government licensing of tubewells.

Q4. You have been asked to maintain a garden. How will you minimise the use of water?

Model answer: Several techniques: (1) DRIP IRRIGATION — install thin pipes that drip water directly to root zones, not on leaves. (2) MULCHING — cover soil around plants with dry leaves or grass clippings; reduces evaporation from soil by ~60%. (3) Water EARLY MORNING or EVENING, never noon. (4) Choose drought-resistant plants (succulents, neem, peepal) over thirsty ones (lawns, exotic ferns). (5) Collect ROOFTOP rainwater in a tank for garden use. (6) Reuse GREYWATER (from rinsing vegetables, used drinking water) on plants. With all six, garden water use can drop by 70%.

Q5. Explain the factors responsible for the depletion of water table.

Model answer: (1) OVER-EXTRACTION — too many tubewells pumping for irrigation, industry, and homes. (2) INCREASED POPULATION — more demand per square km. (3) CITY CONCRETE — paving stops rainwater from soaking into ground; runoff flows into drains and out to sea unused. (4) DEFORESTATION — trees slow rain runoff and let it soak in; clear-cut land loses water fast. (5) CLIMATE CHANGE — irregular monsoons mean drought years aren't compensated by extra rainfall in others. (6) Industrial DUMPING that contaminates the aquifer, making it unusable even when water is present.

Q6. Fill in the blanks. (a) People obtain groundwater through ______ and ______. (b) Three forms of water are ______, ______, and ______. (c) The water bearing layer of the earth is ______. (d) The process of water seepage into the ground is called ______.

Model answer: (a) tubewells and hand-pumps (b) solid (ice), liquid (water), and gas (vapour) (c) aquifer (d) infiltration.

Q7. Tick the correct answer. (a) The water cycle does not involve which of the following — Evaporation, Condensation, Formation of clouds, Photosynthesis? (b) Which one is suitable for absorbing rainwater? — Concrete road, Tarred road, Forest, Marble floor.

Model answer: (a) Photosynthesis. (Water is USED in photosynthesis, but the water CYCLE is about evaporation, condensation, precipitation, and infiltration. Photosynthesis takes water out of the cycle into plants.) (b) Forest — soft soil and tree roots make it the best at absorbing rainwater. Concrete, tar, and marble all REPEL water.

Q8. Saving water and recharging the groundwater are everyone's responsibility. Discuss with your parents and elders, what can be done in your house every day to save water. Prepare a list.

Model answer: Sample list: (1) Take shorter showers (bucket bath uses 25 L; long shower uses 100+ L). (2) Fix leaky taps within a day. (3) Turn off tap while brushing teeth. (4) Reuse vegetable-rinsing water for plants. (5) Use a washing machine on full load only. (6) Use mop water for floor cleaning. (7) Install dual-flush in toilets (saves 50% per flush). (8) Collect AC condensate water for plants. (9) Install rooftop rainwater harvesting if owning a house. (10) Fix any roof seepage into a recharge pit. Small actions × millions of households = saving rivers.
