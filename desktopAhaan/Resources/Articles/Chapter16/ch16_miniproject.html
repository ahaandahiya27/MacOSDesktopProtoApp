Two Mini Projects for Chapter 16

Hands-on activities you can do today — with materials from the kitchen, not the lab. Each project takes about an hour and turns chapter ideas into something you can see and touch.

≈ 60 min total · hands-on

Reading is not the same as doing. These mini projects ask you to use your hands — predict, observe, and write down what surprises you. Try at least one before the next chapter test.

💧 Make a mini water cycle in a sealed bag

~4320 min · one-sitting project

What you'll need

  • A zip-lock plastic bag
  • Water
  • A few drops of blue food colour (optional, easier to see)
  • Tape
  • A sunny window

Steps

  1. Pour 100 ml of water into the bag.
  2. Add 2 drops of blue food colour.
  3. Seal the bag tightly.
  4. Tape it to a sunny window — outside or inside, with sun streaming in.
  5. Observe for 1-3 days.

👀 What you should see

Water in the bottom of the bag slowly evaporates. Tiny droplets form on the upper inside of the bag (condensation). After enough form, they drip down (precipitation). Cycle continues. The same water keeps cycling — it can never escape the sealed bag, just like water on Earth.

Why it works: The bag is a closed system, like Earth's atmosphere. Sun's heat evaporates water from the surface; vapour rises in the bag; cooler top of bag causes vapour to condense back to droplets; gravity brings droplets back down. Earth's water cycle scaled to fit in a bag. Powerful conservation lesson: there is no NEW water; we're recycling the same water humans have used for centuries.

🚰 Conduct a 3-day household water audit

~180 min · one-sitting project

What you'll need

  • A pen and paper
  • A measuring jug (1 L)
  • A timer
  • Family cooperation

Steps

  1. Day 1: Measure how much each person uses in different activities. Bath bucket = 25 L. Shower = use timer + count, ~10 L/min. Brush teeth = ~1 L tap running. Flush toilet = 6 L (full) or 3 L (dual). Wash hands = ~2 L. Dishes = ~25 L. Laundry = ~70 L.
  2. Day 2: Repeat with everyone CONSCIOUS of water use.
  3. Day 3: Introduce changes — close tap while brushing, use bucket for bath, etc.
  4. Calculate per-person daily use across 3 days.
  5. Make a chart.

👀 What you should see

Day 1 average: ~150 L/person/day (Indian urban average ~150 L). Day 2: ~120 L (just awareness saves ~20%). Day 3: ~80 L (active changes save another ~30%). Family of 4 saves ~80,000 L/year. Multiply across India = saving rivers.

Why it works: You can't manage what you don't measure. The audit makes the invisible visible. The 3-day pattern shows the power of awareness alone — and then deliberate change. This is the basis of every water-saving campaign worldwide. Real numbers, real reductions, real impact.

📝 How to use this

Before you start, predict what will happen. Write your prediction down. Then do the project. Compare. If your prediction was wrong, write down why — that's where real learning happens.
