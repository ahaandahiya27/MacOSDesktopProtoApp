Every chapter has a person behind it — someone who first asked the right question, ran the right experiment, or wrote down the answer. This is a one-page portrait of the scientist most tied to this chapter's content.

Rajendra Singh

1959–present · Indian

🎧 In one line

'Waterman of India' — revived 7 dead rivers in Rajasthan and brought groundwater back to 1200 villages through community rainwater harvesting.

Rajendra Singh was born in 1959 in Daula village, Uttar Pradesh. Trained as an Ayurvedic doctor, he took a posting in Alwar, Rajasthan in 1985 — a region of failed agriculture, dry tubewells, men migrating to cities, women walking 5 km a day for water. Villagers told him: 'We don't need medicine; we need water.' He started by helping build a small JOHAD (earthen check dam) in Gopalpura village. By blocking monsoon runoff, the johad allowed rain to soak into the ground. Within 3 years, dried-up wells in the village began giving water again. Singh and the NGO he founded (Tarun Bharat Sangh) built thousands of johads across Alwar district. Seven completely DRIED-UP rivers — Arvari, Ruparel, Jahajwali, Sarsa, Bhagani, Sabi, Maheshwara — started flowing again after 50+ years. The methodology: zero government grants in many villages, ALL community-built, traditional knowledge revived. Singh won the Magsaysay Award (2001), Stockholm Water Prize (2015 — water's equivalent of the Nobel), and is mentioned in UN climate reports. Singh proves that India's water crisis isn't inevitable; it's reversible — but only if communities, not just governments, take the lead. The chapter on Water you read in Class 7 — every fact in it has a counter-example in Alwar today.
