Story Mode — Connecting the Dots in Real Life

The same ideas from this chapter, but out in the world — in shops, on playgrounds, and in everyday choices.

≈ 6 min · see-it-everywhere level

Maths can feel like it lives only inside the textbook. It doesn't. Here are real situations where the ideas of "Connecting the Dots" quietly do the work.

Mean vs median income

A village's 'average income' as the MEAN can look high if one family is very rich — that big value pulls the mean up. The MEDIAN (middle income) better reflects a typical family. Which 'average' is quoted can change the whole story.

A batter's run-rate

To compare two cricketers who played different numbers of matches, compare the MEAN runs per match, not totals. That's why commentators quote batting averages — the average adjusts for how many innings each had.

Misleading bar graphs

Advertisers sometimes draw bar graphs whose axis starts above zero, making a tiny lead look huge. Checking the scale before believing the bars is a real-world defence against being fooled.
