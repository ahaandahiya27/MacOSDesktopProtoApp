Common Mistakes in Connecting the Dots

The places students slip on this chapter — and the one-line fix for each.

&#8776; 5 min read &middot; revision-tier &middot; 5 fixes

These wrong answers come back year after year. None are silly — they are almost right, which is what makes them worth knowing. Read each, then cover the fix and try to spot what is off.

1. “The bigger total always means better performance.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: Only if the groups are the same size. With different match counts, compare the MEAN, not the total.

2. “Mean, median and mode are all 'the average'.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: Three different summaries: mean = total÷count, median = middle, mode = most frequent. They can differ greatly.

3. “You can find the median without sorting.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: You MUST sort first; the median is the middle of the SORTED list.

4. “The mean is always the best 'typical' value.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: One outlier can pull the mean far from typical; the median is often better then.

5. “A taller bar always means a much bigger value.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: Check the SCALE — if the axis doesn't start at zero, small differences look huge. Read the scale, not just the shape.
