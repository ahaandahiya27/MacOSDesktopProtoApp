📊 Mini Project — Class Data Detective

A hands-on activity that turns this chapter into something you actually do, not just read.

≈ 45 min · do-it-yourself level

Reading about maths and doing maths are two different things. This short project lets you see the ideas of "Connecting the Dots" at work with your own hands.

What you'll need

  • A survey question (e.g. shoe size, hours slept)
  • Classmates or family as the sample
  • Graph paper

Steps

  1. Pick a question and collect a number from 8-10 people.
  2. Compute the MEAN (total÷count), MEDIAN (sort, middle), and MODE (most frequent).
  3. Notice how the three compare — does an outlier pull the mean?
  4. Draw a bar graph, choosing a scale so the tallest bar fits.
  5. Write one honest statistical statement your data supports, and one it does NOT.

🔍 What you should notice

Mean, median and mode can differ — especially if one unusual value pulls the mean. The bar graph makes the data's shape visible at a glance.

Why it works

Each representative value compresses the data differently: mean shares the total, median finds the middle, mode finds the most common. Real data rarely makes them identical, which is why choosing the right summary (and reading the scale) matters.
