Connecting the Dots: Worked NCERT Questions

Textbook questions from this chapter, each with a clear worked answer.

These are the kind of questions the NCERT chapter asks. Try each one yourself first, then check the worked answer.

Q1. Two friends are 5 ft and 6 ft tall. Guessing their genders from height — is this certain?

Answer: No, it is statistical thinking: based on the pattern that men are more often taller, we guess the 6-foot person is male, but it is only likely, not certain.

Q2. Shubman scored 0,17,21,90 and Yashasvi 67,55,18,35. Compare by total and consistency.

Answer: Totals: Shubman 128, Yashasvi 175 — Yashasvi more overall. But Shubman had the highest single score (90); Yashasvi was more consistent. 'Better' depends on what you value.

Q3. Find the mean of 0, 17, 21, 90.

Answer: Mean = (0+17+21+90) ÷ 4 = 128 ÷ 4 = 32 runs per match.

Q4. Find the median of 0, 17, 21, 90.

Answer: Sorted, with even count 4 the median is the average of the two middle values: (17+21) ÷ 2 = 19.

Q5. Find the mode of 5, 3, 5, 7, 5, 8, 3.

Answer: 5 occurs three times, more than any other, so the mode is 5.

Q6. Shubman: 5 matches total 110; Yashasvi: 4 matches total 96. Compare fairly.

Answer: Compare means: 110 ÷ 5 = 22 vs 96 ÷ 4 = 24. By mean, Yashasvi did slightly better, though Shubman's total is higher.

Q7. Why might the median be better than the mean when there is an outlier?

Answer: The mean uses every value, so one extreme outlier pulls it from the typical. The median is the middle value and ignores how extreme the ends are, staying closer to typical.

Q8. On a bar graph with 1 cm = 10 runs, how tall is the bar for 75 runs, and what does a 4.5 cm bar mean?

Answer: 75 ÷ 10 = 7.5 cm tall. A 4.5 cm bar = 4.5 × 10 = 45 runs.
