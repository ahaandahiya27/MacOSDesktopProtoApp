Textbook questions from this chapter, each with a clear worked answer.

These are the kind of questions the NCERT chapter asks. Try each one yourself first, then check the worked answer.

Q1. What does it mean for two figures to be congruent?

Answer: They have the same shape AND the same size, so one can be placed exactly on top of the other (by sliding, turning, or flipping). Congruence is written with the symbol ≅.

Q2. Meera says the three side lengths are enough to make a triangle congruent to a given one, with no angles needed. Is she right?

Answer: Yes. By the SSS criterion, three equal sides determine a triangle uniquely up to congruence. Constructing with the three lengths gives exactly one triangle shape.

Q3. Why don't two sides alone determine a unique triangle?

Answer: Drawing the base and swinging arcs of the two given side-lengths from its ends produces two intersection points (one above and one below the base), giving two different triangles. So two sides are not enough.

Q4. List the valid congruence criteria for triangles.

Answer: SSS (three sides), SAS (two sides and the included angle), ASA (two angles and the included side), and RHS (right angle, hypotenuse, one side). SSA is NOT a valid criterion.

Q5. Two triangles have two pairs of equal sides with equal included angles. Are they congruent? By which rule?

Answer: Yes, by SAS (Side-Angle-Side). Two sides and the angle between them being equal forces the triangles to be congruent.

Q6. Prove that the base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal.

Answer: In ∆ABC with AB = AC, drop the altitude AD to BC. Then AB = AC, ∠ADB = ∠ADC = 90°, and AD is common, so ∆ADB ≅ ∆ADC by RHS. Hence ∠B = ∠C by CPCT — the angles opposite the equal sides are equal.

Q7. In isosceles ∆ABC with AB = AC and ∠A = 80°, find ∠B and ∠C.

Answer: ∠B = ∠C (equal sides, equal opposite angles). Their sum is 180 − 80 = 100°, so each is 50°.

Q8. What are the angles of an equilateral triangle, and why?

Answer: Each angle is 60°. All sides are equal, so all angles are equal; three equal angles summing to 180° give 180 ÷ 3 = 60°.
