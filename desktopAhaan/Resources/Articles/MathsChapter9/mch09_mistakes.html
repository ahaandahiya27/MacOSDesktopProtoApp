Common Mistakes in Geometric Twins

The places students slip on this chapter — and the one-line fix for each.

&#8776; 5 min read &middot; revision-tier &middot; 5 fixes

These wrong answers come back year after year. None are silly — they are almost right, which is what makes them worth knowing. Read each, then cover the fix and try to spot what is off.

1. “Figures of the same shape are congruent.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: They must also be the same SIZE. Same shape but different size means similar, not congruent.

2. “Two sides and any angle prove congruence.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: Only if the angle is INCLUDED (between the sides) — that's SAS. A non-included angle (SSA) is ambiguous and does NOT prove congruence.

3. “Three equal angles (AAA) make triangles congruent.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: AAA gives the same SHAPE (similar) but the sizes can differ. You need a side too.

4. “The base angles of an isosceles triangle just happen to look equal.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: They are PROVABLY equal — dropping the altitude makes two congruent (RHS) halves, forcing the base angles to match.

5. “An equilateral triangle's angles are 90° or 360÷3.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: They are 60° each: three equal angles summing to 180° give 180 ÷ 3 = 60°.
