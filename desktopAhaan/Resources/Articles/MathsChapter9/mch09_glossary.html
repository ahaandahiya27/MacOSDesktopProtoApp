Geometric Twins: Key Words

Every term in this chapter, with a one-line meaning and an example.

&#8776; quick reference &middot; 10 terms

Knowing the exact words makes the maths click. Here is every key term from the chapter, defined plainly with an example you can picture.

Congruent

Having the same shape and size; one figure fits exactly on the other. Symbol ≅.

Example: ∆ABC ≅ ∆DEF.

In Hindi: सर्वांगसम

Similar

Same shape but possibly different size (not necessarily congruent).

Example: A photo and its 150% enlargement are similar.

In Hindi: समरूप

SSS criterion

Two triangles are congruent if all three pairs of sides are equal.

Example: 4-6-8 ≅ 4-6-8.

SAS criterion

Congruent if two sides and the included angle are equal.

Example: 5, 7 with 60° between them.

ASA criterion

Congruent if two angles and the included side are equal.

Example: 40°, 60° with the side between them.

RHS criterion

For right triangles: congruent if the right angle, hypotenuse and one side match.

Example: Used to split an isosceles triangle.

Included angle

The angle between two named sides.

Example: In SAS, the angle between the two given sides.

CPCT

Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles (are equal) — used after proving congruence.

Example: ∆ADB ≅ ∆ADC ⇒ ∠B = ∠C by CPCT.

Isosceles triangle

A triangle with two equal sides; the angles opposite them are equal.

Example: AB = AC ⇒ ∠B = ∠C.

In Hindi: समद्विबाहु

Ambiguous case (SSA)

Two sides and a non-included angle do NOT determine a unique triangle.

Example: Two triangles fit the same SSA data.
