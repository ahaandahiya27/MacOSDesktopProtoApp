"What If?" — Parallel and Intersecting Lines

Hypothetical questions with no single right answer. Think hard before you read the discussion.

≈ 7 min read · brain-stretcher level

The textbook tells you what is. The best way to really understand an idea is to ask what if? Pick a question below, pause and think for a minute, then read one possible discussion.

What if 1 — parallel lines secretly met far away?

The question: What if two 'parallel' rails actually joined a million kilometres away?

🧠 Think first

Would they still be parallel?

One discussion path: By definition, parallel lines stay the same distance apart forever and never meet. Lines that eventually join are not parallel — they are intersecting lines we just haven't followed far enough.

What if 2 — there were no straight lines?

The question: What if every line in the world had to curve?

🧠 Think first

Could you still have parallel lines? Triangles?

One discussion path: On a curved surface, like a globe, 'straightest possible' lines behave strangely — they can start parallel and still meet. This is exactly the world of non-Euclidean geometry, and it describes the real, round Earth.

What if 3 — every crossing met at the same angle?

The question: What if all intersecting lines crossed at exactly 90°?

🧠 Think first

What would streets and tiles look like?

One discussion path: Every junction would be a perfect right angle — neat, but rigid. The angles formed when lines cross (and the equal 'vertically opposite' pairs) are what give designers freedom to build slanted, varied shapes.

📝 Stretch your thinking

None of these has one 'correct' answer — they are for arguing about. Try one on a friend or a parent and see whether their discussion path differs from yours.
