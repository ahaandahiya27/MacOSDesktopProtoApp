📐 Mini Project — Paper-Fold Parallel & Perpendicular Hunt

A hands-on activity that turns this chapter into something you actually do, not just read.

≈ 40 min · do-it-yourself level

Reading about maths and doing maths are two different things. This short project lets you see the ideas of "Parallel and Intersecting Lines" at work with your own hands.

What you'll need

  • A square sheet of paper (or newspaper)
  • A pencil and a scale
  • A protractor (optional)

Steps

  1. Fold the sheet in half horizontally and draw a line along the crease.
  2. Fold again and draw another crease-line. How many parallel lines do you have now?
  3. Make a vertical fold and draw it — how does it relate to the horizontal lines?
  4. Now draw any slanted line right across all your parallel lines (a transversal).
  5. Mark and measure a pair of corresponding angles and a pair of alternate angles. Are they equal?

🔍 What you should notice

All the horizontal creases are parallel; the vertical crease is perpendicular to them; and the slanted transversal makes equal corresponding angles and equal alternate angles at every parallel line.

Why it works

Folding produces creases in fixed directions: repeated parallel folds stay the same direction (parallel), and a perpendicular fold turns 90°. A transversal meets equally-directed (parallel) lines at the same tilt, so corresponding and alternate angles come out equal — exactly the chapter's theorems, seen by hand.
