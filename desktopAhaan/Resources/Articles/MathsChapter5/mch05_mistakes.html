Common Mistakes in Parallel and Intersecting Lines

The places students slip on this chapter — and the one-line fix for each.

&#8776; 5 min read &middot; revision-tier &middot; 5 fixes

These wrong answers come back year after year. None are silly — they are almost right, which is what makes them worth knowing. Read each, then cover the fix and try to spot what is off.

1. “All four angles at a crossing are equal.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: Only if the lines are perpendicular. In general there are two pairs: vertically opposite angles are equal, and adjacent angles sum to 180°.

2. “Corresponding/alternate angles are always equal, even for non-parallel lines.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: They are equal ONLY when the two crossed lines are parallel. For non-parallel lines they differ.

3. “Co-interior (same-side interior) angles are equal.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: They are SUPPLEMENTARY — they sum to 180° (when lines are parallel), not equal.

4. “Parallel lines must be horizontal or vertical.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: Parallel lines can run in any direction — they just need the same direction and never meet. Two slanted lines can be parallel.

5. “You must measure with a protractor to find unknown angles.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: You can DEDUCE them: vertically opposite, corresponding and alternate equalities plus linear pairs let you find every angle from one given angle.
