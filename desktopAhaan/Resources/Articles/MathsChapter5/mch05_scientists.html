Mathematics was built by real people, often centuries apart, each adding one idea. Here are the minds behind the ideas in this chapter.

Euclid (c. 300 BCE)

Greek (Alexandria). Wrote The Elements, the geometry textbook used for over 2,000 years.

Euclid taught in Alexandria and organised the geometry of his time into The Elements, building every result from a few starting rules. His famous 'parallel postulate' — about when two lines eventually meet — was so subtle that mathematicians spent more than two thousand years trying, and failing, to prove it from the others.
