Parallel and Intersecting Lines: Key Words

Every term in this chapter, with a one-line meaning and an example.

&#8776; quick reference &middot; 10 terms

Knowing the exact words makes the maths click. Here is every key term from the chapter, defined plainly with an example you can picture.

Intersecting lines

Two lines that meet at a point.

Example: An X shape is made of two intersecting lines.

Vertically opposite angles

The pair of equal angles directly across from each other at an intersection.

Example: At an X, the two 'across' angles are equal.

Linear pair

Two adjacent angles on a straight line; they sum to 180°.

Example: 120° and 60° on a straight line.

Perpendicular lines

Two lines that intersect at right angles (90°).

Example: The corner of a page; written l ⊥ m.

In Hindi: लंब रेखाएँ

Parallel lines

Coplanar lines that never meet, however far extended.

Example: Railway tracks; written l ∥ m.

In Hindi: समांतर रेखाएँ

Transversal

A line that crosses two or more other lines at distinct points.

Example: A rail crossing parallel sleepers.

In Hindi: तिर्यक रेखा

Corresponding angles

Angles in matching positions at the two crossings of a transversal; equal when the lines are parallel.

Example: ∠1 and ∠5 in the F-pattern.

Alternate angles

Angles on opposite sides of the transversal, between the lines; equal when the lines are parallel.

Example: ∠d and ∠f in the Z-pattern.

Co-interior angles

Same-side interior angles between two lines cut by a transversal; sum to 180° when the lines are parallel.

Example: 65° and 115° on the same side.

Right angle

An angle of exactly 90°, a quarter of a full turn.

Example: The corner of a square.

In Hindi: समकोण
