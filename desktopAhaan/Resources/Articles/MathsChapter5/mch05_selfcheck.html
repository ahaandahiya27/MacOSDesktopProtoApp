Question 1 — The warm-up

Two lines intersect and ∠a = 120°. Find ∠b, ∠c and ∠d.

🧠 Answer in your head first

Work it out before you read on — no peeking.

Answer: ∠c (vertically opposite a) = 120°. ∠b and ∠d each form a linear pair with a: 180 − 120 = 60°. So ∠b = ∠d = 60°, ∠c = 120°.

Question 2 — The check

If two intersecting lines form four equal angles, what is each angle?

🧠 Answer in your head first

Work it out before you read on — no peeking.

Answer: Each is 90°. The four angles sum to 360°, and being equal, each is 360 ÷ 4 = 90°. The lines are perpendicular.

Textbook page 108.

Question 3 — The stretch

How would you describe the opposite edges and adjacent edges of a square sheet of paper?

🧠 Answer in your head first

Work it out before you read on — no peeking.

Answer: Opposite edges are parallel (never meet); adjacent edges are perpendicular (meet at 90° at the corners).

Question 4 — The trap

When a transversal crosses two lines, how many angles form, and how many distinct measures are possible?

🧠 Answer in your head first

Work it out before you read on — no peeking.

Answer: 8 angles form (4 at each crossing). Because vertically opposite angles are equal, there are at most 4 distinct measures — and exactly 2 when the two lines are parallel.

Question 5 — The finisher

In Fig. 5.14, ∠1 and ∠5 are corresponding angles. If the lines are parallel and ∠1 = 70°, what is ∠5?

🧠 Answer in your head first

Work it out before you read on — no peeking.

Answer: ∠5 = 70°. Corresponding angles are equal when the two lines cut by the transversal are parallel.
