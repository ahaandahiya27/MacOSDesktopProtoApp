"What If?" — वन्दे भारतमातरम्

Hypothetical questions with no single right answer. Think hard before you read the discussion.

≈ 7 min read · brain-stretcher level

The textbook teaches what IS. Real understanding comes from asking what IF? Pick a question below, pause and think for a minute, then read one possible discussion.

What if 1 — the song had been written in pure Bangla, with no Sanskrit opening?

The question: Bankim Chandra's verse mixes Sanskrit (opening) and Bangla (later verses). What if he had used only Bangla?

🧠 Think first

Would the song still feel like a national anthem to non-Bangla-speakers?

One discussion path: The Sanskrit opening gave the song a pan-Indian solemnity — Sanskrit is no one's mother tongue, but everyone's heritage tongue. Pure Bangla would have made it a regional hymn. The bilingual choice is part of why it became national.

What if 2 — the song had stayed a standalone poem and never gone into a novel?

The question: Bankim placed Vande Mātaram inside Anandamath (1882). Suppose he had published it as just a poem.

🧠 Think first

How does the medium — novel vs poem — change how a piece of writing travels?

One discussion path: A novel was the medium of mass reading in 19th-century Bengal. A standalone poem might have stayed in literary anthologies; the novel carried the song to households and freedom-fighter circles. The container shaped the reach.

What if 3 — a different freedom-era song had become India's National Song?

The question: Several songs competed for the role — सारे जहाँ से अच्छा, झंडा ऊँचा रहे हमारा, etc. Suppose one of those had been chosen.

🧠 Think first

Would Sanskrit's place in India's public ceremonies be different today?

One discussion path: Vande Mātaram's Sanskrit opening keeps Sanskrit at the centre of every Republic Day. Another song's selection would have moved that centre. Languages survive partly through the rituals attached to them.

📝 Stretch your thinking

None of these has one 'correct' answer — they are for arguing about. Try one on a friend or a parent and see if their discussion path differs from yours.
