The 17 terms to know

  • Vasudhaiva kutumbakam · वसुधैव कुटुम्बकम् — A Sanskrit phrase meaning 'the whole world is one family'; the chapter's central value.
    Example: India welcomed many communities in the spirit of vasudhaiva kutumbakam.
  • Atithi devo bhava · अतिथि देवो भव — A Sanskrit saying meaning 'a guest is like God', expressing India's tradition of hospitality.
    Example: The Good Maharaja's care for Polish orphans showed atithi devo bhava.
  • Synagogue · यहूदी प्रार्थनागृह — A place of worship for Jews, where services and ceremonies are held.
    Example: The Cochin Jews built a synagogue on land granted by the Raja.
  • Persecution · उत्पीड़न — Hostility and ill-treatment, often arising from religious, ethnic, social or political motives.
    Example: Many communities came to India to escape persecution at home.
  • Zoroastrianism · पारसी धर्म — One of the world's oldest religions, founded by Zarathushtra, based on worship of one God (Ahura Mazda) and choosing good over evil.
    Example: The Parsis follow Zoroastrianism.
  • Heretic · विधर्मी — A person considered to hold beliefs (especially religious) contrary to those of the dominant view.
    Example: Iran's leaders branded the Baha'is as heretics.
  • Mappila · मापिला — The Muslim community of Kerala formed when Arab merchants settled and married locally.
    Example: The Arab traders became part of the Mappila community.
  • Siddi · सिद्दी — A community of African origin in India, brought enslaved between the 7th and 19th centuries; now a scheduled tribe.
    Example: The Siddis have a distinctive dance with African-style drumming.
  • Sowa Rigpa · सोवा रिग्पा — The Tibetan system of medicine ('Art of Healing'), now part of India's AYUSH programme.
    Example: Tibetan refugees brought Sowa Rigpa to India.
  • Refuge / asylum · शरण — Shelter or protection given to someone fleeing danger or persecution.
    Example: India granted asylum to the 14th Dalai Lama in 1959.
  • Scheduled tribe · अनुसूचित जनजाति — A community officially recognised by the Indian Constitution for special support and protection.
    Example: The Siddis are designated as a scheduled tribe.
  • Cosmopolitan · विश्वजनीन / सर्वदेशीय — At home with people of many cultures; from Greek kosmos ('world') + politēs ('citizen') — literally 'a citizen of the world'.
    Example: Trade made cities like Kochi cosmopolitan, home to many communities.
  • Pluralism · बहुलवाद — The peaceful coexistence of many different communities, faiths and cultures within one society; from Latin plus/pluris ('more').
    Example: India's pluralism lets many religions live side by side.
  • Ethos · आचार-भावना / लोकाचार — The characteristic spirit or guiding values of a people or place; from Greek ēthos ('character, custom').
    Example: India's ethos of acceptance welcomed newcomers for centuries.
  • Migration · प्रवास / प्रवजन — The movement of people to settle in a new land; from Latin migrare ('to move, wander'). To emigrate is to leave a country; to immigrate is to enter one.
    Example: Waves of migration brought traders and refugees to India's shores.
  • Tolerance · सहिष्णुता — Accepting and respecting beliefs and customs different from one's own; from Latin tolerare ('to bear, endure').
    Example: Religious tolerance let synagogues, churches and fire temples thrive together.
  • Synagogue · यहूदी प्रार्थनागृह — A Jewish house of worship and assembly; from Greek synagōgē ('a gathering together').
    Example: India's oldest synagogues are in Kerala.
