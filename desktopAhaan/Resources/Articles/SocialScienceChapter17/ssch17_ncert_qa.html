Q1. What has made India a home that enables people to assimilate here? (NCERT page 140)

India's culture of acceptance and inclusion, rooted in values like vasudhaiva kutumbakam ('the whole world is family'), atithi devo bhava ('a guest is like God') and sarve bhavantu sukhinah ('may all be happy'). These values, drawn from India's many schools of thought, made it a haven where persecuted communities could keep their traditions while becoming part of Indian society.

Q2. Why did the Parsis come to India, and what does the milk-and-sugar legend mean? (NCERT page 131)

The Parsis (Zoroastrians) fled persecution in Persia after the 7th-century Islamic conquest and reached Gujarat between the 8th and 10th centuries. In the legend, Raja Jadi Rana showed a jug full of milk (the kingdom was full); a wise Parsi dissolved sugar into it without spilling, meaning the Parsis would blend in and enrich the land without burdening it. The pleased king let them settle.

Q3. How were the early Arab merchants different from the later invaders? (NCERT page 132)

The Arab merchants came peacefully from the 7th century to trade on India's west coast, married locally, formed the Mappila community and helped build India's oldest mosque. They were traders, not conquerors - very different from the later Turkic invaders who came with armies to plunder or conquer.

Q4. How did India help the Tibetan refugees after 1959? (NCERT page 139)

India granted asylum to the 14th Dalai Lama (who lives in Dharamshala) and the Central Tibetan Administration. It rehabilitated the refugees, educated their children, and helped create Tibetan settlements and monasteries (e.g. Bylakuppe) so the community could preserve its language, culture and Buddhist faith; Tibetan medicine (Sowa Rigpa) became part of India's AYUSH programme.

Q5. What is the central value of this chapter and why is it relevant today? (NCERT page 140)

The central value is vasudhaiva kutumbakam, 'the whole world is one family' - the fundamental unity of humankind. It is relevant today because the world faces crises like wars, climate change, inequality and discrimination, which this ancient Indian value of acceptance and peaceful coexistence can help resolve.
