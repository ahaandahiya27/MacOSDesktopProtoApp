Reading teaches; doing sticks. Try this hands-on activity at home — it turns the chapter's idea into something you can see, build, or trace for yourself.

🗺️ A map of welcomed communities (about 40 min)

What you need

  • A blank map of India and the world
  • Coloured pens
  • String or arrows

Steps

  1. Mark where each community came FROM (Israel, West Asia, Persia, Arabia, Africa, Armenia, Poland, Tibet).
  2. Draw an arrow to where they settled in India (Konkan/Kochi, Malabar, Gujarat, west coast, Kolkata, Dharamshala, etc.).
  3. Colour 'refuge' arrows one colour and 'opportunity' arrows another.
  4. Write one line by each on what they brought to India.

👀 What you should see

You will see communities arriving from all directions and settling across India, with refuge-seekers and traders shown clearly apart.

🧠 Why it works

Mapping the journeys makes the chapter's idea of India as a 'home to many' visible and concrete.

🤝 Find a 'home to many' story near you (about 35 min)

What you need

  • A notebook
  • Help from family or neighbours

Steps

  1. Ask elders whether any community in your town or city came from far away long ago.
  2. Find out when they came, why, and what traditions they kept.
  3. Note one way they have become part of local life and one tradition they preserved.
  4. Share your story with the class to build a collection.

👀 What you should see

You will likely discover that even your own neighbourhood holds a story of migration, refuge or trade - and of identity kept while becoming Indian.

🧠 Why it works

It turns the chapter's big idea into something personal and real, showing acceptance is a living part of Indian life.
