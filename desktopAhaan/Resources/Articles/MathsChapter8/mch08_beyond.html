Beyond the Book — How Ancient Egypt Did Fractions

Fractions are about 4,000 years old, and the oldest way of writing them was beautifully strange.

≈ 6 min read · curious-kid level

The word fraction comes from the Latin fractus, meaning "broken" — a fraction is a broken-off piece of a whole. Humans have been breaking numbers into pieces for a very long time. Read one idea at a time.

1. Egypt's "unit fraction" rule

On the Rhind papyrus — a maths scroll copied around 1650 BCE — almost every fraction is written with a 1 on top: 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, and so on. These are called unit fractions. To write something like 3/4, an Egyptian scribe would add unit fractions together: 1/2 + 1/4.

💡 Try this

Write 3/4 the Egyptian way, as a sum of different unit fractions: 1/2 + 1/4. Now try 2/3 — you can do it as 1/2 + 1/6. Splitting a fraction into unit pieces is a real puzzle, and the Egyptians were experts at it.

2. Why a common denominator is the whole trick

You cannot add 1/2 and 1/3 directly — halves and thirds are different-sized pieces. So you cut both into the same size: sixths. Now 1/2 = 3/6 and 1/3 = 2/6, and the answer is 5/6. Every fraction addition is really this one idea: make the pieces the same size first.

3. "Of" means multiply

Half of a quarter of a pizza is a small slice — and "of" is the clue. 1/2 of 1/4 = 1/2 × 1/4 = 1/8. Multiplying fractions makes things smaller, which surprises people, because with whole numbers multiplying makes things bigger. Taking a part of a part always leaves you with less.

4. Dividing by flipping

"How many quarters fit into 3?" is the division 3 ÷ 1/4, and the answer is 12. Notice that this is the same as 3 × 4 — you flipped the 1/4 and multiplied. Dividing by a fraction means multiplying by its upside-down version, because you are asking how many of those small pieces fit inside.

💡 Try this

Without a calculator, work out how many 1/8 slices are in 2 whole pizzas. Then check by flipping: 2 ÷ 1/8 = 2 × 8 = 16. The flip-and-multiply rule is just counting how many pieces fit.
