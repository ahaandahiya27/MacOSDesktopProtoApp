🍫 Mini Project — Chocolate-Bar Fraction Lab

A hands-on activity that turns this chapter into something you actually do, not just read.

≈ 35 min · do-it-yourself level

Reading about maths and doing maths are two different things. This short project lets you see the ideas of "Working with Fractions" at work with your own hands.

What you'll need

  • A bar of chocolate or a sheet of paper marked in a grid
  • A ruler

Steps

  1. Take one whole bar (or paper rectangle). Cut/mark it into 3 equal parts — each is 1/3.
  2. Now halve ONE of those thirds. What fraction of the whole is that small piece? Confirm it is 1/6.
  3. Write the multiplication: 1/2 of 1/3 = 1/2 × 1/3 = 1/6.
  4. Now take a whole bar and see how many 1/4-pieces it gives: that's 1 ÷ 1/4 = 4.
  5. Predict, then check: how many 1/2-pieces are in 3 bars? (3 ÷ 1/2 = 6.)

🔍 What you should notice

Taking a part OF a part gives a smaller piece (multiplying by a fraction < 1 shrinks), while cutting a whole into small pieces gives MANY of them (dividing by a fraction < 1 grows the count).

Why it works

Multiplying fractions partitions the whole into a grid of (bottom×bottom) pieces and keeps (top×top) of them — a part of a part. Dividing asks how many divisor-pieces fit into the dividend, so small pieces yield a large count. The chocolate makes both rules tangible.
