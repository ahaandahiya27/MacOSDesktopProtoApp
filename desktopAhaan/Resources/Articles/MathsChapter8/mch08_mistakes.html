Common Mistakes in Working with Fractions

The places students slip on this chapter — and the one-line fix for each.

&#8776; 5 min read &middot; revision-tier &middot; 5 fixes

These wrong answers come back year after year. None are silly — they are almost right, which is what makes them worth knowing. Read each, then cover the fix and try to spot what is off.

1. “To multiply a whole number by a fraction, multiply both the numerator and denominator.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: Only multiply the numerator; keep the denominator. 3 × 1/4 = 3/4, not 3/12. The piece size doesn't change.

2. “Multiplying always makes a number bigger.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: Multiplying by a fraction LESS than 1 makes it smaller. 1/2 × 1/3 = 1/6, smaller than both.

3. “Dividing always makes a number smaller.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: Dividing by a number less than 1 makes it BIGGER. 2 ÷ 1/4 = 8, because eight quarter-pieces fit in 2.

4. “To divide by a fraction, flip the first fraction (the dividend).”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: You flip the DIVISOR (the one you're dividing BY) and multiply: 1 ÷ 2/3 = 1 × 3/2.

5. “'Half of a third' means 1/2 + 1/3.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: 'Of' means multiply: 1/2 of 1/3 = 1/2 × 1/3 = 1/6, not 1/2 + 1/3.
