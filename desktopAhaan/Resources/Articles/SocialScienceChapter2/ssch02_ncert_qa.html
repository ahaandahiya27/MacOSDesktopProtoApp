Q1. Match the instrument with the weather element it measures: Hygrometer, Anemometer, Barometer, Thermometer, Rain gauge. (NCERT page 42)

Hygrometer → Humidity; Anemometer → Wind direction and speed; Barometer → Atmospheric pressure; Thermometer → Temperature; Rain gauge → Precipitation (rainfall).

Q2. Jyotsna is deciding what clothes to pack for her school trip to Mumbai in June. The forecast predicts 29°C and 84% humidity. What would be your advice to her? (NCERT page 42)

At 29°C with 84% humidity, the weather in Mumbai will be warm and very sticky, with sweat drying slowly. I would advise her to pack light, loose, breathable cotton clothes that keep her cool and let sweat evaporate, carry a water bottle to stay hydrated, and bring an umbrella or raincoat because June is the start of Mumbai's monsoon, so heavy rain is likely. She should avoid heavy or woollen clothes, which would feel uncomfortable in the humid heat.

Q3. A small group of students is setting up a rain gauge. Which site should they choose — the school vegetable garden, the terrace of the school building, open ground with an elevated platform, the compound wall, or the verandah of the laboratory — and why? (NCERT page 42)

They should choose the open ground with an elevated platform. A rain gauge must be placed in an open area, away from buildings, walls and trees that could block the rain or create wind eddies and give a false reading. The terrace and verandah are near walls or roofs that obstruct rain; the compound wall and parts of the vegetable garden may also be sheltered or shaded. Open ground catches rain fairly, and the elevated platform keeps the gauge flat, stable and free from splashing, so the readings are accurate.

Q4. From the IMD chart for Jammu and Kashmir, how would you report the weather, covering temperature range, maximum and minimum temperatures, humidity and precipitation? (NCERT page 43)

Reading the chart, I would report each station's conditions: for example, Srinagar had a maximum of 6.5°C and a minimum of 0.2°C, giving a temperature range of about 6.3°C, with relative humidity of 89%, and only a trace of rainfall. Higher places like Gulmarg were colder, with the maximum below freezing (about −2.6°C) and snowfall recorded, while Qazigund saw both rain and snow. Overall, Jammu and Kashmir on that date was very cold, with sub-zero minimums in the hills, high humidity, and a mix of light rain and snow in several stations.
