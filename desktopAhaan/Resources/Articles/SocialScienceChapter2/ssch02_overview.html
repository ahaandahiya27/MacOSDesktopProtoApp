What Is Weather?

The meaning of weather, the atmosphere and its layers (especially the troposphere where weather happens), the five elements of weather, and why we need common standards to measure them.

  1. Weather and the AtmosphereWeather is the state of the Earth's atmosphere at a particular time and place.
  2. The Troposphere: Where Weather HappensAlmost all weather happens in the troposphere, the lowest layer of the atmosphere closest to the ground.
  3. The Five Elements of WeatherWeather is described by five elements: temperature, precipitation, atmospheric pressure, wind and humidity.

Temperature and Precipitation

How hot or cold the air is and how we measure it with a thermometer, the useful numbers we can calculate (range and mean), and the different forms of precipitation and how a rain gauge measures rainfall.

  1. Temperature and the ThermometerTemperature tells how hot or cold the atmosphere is, and it is measured with a thermometer.
  2. Temperature Statistics: Range and MeanFrom daily highs and lows we can calculate the temperature range and the mean daily temperature.
  3. Precipitation and the Rain GaugePrecipitation is any water that falls from the sky, and rainfall is measured with a rain gauge.

Atmospheric Pressure and Wind

The weight of the air and how it changes with altitude and forms depressions, how a barometer measures it, and how wind blows from high to low pressure and is measured by wind vanes and anemometers.

  1. Atmospheric Pressure and the BarometerAtmospheric pressure is the weight of the air above us, measured with a barometer in millibars.
  2. Wind: Moving AirWind is the movement of air from areas of high pressure to areas of low pressure, described by its speed and direction.
  3. Measuring Wind: Vanes, Wind Socks and AnemometersA wind vane shows wind direction, a wind sock helps pilots, and an anemometer measures wind speed.

Humidity and Reading Nature's Clues

What humidity is and how relative humidity is measured with a hygrometer, why it affects sweating and drying, and how people once forecast weather by observing animals and plants before meteorology took over.

  1. Humidity and the HygrometerHumidity is the amount of water vapour in the air, measured as relative humidity with a hygrometer.
  2. Reading Nature's CluesBefore modern instruments, people forecast weather by carefully watching animals and plants.
  3. Meteorology: The Science of WeatherMeteorology is the systematic study of weather and its evolution, and it is the basis of forecasting.

Weather Stations and Predicting the Weather

How weather stations and Automated Weather Stations bring all the instruments together, how meteorologists turn data into forecasts, and why accurate predictions help people prepare for floods, cyclones, droughts and heat waves.

  1. Weather Stations and Automated Weather StationsA weather station gathers all the weather instruments in one place; an Automated Weather Station records data by itself using sensors.
  2. Predicting the Weather and Why It MattersMeteorologists turn data into forecasts that warn us of dangerous weather so we can prepare and stay safe.
