History, geography and society are full of 'what if' turning points. Think about each question yourself first — then open the panel to compare your reasoning.

What if there were no atmosphere around the Earth?

Think first, then open to compare

With no atmosphere there would be no air, so there would be no weather at all — no wind, no clouds, no rain, no humidity, and no atmospheric pressure. There would be nothing to breathe, and nothing to trap the Sun's warmth, so the surface would be scorching hot in sunlight and freezing cold in shadow, swinging wildly between extremes (much like the Moon, which has almost no atmosphere). Life as we know it could not survive, because all weather and the very air we breathe depend on the atmosphere.

What if we had no way to forecast the weather?

Think first, then open to compare

Without forecasts we would be taken by surprise by every storm, flood, cyclone, drought and heat wave. Fishermen might sail straight into stormy seas; coastal villages would have no warning to evacuate before a cyclone; farmers could sow at the wrong time and lose their crops; and governments could not prepare resources for disasters. People would be left relying only on rough natural clues, which give little warning. Accurate forecasting, built on meteorology, is what gives us the precious time to prepare and stay safe — and it matters even more as climate change makes extreme weather more frequent.
