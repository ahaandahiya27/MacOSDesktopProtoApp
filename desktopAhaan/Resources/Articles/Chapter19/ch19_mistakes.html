Five Wrong Answers Class 7 Students Give

The five places almost every Class 7 student trips up on Chapter 19 — and the one-line fix for each.

≈ 5 min read · revision-tier

Examiners say the same wrong answers come back year after year. None of these mistakes are silly — they are almost right. That is exactly what makes them dangerous. Read each one, cover the answer, and try to spot what is off before peeking.

1. “Summer is hot because Earth is closer to the Sun.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Earth's orbit is nearly circular. Earth is actually FARTHEST from Sun in July (~152 million km) and CLOSEST in January (~147 million km) — just 3% difference. Seasons are caused by Earth's TILT (23.5°), not distance. When Northern Hemisphere is tilted toward Sun, it gets more direct sunlight and longer days — summer. When tilted away, less direct, shorter days — winter. Australia has opposite seasons because they're tilted opposite at any moment.

2. “Stars twinkle because they're alive / flickering.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Stars don't twinkle — Earth's atmosphere does. Light from a star (a tiny point in the sky) passes through layers of warmer and cooler air. Each layer bends the light slightly differently — so the apparent position and brightness fluctuate. That's twinkling. Planets, being LARGER apparent discs (closer to us), have many points of light averaging out — so they don't twinkle. Look at sky: planets (Venus, Mars, Jupiter) shine STEADY; stars (Sirius, Polaris) twinkle.

3. “The Moon doesn't rotate (because we always see one face).”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: The Moon DOES rotate — once every 27.3 days, which is exactly the same time it takes to orbit Earth. This is called 'tidal locking'. Because rotation and orbit are synchronised, the same face always points at Earth. We see only the 'near side'; the 'far side' was first photographed by Soviet Luna 3 in 1959. Chandrayaan-2 mapped it in detail. If the Moon didn't rotate, we'd see all 360° of its surface as it orbited.

4. “Pluto is a planet (or all planets are roughly equal).”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Pluto was DEMOTED to 'dwarf planet' in 2006 by the International Astronomical Union. The new definition of a planet requires: (1) orbiting the Sun, (2) being spherical, (3) having 'cleared its orbital neighbourhood' of other bodies. Pluto fails #3 — many other Kuiper Belt objects share its orbit. Earth, by contrast, has cleared its orbit (no comparable objects in Earth's path). Solar System has 8 planets, ~5 dwarf planets (including Pluto, Eris, Haumea, Makemake, Ceres).

5. “The Sun is a special star.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: The Sun is a VERY ORDINARY star. It's a yellow dwarf (G-type), middle-aged (~4.6 billion years old, halfway through its 10-billion-year life), modest mass. The galaxy has ~400 billion stars; the Sun is in the middle 50% by mass and luminosity. What's special isn't the Sun — it's our distance from it (right for liquid water) and our solar system's stability. The 'Goldilocks zone' is special, not the star.

📝 Stretch your thinking

Pick any two of these and explain them to a younger sibling using a one-line analogy each. If you can compress a mistake into one sentence with one image, you have understood it.
