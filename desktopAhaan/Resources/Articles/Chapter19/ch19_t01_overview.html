Earth's rotation, revolution, and seasons

8 concepts covering this topic in depth.

≈ 8 concepts · the topic at a glance

The Earth spins on its tilted axis once every 24 hours, giving us day and night, and orbits the Sun once every 365.25 days, giving us seasons. The tilt of the axis — not the distance from the Sun — is the real reason summer is hot and winter is cold.

Why this topic matters

The Earth spins on its tilted axis once every 24 hours, giving us day and night, and orbits the Sun once every 365.25 days, giving us seasons. The tilt of the axis — not the distance from the Sun — is the real reason summer is hot and winter is cold. Understanding these ideas connects classroom science to real phenomena you see every day — from sunrise to seasons to the changing Moon.
