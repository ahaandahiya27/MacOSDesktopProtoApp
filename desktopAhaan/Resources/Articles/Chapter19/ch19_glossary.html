A Class 7 Earth, Moon and the Sun Dictionary

The 10 must-know terms from Chapter 19 — in plain English plus the Hindi name, with a one-line meaning a Class 7 student can actually use.

The 10 terms to know

  • Rotation · घूर्णन — The spinning of a planet on its own axis.
    Example: Earth rotates once every 24 hours, causing day and night.
  • Revolution · परिक्रमण — The movement of a planet around its star (or moon around its planet).
    Example: Earth revolves around the Sun once every 365.25 days.
  • Axial tilt · अक्षीय झुकाव — The angle between Earth's rotation axis and the perpendicular to its orbital plane.
    Example: Earth's 23.5° tilt is what causes our seasons.
  • Phases of Moon · चंद्र कलाएँ — The changing shapes of the Moon as it orbits Earth (new, crescent, quarter, gibbous, full).
    Example: Diwali falls on the new moon; Holi falls on the full moon.
  • Eclipse · ग्रहण — An event where one celestial body blocks light from or to another.
    Example: Solar eclipse (Moon blocks Sun); lunar eclipse (Earth blocks Sun's light to Moon).
  • Tide · ज्वार — The regular rise and fall of sea level caused by the gravitational pull of the Moon (and Sun).
    Example: Mumbai's harbours have ~5 m tidal range; ships must time their movements with tides.
  • Constellation · नक्षत्र मंडल — A recognisable pattern of stars in the sky.
    Example: Orion the Hunter and Ursa Major (Saptarishi) are easily-spotted constellations.
  • Solar System · सौर मंडल — The Sun and all bodies (planets, moons, asteroids, comets) that orbit it.
    Example: Our Solar System has 8 planets and ~5 known dwarf planets.
  • Asteroid · क्षुद्र ग्रह — A rocky body smaller than a planet, mostly found in the asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter.
    Example: Ceres is the largest asteroid in our Solar System; it's also a dwarf planet.
  • Meteor / shooting star · उल्का — A small space rock that burns up in Earth's atmosphere, producing a streak of light.
    Example: Perseid meteor shower in August produces ~60 meteors per hour, visible from any dark Indian sky.
