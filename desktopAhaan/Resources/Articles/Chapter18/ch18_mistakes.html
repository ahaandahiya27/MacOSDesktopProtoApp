Five Wrong Answers Class 7 Students Give

The five places almost every Class 7 student trips up on Chapter 18 — and the one-line fix for each.

≈ 5 min read · revision-tier

Examiners say the same wrong answers come back year after year. None of these mistakes are silly — they are almost right. That is exactly what makes them dangerous. Read each one, cover the answer, and try to spot what is off before peeking.

1. “Flushed water disappears — no need to think about it.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: It goes through pipes to either a sewage treatment plant (cleaned), a septic tank (partial cleaning), or directly into a river/sea (untreated). In India, less than 50% of urban sewage is treated. The rest contaminates rivers, ponds, and groundwater. 'Out of sight' doesn't mean 'out of system'. Your flush is the start of a journey that may end on someone else's drinking water source.

2. “Pouring oil down the sink is fine.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Oil cools and SOLIDIFIES in pipes, especially in winter. Combined with other waste, it forms 'fatbergs' — huge blockages weighing tonnes. London famously removed a 250-tonne fatberg from sewers in 2017. Mumbai's monsoon waterlogging is partly fatbergs blocking drains. Correct disposal: pour cooled oil into a bottle, throw in dustbin (or use a separate oil-recycling collection — coming to Indian cities).

3. “Bottled water makes plastic toilets unnecessary.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Even with bottled water for drinking, you still need a toilet for waste. The problem is excrement, not water. India's open defecation problem affects 600+ million people historically and causes diarrhoea deaths in 150,000 children annually. Bottled water solves drinking; toilets solve sanitation — they're DIFFERENT problems.

4. “All sewage smells terrible — that's why it's bad.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Sewage SMELLS bad because anaerobic bacteria (working without oxygen) produce smelly compounds — hydrogen sulphide (rotten eggs), ammonia (urine smell), methane. Modern aerobic treatment uses AIR to support different (smelless) bacteria — and the treated water doesn't smell. Smell isn't the threat — pathogens are. Treated water can be odourless but contain cholera bacteria; untreated water can smell mild but be deadly.

5. “Just build more WWTPs to solve the problem.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: WWTPs are necessary BUT NOT SUFFICIENT. The Yamuna problem isn't just lack of plants — it's: (1) Existing plants operating at 60% capacity due to electricity shortages. (2) Plants not connected to all sewers (some sewage bypasses). (3) Industrial effluent that bypasses sewers entirely. (4) Plastic and trash choking screens. Plus people throwing waste directly into rivers. Holistic solution requires: WWTPs + capacity utilisation + industrial enforcement + behaviour change + plastic ban. Building alone won't help.

📝 Stretch your thinking

Pick any two of these and explain them to a younger sibling using a one-line analogy each. If you can compress a mistake into one sentence with one image, you have understood it.
