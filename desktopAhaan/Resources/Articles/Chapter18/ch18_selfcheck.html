Question 1 — from Chapter 18

What is sewage? List three components found in it.

🧠 Answer in your head first

Don't peek. Speak the answer out loud, then check.

Answer: Sewage is the dirty wastewater from homes, industries, and agriculture that flows through underground sewers. Three components: (1) Organic matter — food waste, faeces, plant matter. (2) Inorganic chemicals — soaps, detergents, fertilisers. (3) Pathogenic microbes — bacteria, viruses that cause diseases like cholera and typhoid.

Why: Define: sewage = liquid waste from homes, factories, farms.

Question 5 — from Chapter 18

Design a household composting system for a typical Indian urban flat. List the inputs, process, common problems, and outputs. Justify why this is a sanitation intervention, not just a gardening hobby.

🧠 Answer in your head first

Don't peek. Speak the answer out loud, then check.

Answer: Setup: two 20-litre buckets with drainage holes. Inputs: vegetable peels, fruit waste, tea leaves (greens — nitrogen) + dried leaves, paper, cardboard (browns — carbon). Ratio: ~3 browns : 1 green. Process: layer materials, sprinkle water weekly, turn fortnightly. Compost ready in 8-12 weeks. Outputs: ~10 kg of nutrient-rich soil amendment for plants, plus liquid leachate (collected from drainage) as plant feed. Common problems: too wet (smells — add browns), too dry (no decomposition — add water), pests (cover top — keeps flies out). Why sanitation intervention: 30-40% of a typical Indian household's waste is biodegradable. Composting REMOVES this stream from municipal collection → less landfill → less methane (composting at home produces less methane than anaerobic landfill) → reduced contamination of mixed waste. One household composting = 60-100 kg/year of waste diverted.

Why: Set up: two buckets, drainage, mix greens+browns.
