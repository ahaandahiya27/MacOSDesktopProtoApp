Bindeshwar Pathak

1943–2023 · Indian

🎧 In one line

Founder of Sulabh International — invented the twin-pit toilet that brought safe sanitation to millions and rehabilitated manual scavengers.

Bindeshwar Pathak was born in Hajipur, Bihar, in 1943. As a Brahmin growing up in upper-caste Bihar, he was shocked by the practice of MANUAL SCAVENGING — Dalits (then 'untouchables') were forced to clean human excreta from dry latrines with their bare hands, then carry it on their heads to disposal sites. The Indian Constitution had abolished untouchability in 1950 but the practice continued for lack of alternative technology. After meeting Gandhians who suggested 'invent a technology that ends the need for scavenging,' Pathak studied global sanitation models. In 1970 he founded Sulabh International, headquartered in Delhi. His SULABH TWO-PIT TOILET innovation: two underground pits, the household uses one at a time. When pit 1 fills (in 2-3 years), they switch to pit 2; pit 1's contents naturally compost in 2 years into safe manure. Total cost: ₹500. Zero scavenging needed. Over 50 years, Sulabh installed ~1.5 million toilets and 9000 public toilet complexes (charging ₹2 per use). Pathak rehabilitated 60,000+ scavenger families into mainstream jobs — teachers, beauticians, tailors. He won the Stockholm Water Prize, Magsaysay Award, and Padma Bhushan. He died in 2023 still wearing the simple kurta of a worker. The chapter on Wastewater you're reading — every paragraph in it has Pathak's fingerprints on what India did with that science.
