Wastewater Story

Chapter 18 · Class 7 Science

What is wastewater?

Every time you wash dishes, take a bath, flush a toilet, or empty a kitchen sink, you create wastewater (also called sewage). It contains dissolved chemicals, organic matter, and sometimes germs. If it's not handled properly, it spreads disease.

The journey of a drop

Your kitchen sink → house drain → sewer pipe → wastewater treatment plant (WWTP) → river or sea. The whole point of the system is that the water that finally returns to nature is clean enough not to poison it.

Inside a treatment plant

  • Bar screen — metal bars catch rags, plastics, and large solids.
  • Grit chamber — slow tank where sand, gravel and coffee grounds sink.
  • Aeration tank — air bubbled in; bacteria eat dissolved organic waste.
  • Clarifier — calm tank where dead bacteria settle as sludge.
  • Sludge digester — sludge is broken down; some plants produce biogas (methane) from it.

What pollutes wastewater?

  • Organic — food scraps, oil, paper, hair. Breaks down with microbes.
  • Inorganic — detergents, salts, heavy metals. Harder to remove.
  • Pathogens — disease-causing bacteria like cholera and typhoid.

Why open drains are dangerous

  • Mosquitoes breed in stagnant sewage.
  • Diseases like cholera, dysentery, and typhoid spread fast.
  • Smells affect nearby homes.
  • Heavy monsoon rain causes overflow and flooding.
India's Swachh Bharat Mission has built over 100 million toilets since 2014, drastically cutting open defecation and water-borne disease.

Better habits at home

  • Don't pour cooking oil down the drain — it clogs sewers.
  • Compost vegetable peels — they don't belong in pipes.
  • Throw used diapers and sanitary waste in the bin, not the toilet.
  • Fix leaking taps the day you spot them.

Low-cost sanitation

  • Pit latrines — affordable, but must be far from drinking-water wells.
  • Septic tanks — for homes without a sewer line.
  • Soak-pits — let greywater seep into the ground through gravel and sand.
  • Bio-toilets — bacteria digest waste right inside the toilet (used in trains).
