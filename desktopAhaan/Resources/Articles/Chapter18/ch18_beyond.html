Beyond the Book — Three Mind-Stretchers About Wastewater Story

Three ideas that don't show up in the NCERT textbook but will change how you see this chapter.

≈ 9 min read · curious-kid level

The chapter teaches you wastewater (sewage) is the dirty water from homes, factories, and farms. This article goes a step further — three stretch topics that lead naturally into Class 8 and beyond. Read one a day; don't try to absorb them all at once.

1. Sewage chemistry — what's actually IN that grey water

Class 7 says sewage is wastewater. Class 8 details what's in it. ORGANIC matter: faeces, urine, food bits (mostly carbohydrates, proteins, fats — biodegradable). INORGANIC: phosphates from detergents (cause algal blooms), nitrates from urine, salts. PATHOGENS: E. coli, salmonella, cholera vibrio, hepatitis A virus. HEAVY METALS from industrial sources: chromium, lead, mercury (don't biodegrade — accumulate in food chain). PERSISTENT ORGANICS: pesticides, pharmaceuticals (paracetamol traces, antibiotics — modern problem). A litre of Indian municipal sewage contains: ~250 mg organic load (BOD), ~10 mg nitrogen, ~2 mg phosphorus, billions of bacteria, hundreds of viruses. Treatment must remove ALL — different processes for each class of contaminant.

2. BOD — measuring water pollution numerically

Class 7 says polluted water has lots of organic waste. Class 10 introduces BOD — BIOLOGICAL OXYGEN DEMAND. Definition: how much oxygen (in mg/L) bacteria need to digest all organic matter in 1 litre of water over 5 days. Clean water: BOD < 5 mg/L. Sewage: 200-400 mg/L. Industrial effluent: 1000+ mg/L. Yamuna in Delhi: 30-50 mg/L (6× the safe limit). High BOD means bacteria consume all dissolved oxygen → fish + aquatic life suffocate. Indian Pollution Control Boards monitor BOD daily at hundreds of river points. COD (Chemical Oxygen Demand) is similar but uses chemical oxidation — catches non-biodegradable pollutants BOD misses. WWTP success measured in BOD reduction: input 250 mg/L → output 10 mg/L = 96% treatment efficiency.

3. Anaerobic digesters — turning sewage into electricity

Class 7 mentions sludge digesters. Class 11 reveals the biochemistry. ANAEROBIC DIGESTION: bacteria break down organic matter WITHOUT oxygen in 4 stages. Stage 1 HYDROLYSIS: enzymes break long molecules to monomers (proteins to amino acids, etc.). Stage 2 ACIDOGENESIS: monomers to organic acids + CO₂ + H₂. Stage 3 ACETOGENESIS: organic acids to acetic acid. Stage 4 METHANOGENESIS: acetic acid + CO₂ + H₂ → METHANE (CH₄) + water. The methane gas is BIOGAS — pure fuel! Indian biogas plants (~5 million installations) capture this, burn it for cooking or electricity. A single household digester (cow dung + food waste) produces 1-2 cubic metres of biogas daily — enough for 3-4 hours of cooking. Some Indian municipalities now run buses on biogas from sewage. Closed loop: sewage → biogas → energy → less reliance on imported fuel.
