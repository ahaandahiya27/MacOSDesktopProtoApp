How Water Moves Through Soil

Percolation, capillary action, and crops · Class 7 Science

Why some soils drain fast, others slow

Pour the same litre of water on sand, garden soil, and clay-rich mud. The water vanishes in seconds through sand, takes a few minutes through garden soil, and sits on top of clay for hours. Why?

Two competing forces decide:

  • Gravity pulls water straight down through the gaps between soil grains.
  • Adhesion (water sticking to soil particles) and cohesion (water sticking to itself) hold water in place.

Big grains (sand) = big gaps = gravity wins. Tiny grains (clay) = tiny gaps but huge surface area = adhesion wins.

Three soil-water properties matter for farming

  • Percolation rate — how fast water sinks down. Sandy soils: 30 ml/min; loamy: 15; clayey: 4.
  • Water-holding capacity — how much water the soil keeps after gravity drains the rest. Clay holds the most; sand holds the least.
  • Field capacity — the sweet spot a few hours after irrigation: enough water for roots, but air still in the pores so roots can breathe.
Loamy soil — equal parts sand, silt, clay — is the goldilocks medium. Drains enough to prevent waterlogging but holds enough to feed plants for days. That's why most vegetables grow best in loam.

Capillary action — water that climbs up

Soil water doesn't only go down. In narrow soil pores, water can climb UP against gravity through capillary action — the same thing that makes a tissue paper soak up a spill. After a rain, water from deep down rises by capillary action to refill the topsoil where roots live.

Crop matching

  • Paddy (rice) needs standing water → clayey soil (waterlogged).
  • Cotton thrives in mineral-rich black soil (high water-holding).
  • Groundnut, watermelon like loose sandy soil (good aeration for the underground crops).
  • Vegetables, wheat do best in balanced loamy soil.

Try this at home

Three plastic cups with the same number of holes in the bottom. Fill one with dry sand, one with garden soil, one with clay-rich mud. Pour the same amount of water in each. Time how fast it drains. The order is your percolation ranking.
