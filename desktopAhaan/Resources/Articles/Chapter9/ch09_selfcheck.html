Question 2 — from Chapter 9

A 1-litre bottle of clayey soil from a riverbank, when shaken with water, takes hours to settle. The same bottle of sandy soil settles in seconds. Explain this using the physics of falling particles, and predict what happens with loamy soil.

🧠 Answer in your head first

Don't peek. Speak the answer out loud, then check.

Answer: Stokes' law: a particle falling through fluid reaches a terminal velocity proportional to (particle diameter)². Sand particles are ~100x larger in diameter than clay particles → ~10,000x the settling speed. Sand drops in seconds; clay drifts for hours, possibly days. Loam is a mix — sand fraction settles first (visible layer at bottom in minutes), silt next (10s of minutes), clay last (hours). You'd see DISTINCT LAYERS in a settled loam bottle, with sand at the bottom and clay on top.

Why: Stokes' law: terminal velocity ∝ (diameter)².

Question 4 — from Chapter 9

Design a simple experiment to compare the PERCOLATION RATE (how fast water drains) of three soil samples — sandy, clayey, loamy — using only items in a kitchen. Specify your method, what you measure, and rank the soils.

🧠 Answer in your head first

Don't peek. Speak the answer out loud, then check.

Answer: Method: Take three identical empty plastic bottles. Cut off the bottoms; flip them upside-down so the neck points down. Stuff a small piece of cotton in each neck (filter). Fill each with the SAME volume (e.g., 200 g) of one soil — bottle A sand, bottle B clay, bottle C loam. Hold each over a measuring cup. Pour the SAME volume of water (100 mL) into each at the same moment. Measure the TIME from pouring until the first drip stops, AND the volume collected. Result: bottle A (sand) drains in seconds, collects most of the water. Bottle B (clay) drips for many minutes, collects little (most water held in clay). Bottle C (loam) drains moderately. Ranking by percolation rate (fast → slow): sand > loam > clay.

Why: Set up identical apparatus (same bottle, same cotton, same soil mass) so the only variable is soil type.
