Every chapter has a person behind it — someone who first asked the right question, ran the right experiment, or wrote down the answer. This is a one-page portrait of the scientist most tied to this chapter's content.

Sir Albert Howard

1873–1947 · British (worked extensively in India)

🎧 In one line

Developed the Indore method of composting in Bihar, India — the foundation of the modern organic farming movement.

Albert Howard arrived in British India in 1905 as an agricultural advisor. He was supposed to teach Indian farmers 'modern' chemical methods. Instead, working at the Pusa Institute (Bihar) and later in Indore (Madhya Pradesh), he watched Indian farmers carefully and realised they knew things Western agriculture didn't. He spent 25 years systematically studying how Indian villagers built soil fertility — using cattle manure, plant residues, and patient layering. His 'Indore method' of composting (alternating layers of plant waste and animal manure, turned periodically) became the global standard. In 1940 he published 'An Agricultural Testament', which is the founding text of the organic farming movement. Today's organic certification standards worldwide trace directly to methods Howard learned from Indian farmers in Bihar. The lesson he kept stressing: soil is alive, and you feed the soil, not the plant.
