Five Wrong Answers Class 7 Students Give

The five places almost every Class 7 student trips up on Chapter 9 — and the one-line fix for each.

≈ 5 min read · revision-tier

Examiners say the same wrong answers come back year after year. None of these mistakes are silly — they are almost right. That is exactly what makes them dangerous. Read each one, cover the answer, and try to spot what is off before peeking.

1. “Soil is just dust on the ground.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Soil is a complex ecosystem with mineral particles, organic matter (humus, decaying leaves), water, air, and an enormous population of living organisms (bacteria, fungi, worms, insects). One handful of healthy soil contains BILLIONS of microorganisms — more than there are humans on Earth. Soil isn't just substrate — it's the most biodiverse habitat on the planet, gram for gram.

2. “Clay soil is bad and sandy soil is good.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Both have strengths. Clay holds water and nutrients well — perfect for crops that need wet roots (rice paddy, jute). Sand drains fast and warms quickly — perfect for plants that hate wet roots (potato, peanut, watermelon). Loam (mix of sand + silt + clay) is best for MOST crops but not all. The 'best' soil depends on what you're growing.

3. “Soil erosion only happens during heavy rain.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Rain (monsoon) is the biggest single eroder, but soil also erodes by wind (Thar Desert, Rajasthan drifts cover roads every summer), by water (small streams cutting gullies), by humans (over-grazing, deforestation, tilling on slopes), and even by humans walking the same path daily (creating a hardpan that lets water flow on top). The Chambal ravines didn't form in one monsoon — they took centuries of slow loss.

4. “Topsoil regrows quickly.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Topsoil takes 500 to 1000 years to form 1 centimetre under natural conditions. When monsoon washes away 30 cm in a single Chambal ravine, that's 15,000 years of soil-building gone in months. Erosion is essentially irreversible on human timescales. This is why conservation is so urgent: every farmer's choice this year affects soil future generations will need.

5. “Earthworms are pests because they eat plant roots.”

🚫 The mistake

A common belief that's almost right — but misses the key detail.

The fix: Earthworms eat dead organic matter, not living roots. They tunnel through soil (aerating it), excrete nutrient-rich castings (best natural fertiliser), and mix layers (bringing up minerals from below). Charles Darwin spent his last 40 years studying earthworms and called them 'nature's plough'. A garden with abundant earthworms is a garden with rich soil.

📝 Stretch your thinking

Pick any two of these and explain them to a younger sibling using a one-line analogy each. If you can compress a mistake into one sentence with one image, you have understood it.
