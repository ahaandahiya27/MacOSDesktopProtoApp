"What If?" — हितं मनोहारि च दुर्लभं वचः

Hypothetical questions with no single right answer. Think hard before you read the discussion.

≈ 7 min read · brain-stretcher level

The textbook teaches what IS. Real understanding comes from asking what IF? Pick a question below, pause and think for a minute, then read one possible discussion.

What if 1 — you could only be either honest or kind, never both?

The question: The chapter's title says it's rare to be both. Imagine you had to pick ONE.

🧠 Think first

Which would you choose, and what would you lose by choosing it?

One discussion path: Choosing kindness alone leaves you sweet but unreliable as a source of truth. Choosing honesty alone leaves you respected but lonely. The chapter's point is that the EFFORT to be both is what defines a mature speaker — even though we fall short of it most days.

What if 2 — children were never taught speech-ethics verses?

The question: Imagine a culture where 'how to speak well' was a personal discovery, not a curriculum.

🧠 Think first

Would adults still arrive at the same rules?

One discussion path: Some would, by trial and error. Many would not — they would shout, or flatter, all their lives. Cultures that codify speech-ethics give their members a head-start. Indian, Greek, Confucian, Buddhist traditions all do this. The chapter is one tiny moment in a centuries-long deliberate effort.

What if 3 — you spent one whole day saying only things that were both useful and pleasant?

The question: A practical experiment.

🧠 Think first

What would change about how often you spoke?

One discussion path: Most people would find themselves silent much more often. That is the chapter's invitation: not perfection of speech, but RESTRAINT — saying less, choosing more carefully. Quiet is the first kindness.

📝 Stretch your thinking

None of these has one 'correct' answer — they are for arguing about. Try one on a friend or a parent and see if their discussion path differs from yours.
