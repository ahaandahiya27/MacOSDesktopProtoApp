Q1. What is infrastructure, and what are its two types? (NCERT page 169)

Infrastructure is the basic facilities and systems a country needs to function. It has two types: physical infrastructure (transport, utilities, communication and energy systems) and social infrastructure (schools, colleges, hospitals, police and fire stations, courts, parks and libraries). Both are essential for daily life, the economy and development.

Q2. Why is physical infrastructure called the backbone of the nation? (NCERT page 172)

Because it supports all other activity: transport connects production to markets, boosts trade, supports tourism, connects remote areas, helps in emergencies and strengthens security. The tomato-farmer example shows infrastructure working like puzzle pieces - canals, roads, cold storage and the internet together get a farmer's crop to market.

Q3. Why and when did the British build railways in India? (NCERT page 176)

The British introduced railways in 1853, mainly for their own benefit: to transport raw materials like cotton and tea to ports for export, to sell British goods across India, to exploit resources, and to move troops to control the territory. The network later became India's lifeline.

Q4. Who was J.C. Bose and what did he contribute? (NCERT page 186)

Jagadish Chandra Bose (1858-1937) was an Indian scientist who pioneered wireless transmission, demonstrating in 1895 that signals could pass through a wall, and inventing an early coherer and the galena crystal detector. He rarely patented his work, so Marconi (who patented a similar device) received more recognition.

Q5. What does Kautilya's Arthashastra say about infrastructure? (NCERT page 189)

It shows ancient concern for infrastructure: the state, villages and assemblies built and maintained roads and waterways, roads had different widths based on traffic, and there were punishments and fines for damaging public works like reservoir dams and parks - showing that building, maintaining and protecting infrastructure is a long Indian tradition.
