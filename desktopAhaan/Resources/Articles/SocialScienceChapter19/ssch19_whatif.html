History, geography and society are full of 'what if' turning points. Think about each question yourself first — then open the panel to compare your reasoning.

What if a town had no internet or phone services for a month?

Think first, then open to compare

Daily life would slow dramatically. People could not call for help in emergencies, students could not learn online, and shops and farmers could not check prices or place orders. Banking, government services and businesses that depend on the internet would struggle. Families separated by distance could not stay in touch easily. This shows how deeply communications infrastructure is woven into modern life - it is as essential as roads or power, even though we cannot see it.

What if a country built lots of roads but neglected schools and hospitals?

Think first, then open to compare

The country might move goods quickly, but its people would not develop fully. Without good schools, children would not get educated; without hospitals, people would fall ill and die younger. A truly developed country needs both physical infrastructure (roads, power) AND social infrastructure (schools, hospitals), because development means improving both the economy and people's quality of life. Roads alone cannot make a society healthy, educated and just.

What if a region had no roads, power or communication links?

Think first, then open to compare

Without infrastructure, farmers could not get crops to market, factories could not run, students could not reach schools, and businesses could not connect with customers — so the region would stay poor no matter how hard people worked. This is why infrastructure is called the 'backbone' of development: it multiplies the value of everything else. Building roads, power and networks is often the first step in lifting an area out of poverty.
