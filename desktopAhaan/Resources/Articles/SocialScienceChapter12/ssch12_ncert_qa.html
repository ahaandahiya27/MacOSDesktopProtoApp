Q1. What are the main features of a market? Recall a recent visit to a market to purchase a product, and describe the features you observed. (NCERT page 271)

The main features of a market are a buyer, a seller and a price that both agree on for a transaction to take place. On a recent visit to a vegetable market, for example, I (the buyer) wanted to buy tomatoes, the vendor (the seller) had tomatoes to sell, and we agreed on a price. I observed the vendor quoting a price, some bargaining or negotiation to reach an acceptable price, the exchange of money for goods, and many other buyers and sellers around, all features that make the place a market.

Q2. Match the following types of markets with their characteristics: Physical market, Online market, Domestic market, International market, Wholesale market, Retail market. (NCERT page 271)

Physical market → requires physical presence of buyers and seller. Online market → buyers and sellers meet virtually and can transact at any time. Domestic market → lies within the boundaries of a nation. International market → goods and services flow outside the nation's boundaries. Wholesale market → deals in bulk quantities. Retail market → serves the final consumers with goods and services.

Q3. Prices are generally determined by the interaction between demand from buyers and supply by sellers. Can you think of products where prices are high despite fewer buyers demanding the product? What could be the reasons? (NCERT page 271)

Yes. Products like rare artwork, antiques, precious gems, designer or limited-edition items, and even Aakriti's oil paintings can have high prices despite few buyers. The reason is that their supply is very low (they are rare or unique) and the few buyers who want them place a very high value on them. So price depends not just on the number of buyers but on how scarce the good is and how much those buyers are willing to pay — a low supply combined with strong willingness to pay keeps the price high.

Q4. A family refuses to buy beans at ₹30/kg from a cart seller (bargaining for ₹25) but then pays ₹40/kg for neatly packed beans at a super bazaar. What are the reasons, and are there factors affecting buying and selling that are not directly connected to price? (NCERT page 272)

Yes, there are factors beyond price. The family may prefer the super bazaar because the beans are neatly packed and hygienically presented, the shop is cleaner and more convenient, the quality may seem more reliable or assured, and the experience of shopping there is more comfortable. Some buyers also trust a known store's quality or enjoy not having to bargain. So buying decisions are influenced by convenience, hygiene, packaging, perceived quality, trust and the shopping experience — not by price alone.
