The journey of a product: from producer to consumer

Follow a typical good as it travels through the chain of markets and participants until it reaches you.

  • Producer makes the goods
    A producer or manufacturer makes goods for sale — for example, a farmer grows vegetables or a Surat factory weaves cloth from raw cotton bought through a mandī.
  • Wholesaler buys in bulk
    A wholesaler buys the goods in large quantities directly from the producer and stores them in big warehouses called godowns, using cold storage for perishables.
  • Distributor bridges the distance
    Where shops are far away or hard to reach, a distributor carries the goods from the wholesaler onward to scattered retailers across regions.
  • Retailer sells small quantities
    A retailer — the shopkeeper near your home — splits the bulk into small quantities and sells them to final consumers, for use rather than resale.
  • Consumer buys at an agreed price
    Finally the consumer buys the good at a price agreed with the seller, completing the transaction. Online, an aggregator app may replace much of this chain and deliver to the doorstep.
