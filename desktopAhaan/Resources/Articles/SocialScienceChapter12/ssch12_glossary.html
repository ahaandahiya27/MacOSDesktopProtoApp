The 17 terms to know

  • Market · बाज़ार / हाट — A place or arrangement — physical or online — where people buy and sell goods and services.
    Example: A weekly haat, a mall and a shopping app are all markets.
  • Price · मूल्य / दाम — The amount at which a buyer is willing to buy, and a seller is willing to sell, particular goods or services.
    Example: Guavas were finally sold at a price of ₹40 per kg.
  • Demand · माँग — The quantity of a product or service that consumers are willing and able to purchase at a particular price at a given time.
    Example: When demand for energy-efficient fridges rose, producers made more of them.
  • Supply · आपूर्ति — The quantity of a product or service that sellers are willing and able to sell at a particular price at a given time.
    Example: When the supply of onions falls, their price tends to rise.
  • Wholesaler · थोक विक्रेता — A trader who buys goods in large (bulk) quantities from producers and sells them to retailers.
    Example: Wholesalers buy vegetables directly from farms and store them in godowns.
  • Retailer · फुटकर / खुदरा विक्रेता — A shopkeeper who sells goods in small quantities to final consumers for use, not resale.
    Example: The grocery store near your home is a retailer.
  • Distributor · वितरक — An individual or business who supplies goods from manufacturers and wholesalers to retailers, especially across distances.
    Example: Distributors carry biscuits from a city wholesaler to far-off village shops.
  • Aggregator · एग्रीगेटर / संग्राहक — A website or mobile application that organises and combines offers from multiple sellers and sells them to consumers at one place.
    Example: An online shopping app that gathers many sellers and delivers to your door is an aggregator.
  • Export and Import · निर्यात और आयात — Export is selling goods or services produced in one country to a buyer in another; import is buying goods or services from other countries and bringing them into one's own.
    Example: India exports software and imports crude petroleum.
  • Public goods · सार्वजनिक वस्तुएँ — A service or commodity accessible to all members of a society, whose present use does not diminish its availability for future use, usually provided by the government.
    Example: Roads, public parks and policing are public goods.
  • Bazaar · बाज़ार — A permanent market of many shops along streets or in an enclosure; from Persian bāzār ('market').
    Example: The Hampi Bazaar once bustled with traders from many lands.
  • Haat · हाट / पैंठ — A periodic rural market that gathers on fixed days of the week rather than every day.
    Example: Villagers sell vegetables and crafts at the weekly haat.
  • Commerce · वाणिज्य — The buying and selling of goods and services on a large scale; from Latin com- ('together') + merx ('goods') — the root also of 'merchant' and 'e-commerce'.
    Example: E-commerce is simply commerce carried on over the internet.
  • Negotiation · मोल-भाव / वार्ता — Bargaining between a buyer and a seller until both agree on a price; from Latin negotium ('business' — literally neg-otium, 'not-leisure').
    Example: After some negotiation, the buyer and shopkeeper settled on a 'just right' price.
  • Subsidy · अनुदान / सब्सिडी — Money the government gives to lower a price or support producers; from Latin subsidium ('help, support').
    Example: A subsidy on cooking gas lowers the price families pay.
  • Consumer · उपभोक्ता — The person who finally buys and uses a good or service; from Latin consumere ('to use up').
    Example: A consumer checks quality and price before buying.
  • MRP (Maximum Retail Price) · अधिकतम खुदरा मूल्य — The highest price, printed on a pack, at which a product may be sold to a consumer in India, taxes included.
    Example: A shop may sell below the MRP but never above it.
