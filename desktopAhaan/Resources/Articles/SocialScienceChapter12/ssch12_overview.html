What Is a Market?

The meaning of a market, its many names, the basic features every market shares — buyers, sellers and an agreed price — and a glimpse of the famous Hampi Bazaar of the Vijayanagara Empire.

  1. The Meaning of a MarketA market is any place — physical or online — where people buy and sell goods and services.
  2. Needs, Wants and Why Markets ExistMarkets exist because people have needs and wants they cannot meet on their own, so they trade for them.
  3. Features of a Market: Buyer, Seller and PriceEvery market must have a buyer, a seller and a price both agree on for a transaction to happen.

How Prices Are Determined

How bargaining settles a price, what happens when a seller sets it too high or too low, and how the interaction of demand from buyers and supply from sellers fixes a price that is 'just right'.

  1. Negotiation and the 'Just Right' PriceA seller who asks too much finds no buyers, one who asks too little loses money, so bargaining settles a price that is just right for both.
  2. Demand and SupplyPrices are set by the interaction of demand from buyers and supply from sellers.

Markets Around Us

The many forms markets take — physical and online, domestic and international — including malls, shopping apps, the stock market, and trade across borders through exports and imports.

  1. Physical and Online MarketsPhysical markets are where buyers meet sellers in person; online markets let people trade through apps and websites without meeting.
  2. Domestic and International MarketsA domestic market trades within a country's borders; an international market trades across borders through exports and imports.

The Chain from Producer to Consumer

How goods travel from producer to consumer through wholesalers, distributors and retailers in physical markets, and through aggregators in online markets, illustrated by the Surat textile market.

  1. Wholesalers and RetailersWholesalers buy in bulk from producers and sell to retailers, who then sell smaller quantities to final consumers.
  2. Distributors and AggregatorsDistributors carry goods from wholesalers to far-flung retailers; aggregators are online platforms that combine many sellers' offers for consumers.
  3. The Supply Chain: The Surat Textile ExampleA product passes through many stages and markets — as shown by raw cotton becoming finished sarees in Surat — before reaching the consumer.

Markets, Government and the Consumer

How markets benefit society beyond money, the government's roles in controlling prices, ensuring quality and safety, curbing pollution and providing public goods, and how consumers judge quality using certification marks and reviews.

  1. How Markets Benefit SocietyMarkets do more than enable buying and selling — they signal what to produce and build lasting human relationships.
  2. The Government's Role in MarketsWhen markets do not work well on their own, the government steps in to control prices, ensure quality and safety, curb pollution and provide public goods.
  3. How Consumers Assess QualityConsumers judge a product's quality using government certification marks, package labels, reputation and online reviews.
