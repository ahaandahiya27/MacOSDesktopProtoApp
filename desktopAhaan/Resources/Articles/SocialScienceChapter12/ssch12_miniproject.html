🧾 Trace a product's supply chain (about 60 min)

What you need

  • A notebook and pen
  • A product from home (e.g. a packet of biscuits or a vegetable)
  • Permission to talk to a nearby retailer

Steps

  1. Pick one product you can find in your home or local shop.
  2. Visit or ask your nearest retailer where the product came from.
  3. Ask who supplied it to them (a distributor or wholesaler) and where the wholesaler got it.
  4. Try to trace it back to the producer or manufacturer and the place of origin.
  5. Draw a flow chart like the diagram in the chapter: Producer → Wholesaler → Distributor → Retailer → Consumer.
  6. Label each box with the real name or place you discovered for your product.
  7. Share your flow chart with the class and compare chains for different products.

👀 What you should see

You will discover that even a simple product passes through several hands and places — often from another district or state — before it reaches your home.

🧠 Why it works

Building the chain yourself turns the abstract diagram into a real story, fixing the roles of producer, wholesaler, distributor and retailer firmly in memory and showing why each middleman exists.

⭐ Make a BEE Star energy chart (about 45 min)

What you need

  • A notebook and pen
  • Access to electronic appliances at home (fridge, AC, TV, fan, etc.)
  • An adult's help to read labels safely

Steps

  1. List the electronic appliances in your home that have a BEE Star label.
  2. For each appliance, carefully read and note the number of stars on its rating label.
  3. Also note any other certification marks you find (FSSAI, ISI, AGMARK) on products at home.
  4. Arrange your appliances in increasing order of energy efficiency (fewest stars to most stars).
  5. Mark which appliances are the most and least energy-efficient.
  6. Write one sentence on how choosing more stars could lower the electricity bill and help the environment.

👀 What you should see

You will see that appliances differ in their star ratings, and that higher-star appliances use less electricity — saving money and protecting the environment.

🧠 Why it works

Reading real labels makes certification marks meaningful and shows how a consumer can assess quality and efficiency before buying, linking the chapter's ideas to everyday decisions.
