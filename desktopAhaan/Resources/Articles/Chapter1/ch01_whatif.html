Five "What Ifs?" About Plants

Hypothetical questions with no single right answer. Think hard before you read the discussion.

≈ 8 min read · brain-stretcher level

The textbook tells you what is. The best science teachers also ask what if? Pick any one of the five questions below. Pause. Think for at least one minute before scrolling. Then read the discussion.

What if 1 — Plants were black, not green?

The question: Black surfaces absorb every colour of light. Green surfaces throw away the green part. If plants absorbed every colour, they would catch more sunlight per leaf and make more food. So why didn't evolution make plants black?

🧠 Think first

Pause. What problems could "more sunlight per leaf" cause?

One discussion path: More absorbed sunlight = more heat. Chloroplast machinery is fragile and starts breaking down at high temperatures. Green is a compromise: enough light captured to feed the plant, but not so much that the leaf cooks itself. Evolution is full of trade-offs like this.

What if 2 — Photosynthesis happened at night?

The question: Right now plants need daylight to make food and then store some of it for the night. If photosynthesis somehow worked in moonlight, what would the world look like?

🧠 Think first

What would change about how plants are built, how forests look, how the oxygen we breathe is made?

One discussion path: Plants wouldn't need to "save up" sugar through the night, so they could spend more on growing tall. Leaves could be smaller (less daytime exposure needed). The day-night oxygen cycle would smooth out. Most importantly: the Sun is roughly 400,000 times brighter than the full Moon, so even a perfect night-photosynthesis system would only produce ~1/400,000 of what daytime gives us. Some chemistry can't get around physics.

What if 3 — Animals had chlorophyll in their skin?

The question: Some sea slugs (e.g. Elysia chlorotica) steal chloroplasts from algae and keep them alive in their skin — they photosynthesise. What if humans evolved this trick?

🧠 Think first

How much skin would you need? How much sunlight? Could you survive without breakfast?

One discussion path: A 60 kg human burns about 8,400 kJ of energy per day. Plants are only 3–6% efficient at turning sunlight into food. Even at perfect efficiency and full noon sun, you would need roughly 9 square metres of "skin" (more than four times the surface area you have) to feed yourself. So a sun-eating human would have to be either tiny or VERY flat. Trees solved this by becoming wide and flat (leaves). Evolution doesn't repeat the same solution for animals because we already eat with our mouths — a much more compact engine.

What if 4 — There were no decomposers?

The question: Saprotrophs (mushrooms, bacteria) break down dead plants and recycle nutrients back into the soil. If decomposers disappeared overnight, what would happen?

🧠 Think first

What would build up? What would run out?

One discussion path: Dead leaves and fallen trees would pile up forever. Within a few decades, forests would become physically impassable. Worse, the nitrogen and phosphorus locked inside those dead bodies would never return to the soil. New plants couldn't get the minerals they need. Within a century, photosynthesis itself would slow because there'd be no usable nitrogen for new chlorophyll. Decomposers are the unseen pillar of the food chain.

What if 5 — You could photosynthesise once a week?

The question: Imagine you had a special button: once a week, lie in the sun for two hours and skip a meal. Sound nice? What would it actually take?

🧠 Think first

What's the energy maths? What would your body need to store?

One discussion path: One meal is roughly 2,000 kJ. Two hours of strong sun on your full skin (~2 m²) at plant efficiency gives about 200 kJ — only 10% of a meal. So one weekly sunbath would be far from a full meal's worth. But it might be enough to skip a snack. Real cyanobacteria — the simplest photosynthesising life — get away with this because they are microscopic and barely consume any energy. Our bodies are too hungry for solar to work alone.

📝 Stretch your thinking

Pick your favourite "what if?" from above and explain your answer to a sibling or parent at dinner tonight. Bonus: ask them the same question first and see if they reach the same conclusion.
