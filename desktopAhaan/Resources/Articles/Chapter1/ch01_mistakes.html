Ten Wrong Answers Class 7 Students Give

The ten places almost every Class 7 student trips up on Chapter 1 — and the one-line fix for each.

≈ 10 min read · revision-tier

Examiners say the same wrong answers come back year after year. None of these mistakes are silly — they are almost right. That is exactly what makes them dangerous. Read each one, cover the answer, and try to spot what is off before peeking.

1. “Plants breathe in CO₂ and breathe out O₂.”

🚫 The mistake

Students confuse photosynthesis with respiration. They are not the same thing.

The fix: Plants do both. Photosynthesis takes in CO₂ and gives out O₂ — but it needs sunlight, so it only runs during the day. Respiration takes in O₂ and gives out CO₂ — and it runs 24 hours a day, just like in animals. At night, plants only respire. During the day, photosynthesis is much faster than respiration, so the net effect is CO₂ in, O₂ out. The correct sentence is: “In sunlight, plants release more O₂ than they take in.”

2. “Cuscuta is a plant, so it must be an autotroph.”

🚫 The mistake

Students assume all green-looking organisms photosynthesise.

The fix: Cuscuta (amarbel / आमरबेल) has no chlorophyll — it is yellow-orange, not green. It is a parasitic heterotroph. It twines around a host plant, sinks tiny pegs called haustoria into the host's phloem, and steals ready-made food. Being a plant does not make you an autotroph; having chlorophyll and using it does.

3. “Plants get their food from the soil.”

🚫 The mistake

The textbook image of roots in soil makes students think soil is plant food.

The fix: Plants get minerals and water from soil — but soil contains no glucose, no sugar, no starch. The actual food (glucose) is built inside the leaf from CO₂ (from air) and H₂O (from soil). Van Helmont's 1648 experiment proved this: a 76 kg willow grew from a 2 kg sapling and almost none of the new weight came from the soil. Most of a tree is captured sky.

4. “The green colour of leaves is chlorophyll, which absorbs green light.”

🚫 The mistake

Sounds logical — green leaf, green light. It is the opposite.

The fix: Chlorophyll reflects green light, which is why we see it as green. It absorbs red and blue wavelengths and uses that absorbed energy to drive photosynthesis. A leaf is green because green is the part of sunlight the leaf throws away.

5. “Lichen is one organism.”

🚫 The mistake

Lichen looks like a single crusty thing on a rock, so students treat it as one species.

The fix: Lichen is a symbiosis — two organisms living together. A fungus provides the body, water, and minerals. An alga (or sometimes a cyanobacterium) lives inside the fungus and provides food by photosynthesis. Neither could survive alone in the harsh places lichens grow (rocks, tree bark, the Arctic). Always describe lichen as a partnership, never as a single plant.

6. “Insectivorous plants eat insects because they cannot photosynthesise.”

🚫 The mistake

Students assume eating insects replaces photosynthesis. It does not.

The fix: Plants like the pitcher plant and Venus flytrap are autotrophs — they have green leaves and run photosynthesis normally. They trap insects to get nitrogen, which the boggy soils they grow in don't supply. So they are autotrophs for energy and partially heterotrophic for one specific nutrient. The correct sentence is: “They photosynthesise for energy, and eat insects for nitrogen.”

7. “The leaf is the only place photosynthesis happens.”

🚫 The mistake

Almost right — but not the whole story.

The fix: Photosynthesis happens anywhere there is chlorophyll. Green stems (like in a cactus or a pea plant), green sepals on a flower, even green tomatoes, can all photosynthesise. In a fully leafless cactus, the entire green stem is the “leaf.” The general rule: green = chlorophyll = food factory.

8. “Stomata are pores on the upper side of the leaf.”

🚫 The mistake

Pure direction error in diagrams.

The fix: In most plants stomata are mostly on the lower (under) surface of the leaf, because the upper surface gets hot sun. Putting stomata in the shade reduces water loss while still letting CO₂ in. A floating leaf like a lotus does the opposite — stomata on top, because the bottom is in water. Memorise: most land plants → stomata below; floating water plants → stomata above.

9. “A saprotroph is a parasite.”

🚫 The mistake

Both feed off something else, so they sound the same.

The fix: Parasites feed on living hosts (Cuscuta on a tree, tapeworm in a human). Saprotrophs feed on dead organic matter (bread mould on stale bread, mushrooms on a rotting log). One harms; the other recycles. A simple test: if the host has to be alive for the feeding to work, it is a parasite.

10. “Photosynthesis happens in the chloroplast's nucleus.”

🚫 The mistake

Confusing the cell's parts.

The fix: Chloroplasts don't have a nucleus — the nucleus is in the plant cell. Photosynthesis happens inside thylakoids, the stacked green discs inside the chloroplast. The chloroplast itself has its own tiny DNA loop (a leftover from when it was a free-living bacterium, billions of years ago). One useful sentence: Photosynthesis happens inside the green discs (thylakoids) inside the chloroplast.

📝 Stretch your thinking

Pick any three of these and explain them to a younger sibling using a one-line analogy each. If you can compress a mistake into one sentence with one image, you have understood it.
