A Kid's Plant Biology Dictionary

Thirty must-know terms from Chapter 1 — each in English, Hindi, and Sanskrit, with a one-line meaning a 12-year-old can actually use.

≈ 6 min read · revision-ready

Reading the chapter once isn't enough. Knowing the words is what makes you confident. Glance through this deck every Sunday for four weeks and these terms will be yours.

The big six

If you only memorise six terms from Chapter 1, make it these:

  • Photosynthesis · प्रकाशसंश्लेषण (prakāsh-saṃśleṣaṇ) — making food from sunlight, water, and CO₂.
  • Chlorophyll · पर्णहरित (parna-harit, "leaf-green") — the pigment in chloroplasts that catches light.
  • Chloroplast · हरितलवक (harita-lavak) — the oval green factory inside leaf cells.
  • Stomata · रंध्र (randhra) — tiny mouths on the underside of leaves that let CO₂ in and O₂ out.
  • Autotroph · स्वपोषी (sva-poshi, "self-feeder") — an organism that makes its own food (e.g. green plants).
  • Heterotroph · परपोषी (par-poshi, "other-feeder") — an organism that eats others (you, all animals).

Modes of plant nutrition

  • Saprotroph · मृतजीवी (mṛta-jīvī, "dead-feeder") — feeds on dead matter, e.g. mushrooms.
  • Parasite · परजीवी (par-jīvī, "other-living") — lives off another organism, often harming it; e.g. Cuscuta.
  • Insectivorous · कीटभक्षी (kīṭa-bhakṣī, "insect-eater") — eats insects for nitrogen; e.g. pitcher plant.
  • Symbiosis · सहजीविता (saha-jīvitā, "together-life") — two organisms living together for mutual benefit; e.g. lichen.
  • Holozoic · सकलाहारी (sakala-āhārī, "whole-eater") — taking in whole food particles; how animals eat.

Plant anatomy

  • Cuticle · वसा-त्वचा (vasā-tvachā, "waxy skin") — waxy outer layer that stops a leaf drying out.
  • Epidermis · बाह्यत्वचा (bāhya-tvachā, "outer skin") — single-cell-thick skin on the leaf surface.
  • Mesophyll · पर्णमध्य (parṇa-madhya, "leaf-middle") — green tissue inside the leaf where photosynthesis happens.
  • Palisade · स्तंभाकार (stambhākār, "pillar-shaped") — tall green cells stacked just under the upper epidermis.
  • Xylem · दारु-नलिका (dāru-nalikā, "wood-pipes") — tubes that pull water up from roots to leaves.
  • Phloem · रसवाहिनी (rasa-vāhinī, "sap-carrier") — tubes that carry sugar from leaves down to roots.
  • Guard cells · रक्षक कोशिका (rakṣak koshikā) — bean-shaped cells that open and close each stoma.

Chemistry words

  • Glucose · ग्लूकोज (glūkoj) — the simple sugar plants make and animals burn.
  • Starch · स्टार्च / मांड (māṇḍ) — chain of glucose molecules used to store extra food.
  • Oxygen · ऑक्सीजन / प्राणवायु (prāṇa-vāyu, "life-air") — gas plants release; we breathe it in.
  • Carbon dioxide · कार्बन डाइऑक्साइड (CO₂) — gas plants breathe in; we breathe out.
  • Nitrogen · नाइट्रोजन / यवक्षार — element plants need to build proteins and chlorophyll.
  • Calvin Cycle · केल्विन चक्र — the chemical assembly line that builds glucose, named after Melvin Calvin (1961 Nobel).

Cycle & ecology

  • Nitrogen cycle · नाइट्रोजन-चक्र — the wheel that moves nitrogen between air, soil, plants, and decomposers.
  • Rhizobium · राइजोबियम — nitrogen-fixing bacteria that live in legume root nodules.
  • Nitrification · नाइट्रीकरण — bacteria converting ammonia to nitrates the plant can absorb.
  • Decomposer · अपघटक (apaghaṭak) — fungus or bacterium that breaks dead matter back into soil nutrients.
  • Food chain · खाद्य-श्रृंखला (khādya-shṛnkhalā, "food-chain") — sequence of who-eats-whom starting from a producer.
  • Producer · उत्पादक (ut-pādak, "maker") — first link in any food chain; always an autotroph.
  • Consumer · उपभोक्ता (upabhoktā) — anything that eats a producer or another consumer.
