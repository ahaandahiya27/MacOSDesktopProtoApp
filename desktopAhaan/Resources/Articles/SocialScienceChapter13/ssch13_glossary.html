Farming, or agriculture, is one of humankind's oldest occupations and the backbone of the Indian economy. This chapter follows the long story of Indian farming

These are the chapter's key words, drawn from its own concept list. Knowing the words is what makes you confident in the questions and the exam.

The 18 terms to know

  • Agriculture · कृषि — The work of cultivating soil, growing crops and rearing animals; broadly it also includes forestry, horticulture, fisheries and beekeeping.
    Example: Nearly half of India's workers depend on agriculture and allied activities.
  • Kharif crops · खरीफ़ फ़सल — Monsoon crops, sown with the southwest monsoon and grown in the hot, rainy season.
    Example: Rice, maize, cotton and sugarcane are kharif crops.
  • Rabi crops · रबी फ़सल — Winter crops, grown in the cool season with less water and harvested in spring.
    Example: Wheat, gram and mustard are rabi crops.
  • Zaid crops · ज़ायद फ़सल — Short summer crops grown in the period between the rabi and kharif seasons.
    Example: Watermelon, muskmelon and cucumber are zaid crops.
  • Monsoon · मानसून — The seasonal winds that bring most of India's rainfall; the southwest monsoon (June–September) and the northeast monsoon (October–December).
    Example: Kharif crops depend on the timely arrival of the southwest monsoon.
  • Alluvial soil · जलोढ़ मिट्टी — Fertile soil made of silt (mud, sand and fine particles) brought by rivers from the mountains and plateaus.
    Example: The Northern Plains have rich alluvial soil ideal for wheat and rice.
  • Humus · ह्यूमस / मृदा-कल्क — The dark organic matter in soil formed by the decomposition of plant and animal matter; it is rich in nutrients and retains moisture.
    Example: Healthy topsoil is rich in humus.
  • Crop rotation · फ़सल चक्र — Growing different crops in the same field in different seasons to prevent the soil from losing specific nutrients.
    Example: A farmer may follow a cereal with a legume to restore nitrogen to the soil.
  • Irrigation · सिंचाई — Supplying water to crops by artificial means such as canals, wells, pumps, drip or sprinkler systems.
    Example: Drip irrigation delivers water slowly and directly to the roots.
  • Green Revolution · हरित क्रांति — The 1960s–70s transformation of Indian farming using high-yielding seeds, irrigation, chemicals and machinery to boost food production.
    Example: The Green Revolution made India self-sufficient in food grains.
  • Organic farming · जैविक खेती — Farming that avoids chemical fertilisers and pesticides, using natural fertilisers like compost and manure and natural pesticides like neem.
    Example: Sikkim is the world's first 100% organic state.
  • Sustainable farming · टिकाऊ खेती — Farming that meets today's needs without harming the soil, water or the ability to farm in future; from Latin sustinere ('to hold up, maintain').
    Example: Rotating crops and saving water are part of sustainable farming.
  • Fertiliser · उर्वरक / खाद — A substance added to soil to supply nutrients and raise crop yield; from Latin ferre ('to bear, produce').
    Example: Chemical fertilisers boosted yields during the Green Revolution.
  • Terrace farming · सीढ़ीदार खेती — Cutting a hillside into level steps so crops can be grown and rainwater held back on steep slopes.
    Example: Terrace farming turns Himalayan hillsides into staircases of paddy fields.
  • Subsistence farming · निर्वाह खेती — Growing mainly enough to feed one's own family, with little or nothing left to sell; from Latin subsistere ('to hold out, survive').
    Example: A subsistence farmer keeps most of the harvest to eat.
  • Horticulture · बागवानी / उद्यान-कृषि — The cultivation of fruits, vegetables and flowers; from Latin hortus ('garden') + cultura ('cultivation').
    Example: Growing mangoes and vegetables for sale is horticulture.
  • Pesticide · कीटनाशक — A chemical used to kill pests that damage crops; from Latin pestis ('plague') + caedere ('to kill') — the '-cide' ending means 'killer'.
    Example: Overusing pesticides can harm the soil and helpful insects.
  • Bund · बंध / मेड़ — A low raised embankment around or across a field that holds water in or keeps soil from washing away.
    Example: Farmers plant bananas on the bund between paddy fields.
