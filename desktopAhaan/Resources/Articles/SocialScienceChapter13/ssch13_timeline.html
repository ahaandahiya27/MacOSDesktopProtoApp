The farming year: kharif, rabi and zaid

How Indian farmers organise a single year around three cropping seasons, following the rhythm of the monsoons.

  • June–July: Kharif sowing
    The southwest monsoon arrives, and farmers sow kharif crops like rice, maize, cotton and bajra in the warm, rainy soil.
  • Sept–Oct: Kharif harvest
    As the southwest monsoon ends, the kharif crops are harvested. A good monsoon means a good harvest.
  • Oct–Nov: Rabi sowing
    In the cooler weather, farmers sow rabi crops like wheat, gram, peas and mustard, relying on irrigation and stored soil moisture.
  • March–April: Rabi harvest
    The winter rabi crops ripen and are harvested in spring — the golden wheat fields of Punjab are a famous sight.
  • Summer: Zaid crops
    In the short, warm gap before the next monsoon, fast-growing zaid crops like watermelon, muskmelon and cucumber are grown with irrigation.
