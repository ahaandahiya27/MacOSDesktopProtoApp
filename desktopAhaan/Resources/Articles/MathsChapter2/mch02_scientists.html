Minds Behind Arithmetic Expressions

Real mathematicians whose work lies underneath this chapter.

≈ 7 min read · for curious 12-year-olds

Mathematics was built by real people, often centuries apart, each adding one idea. Here are the minds behind the ideas in this chapter.

Robert Recorde (c. 1512 – 1558)

Welsh. Invented the equals sign (=) in 1557.

Robert Recorde was a Welsh physician and mathematician who wrote the first algebra book in English, The Whetstone of Witte (1557). Tired of writing the words 'is equal to' again and again, he introduced a pair of equal parallel lines as a shorthand — the '=' sign we still use — remarking that no two things can be more equal than two parallel lines of the same length.

💡 What they gave us

A good piece of notation, invented once, can outlive its inventor by centuries.

Johannes Widmann (c. 1460 – after 1498)

German. First to use the + and − signs in print (1489).

Widmann was a German mathematician whose 1489 arithmetic book is the earliest known printed work to use the '+' and '−' symbols. At first they marked surplus and shortfall in commercial sums rather than 'add' and 'subtract', but the symbols spread across Europe and never left.
