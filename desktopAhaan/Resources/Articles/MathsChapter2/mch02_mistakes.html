Common Mistakes in Arithmetic Expressions

The places students slip on this chapter — and the one-line fix for each.

&#8776; 5 min read &middot; revision-tier &middot; 5 fixes

These wrong answers come back year after year. None are silly — they are almost right, which is what makes them worth knowing. Read each, then cover the fix and try to spot what is off.

1. “You always evaluate an expression strictly left to right.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: Multiplication and division are done before addition and subtraction (unless brackets say otherwise). 30 + 5 × 4 = 50, not 140.

2. “Removing a bracket after a minus sign just drops the bracket.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: The minus flips EVERY inner sign: 100 − (15 + 56) = 100 − 15 − 56, and 500 − (250 − 100) = 500 − 250 + 100.

3. “Distributing means multiplying only the first term in the bracket.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: You must multiply EVERY term inside: 2 × (5 + 3) = 2×5 + 2×3, not 2×5 + 3.

4. “Subtraction is commutative like addition.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: It is not: 6 − 4 = 2 but 4 − 6 = −2. Only after rewriting as adding the inverse can you reorder safely.

5. “If you forget a number in a long sum, you must restart.”

🚫 The mistake

A belief that feels right — but misses a key detail.

The fix: By the associative and commutative properties, you can just add the forgotten number to your running total.
