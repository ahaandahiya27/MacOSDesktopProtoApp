Arithmetic Expressions: Key Words

Every term in this chapter, with a one-line meaning and an example.

&#8776; quick reference &middot; 10 terms

Knowing the exact words makes the maths click. Here is every key term from the chapter, defined plainly with an example you can picture.

Arithmetic expression

A mathematical phrase combining numbers with operations (+, −, ×, ÷) that evaluates to a single value.

Example: 5 × 25 is an expression with value 125.

Value (of an expression)

The single number an expression evaluates to.

Example: The value of 13 + 2 is 15.

Term

A part of an expression separated by + signs (after converting subtractions to adding the inverse).

Example: In 30 + 5 × 4, the terms are 30 and 5×4.

In Hindi: पद

Brackets

Symbols ( ) that force the enclosed expression to be evaluated first.

Example: 30 + (5 × 4) = 50, but (30 + 5) × 4 = 140.

In Hindi: कोष्ठक

Additive inverse

The number that, added to a given number, gives 0; it has the opposite sign.

Example: The inverse of 14 is −14.

Commutative property

Swapping the order of added terms does not change the sum: a + b = b + a.

Example: 6 + (−4) = (−4) + 6 = 2.

Associative property

Regrouping added terms does not change the sum: (a + b) + c = a + (b + c).

Example: (−7 + 10) + (−11) = −7 + (10 + (−11)) = −8.

Distributive property

Multiplying a sum by a number equals the sum of the individual products: a × (b + c) = a × b + a × c.

Example: 2 × (5 + 3) = 2×5 + 2×3 = 16.

Evaluate

To compute the value of an expression by carrying out its operations in the correct order.

Example: Evaluating 6 × 3 − 4 gives 14.

Order of operations

The convention that brackets come first, then ×/÷, then +/−.

Example: In 30 + 5 × 4, multiply before adding → 50.
