Story Mode — Arithmetic Expressions in Real Life

The same ideas from this chapter, but out in the world — in shops, on playgrounds, and in everyday choices.

≈ 6 min · see-it-everywhere level

Maths can feel like it lives only inside the textbook. It doesn't. Here are real situations where the ideas of "Arithmetic Expressions" quietly do the work.

The shopkeeper's change

When Irfan pays ₹100 for a ₹15 biscuit and ₹56 dal, the change 100 − (15 + 56) = ₹29 only comes out right if the purchases are bracketed together first. Drop the brackets carelessly and you get ₹141 — more than he paid. Order of operations is not abstract; it's the difference between correct and absurd change.

Socks before shoes

Manasa's mother says 'wear your hat and shoes' — order doesn't matter, she looks the same either way (like commutative addition). But 'wear your socks and shoes' — order matters! Shoes-then-socks is uncomfortable and wrong. Some real-world operations commute, some don't, exactly like maths operations.

Paying ₹432 with mixed notes

Kannan can pay ₹432 as 4×100 + 1×20 + 1×10 + 2×1, or as 8×50 + 1×10 + 4×5 + 2×1. Each way is an expression whose terms describe a different bundle of notes and coins — and all of them have the same value, ₹432.
