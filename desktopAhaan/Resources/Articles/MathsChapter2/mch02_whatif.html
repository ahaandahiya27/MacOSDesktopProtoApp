"What If?" — Arithmetic Expressions

Hypothetical questions with no single right answer. Think hard before you read the discussion.

≈ 7 min read · brain-stretcher level

The textbook tells you what is. The best way to really understand an idea is to ask what if? Pick a question below, pause and think for a minute, then read one possible discussion.

What if 1 — everyone ignored 'multiply before add'?

The question: What if there were no agreed order of operations and people just worked left to right?

🧠 Think first

Would 2 + 3 × 4 still have one answer?

One discussion path: Two people would get 20 and 14 for the same expression. Maths would stop being a shared language. The order of operations is a convention we all agree to so that one expression means one thing.

What if 2 — brackets had never been invented?

The question: Without brackets, how would you tell someone to add first, then multiply?

🧠 Think first

Could you always rewrite it another way?

One discussion path: You would have to spell every calculation out in words, or invent a different separator. Brackets are a tiny tool that lets one line of symbols carry an unambiguous order — they override the default rule on demand.

What if 3 — an expression could be read two ways?

The question: What if 100 − 15 + 56 were read as 100 − (15 + 56) by some and (100 − 15) + 56 by others?

🧠 Think first

Which gives the shopkeeper the right change?

One discussion path: The two readings give ₹29 and ₹141 — wildly different. Real money forces a single correct reading. This is why the structure of an expression (its terms) matters as much as the numbers.

📝 Stretch your thinking

None of these has one 'correct' answer — they are for arguing about. Try one on a friend or a parent and see whether their discussion path differs from yours.
