1. In a percolation test 200 mL of water drains through soil A in 8 minutes, while soil B passes the same 200 mL in 20 minutes. By how much is soil A's percolation rate greater, and which soil is more likely sandy? Soil

(A) 15 mL/min greater; soil B is sandy
(B) 5 mL/min greater; soil A is sandy
(C) 35 mL/min greater; soil A is sandy
(D) 15 mL/min greater; soil A is sandy ✔
(E) 10 mL/min greater; soil A is sandy
(F) 25 mL/min greater; soil A is sandy
The ideaPercolation rate = water volume / time; sandy soil has the largest particles so it percolates fastest.
Step by step
  1. Rate of A = 200 / 8 = 25 mL/min.
  2. Rate of B = 200 / 20 = 10 mL/min.
  3. Difference = 25 - 10 = 15 mL/min.
  4. Faster percolation means bigger gaps between particles.
  5. Largest particles = sand, so soil A is sandy.
AnswerOption (D) — 15 mL/min greater; soil A is sandy
Watch outPicking clayey reverses the link: clayey soil drains slowest, not fastest, so the high-rate soil is sandy.
Where you would use itBuilders run percolation tests before installing a soakaway or septic drain field.
