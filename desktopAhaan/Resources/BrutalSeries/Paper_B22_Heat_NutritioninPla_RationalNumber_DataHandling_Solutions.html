1. A clinical thermometer reads 98.6 degrees Fahrenheit. Using C = 5/9 x (F - 32), what is this in Celsius, and is it normal body temperature? Heat

(A) 38 C, a mild fever
(B) 42 C, dangerously high
(C) 37 C, and this is normal body temperature ✔
(D) 37 C, this is a high fever
(E) 36 C, slightly below normal
(F) 66.6 C, normal
(G) 40 C, normal
The ideaTo change Fahrenheit to Celsius, subtract 32 first, then multiply by 5/9; healthy body temperature is about 37 C.
Step by step
  1. F - 32 = 98.6 - 32 = 66.6.
  2. Multiply by 5: 66.6 x 5 = 333.
  3. Divide by 9: 333 / 9 = 37.
  4. So 98.6 F = 37 C, the normal body temperature.
AnswerOption (C) — 37 C, and this is normal body temperature
Watch out'66.6 C' forgets to multiply by 5/9 and just keeps the 'F - 32' figure - that is far hotter than any human.
Where you would use itDoctors and nurses read the same fever on either scale depending on the thermometer.
