1. When iron rusts, 56 parts of iron combine with 24 parts of oxygen by mass to form rust. If 90 grams of rust is collected, how much iron had rusted, and is rusting reversible? Physical & Chemical Changes

(A) 34 grams of iron; not reversible
(B) 63 grams of iron; not reversible ✔
(C) 80 grams of iron; not reversible
(D) 90 grams of iron; not reversible
(E) 27 grams of iron; not reversible
(F) 63 grams of iron; reversible
The ideaRust mass splits as iron:oxygen = 56:24 = 7:3, so iron is 7 out of 10 parts; rusting makes a new substance, so it is irreversible.
Step by step
  1. Total parts of rust = 56 + 24 = 80.
  2. Iron's share = 56/80 = 7/10 of the rust.
  3. Iron = 7/10 x 90 = 63 grams.
  4. A new substance (rust) forms, so the change is chemical and not reversible.
AnswerOption (B) — 63 grams of iron; not reversible
Watch out'90 grams of iron' wrongly assumes all the rust came from iron, ignoring the oxygen that joined in.
Where you would use itEngineers estimate how much steel is lost to rust on bridges and ships using such mass ratios.
