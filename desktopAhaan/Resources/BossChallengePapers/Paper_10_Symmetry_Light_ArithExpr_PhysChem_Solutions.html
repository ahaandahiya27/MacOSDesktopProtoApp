Q1Symmetry A figure has a line of symmetry when a mirror placed on that line shows that:
(A) the two halves are different shapes
(B) the figure has no halves at all
(C) one half is the exact mirror image of the other half ✓
(D) the figure becomes larger
Correct answer: C

The idea A line of symmetry folds a figure into two halves that match perfectly, each being the reflection of the other.

Place a mirror along the line
Look at the half plus its reflection
If it rebuilds the whole figure, the line is a line of symmetry.

Where you meet it in real life This is why you can fold a paper butterfly in half and the two wings land exactly on top of each other.

Why the other options miss
  • A: If the halves differed, the fold would NOT match, so it would not be a line of symmetry.
  • B: A line of symmetry splits the figure into two halves, so it must have halves.
  • D: Reflection does not change size; the halves stay the same size.
