Q2The Triangle & its Properties A forester leans a 5 m ladder against a tree so its foot is 3 m from the base of the trunk. By the right-angle (Pythagoras) property, how high up the trunk does the ladder reach?
(A) 8 m
(B) 6 m
(C) 4 m ✓
(D) 2 m
Correct answer: C

The idea In a right triangle, (height)² = (ladder)² − (foot distance)² = 5² − 3² = 25 − 9 = 16, so height = 4 m.

The trunk, ground and ladder form a right triangle
height² = 5² − 3² = 25 − 9 = 16
height = √16 = 4 m.

Where you meet it in real life A painter leaning a ladder against a wall uses this same right-angle rule to work out how high it will reach.

Why the other options miss
  • A: 8 m is just 5 + 3; you must use 5² − 3² = 16, giving 4 m.
  • B: 6 m does not fit; √(5² − 3²) = √16 = 4 m.
  • D: 2 m is 5 − 3; the Pythagoras rule gives √(25 − 9) = 4 m.
