Q1Heat A clinical thermometer (for body temperature) usually has a scale of about:
(A) 0–100°C
(B) 35–42°C ✓
(C) −10–110°C
(D) 0–50°C
Correct answer: B

The idea A clinical thermometer only needs to read human body temperatures, which stay near 37°C, so its scale is short: about 35–42°C.

Why the other options miss
  • A: 0–100°C is far wider than a body ever reaches.
  • C: −10–110°C is a laboratory thermometer's range.
  • D: 0–50°C wastes range below body temperature.

Where you meet it in real life The doctor's thermometer at home reads 35–42°C — enough to spot a fever above 37°C.
